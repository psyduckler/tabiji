## Book Description (HTML, ≤ 4000 chars)

Paste this directly into the KDP description field. Uses HTML formatting that Amazon renders.

```html
<h2>You don't need to travel less. You need a few specific habits.</h2>

<p>Most travel-safety advice for older adults is either useless ("Just be careful") or fearful ("Don't go"). <em>Travel Scams for Seniors</em> offers a third path: the calm, repeatable habits that protect independent travelers without shrinking the trip.</p>

<p>Each of nineteen chapters covers one scam category — from airport "porters" who want to disappear with your luggage, to fake hotel calls, to the "grandparent emergency" scam targeting your family at home — with ready-to-use refusal scripts, red-flag checklists, and printable appendices.</p>

<h3>What's inside:</h3>
<ul>
<li><b>The four pressures every scam uses</b> — and how to interrupt them in 30 seconds</li>
<li><b>Airport, taxi, ATM, and hotel safety</b> — exactly what to say and when to walk away</li>
<li><b>The family verification system</b> that stops "grandparent emergency" scams targeting your relatives at home</li>
<li><b>Phone, Wi-Fi, and QR-code traps</b> — the specific habits that protect your accounts without giving up your phone</li>
<li><b>Cruise and tour shopping pressure</b> — how recommended stores work, and when "guaranteed" doesn't mean what you think</li>
<li><b>Romance and "new traveling friend" scams</b> — the early warning signs and the calm refusal scripts</li>
<li><b>Lost passport, lost wallet, lost phone recovery</b> — step-by-step from the first hour through the trip home</li>
</ul>

<p><b>Plus printable appendices:</b> a Pre-Trip Scam Defense Checklist, an Emergency Contact Worksheet, a Travel Companion Planning Agreement, and a complete Scripts Library you can practice before you leave.</p>

<h3>Written for:</h3>
<ul>
<li><b>Older travelers</b> who want to keep traveling internationally with confidence</li>
<li><b>Adult children and caregivers</b> who want a shared plan that supports independence rather than replacing it</li>
<li><b>Solo travelers</b>, retired couples, group-tour participants, cruise passengers, and first-time international travelers over 60</li>
<li><b>Anyone who has ever said:</b> "I want to keep traveling — I just want to do it smart."</li>
</ul>

<h3>Why this book is different:</h3>

<p>This isn't a list of warnings. It's a calm field guide written for the way older travelers actually read: non-linearly, looking up specific situations when they need them. Every chapter is structured the same way: <b>Quick Take</b> summary up front, body discussion in the middle, <b>Red Flags</b> and <b>Scripts</b> callouts you can practice, <b>Recovery Steps</b> if something has already happened.</p>

<p>The book covers the full arc of a trip — preparation at home, airport arrival, daily money habits, hotels and tours, friendly strangers, real emergencies — with one consistent voice and one underlying principle: <b>scammers want speed and secrecy; you have time and the right to verify.</b></p>

<p><i>Travel changes the rules. This book gives you the new ones.</i></p>
```
