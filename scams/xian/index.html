The 7 Scams

Scam #1
Fake Terracotta Army Site & 'Shortcut' Tour
⚠️ High
📍 Outside Xi'an city center on the road to Lintong; fake 'Terracotta Museum' venues along the route, hotel lobby tour-booth referrals

Taxi drivers and cheap tour operators in Xi'an divert tourists to fake 'Qin Museum' or 'Warriors Heritage Park' venues along the Lintong road — purpose-built scam sites charging ¥100–¥200 entry that are not the real Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum — while cut-price 'all-inclusive' tours that do reach the real site spend 80% of the day at commission-paying jade factories and silk showrooms.

You book a ¥150 'Terracotta Warriors day trip' from a hotel lobby or a tout at Xi'an Railway Station. The driver is friendly and stops at a venue signed 'Qin Museum' or 'Terracotta Warriors Heritage Park.' Inside are rows of mass-produced warrior replicas and an entry charge of ¥100–¥200. Everything looks plausible if you have never seen photos of the real site.

The real Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum is at Lintong, 35 km east of Xi'an — administered by Shaanxi Provincial Bureau of Cultural Heritage, with official ticketing at ¥120 per adult. Cut-price tours that do reach the real site structure the day differently: 60–90 minutes at the actual museum, then three hours of mandatory shopping at 'government-certified' jade factories and silk showrooms where the guide earns a 20–30% commission on every sale.

Take Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza directly to Lintong — ¥8, departing every 20 minutes — and book the ¥120 entry ticket via the official WeChat mini-program 'Qin Shi Huang Di Ling Bowu Yuan' before you travel. If you want a guide, verify in writing that the tour has zero shopping stops; legitimate small-group tours on Viator or GetYourGuide run ¥300–¥500 per person. Reject any Terracotta tour below ¥200 from a taxi driver, hotel booth, or station tout.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥200 for a full-day Terracotta trip (genuine tours are ¥300–¥500 without shopping)
  • Itinerary includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' 'Tibetan medicine,' or 'tea ceremony' stops
  • Site name is 'Qin Museum,' 'Warriors Heritage Park,' or anything other than 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum'
  • Driver offers to 'take you a different way' for a 'better price'
  • Location is on the Xi'an side of Lintong — the real site is past Lintong town center

How to Avoid

  • Take Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza — ¥8, 1 hour, every 20 min.
  • Book entry ticket (¥120 adult) at the official WeChat mini-program 'Qin Shi Huang Di Ling Bowu Yuan' or Trip.com.
  • For guided tours, use Viator, GetYourGuide, or hotel Ctrip partnership; verify ZERO shopping stops in writing.
  • Reject any tour booth at Xi'an Railway Station or hotel lobby offering 'discount Terracotta tour' under ¥200.
  • Confirm the destination as 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum' — nothing else.
Scam #2
Muslim Quarter Tourist-Menu & 'Famous' Food Stall Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Muslim Quarter (Beiyuanmen / North Gate Market) main street, around Drum Tower south entrance, Huimin Street

Stalls on Beiyuanmen — Xi'an's main tourist food strip north of the Drum Tower — charge ¥30–¥50 for bowls that cost ¥15–¥25 one street over, while pushcart vendors run a 'Xinjiang cake' scam where a piece that looks small is weighed at ¥12 per gram, generating bills of ¥2,000 or more for what appeared to be a casual snack.

You follow the crowds into the Muslim Quarter's main strip, Beiyuanmen, drawn by the aromas and the parade of vendor stalls. A tout waves an English photo menu in your direction. You sit down and order yangrou paomo — lamb bread soup — and a plate of biangbiang noodles. The stall looks authentic and busy.

The bill arrives: ¥30–¥50 for the soup that costs ¥15–¥20 one street over on Huajue Xiang; ¥40–¥60 for noodles priced at ¥15–¥25 at local stalls. Pushcart Xinjiang cake vendors add a separate hazard: they cut what looks like a small slice of dried-fruit-and-nut bar and weigh it in grams at ¥12 per gram. The slice turns out to be 200+ grams — ¥2,400 for a snack you cannot easily hand back once it has been cut.

Walk one street off Beiyuanmen to Huajue Xiang or Xiyangshi Street to eat at local pricing, and confirm 'duo shao qian' (how much) with any vendor before they start cutting or serving. Skip pushcart Xinjiang cake vendors entirely; buy a pre-packaged version at a supermarket for ¥5–¥15. Community-verified honest venues include Lao Sun Jia (yangrou paomo, ¥45), Jia San Guantang Baozi (soup dumplings, ¥30), and the Yongxing Fang food court with posted prices.

Red Flags

  • Vendor pushes 'try a small piece' of Xinjiang cake from a pushcart on Beiyuanmen
  • No posted prices visible; prices quoted verbally after service begins
  • Tout outside restaurant pulls tourists in with English 'menu, menu'
  • Bowl/skewer/noodle prices 2–3x what's listed at residential venues one street over
  • Venue is directly on Beiyuanmen tourist strip with laminated English photo menu

How to Avoid

  • Eat one street off Beiyuanmen: Huajue Xiang, Xiyangshi Street, or Yongxing Fang food court.
  • Community-verified venues: Lao Sun Jia (yangrou paomo ¥45), Jia San (soup dumplings ¥30), Yongxing Fang (posted prices).
  • Don't buy 'Xinjiang cake' from a pushcart — supermarket pre-packaged versions are ¥5–¥15.
  • Confirm 'duo shao qian' (how much) BEFORE the vendor starts cutting or serving.
  • Reject any tout pulling you toward a restaurant with English-photo menus.
Scam #3
Xi'an Airport (XIY) Taxi Overcharge & Station Touts
⚠️ High
📍 Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY) arrivals, Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza, Xi'an North High-Speed Rail Station

Unlicensed taxi touts swarm Xi'an Xianyang International Airport arrivals quoting flat fares of ¥300–¥400 to the city center — two to three times the legitimate metered range of ¥120–¥160 for the 40-kilometer trip — while a secondary scam in the arrivals hall involves strangers asking to borrow tourists' unlocked phones and then walking off with them.

You clear immigration at Xi'an Xianyang Airport after a long flight and follow the ground transportation signs. A man in a crisp polo steps forward immediately: 'Taxi to Xi'an city center? Three hundred yuan, very fast.' XIY is 40 km northwest of the city center and the legitimate metered fare for the 45-minute ride runs ¥120–¥160.

The unofficial driver refuses to run the meter, quotes a flat price, and moves toward your luggage. A separate scam runs in the arrivals hall: a stranger approaches saying they lost their phone in a taxi and needs to make one call. They take your unlocked phone and either walk off with it or spend the thirty seconds logging into your WeChat Pay or mobile banking before handing it back.

Install DiDi before flying with your international number and use it at the official rideshare pickup zone — or take airport Metro Line 14 to Xi'an Railway Station for ¥17 in 70 minutes, which is completely scam-proof. If taking a licensed taxi, say 'da biao' (打表 — run the meter) before boarding and screenshot a DiDi fare estimate as a benchmark. Never hand your unlocked phone to a stranger in the arrivals hall for any reason.

Red Flags

  • Driver approaches inside terminal offering 'fixed price 300+' to Xi'an center
  • Driver refuses meter ('da biao'); claims it is 'broken' or 'too complicated'
  • Stranger at arrivals asks to 'borrow your phone' for any reason
  • Meter runs oddly fast or switches modes during daytime
  • No fapiao receipt offered on arrival

How to Avoid

  • Install DiDi before flying with international number; use at official rideshare pickup zone.
  • Take airport Metro Line 14 (¥17, 70 min) or shuttle bus (¥25, 60 min) for scam-proof option.
  • If licensed taxi, say 'da biao' (打表) before boarding; Traveler reports confirm ¥120–¥160 range.
  • Don't hand your unlocked phone to any stranger at the airport.
  • Photograph taxi plate number from rear windscreen on entering.

Scam #4
Bell Tower & Drum Tower Area Pickpockets
🔶 Medium
📍 Xi'an Bell Tower and Drum Tower squares, City Wall South Gate tourist crowd, Xi'an Ancient City Wall bike-rental area

Opportunistic pickpockets target the compression points around Xi'an's Bell Tower, Drum Tower, and City Wall South Gate — particularly during the 10 AM and 2 PM tour-bus peaks — using staged photo requests or direction questions to position themselves near pockets and bags while an accomplice completes the lift.

You arrive at the Bell Tower square at mid-morning. Tour buses have just unloaded and the underground pedestrian passage connecting all four sides is dense with groups moving in both directions. A stranger asks if you could take their photo and you reach for your phone to help.

While your attention is on the camera screen, a second person is positioned at your blind side near your bag's outer pocket or your back trouser pocket. The lift takes seconds. The same risk concentrates at the Drum Tower's Muslim Quarter south entrance where food crowds mix with tour groups, at the Yongningmen ticket queue, and at the Big Wild Goose Pagoda square during the evening fountain show.

Wear a crossbody bag zipped to the front during all Bell Tower, Drum Tower, and City Wall visits, and keep your phone in a zipped inner pocket — never a back pocket or an open outer compartment. Carry only ¥200 and one card for the day; leave your passport and backup card in the hotel safe. If theft occurs, report to 110 (police) and 12315 (English consumer protection line) within 24 hours.

Red Flags

  • Bell Tower underground passage during 10 AM / 2 PM tour-bus peak — compression moments
  • Drum Tower south entrance to Muslim Quarter where food crowds mix with tour groups
  • City Wall South Gate (Yongningmen) ticket queue with someone standing unusually close
  • Stranger asks for photo help or directions while a companion approaches blind side
  • Bike rental station at City Wall where deposits are handled manually in cash

How to Avoid

  • Wear crossbody bag zipped in front during Bell Tower / Drum Tower / City Wall walks.
  • Never place phone in back pocket or outer backpack compartment.
  • Carry only ¥200 + one card; passport + backup in hotel safe.
  • Bike rental at City Wall is ¥45 for 100 min (published) — verify deposit returned in full.
  • If theft occurs, call 110 and 12315 (English-line consumer protection) within 24 hours.
Scam #5
Jade 'Factory' Forced-Shopping Tour Stops
⚠️ High
📍 Along tour bus routes between Xi'an city center and Terracotta Warriors / Huaqing Hot Spring, 'government jade museums' bundled with Terracotta day trips

Xi'an day-trip bus tours priced under ¥250 per person routinely include mandatory 60–120-minute stops at 'government-certified jade factories,' traditional Chinese medicine clinics, and silk showrooms — where guides earn 20–30% commissions on sales — while serpentine and dyed quartz are marketed as imperial jade at five to twenty times their market value.

You book a full-day 'Terracotta Warriors and surroundings' tour at ¥150–¥200 from a hotel lobby or a railway station booth. The itinerary includes the Terracotta Army, Huaqing Hot Spring, and a brief mention of a 'jade cultural museum.' The price seems fair for a full day with transport included.

The bus stops at a 'government-certified jade factory' and the guide announces a 60-minute visit. A sales representative in an official-looking coat presents imperial jade pieces — actually serpentine or dyed quartz — at ¥2,000–¥20,000 each. The guide earns 20–30% of every purchase. The same pattern repeats at a traditional Chinese medicine clinic where doctors recommend expensive herbal supplements. The actual Terracotta Army receives 60–90 minutes — the shortest slot of the day. The 'government certification' is a standard business license that all companies in China hold, presented as something more official-sounding than it is.

Before paying for any Xi'an day tour, confirm in writing that the itinerary contains zero shopping stops — and expect to pay ¥300–¥500 per person for an honest full-day tour from a licensed operator like Viator or GetYourGuide. If you are already on a tour and the bus stops at a jade factory or TCM clinic, do not buy anything and wait the 45–60 minutes for departure — the guide has no legal power to compel purchases.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥250 per person for full-day Terracotta + surroundings — shopping stops are baked in
  • Itinerary lists 'jade museum,' 'TCM clinic,' 'silk factory,' 'tea ceremony,' or 'Tibetan medicine'
  • Guide presents 'government-certified' credentials of the shopping venue
  • Stop duration is 60–120 minutes with a 'sample' or 'demonstration' before sales begin
  • Pressure to buy 'for your family,' 'for health,' 'once-in-a-lifetime' pricing

How to Avoid

  • Book only tours with 'ZERO shopping stops' verified in writing before paying.
  • Use licensed operators: Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets, or hotel-partner Ctrip tours.
  • Expect ¥300–¥500 per person for honest full-day tours; anything lower includes shopping.
  • If already on a shopping-stop tour, simply don't buy — wait out the 45–60 min stop.
  • Report shopping-stop tours to 12315 (consumer protection English line) for documentation.
Scam #6
Tang Paradise / Huaqing Hot Spring 'Show' Ticket Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Tang Paradise (曲江大唐芙蓉园) evening water show area, Huaqing Palace hot spring evening shows, hotel-concierge Tang Dynasty Show packages

Hotel concierges and street touts in Xi'an mark up tickets to the Tang Dynasty Show, the Huaqing Palace 'Song of Everlasting Sorrow' musical, and the Tang Paradise water show by 50–100% above direct-booking prices of ¥298–¥498 — packaging them with overpriced buffets in bundles that reach ¥1,000 or more per person for a ¥298 show.

Your hotel concierge recommends the Tang Dynasty Show as the cultural highlight of any Xi'an visit and offers to book a dinner-and-show package for ¥850 per person. The suggestion sounds like a genuine service — he knows the venues and the traffic. The show itself is legitimate and worth seeing.

The problem is the price. A standard Tang Dynasty Show ticket costs ¥298 direct through the venue's WeChat mini-program; VIP seating is ¥398; and the official dinner-and-show bundle is ¥598. The concierge's ¥850 package adds a ¥250 commission markup. Street touts outside Tang Paradise and Huaqing Palace sell 'VIP' tickets at 1.5 to 2 times face value for standard seats. Some touts bundle a mediocre ¥300 buffet to push the total above ¥1,000 per person.

Book Tang Dynasty Show, Huaqing Palace evening show, and Tang Paradise water show directly via the venue's WeChat mini-program or Trip.com at published rates of ¥298–¥498 — ignore any hotel-concierge package above ¥600 per person. Metro Line 2 reaches the Tang Paradise area for ¥5; Huaqing Palace is served by the Lintong tourist bus (¥8) plus a short taxi. For the evening return, use DiDi rather than venue taxi touts.

Red Flags

  • Hotel-concierge show package over ¥600 per person (official rates ¥298–¥498)
  • Street tout offers 'VIP ticket' at the venue itself
  • 'Dinner + show' bundle over ¥700 per person for a buffet + standard seating
  • Tout's 'package' includes mandatory transportation markup (¥100+ per person)
  • Claim of 'last seats available' or 'English audio included at premium'

How to Avoid

  • Book Tang Dynasty Show or Huaqing Palace show directly via venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com.
  • Legitimate rates: ¥298 standard, ¥398 VIP, ¥598 dinner+show.
  • Ignore hotel-concierge packages over ¥600 per person — all are commission markups.
  • Metro Line 2 serves Tang Paradise area (¥5); Lintong tourist bus ¥8 + taxi ¥20 for Huaqing Palace.
  • For evening return, use DiDi (app-regulated) rather than venue taxi touts.
Scam #7
Xi'an Tea Ceremony / Tea House Invitation
⚠️ High
📍 Bell Tower plaza, Drum Tower approach, Muslim Quarter entrance, around tourist-heavy Yong Ning Gate

English-speaking strangers — often claiming to be students at Xi'an Jiaotong or Shaanxi Normal University — approach tourists at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, and the Muslim Quarter under the pretext of practicing English or offering a guided tour, then pivot to a 'traditional Shaanxi tea ceremony' at a side-street venue where bills of ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person arrive for a 30-minute tea tasting.

You are standing at the Bell Tower plaza when an attractive, English-speaking young person approaches. They introduce themselves as a local university student who wants to practice English, or they offer to show you the authentic parts of the Muslim Quarter that most tourists miss. The conversation is natural and the offer sounds genuinely useful.

Within twenty minutes, a 'traditional Shaanxi tea ceremony' is suggested at a venue a few streets north or east of the Drum Tower — up a staircase or through an unlabeled door. The tea is real and the presentation is elaborate. The bill arrives: ¥2,000 to ¥5,000 per person for a 30-minute tasting. Staff cluster near the exit. The tea itself is overpriced thirty to eighty times the residential rate at the same quality.

Any stranger who approaches at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or the Muslim Quarter with an invitation to tea, a 'cultural experience,' or a Muslim Quarter tour is running this scam — walk past with 'bu yao, xie xie' and do not stop to explain. For genuine Xi'an tea, visit the Defuxiang tea market southwest of the Bell Tower with posted prices, or a hotel-recommended teahouse with an English-language menu. If you are trapped, pay by credit card for a chargeback route and report to 12315 and 110.

Red Flags

  • English-speaking strangers at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter invite you to tea or cultural experience
  • Claim to be students at Xi'an Jiaotong or Shaanxi Normal University
  • Invitation to 'Muslim Quarter tour' that transitions to tea-house proposal
  • Venue is side street north or east of Drum Tower, not on a main walkway
  • No menu or prices shown before serving begins

How to Avoid

  • Walk past any stranger approaching at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter entrance.
  • Never accept 'cultural experience' or 'tea ceremony' invitations from strangers.
  • For genuine Xi'an tea, visit Defuxiang tea market (posted prices) or hotel-verified venues.
  • Pay with credit card for chargeback leverage if trapped.
  • Call 12315 (English-line) and 110 (police) for both scam reports.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Chinese Police (公安局) station. Call 110 (Police) or 120 (Ambulance). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at mps.gov.cn.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Beijing is at No. 55 An Jia Lou Road, Chaoyang District, Beijing 100600. For emergencies: +86 10-8531-3000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
