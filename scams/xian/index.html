The 7 Scams

Scam #1
Fake Terracotta Army Site & 'Shortcut' Tour
⚠️ High
📍 Outside Xi'an city center on the road to Lintong; fake 'Terracotta Museum' venues along the route, hotel lobby tour-booth referrals

Taxi drivers and hotel-lobby touts in Xi'an sell "Terracotta tours" that drop tourists at privately run replica venues far short of the real Qin Shi Huang Mausoleum site — charging ¥100–¥200 entry for mass-produced warrior replicas and relying on tourists unfamiliar with which institution is legitimate.

The real Terracotta Army site is a specific institution: the Qin Shi Huang Mausoleum Museum (秦始皇帝陵博物院) at Lintong, operated by the Shaanxi Provincial Bureau of Cultural Heritage, 35 km east of central Xi'an. That distance, and tourist unfamiliarity with the difference between the genuine site and convincing-looking replica venues along the route, is what the scam depends on.

A taxi driver, hotel-lobby tout, or informal guide offers a discounted "Terracotta tour." The vehicle pulls up to a venue displaying "Terracotta Warriors" or "Qin Museum" signage. Inside are mass-produced warrior replicas and ¥100–¥200 entry fees for something with no connection to the UNESCO site. A related scam is the all-inclusive bus tour that gives tourists 60–90 minutes at the real site before spending the rest of the day at shopping stops — jade factories, silk "museums," tea rooms — where the guide earns 20–30% commission on everything sold.

Public transit to the genuine site eliminates all scam exposure. Take Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza to Lintong for ¥8, then buy the ¥120 entry ticket at the gate or via the official WeChat mini-program 秦始皇帝陵博物院. For a guided experience, book through Viator or GetYourGuide and confirm in writing that the tour includes zero shopping stops; any reputable operator will provide that confirmation before payment.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥200 for a full-day Terracotta trip (genuine tours are ¥300–¥500 without shopping)
  • Itinerary includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' 'Tibetan medicine,' or 'tea ceremony' stops
  • Site name is 'Qin Museum,' 'Warriors Heritage Park,' or anything other than 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum'
  • Driver offers to 'take you a different way' for a 'better price'
  • Location is on the Xi'an side of Lintong — the real site is past Lintong town center

How to Avoid

  • Take Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza — ¥8, 1 hour, every 20 min.
  • Book entry ticket (¥120 adult) at the official WeChat mini-program 'Qin Shi Huang Di Ling Bowu Yuan' or Trip.com.
  • For guided tours, use Viator, GetYourGuide, or hotel Ctrip partnership; verify ZERO shopping stops in writing.
  • Reject any tour booth at Xi'an Railway Station or hotel lobby offering 'discount Terracotta tour' under ¥200.
  • Confirm the destination as 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum' — nothing else.
Scam #2
Muslim Quarter Tourist-Menu & 'Famous' Food Stall Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Muslim Quarter (Beiyuanmen / North Gate Market) main street, around Drum Tower south entrance, Huimin Street

Food stalls on Beiyuanmen Street in Xi'an's Muslim Quarter charge tourists 2–4x local prices for yangrou paomo, biangbiang noodles, and lamb skewers — and a nearby "Xinjiang cake" cart cuts a piece that weighs hundreds of grams and produces bills of ¥2,000+ for what looked like a snack.

The Muslim Quarter's main street, Beiyuanmen, is Xi'an's most-photographed food strip and its most tourist-facing. The stalls are visually spectacular, the foot traffic constant, and the prices calibrated to what foreign visitors expect to pay rather than what locals actually pay. Locals eat on the side streets — Huajue Xiang, Xiyangshi Street — one block away at half the price or less.

A bowl of yangrou paomo costs ¥15–¥20 around the corner but ¥30–¥50 on Beiyuanmen. Biangbiang noodles run ¥15–¥25 off the main street and ¥40–¥60 on it. Lamb skewers are ¥5–¥10 each at local stalls and ¥35 at tourist stalls. The Xinjiang cake scam operates separately: a cart displays a large wheel of dried-fruit-and-nut bar; a vendor cuts what looks like a small piece, weighs it on a scale in grams, and the total comes to ¥2,000+ because the "small piece" weighed 200+ grams at ¥12 per gram.

Asking the price before anything is cut, weighed, or served is the single most effective defense. Say "duo shao qian?" (how much?) before the vendor starts serving or cutting, and confirm the unit — per piece, per bowl, per 100 grams — not just the number. For the Xinjiang cake, do not buy from pushcart vendors: the same product is available pre-packaged at supermarkets for ¥5–¥15. For fair-priced Muslim Quarter dining, Yongxing Fang food court and Lao Sun Jia restaurant (established 1898) both have posted menus and consistent pricing.

Red Flags

  • Vendor pushes 'try a small piece' of Xinjiang cake from a pushcart on Beiyuanmen
  • No posted prices visible; prices quoted verbally after service begins
  • Tout outside restaurant pulls tourists in with English 'menu, menu'
  • Bowl/skewer/noodle prices 2–3x what's listed at residential venues one street over
  • Venue is directly on Beiyuanmen tourist strip with laminated English photo menu

How to Avoid

  • Eat one street off Beiyuanmen: Huajue Xiang, Xiyangshi Street, or Yongxing Fang food court.
  • Community-verified venues: Lao Sun Jia (yangrou paomo ¥45), Jia San (soup dumplings ¥30), Yongxing Fang (posted prices).
  • Don't buy 'Xinjiang cake' from a pushcart — supermarket pre-packaged versions are ¥5–¥15.
  • Confirm 'duo shao qian' (how much) BEFORE the vendor starts cutting or serving.
  • Reject any tout pulling you toward a restaurant with English-photo menus.
Scam #3
Xi'an Airport (XIY) Taxi Overcharge & Station Touts
⚠️ High
📍 Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY) arrivals, Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza, Xi'an North High-Speed Rail Station

Unofficial drivers inside Xi'an Xianyang International Airport terminal quote ¥300–¥400 fixed prices for a city-center ride the metered fare covers for ¥120–¥160 — and a separate phone-borrowing ruse at arrivals targets tourists into handing over unlocked devices.

Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY) sits 40 km northwest of the city center, making it a long, expensive ride that arriving tourists don't know the benchmark for. Unofficial drivers position themselves inside the terminal before the official taxi queue, approaching in polo shirts with confident offers: "Fixed price 300 yuan, very fast." The metered fare to central Xi'an runs ¥120–¥160 for the 45-minute ride.

The fixed-price offer is always 2–3x the metered rate. A related scam targets smartphones: a stranger at arrivals says they lost their phone in a taxi and needs to borrow yours to call it. They either walk off with the unlocked device or use the 30 seconds of contact to access WeChat Pay or mobile banking. The approach sounds urgent and sympathetic — that urgency is the mechanism.

The metered fare from XIY should be ¥120–¥160 — any driver quoting ¥300+ inside the terminal is unofficial. Walk past everyone soliciting rides inside the terminal, take Metro Line 14 to Xi'an Railway Station for ¥17 (70 minutes), or use DiDi from the official rideshare zone with app-regulated fares. Never hand your unlocked phone to a stranger at the airport for any reason; if someone needs help making a call, offer to dial for them while keeping the device in your hands.

Red Flags

  • Driver approaches inside terminal offering 'fixed price 300+' to Xi'an center
  • Driver refuses meter ('da biao'); claims it is 'broken' or 'too complicated'
  • Stranger at arrivals asks to 'borrow your phone' for any reason
  • Meter runs oddly fast or switches modes during daytime
  • No fapiao receipt offered on arrival

How to Avoid

  • Install DiDi before flying with international number; use at official rideshare pickup zone.
  • Take airport Metro Line 14 (¥17, 70 min) or shuttle bus (¥25, 60 min) for scam-proof option.
  • If licensed taxi, say 'da biao' (打表) before boarding; Traveler reports confirm ¥120–¥160 range.
  • Don't hand your unlocked phone to any stranger at the airport.
  • Photograph taxi plate number from rear windscreen on entering.

Scam #4
Bell Tower & Drum Tower Area Pickpockets
🔶 Medium
📍 Xi'an Bell Tower and Drum Tower squares, City Wall South Gate tourist crowd, Xi'an Ancient City Wall bike-rental area

Pickpockets work the underground passage at Bell Tower, the Muslim Quarter entrance at Drum Tower, and the City Wall South Gate queue during peak tour-bus arrivals — targeting tourists in the brief dense crowd moments when buses unload and everyone is looking up at the landmarks.

Xi'an's old-city core sees constant tour-group arrivals at the Bell Tower, Drum Tower, and Ancient City Wall from morning through afternoon. The underground passage beneath the Bell Tower connects all four sides of the intersection and concentrates foot traffic from multiple directions into a single corridor during the 10 AM and 2 PM bus-arrival windows. The South Gate bike-rental queue at the City Wall and the Drum Tower's Muslim Quarter entrance create similar pinch points during peak hours.

The theft is opportunistic rather than organized: a bag bumped in the crowd, a backpack unzipped from behind on the escalator, a phone lifted from a pocket while someone is looking up at the pagoda. The Big Wild Goose Pagoda fountain show in the evening draws large crowds in a dark open square — a second peak window. Xi'an is below Beijing and Shanghai for pickpocket frequency overall, but the old-city tourist sites are exceptions to that average.

Tourist sites create predictable moments of distraction — that is when the risk concentrates. Wear a crossbody bag zipped and held in front at all times in the Bell Tower passage, the Drum Tower area, and the City Wall queue, and keep your phone in a zipped inner pocket rather than a back pocket. For City Wall bike rental (¥45 for 100 minutes), keep the deposit receipt and ensure the full amount is returned when you bring the bike back. Split your valuables: carry only ¥200 and one card on your person, and leave your passport and backup cards in the hotel safe.

Red Flags

  • Bell Tower underground passage during 10 AM / 2 PM tour-bus peak — compression moments
  • Drum Tower south entrance to Muslim Quarter where food crowds mix with tour groups
  • City Wall South Gate (Yongningmen) ticket queue with someone standing unusually close
  • Stranger asks for photo help or directions while a companion approaches blind side
  • Bike rental station at City Wall where deposits are handled manually in cash

How to Avoid

  • Wear crossbody bag zipped in front during Bell Tower / Drum Tower / City Wall walks.
  • Never place phone in back pocket or outer backpack compartment.
  • Carry only ¥200 + one card; passport + backup in hotel safe.
  • Bike rental at City Wall is ¥45 for 100 min (published) — verify deposit returned in full.
  • If theft occurs, call 110 and 12315 (English-line consumer protection) within 24 hours.
Scam #5
Jade 'Factory' Forced-Shopping Tour Stops
⚠️ High
📍 Along tour bus routes between Xi'an city center and Terracotta Warriors / Huaqing Hot Spring, 'government jade museums' bundled with Terracotta day trips

Budget Xi'an day-trip bus tours include mandatory 60–120 minute stops at "government-certified jade factories" and "traditional Chinese medicine museums" where guides earn 20–30% commission on tourist purchases — and the "imperial jade" sold is typically serpentine or dyed quartz at 5–20x market price.

Xi'an's Terracotta Army day trips run at two price tiers. Budget tours at ¥150–¥250 per person for a full day look comprehensive in the listing — covering the Terracotta Warriors, Huaqing Hot Springs, and "historical sites." The economics of these tours don't work at that price without the guide earning significant commission, which means the itinerary is built around retail stops.

The Terracotta Army visit gets 60–90 minutes before the bus moves to the next stop: a "government-certified jade factory" with a showroom staffed by high-pressure salespeople, or a "traditional Chinese medicine museum" that is a private retail operation with a standard business license the guide presents as government endorsement. Tourists are held at each stop for 60–120 minutes. The jade sold as "imperial jade" is typically serpentine or dyed quartz; the "government certification" is meaningless. The guide's commission on each sale is 20–30%, which is why the pressure to buy is intense.

The only reliable defense is to filter the tour before paying. Book Terracotta tours only through operators who confirm in writing that the itinerary includes zero shopping stops — Viator, GetYourGuide, and hotel Ctrip partnerships all offer this, typically in the ¥300–¥500 per person range for an honest full-day tour. If you are already on a tour that stops at a jade factory or TCM clinic, simply do not purchase anything and wait for the bus to leave; the guide will be displeased but has no legal power to compel a purchase.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥250 per person for full-day Terracotta + surroundings — shopping stops are baked in
  • Itinerary lists 'jade museum,' 'TCM clinic,' 'silk factory,' 'tea ceremony,' or 'Tibetan medicine'
  • Guide presents 'government-certified' credentials of the shopping venue
  • Stop duration is 60–120 minutes with a 'sample' or 'demonstration' before sales begin
  • Pressure to buy 'for your family,' 'for health,' 'once-in-a-lifetime' pricing

How to Avoid

  • Book only tours with 'ZERO shopping stops' verified in writing before paying.
  • Use licensed operators: Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets, or hotel-partner Ctrip tours.
  • Expect ¥300–¥500 per person for honest full-day tours; anything lower includes shopping.
  • If already on a shopping-stop tour, simply don't buy — wait out the 45–60 min stop.
  • Report shopping-stop tours to 12315 (consumer protection English line) for documentation.
Scam #6
Tang Paradise / Huaqing Hot Spring 'Show' Ticket Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Tang Paradise (曲江大唐芙蓉园) evening water show area, Huaqing Palace hot spring evening shows, hotel-concierge Tang Dynasty Show packages

Hotel concierges and street touts in Xi'an sell "exclusive" evening show packages for the Tang Dynasty Show and Huaqing Palace performance at ¥800–¥1,200 per person — a pure commission markup on tickets that cost ¥298–¥498 booked directly through official channels.

Xi'an's major cultural evening performances — the Tang Dynasty Show at the Xi'an Hotel, the "Song of Everlasting Sorrow" at Huaqing Palace, and the Tang Paradise water show — are legitimate, well-reviewed experiences. Hotel concierges and street touts outside the venues position themselves as the most convenient way to secure tickets, offering "exclusive packages" or "VIP seating" that sounds more valuable than the standard option.

The Tang Dynasty Show's legitimate mid-tier ticket is ¥298; VIP is ¥398; the dinner-plus-show bundle is ¥598. Hotel concierge packages for the same show run ¥800–¥1,200 per person, with the difference going entirely to commission. Street flyer touts outside the venue sell standard seats as "VIP" at 1.5–2x face value. "Dinner + Show" bundles from unofficial sellers sometimes include a mediocre buffet priced at ¥400 that adds little value while inflating the total significantly.

All Xi'an evening performances sell directly at published rates through their own channels. Book the Tang Dynasty Show, Huaqing Palace evening performance, or Tang Paradise water show directly via the official venue WeChat mini-program or through Trip.com at the published ¥298–¥498 price — any offer above ¥600 per person through a hotel concierge or street tout is a markup. For transit to the venues, Metro Line 2 reaches the Tang Paradise area for ¥5; Huaqing Palace is accessible by the Lintong tourist bus (¥8) plus a short taxi.

Red Flags

  • Hotel-concierge show package over ¥600 per person (official rates ¥298–¥498)
  • Street tout offers 'VIP ticket' at the venue itself
  • 'Dinner + show' bundle over ¥700 per person for a buffet + standard seating
  • Tout's 'package' includes mandatory transportation markup (¥100+ per person)
  • Claim of 'last seats available' or 'English audio included at premium'

How to Avoid

  • Book Tang Dynasty Show or Huaqing Palace show directly via venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com.
  • Legitimate rates: ¥298 standard, ¥398 VIP, ¥598 dinner+show.
  • Ignore hotel-concierge packages over ¥600 per person — all are commission markups.
  • Metro Line 2 serves Tang Paradise area (¥5); Lintong tourist bus ¥8 + taxi ¥20 for Huaqing Palace.
  • For evening return, use DiDi (app-regulated) rather than venue taxi touts.
Scam #7
Xi'an Tea Ceremony / Tea House Invitation
⚠️ High
📍 Bell Tower plaza, Drum Tower approach, Muslim Quarter entrance, around tourist-heavy Yong Ning Gate

Attractive English-speaking strangers at Xi'an's Bell Tower and Drum Tower approach tourists with "English practice" or "Muslim Quarter tour" offers, then lead them to side-street tea houses where bills of ¥2,000–¥5,000 arrive — the same tea-house scam network documented across Beijing and Shanghai, running on Xi'an's core tourist landmarks.

Xi'an's version of the tea-house scam follows the same pattern documented in Beijing and Shanghai. The approach happens at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, and the Muslim Quarter — the highest tourist-density spots in the city. Attractive English-speaking women open conversation by asking to "practice English," offering to "show you around the Muslim Quarter," or requesting help with a photo. The invitation feels organic and personal.

Within 20 minutes, the conversation steers toward a "traditional Shaanxi tea ceremony" at a venue they know nearby. The tea house is always on a quiet side street north or east of the Drum Tower — always their choice, never a posted menu at the door. The tea is real. The bill is ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person for 30 minutes, invoicing legitimate-looking items at 30–80 times the residential market rate. Some scammers claim to be students at Xi'an Jiaotong University or Shaanxi Normal University to establish credibility at the opening.

The pattern is identical in Xi'an, Shanghai, and Beijing — the approach, the pivot, and the locked door are the same script. Any stranger who approaches you at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or the Muslim Quarter and steers the conversation toward "tea," "cultural experience," or a tour of their choosing is running this scam — walk past with "bu yao xie xie" and do not engage further. For genuine Xi'an tea culture, the Defuxiang tea market southwest of Bell Tower has posted prices and no solicitation; hotel-concierge-recommended teahouses with English-language menus are the other legitimate option.

Red Flags

  • English-speaking strangers at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter invite you to tea or cultural experience
  • Claim to be students at Xi'an Jiaotong or Shaanxi Normal University
  • Invitation to 'Muslim Quarter tour' that transitions to tea-house proposal
  • Venue is side street north or east of Drum Tower, not on a main walkway
  • No menu or prices shown before serving begins

How to Avoid

  • Walk past any stranger approaching at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter entrance.
  • Never accept 'cultural experience' or 'tea ceremony' invitations from strangers.
  • For genuine Xi'an tea, visit Defuxiang tea market (posted prices) or hotel-verified venues.
  • Pay with credit card for chargeback leverage if trapped.
  • Call 12315 (English-line) and 110 (police) for both scam reports.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Chinese Police (公安局) station. Call 110 (Police) or 120 (Ambulance). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at mps.gov.cn.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Beijing is at No. 55 An Jia Lou Road, Chaoyang District, Beijing 100600. For emergencies: +86 10-8531-3000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
