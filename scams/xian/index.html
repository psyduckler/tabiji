The 7 Scams

Scam #1
Fake Terracotta Army Site & 'Shortcut' Tour
⚠️ High
📍 Outside Xi'an city center on the road to Lintong; fake 'Terracotta Museum' venues along the route, hotel lobby tour-booth referrals

A taxi or cheap tour from Xi'an drops you at a roadside "Terracotta Museum" charging ¥100–¥200 entry — entire fake venues with mass-produced warrior replicas — while the real Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum is 35 km east at Lintong; budget tours that include the real site bury it under 80% shopping stops.

The Terracotta Warriors are one of China's most visited attractions, and the scam ecosystem around them is proportionally large. Touts at Xi'an Railway Station and hotel lobby tour booths offer day trips at ¥100–¥200 that seem like great deals. Taxi drivers outside the station offer "I take you to Terracotta, fixed price." The real site — the Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum — is at Lintong, 35 km east of the city center, operated by Shaanxi Provincial Bureau of Cultural Heritage.

Two distinct scams exist. In the first, the taxi or bus stops at a roadside venue with a sign reading "Terracotta Warriors" or "Qin Museum" — an entirely fake archaeological site with mass-produced replicas and a ¥100–¥200 entry fee. The building looks substantial; the "warriors" are convincing from a distance. You only realize you've been fooled when you look for the famous Pit 1 and it isn't there. In the second variant, the tour does reach the real site — but only for 60–90 minutes before returning to mandatory jade factory, silk "museum," and tea-ceremony stops where the guide earns 20–30% commission on purchases.

The scam-proof route: take the official Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza — ¥8 one-way, departures every 20 minutes, 1 hour to the real museum entrance. Buy your entry ticket at ¥120 adult via the official WeChat mini-program "Qin Shi Huang Di Ling Bowu Yuan" (秦始皇帝陵博物院) or Trip.com. For a guided experience, verify in writing that the tour includes "zero shopping stops" and budget ¥300–¥500 per person; anything priced lower includes commission stops because the economics require it.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥200 for a full-day Terracotta trip (genuine tours are ¥300–¥500 without shopping)
  • Itinerary includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' 'Tibetan medicine,' or 'tea ceremony' stops
  • Site name is 'Qin Museum,' 'Warriors Heritage Park,' or anything other than 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum'
  • Driver offers to 'take you a different way' for a 'better price'
  • Location is on the Xi'an side of Lintong — the real site is past Lintong town center

How to Avoid

  • Take Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza — ¥8, 1 hour, every 20 min.
  • Book entry ticket (¥120 adult) at the official WeChat mini-program 'Qin Shi Huang Di Ling Bowu Yuan' or Trip.com.
  • For guided tours, use Viator, GetYourGuide, or hotel Ctrip partnership; verify ZERO shopping stops in writing.
  • Reject any tour booth at Xi'an Railway Station or hotel lobby offering 'discount Terracotta tour' under ¥200.
  • Confirm the destination as 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum' — nothing else.
Scam #2
Muslim Quarter Tourist-Menu & 'Famous' Food Stall Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Muslim Quarter (Beiyuanmen / North Gate Market) main street, around Drum Tower south entrance, Huimin Street

Beiyuanmen's tourist-facing food stalls charge 2–3x residential prices — ¥30–¥50 for yangrou paomo that costs ¥15–¥20 one street back — and the Xinjiang cake pushcart extracts ¥2,000+ for what looks like a small snack by weighing a deceptively large piece at ¥12 per gram.

The Muslim Quarter's main street — Beiyuanmen, running north from the Drum Tower — is Xi'an's most photographed food street and a genuine part of the city's Hui Muslim cultural heritage. It's also an overtouristed corridor where stalls nearest the tourist flow charge prices that local residents don't pay. The legitimate food culture exists; the issue is knowing which venues are tourist-facing markups. Local residents consistently eat one street off the main strip.

Common overcharges on the Beiyuanmen tourist strip: ¥30–¥50 for a bowl of yangrou paomo (lamb bread soup) versus ¥15–¥20 one street over; ¥40–¥60 for biangbiang noodles versus ¥15–¥25 at residential venues; ¥35 per lamb skewer versus ¥5–¥10 elsewhere. A distinct and more aggressive scam is the Xinjiang cake cart — a wheel of dried fruit and nut bar displayed attractively on the street. A vendor cuts what looks like a small piece, but it weighs 200+ grams at ¥12 per gram, producing a ¥2,000–¥3,000 bill for a snack. The vendor starts cutting before you confirm the price; by the time you see the scale, the piece is already cut.

Ask "duo shao qian" (how much) and confirm the price before any vendor starts cutting, pouring, or serving — once food is prepared your negotiating position is gone. For Xinjiang cake: don't engage with pushcart vendors on Beiyuanmen; buy a pre-packaged version at a supermarket for ¥5–¥15 instead. For honest food, walk one street off Beiyuanmen to Huajue Xiang or Xiyangshi Street. Community-verified venues: Lao Sun Jia (established 1898, yangrou paomo ¥45/bowl), Jia San Guantang Baozi (¥30/basket of soup dumplings), and Yongxing Fang food court (2018, posted prices throughout).

Red Flags

  • Vendor pushes 'try a small piece' of Xinjiang cake from a pushcart on Beiyuanmen
  • No posted prices visible; prices quoted verbally after service begins
  • Tout outside restaurant pulls tourists in with English 'menu, menu'
  • Bowl/skewer/noodle prices 2–3x what's listed at residential venues one street over
  • Venue is directly on Beiyuanmen tourist strip with laminated English photo menu

How to Avoid

  • Eat one street off Beiyuanmen: Huajue Xiang, Xiyangshi Street, or Yongxing Fang food court.
  • Community-verified venues: Lao Sun Jia (yangrou paomo ¥45), Jia San (soup dumplings ¥30), Yongxing Fang (posted prices).
  • Don't buy 'Xinjiang cake' from a pushcart — supermarket pre-packaged versions are ¥5–¥15.
  • Confirm 'duo shao qian' (how much) BEFORE the vendor starts cutting or serving.
  • Reject any tout pulling you toward a restaurant with English-photo menus.
Scam #3
Xi'an Airport (XIY) Taxi Overcharge & Station Touts
⚠️ High
📍 Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY) arrivals, Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza, Xi'an North High-Speed Rail Station

Unofficial drivers at Xi'an Xianyang Airport (XIY) quote ¥300–¥400 for the city-center run that costs ¥120–¥160 by metered licensed cab — and a documented arrivals-hall scam has strangers asking to borrow your phone then walking off with it or logging into your WeChat.

Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY) sits 40 km north-west of the city center — a 45-minute ride that costs ¥120–¥160 by metered licensed taxi. The arrivals hall is where unofficial drivers work most aggressively, approaching travelers as they exit customs with luggage: "Taxi? Fixed price 300 yuan, very fast." The quote sounds authoritative because it's presented as a flat rate, implying the meter would cost more — when in reality it's two to three times the legitimate fare.

Licensed drivers at the official taxi queue can also overcharge: refusing the meter, claiming it's "broken," or padding the route while the passenger doesn't know the road. DiDi's GPS-verified routing makes route-padding unworkable on the app, which is why unofficial drivers specifically avoid it. A secondary scam documented at XIY arrivals involves strangers asking to borrow your phone — "I lost mine in a taxi, can I make a call?" — who then either walk off with the device or, if it's unlocked, log into your WeChat or mobile banking before returning it.

Install DiDi before your flight with an international number and order at the official rideshare pickup zone — regulated fares and a digital receipt protect you on every ride. The scam-proof ground option is the airport Metro Line 14, running to Xi'an Railway Station for ¥17 in 70 minutes. If taking a licensed taxi, say "da biao" (打表) before boarding and screenshot a DiDi fare estimate as a price reference. Don't hand your unlocked phone to any stranger at the airport for any reason.

Red Flags

  • Driver approaches inside terminal offering 'fixed price 300+' to Xi'an center
  • Driver refuses meter ('da biao'); claims it is 'broken' or 'too complicated'
  • Stranger at arrivals asks to 'borrow your phone' for any reason
  • Meter runs oddly fast or switches modes during daytime
  • No fapiao receipt offered on arrival

How to Avoid

  • Install DiDi before flying with international number; use at official rideshare pickup zone.
  • Take airport Metro Line 14 (¥17, 70 min) or shuttle bus (¥25, 60 min) for scam-proof option.
  • If licensed taxi, say 'da biao' (打表) before boarding; Traveler reports confirm ¥120–¥160 range.
  • Don't hand your unlocked phone to any stranger at the airport.
  • Photograph taxi plate number from rear windscreen on entering.

Scam #4
Bell Tower & Drum Tower Area Pickpockets
🔶 Medium
📍 Xi'an Bell Tower and Drum Tower squares, City Wall South Gate tourist crowd, Xi'an Ancient City Wall bike-rental area

Opportunistic pickpockets work Bell Tower's underground tunnel, the Drum Tower–Muslim Quarter south entrance, and the City Wall South Gate ticket queue during tour-bus arrival peaks — Xi'an's tourist core runs a concentrated window of crowd-assisted theft between 10 AM and 2 PM on summer and holiday days.

Xi'an's old-city core — Bell Tower, Drum Tower, the Ancient City Wall, and Big Wild Goose Pagoda — draws massive tour-group traffic, particularly in summer peak and during Spring Festival. Unlike organized pickpocket gangs in other cities, Xi'an's theft pattern is largely opportunistic: individuals watching for unguarded pockets and unzipped bags during the natural compression moments in tourist flow, rather than coordinated teams running specific plays.

The highest-risk moments: Bell Tower's underground passage (the pedestrian tunnel connecting all four exits) during the 10 AM and 2 PM tour-bus arrival peaks, when the corridor compresses with slow-moving crowds and backpacks are at arm's reach; the Drum Tower south entrance to the Muslim Quarter, where food-stall crowds and tour groups merge; the City Wall South Gate (Yongningmen) ticket queue and the bike-rental station, where tourists are distracted handling cards and deposits; and Big Wild Goose Pagoda square during the evening fountain show, when attention is directed upward.

Wear a crossbody bag zipped to the front during any Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or City Wall visit, and keep your phone in a zipped inner pocket rather than a back pocket or outer compartment. Carry only ¥200 and one card for the day; leave your passport and backup card in the hotel safe. For City Wall bike rental (¥45 for 100 minutes, published price), confirm the deposit is returned in full before leaving the station. Xi'an's pickpocket risk is generally lower than Beijing or Shanghai, but the tourist-core compression windows are real — the standard precautions eliminate the exposure.

Red Flags

  • Bell Tower underground passage during 10 AM / 2 PM tour-bus peak — compression moments
  • Drum Tower south entrance to Muslim Quarter where food crowds mix with tour groups
  • City Wall South Gate (Yongningmen) ticket queue with someone standing unusually close
  • Stranger asks for photo help or directions while a companion approaches blind side
  • Bike rental station at City Wall where deposits are handled manually in cash

How to Avoid

  • Wear crossbody bag zipped in front during Bell Tower / Drum Tower / City Wall walks.
  • Never place phone in back pocket or outer backpack compartment.
  • Carry only ¥200 + one card; passport + backup in hotel safe.
  • Bike rental at City Wall is ¥45 for 100 min (published) — verify deposit returned in full.
  • If theft occurs, call 110 and 12315 (English-line consumer protection) within 24 hours.
Scam #5
Jade 'Factory' Forced-Shopping Tour Stops
⚠️ High
📍 Along tour bus routes between Xi'an city center and Terracotta Warriors / Huaqing Hot Spring, 'government jade museums' bundled with Terracotta day trips

Xi'an day-trip bus tours routinely include mandatory 60–120-minute stops at "government-certified jade factories" where guides earn 20–30% commission — the jade is typically serpentine or dyed quartz sold as "imperial jade" at 5–20x market prices, and the "government certification" is a standard business license every shop in China holds.

Budget bus tours from Xi'an to the Terracotta Warriors and Huaqing Hot Spring are priced at ¥150–¥250 per person for a full-day trip. The economics only work for the operator if tourists spend money at commission stops along the route. The itinerary includes "jade museum," "silk factory," "Tibetan medicine clinic," or "traditional Chinese medicine demonstration" — each a sales venue that pays the guide 20–30% commission on whatever tourists buy. The guide presents the "government certification" of each venue as a mark of authenticity; in reality, every business in China holds a standard government business license.

At each stop, tourists are held for 60–120 minutes through a presentation — a jade carving demonstration, a silk weaving display — before being released into the attached shop. The jade sold as "imperial jade" (hetian or nephrite) is typically serpentine or dyed quartz at 5–20x market price. Prices are presented as heavily discounted with urgency language: "only two pieces left" or "this price is only for today's group." The actual Terracotta Army or Huaqing site visit gets a compressed 60–90 minutes to make time for the shopping stops. The same commission-stop model runs identically in Xi'an, Beijing, Chengdu, and other Chinese tourist cities.

The only reliable defense is to book only tours that explicitly state "zero shopping stops" in writing before payment. Licensed operators on Viator, GetYourGuide, and Tiqets offer these at ¥300–¥500 per person — the premium exists because the operator's revenue comes from your booking fee rather than shop commissions. If you're already on a tour that stops at a jade factory or TCM clinic, simply wait out the stop without purchasing anything; the guide cannot legally compel purchases.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥250 per person for full-day Terracotta + surroundings — shopping stops are baked in
  • Itinerary lists 'jade museum,' 'TCM clinic,' 'silk factory,' 'tea ceremony,' or 'Tibetan medicine'
  • Guide presents 'government-certified' credentials of the shopping venue
  • Stop duration is 60–120 minutes with a 'sample' or 'demonstration' before sales begin
  • Pressure to buy 'for your family,' 'for health,' 'once-in-a-lifetime' pricing

How to Avoid

  • Book only tours with 'ZERO shopping stops' verified in writing before paying.
  • Use licensed operators: Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets, or hotel-partner Ctrip tours.
  • Expect ¥300–¥500 per person for honest full-day tours; anything lower includes shopping.
  • If already on a shopping-stop tour, simply don't buy — wait out the 45–60 min stop.
  • Report shopping-stop tours to 12315 (consumer protection English line) for documentation.
Scam #6
Tang Paradise / Huaqing Hot Spring 'Show' Ticket Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Tang Paradise (曲江大唐芙蓉园) evening water show area, Huaqing Palace hot spring evening shows, hotel-concierge Tang Dynasty Show packages

Hotel concierges sell "exclusive" packages for the Tang Dynasty Show and Tang Paradise water show at ¥600–¥1,200 per person when direct booking costs ¥298–¥498 — and "Dinner + Show" bundles inflate the total to ¥1,000+ with a ¥400 buffet that doesn't justify the markup.

Xi'an's premier cultural evening shows — the Tang Dynasty Show at the Xi'an Hotel, the "Song of Everlasting Sorrow" musical at Huaqing Palace, and the Tang Paradise evening water show at the Qujiang lake park — are genuine and worth attending. The scam is in the distribution layer: hotel concierges, street flyer touts outside venues, and tour-package sellers all add substantial markup over the direct booking price, counting on visitors not knowing the official rates.

The Tang Dynasty Show's official price structure is publicly posted: ¥298 standard, ¥398 VIP, ¥598 dinner + show bundle. Hotel concierge "exclusive" packages for the same show run ¥800–¥1,200 per person. Street touts outside the Tang Paradise venue sell "VIP upgrade" tickets at 1.5–2x face value for what turns out to be standard seating. "Dinner + Show" bundles through unofficial channels tack on a ¥400 buffet — the total exceeds ¥1,000 per person for an experience the venue sells directly at ¥598. The same overpricing pattern runs across Xi'an, Beijing, and Shanghai for any tourist-facing evening performance.

Book the Tang Dynasty Show, Huaqing Palace evening performance, or Tang Paradise water show directly via the official venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com / Ctrip — published rates are ¥298–¥498 depending on tier, and the transaction is instantaneous. Ignore any hotel-concierge package above ¥600 per person; those are commission markups with no added value. For transport, Metro Line 2 serves the Tang Paradise area (¥5); Huaqing Palace is reached via the Lintong tourist bus ¥8 plus ¥20 taxi from the stop.

Red Flags

  • Hotel-concierge show package over ¥600 per person (official rates ¥298–¥498)
  • Street tout offers 'VIP ticket' at the venue itself
  • 'Dinner + show' bundle over ¥700 per person for a buffet + standard seating
  • Tout's 'package' includes mandatory transportation markup (¥100+ per person)
  • Claim of 'last seats available' or 'English audio included at premium'

How to Avoid

  • Book Tang Dynasty Show or Huaqing Palace show directly via venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com.
  • Legitimate rates: ¥298 standard, ¥398 VIP, ¥598 dinner+show.
  • Ignore hotel-concierge packages over ¥600 per person — all are commission markups.
  • Metro Line 2 serves Tang Paradise area (¥5); Lintong tourist bus ¥8 + taxi ¥20 for Huaqing Palace.
  • For evening return, use DiDi (app-regulated) rather than venue taxi touts.
Scam #7
Xi'an Tea Ceremony / Tea House Invitation
⚠️ High
📍 Bell Tower plaza, Drum Tower approach, Muslim Quarter entrance, around tourist-heavy Yong Ning Gate

English-speaking strangers at Bell Tower and Drum Tower ask tourists to "practice English" or offer to show them around the Muslim Quarter, then lead them to a side-street tea house and bill ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person for a 30-minute tasting — the same organized ring documented in Beijing and Shanghai runs identically in Xi'an.

The tea-house scam running at Xi'an's Bell Tower and Drum Tower area is part of the same organized network documented in Beijing, Shanghai, and other Chinese tier-1 tourist cities. The actors at Xi'an operate with a locally credible cover: they claim to be students at Xi'an Jiaotong University or Shaanxi Normal University and open with an offer to "show you around the Muslim Quarter" — a softer hook than the standard "practice English" opener. The real invitation appears within twenty minutes: "I know a traditional Shaanxi tea house nearby, very authentic, come with us."

The venue is always on a quiet side street north or east of the Drum Tower, reachable on foot in under five minutes. No posted prices are visible at the entrance. The tea served is real — Shaanxi varieties, properly prepared — but the bill at the end runs ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person for a 30-minute sitting that would cost ¥80–¥150 at a residential teahouse. Staff position themselves near the door when the bill arrives. Police historically take the venue's side when called, citing voluntary attendance; the practical remedy is a credit-card chargeback plus a 12315 complaint filed simultaneously.

Walk past any stranger who approaches at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, Muslim Quarter entrance, or Yongningmen with an invitation to tea, a "Muslim Quarter tour," or any "traditional Shaanxi culture" experience — the tea invitation always follows, regardless of how the initial contact was framed. For genuine Xi'an tea culture, the Defuxiang tea market south-west of Bell Tower has posted prices and no solicitation; hotel-concierge-verified teahouses with English menus are the other safe option. If you're already inside a venue and the bill has arrived, pay with a credit card and file a 12315 report and chargeback immediately.

Red Flags

  • English-speaking strangers at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter invite you to tea or cultural experience
  • Claim to be students at Xi'an Jiaotong or Shaanxi Normal University
  • Invitation to 'Muslim Quarter tour' that transitions to tea-house proposal
  • Venue is side street north or east of Drum Tower, not on a main walkway
  • No menu or prices shown before serving begins

How to Avoid

  • Walk past any stranger approaching at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter entrance.
  • Never accept 'cultural experience' or 'tea ceremony' invitations from strangers.
  • For genuine Xi'an tea, visit Defuxiang tea market (posted prices) or hotel-verified venues.
  • Pay with credit card for chargeback leverage if trapped.
  • Call 12315 (English-line) and 110 (police) for both scam reports.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Chinese Police (公安局) station. Call 110 (Police) or 120 (Ambulance). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at mps.gov.cn.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Beijing is at No. 55 An Jia Lou Road, Chaoyang District, Beijing 100600. For emergencies: +86 10-8531-3000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
