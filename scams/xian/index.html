The 7 Scams

Scam #1
Fake Terracotta Army Site & 'Shortcut' Tour
⚠️ High
📍 Outside Xi'an city center on the road to Lintong; fake 'Terracotta Museum' venues along the route, hotel lobby tour-booth referrals

Taxi drivers and cheap-tour operators drop tourists at fake "Terracotta Museum" venues on the Lintong road — mass-produced replica sites charging ¥100–¥200 entry that are not the real Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum — and budget "all-inclusive" tours spend 80% of the day at jade-factory commission stops with 60–90 minutes at the actual site.

The real Terracotta Army site is at Lintong, 35 km east of Xi'an, operated by Shaanxi Provincial Bureau of Cultural Heritage under the official name "Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum." Unofficial taxis and tout-booked cheap tours exploit the unfamiliar route to deliver visitors to look-alike venues named "Qin Museum," "Warriors Heritage Park," or similar — entire faux-archaeological sites with mass-produced replica warriors charging ¥100–¥200 for entry to something completely fraudulent.

The budget "all-inclusive" variant is a different operation: a ¥150 "Terracotta tour" spends 80% of the day at jade factories, tea "museums," and silk "factories" where the guide earns 20–30% commission on purchases. The real site gets 60–90 minutes before the bus moves on. Any itinerary that mentions jade, silk, TCM clinics, or tea ceremony alongside the Terracotta Warriors is a commission-shopping tour — the economics of the low ticket price only work because of what you spend at the stops.

Official Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) runs from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza directly to Lintong for ¥8 one-way — it cannot stop at a fake site because it operates on a government-contracted fixed route. Book your entry ticket (¥120 adult) through the official WeChat mini-program or Trip.com, and confirm zero shopping stops in writing before paying any licensed guide from Viator or GetYourGuide; any tour priced under ¥250 per person for the full day includes shopping stops as a structural feature of its economics.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥200 for a full-day Terracotta trip (genuine tours are ¥300–¥500 without shopping)
  • Itinerary includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' 'Tibetan medicine,' or 'tea ceremony' stops
  • Site name is 'Qin Museum,' 'Warriors Heritage Park,' or anything other than 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum'
  • Driver offers to 'take you a different way' for a 'better price'
  • Location is on the Xi'an side of Lintong — the real site is past Lintong town center

How to Avoid

  • Take Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza — ¥8, 1 hour, every 20 min.
  • Book entry ticket (¥120 adult) at the official WeChat mini-program 'Qin Shi Huang Di Ling Bowu Yuan' or Trip.com.
  • For guided tours, use Viator, GetYourGuide, or hotel Ctrip partnership; verify ZERO shopping stops in writing.
  • Reject any tour booth at Xi'an Railway Station or hotel lobby offering 'discount Terracotta tour' under ¥200.
  • Confirm the destination as 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum' — nothing else.
Scam #2
Muslim Quarter Tourist-Menu & 'Famous' Food Stall Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Muslim Quarter (Beiyuanmen / North Gate Market) main street, around Drum Tower south entrance, Huimin Street

Beiyuanmen, the Muslim Quarter's main tourist food street, charges two to three times local prices — ¥30–¥50 for lamb soup that costs ¥15–¥20 one street over — and Xinjiang cake vendors cut a "small piece" that weighs 200+ grams at ¥12 per gram, producing bills over ¥2,000 for what looked like a snack.

Beiyuanmen, the main pedestrian food street north of the Drum Tower, is Xi'an's most-photographed eating destination and where tourist pricing is most severe. Stalls on the main drag charge ¥30–¥50 for a bowl of yangrou paomo (lamb bread soup) that costs ¥15–¥20 at residential stalls one block east or west; biangbiang noodles go for ¥40–¥60 versus ¥15–¥25 off the main stretch; lamb skewers are ¥35 each rather than ¥5–¥10. Locals eat on Huajue Xiang and Xiyangshi Street, parallel to Beiyuanmen, not on the tourist main street itself.

The Xinjiang cake scam runs on pushcarts along Beiyuanmen. A vendor displays a wheel of dried-fruit-and-nut bar and offers to cut "a small piece." The piece looks modest but is weighed in grams — a "small piece" typically comes in at 200+ grams at ¥12 per gram, producing a bill of ¥2,400 or more for what appeared to be a light snack. Refusing to pay at that point puts you in a public confrontation the vendor has managed many times before.

Walking one block off Beiyuanmen to Huajue Xiang or Xiyangshi Street cuts prices in half at stalls where locals actually eat. Ask "duō shao qián?" (how much?) and confirm a specific per-bowl or per-piece price before any vendor starts cutting or serving — once food is weighed or on the plate, your negotiating position is gone; Lao Sun Jia and Jia San Guantang Baozi both have posted English menus and are community-verified for honest pricing.

Red Flags

  • Vendor pushes 'try a small piece' of Xinjiang cake from a pushcart on Beiyuanmen
  • No posted prices visible; prices quoted verbally after service begins
  • Tout outside restaurant pulls tourists in with English 'menu, menu'
  • Bowl/skewer/noodle prices 2–3x what's listed at residential venues one street over
  • Venue is directly on Beiyuanmen tourist strip with laminated English photo menu

How to Avoid

  • Eat one street off Beiyuanmen: Huajue Xiang, Xiyangshi Street, or Yongxing Fang food court.
  • Community-verified venues: Lao Sun Jia (yangrou paomo ¥45), Jia San (soup dumplings ¥30), Yongxing Fang (posted prices).
  • Don't buy 'Xinjiang cake' from a pushcart — supermarket pre-packaged versions are ¥5–¥15.
  • Confirm 'duo shao qian' (how much) BEFORE the vendor starts cutting or serving.
  • Reject any tout pulling you toward a restaurant with English-photo menus.
Scam #3
Xi'an Airport (XIY) Taxi Overcharge & Station Touts
⚠️ High
📍 Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY) arrivals, Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza, Xi'an North High-Speed Rail Station

Unofficial drivers inside Xi'an Xianyang Airport quote ¥300–¥400 for a city ride whose legitimate metered fare is ¥120–¥160 — and a separate phone-borrowing scam runs at arrivals, where a stranger asks to "call a taxi" on your unlocked phone and uses the access to transfer money through WeChat Pay or simply walks off with the device.

Xi'an Xianyang International Airport sits 40 km northwest of the city center, and the distance creates an information gap that unofficial taxi touts exploit at the arrivals exit. Men in polo shirts or carrying printed signs position themselves before the official metered taxi queue, quoting confident "fixed prices" of ¥300–¥400 for a ride whose real metered cost is ¥120–¥160 for the 45-minute journey into Xi'an. They refuse the meter entirely and project authority about knowing a "faster route."

A separate operation runs at the arrivals hall: a stranger claims to have lost their phone in a taxi and asks to borrow yours to make one call. Handing over an unlocked phone gives access to WeChat Pay, mobile banking, and the device itself — they walk a few steps away, transfer what they can in 30 seconds, and return it. Both operations target exhausted international arrivals who have just landed after a long-haul flight and are the least equipped to recognize and resist the pitch.

Installing DiDi before you fly and ordering at the official rideshare pickup zone provides GPS-verified routing and a fixed digital fare with no negotiation. Walk past anyone soliciting rides inside the terminal; if someone asks to borrow your phone, decline — the airport Metro Line 14 to Xi'an Railway Station (¥17, 70 min) is scam-proof if you prefer to skip the taxi question entirely; for a licensed metered taxi, say 打表 ("da biao") before getting in and screenshot a DiDi fare estimate as your reference.

Red Flags

  • Driver approaches inside terminal offering 'fixed price 300+' to Xi'an center
  • Driver refuses meter ('da biao'); claims it is 'broken' or 'too complicated'
  • Stranger at arrivals asks to 'borrow your phone' for any reason
  • Meter runs oddly fast or switches modes during daytime
  • No fapiao receipt offered on arrival

How to Avoid

  • Install DiDi before flying with international number; use at official rideshare pickup zone.
  • Take airport Metro Line 14 (¥17, 70 min) or shuttle bus (¥25, 60 min) for scam-proof option.
  • If licensed taxi, say 'da biao' (打表) before boarding; Traveler reports confirm ¥120–¥160 range.
  • Don't hand your unlocked phone to any stranger at the airport.
  • Photograph taxi plate number from rear windscreen on entering.

Scam #4
Bell Tower & Drum Tower Area Pickpockets
🔶 Medium
📍 Xi'an Bell Tower and Drum Tower squares, City Wall South Gate tourist crowd, Xi'an Ancient City Wall bike-rental area

Opportunistic pickpocket teams work the Bell Tower underground passage, Drum Tower south entrance, and City Wall South Gate during tour-bus peak hours — the densest crowd moments on Xi'an's main tourist circuit — with phones, wallets, and cameras disappearing in the distraction and compression.

Xi'an's historic core draws massive tour-group traffic, particularly during summer peak season and Spring Festival. The Bell Tower's underground pedestrian tunnel connecting all four sides compresses crowds during the 10 AM and 2 PM tour-bus arrival windows into a narrow, disorienting space; the Drum Tower's south entrance where Muslim Quarter food crowds merge with coach groups creates a second dense pinch point; the City Wall South Gate (Yongningmen) ticket queue and bike-rental station add a third, where cash changes hands in the open and crowds are stationary.

Pickpocket teams use the standard two-person approach — one bumps into you or asks a complex question while a partner works from the blind side. The Big Wild Goose Pagoda square during the evening fountain show creates another compressed cluster where phones go out for photos and the stationary crowd offers easy access from behind. Victims typically don't notice anything until they try to pay later.

Wearing your bag zipped on your front and keeping your phone in a zipped inner pocket through Bell Tower, Drum Tower, and the City Wall removes the most common access points. At the City Wall bike-rental station, confirm the deposit amount before paying and count it back in full when the bike is returned — deposit disputes are a documented secondary revenue source for some rental operators; Xi'an's pickpocket risk is lower than Beijing or Shanghai overall, but the three main tourist sites are concentrated enough to warrant the standard precautions.

Red Flags

  • Bell Tower underground passage during 10 AM / 2 PM tour-bus peak — compression moments
  • Drum Tower south entrance to Muslim Quarter where food crowds mix with tour groups
  • City Wall South Gate (Yongningmen) ticket queue with someone standing unusually close
  • Stranger asks for photo help or directions while a companion approaches blind side
  • Bike rental station at City Wall where deposits are handled manually in cash

How to Avoid

  • Wear crossbody bag zipped in front during Bell Tower / Drum Tower / City Wall walks.
  • Never place phone in back pocket or outer backpack compartment.
  • Carry only ¥200 + one card; passport + backup in hotel safe.
  • Bike rental at City Wall is ¥45 for 100 min (published) — verify deposit returned in full.
  • If theft occurs, call 110 and 12315 (English-line consumer protection) within 24 hours.
Scam #5
Jade 'Factory' Forced-Shopping Tour Stops
⚠️ High
📍 Along tour bus routes between Xi'an city center and Terracotta Warriors / Huaqing Hot Spring, 'government jade museums' bundled with Terracotta day trips

Budget Terracotta Army day trips routinely make mandatory 60–120 minute stops at "government-certified jade factories" where guides earn 20–30% commission — the jade sold is serpentine or dyed quartz at five to twenty times market price, and any tour priced under ¥250 per person structurally depends on what you spend at these stops.

Xi'an day-trip buses to the Terracotta Warriors and surrounding sites routinely include mandatory stops at "government-certified jade factories" or "traditional Chinese medicine museums." Tourists are held for 60–120 minutes of high-pressure sales before the bus continues. The "government certification" is a standard business license — required for all Chinese businesses — presented as exclusive government endorsement. The Terracotta Army visit itself gets 60–90 minutes while the shopping stops receive twice that.

The guide earns 20–30% commission on every purchase, making the tour's actual revenue model dependent on what you buy rather than what you paid for the ticket. The jade sold is typically serpentine or dyed quartz described as "imperial jade" at five to twenty times its market value. Tours priced at ¥150–¥250 per person for a full-day Terracotta trip can only sustain their economics with shopping-stop commissions — the itinerary description may not mention jade or silk, but the stops appear regardless.

Book through licensed operators on Viator or GetYourGuide and confirm zero shopping stops in writing before payment — that written confirmation is the only enforceable filter. Expect ¥300–¥500 per person for an honest full-day Terracotta tour with no shopping stops; anything priced below ¥250 includes them as a structural feature — if you are already on a tour that stops at a jade factory, you have no obligation to buy and can wait out the 45–60 minutes on the bus without incident.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥250 per person for full-day Terracotta + surroundings — shopping stops are baked in
  • Itinerary lists 'jade museum,' 'TCM clinic,' 'silk factory,' 'tea ceremony,' or 'Tibetan medicine'
  • Guide presents 'government-certified' credentials of the shopping venue
  • Stop duration is 60–120 minutes with a 'sample' or 'demonstration' before sales begin
  • Pressure to buy 'for your family,' 'for health,' 'once-in-a-lifetime' pricing

How to Avoid

  • Book only tours with 'ZERO shopping stops' verified in writing before paying.
  • Use licensed operators: Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets, or hotel-partner Ctrip tours.
  • Expect ¥300–¥500 per person for honest full-day tours; anything lower includes shopping.
  • If already on a shopping-stop tour, simply don't buy — wait out the 45–60 min stop.
  • Report shopping-stop tours to 12315 (consumer protection English line) for documentation.
Scam #6
Tang Paradise / Huaqing Hot Spring 'Show' Ticket Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Tang Paradise (曲江大唐芙蓉园) evening water show area, Huaqing Palace hot spring evening shows, hotel-concierge Tang Dynasty Show packages

Hotel concierges in Xi'an sell "exclusive" Tang Dynasty Show packages at ¥600–¥900 per person for tickets that cost ¥298–¥498 direct — and touts outside Tang Paradise and Huaqing Palace sell "VIP upgrade" seats at 1.5–2× face value for standard positions in the same hall.

Three Xi'an evening shows draw cultural tourists seeking a traditional performance: the Tang Dynasty Show at the Xi'an Hotel, the "Song of Everlasting Sorrow" at Huaqing Palace, and the Tang Paradise evening water show near the Big Wild Goose Pagoda. All three have fixed-price tickets available directly through official channels — and all three support a secondary market of markup sellers operating through hotel concierge desks and street tout flyers outside the venues.

Hotel concierge "exclusive" packages for the Tang Dynasty Show run ¥600–¥900 per person for tickets that cost ¥298–¥498 direct — the markup is pure commission. Street touts outside Tang Paradise and Huaqing Palace sell "VIP upgrade" passes at 1.5 to 2 times face value for standard seating. "Dinner + show" bundles add a mediocre ¥300–¥400 buffet that pushes the total over ¥1,000 per person for a show priced under ¥500 when booked directly with a better restaurant chosen separately.

Book Tang Dynasty Show, Huaqing Palace evening show, and Tang Paradise tickets directly through the official venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com — the listed price is the real price. Ignore any hotel-concierge package priced above ¥600 per person and do not buy from touts outside the venue, who resell standard seats as VIP at double the face value; Metro Line 2 serves the Tang Paradise area for ¥5, so there is no reason to bundle transport into an overpriced hotel package either.

Red Flags

  • Hotel-concierge show package over ¥600 per person (official rates ¥298–¥498)
  • Street tout offers 'VIP ticket' at the venue itself
  • 'Dinner + show' bundle over ¥700 per person for a buffet + standard seating
  • Tout's 'package' includes mandatory transportation markup (¥100+ per person)
  • Claim of 'last seats available' or 'English audio included at premium'

How to Avoid

  • Book Tang Dynasty Show or Huaqing Palace show directly via venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com.
  • Legitimate rates: ¥298 standard, ¥398 VIP, ¥598 dinner+show.
  • Ignore hotel-concierge packages over ¥600 per person — all are commission markups.
  • Metro Line 2 serves Tang Paradise area (¥5); Lintong tourist bus ¥8 + taxi ¥20 for Huaqing Palace.
  • For evening return, use DiDi (app-regulated) rather than venue taxi touts.
Scam #7
Xi'an Tea Ceremony / Tea House Invitation
⚠️ High
📍 Bell Tower plaza, Drum Tower approach, Muslim Quarter entrance, around tourist-heavy Yong Ning Gate

English-speaking women posing as students approach solo tourists at Bell Tower and Drum Tower, offer to "show you the Muslim Quarter," and within 20 minutes invite them to a "traditional Shaanxi tea ceremony" — billing ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person at a quiet side-street venue for tea that costs ¥15–¥30 at any legitimate Xi'an teahouse.

Xi'an's tourist core runs a variant of the same teahouse scam organized by rings operating across China's major tourist cities. English-speaking women posing as students at Xi'an Jiaotong University or Shaanxi Normal University approach solo tourists at Bell Tower or Drum Tower with a warm opening — English practice, a photo together, an offer to show you around the Muslim Quarter. The pitch sounds like a natural, local thing to offer.

Within 20 minutes, a "traditional Shaanxi tea ceremony" invitation appears as a casual afterthought. The venue is always on a quiet side street north or east of the Drum Tower area — never a posted-menu establishment visible from the main road. Tea is served and tastes fine. The bill is ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person — 30 to 80 times the price at any legitimate Xi'an teahouse. Staff position near the exit while the amount is presented, and there is no practical way out without paying or causing a serious scene.

Any stranger who approaches at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, the Muslim Quarter entrance, or Yongningmen within the first ten minutes of conversation and mentions "tea," "Muslim Quarter tour," or "traditional Shaanxi culture" is running this scam — the approach is identical to Shanghai and Beijing every time. Walk past with a firm "bù yào, xièxie"; for genuine Xi'an tea, visit the Defuxiang tea market southwest of Bell Tower where prices are posted and no one recruits from the street, or ask your hotel concierge for a teahouse with an English-language menu and advance reservation.

Red Flags

  • English-speaking strangers at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter invite you to tea or cultural experience
  • Claim to be students at Xi'an Jiaotong or Shaanxi Normal University
  • Invitation to 'Muslim Quarter tour' that transitions to tea-house proposal
  • Venue is side street north or east of Drum Tower, not on a main walkway
  • No menu or prices shown before serving begins

How to Avoid

  • Walk past any stranger approaching at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter entrance.
  • Never accept 'cultural experience' or 'tea ceremony' invitations from strangers.
  • For genuine Xi'an tea, visit Defuxiang tea market (posted prices) or hotel-verified venues.
  • Pay with credit card for chargeback leverage if trapped.
  • Call 12315 (English-line) and 110 (police) for both scam reports.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Chinese Police (公安局) station. Call 110 (Police) or 120 (Ambulance). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at mps.gov.cn.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Beijing is at No. 55 An Jia Lou Road, Chaoyang District, Beijing 100600. For emergencies: +86 10-8531-3000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
