The 7 Scams

Scam #1
Fake Terracotta Army Site & 'Shortcut' Tour
⚠️ High
📍 Outside Xi'an city center on the road to Lintong; fake 'Terracotta Museum' venues along the route, hotel lobby tour-booth referrals

Taxis and discount tour operators from Xi'an city center divert tourists to fake "Terracotta Museum" venues charging ¥100–¥200 for replica warrior displays, while the real Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum is 35 km east in Lintong and reachable by the official Bus Line 5 for ¥8.

From Xi'an city center, taxis and "cheap tour" operators approach tourists at the railway station and hotel lobbies offering discount Terracotta trips. The real Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum is at Lintong, 35 km east, with ¥120 adult entry. Any offer under ¥200 for the full-day trip is the first sign something is wrong.

Some drivers pull up to venues marked "Qin Museum" or "Terracotta Warriors Heritage Park" that are not the real site — private operations displaying mass-produced warrior replicas for ¥100–¥200 entry. The related version does reach the real site: a ¥150 "Terracotta tour" spends 80% of the day at jade factories, silk workshops, and tea "museums" where the guide earns 20–30% commission on every purchase, leaving only 60–90 minutes at the actual warriors.

The official route requires no middleman. Take Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza directly to Lintong — ¥8 one-way, departures every 20 minutes, 1 hour — and buy your ¥120 entry ticket at the official WeChat mini-program or Trip.com. If you want a guide, verify "ZERO shopping stops" in writing before paying and budget ¥300–¥500 per person for an honest small-group tour.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥200 for a full-day Terracotta trip (genuine tours are ¥300–¥500 without shopping)
  • Itinerary includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' 'Tibetan medicine,' or 'tea ceremony' stops
  • Site name is 'Qin Museum,' 'Warriors Heritage Park,' or anything other than 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum'
  • Driver offers to 'take you a different way' for a 'better price'
  • Location is on the Xi'an side of Lintong — the real site is past Lintong town center

How to Avoid

  • Take Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza — ¥8, 1 hour, every 20 min.
  • Book entry ticket (¥120 adult) at the official WeChat mini-program 'Qin Shi Huang Di Ling Bowu Yuan' or Trip.com.
  • For guided tours, use Viator, GetYourGuide, or hotel Ctrip partnership; verify ZERO shopping stops in writing.
  • Reject any tour booth at Xi'an Railway Station or hotel lobby offering 'discount Terracotta tour' under ¥200.
  • Confirm the destination as 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum' — nothing else.
Scam #2
Muslim Quarter Tourist-Menu & 'Famous' Food Stall Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Muslim Quarter (Beiyuanmen / North Gate Market) main street, around Drum Tower south entrance, Huimin Street

Tourist-facing stalls along Beiyuanmen — Xi'an's Muslim Quarter main street — charge 2–3× local prices for yangrou paomo and biangbiang noodles, and pushcart "Xinjiang cake" vendors weigh small-looking pieces in grams to produce bills of ¥2,000+ for what appears to be a ¥20 snack.

Beiyuanmen, just north of the Drum Tower, is Xi'an's most-photographed food street and the first stop for most visitors. The stalls have English menus, bright displays, and visible queues — but prices run 2–3× what the same dishes cost one street over in the residential lanes where locals eat. Yangrou paomo that costs ¥15–¥20 off Beiyuanmen runs ¥30–¥50 on the main strip; biangbiang noodles jump from ¥15–¥25 to ¥40–¥60.

The sharpest trap is the "Xinjiang cake" pushcart: a compressed bar of dried fruit and nuts displayed on a wheel. Vendors invite you to "try a small piece" and cut what looks like a thin slice — then weigh it in grams. A piece that appears snack-sized can be 200+ grams at ¥12 per gram, producing a bill of ¥2,000 or more. By the time the scale shows the weight, the transaction is framed as complete.

Ask the price before anything is cut, poured, or served. Walk one street off Beiyuanmen to Huajue Xiang or Xiyangshi Street for local prices, and never let a pushcart vendor cut Xinjiang cake before you have confirmed the gram price in advance — or skip the cart and buy a pre-packaged version at any supermarket for ¥5–¥15. Community-verified spots with posted prices: Lao Sun Jia (yangrou paomo ¥45), Jia San Guantang Baozi (soup dumplings ¥30), Yongxing Fang food court.

Red Flags

  • Vendor pushes 'try a small piece' of Xinjiang cake from a pushcart on Beiyuanmen
  • No posted prices visible; prices quoted verbally after service begins
  • Tout outside restaurant pulls tourists in with English 'menu, menu'
  • Bowl/skewer/noodle prices 2–3x what's listed at residential venues one street over
  • Venue is directly on Beiyuanmen tourist strip with laminated English photo menu

How to Avoid

  • Eat one street off Beiyuanmen: Huajue Xiang, Xiyangshi Street, or Yongxing Fang food court.
  • Community-verified venues: Lao Sun Jia (yangrou paomo ¥45), Jia San (soup dumplings ¥30), Yongxing Fang (posted prices).
  • Don't buy 'Xinjiang cake' from a pushcart — supermarket pre-packaged versions are ¥5–¥15.
  • Confirm 'duo shao qian' (how much) BEFORE the vendor starts cutting or serving.
  • Reject any tout pulling you toward a restaurant with English-photo menus.
Scam #3
Xi'an Airport (XIY) Taxi Overcharge & Station Touts
⚠️ High
📍 Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY) arrivals, Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza, Xi'an North High-Speed Rail Station

Unlicensed taxi touts at Xi'an Xianyang Airport (XIY) quote ¥300–¥400 fixed prices for a ride the legitimate meter covers for ¥120–¥160, and a secondary phone-borrowing scam at arrivals uses the "I lost my phone in a taxi" opener to steal devices or access mobile banking.

XIY is 40 km north-west of Xi'an city center and the legitimate metered fare runs ¥120–¥160 for the 45-minute trip. Before you reach the official taxi queue, drivers in casual dress intercept you inside the terminal — "Fixed price 400 yuan, very fast" — and refuse the meter entirely. The confidence and location make them hard to distinguish from airport staff on first glance.

If you follow an unlicensed driver, you have no recourse when they demand more at the destination. A secondary scam at arrivals uses the opener "I lost my phone in a taxi, can I borrow yours to call?" — the stranger either walks off with your phone or uses it to access WeChat Pay or mobile banking before returning it.

The safest arrival route requires no cash negotiation. Pre-install DiDi before flying, walk past anyone soliciting rides inside the terminal, and order at the official rideshare pickup zone with a fixed digital fare of ¥120–¥150. Airport Metro Line 14 to Xi'an Railway Station (¥17, 70 min) is slower but entirely scam-proof. Never hand an unlocked phone to a stranger at arrivals for any reason.

Red Flags

  • Driver approaches inside terminal offering 'fixed price 300+' to Xi'an center
  • Driver refuses meter ('da biao'); claims it is 'broken' or 'too complicated'
  • Stranger at arrivals asks to 'borrow your phone' for any reason
  • Meter runs oddly fast or switches modes during daytime
  • No fapiao receipt offered on arrival

How to Avoid

  • Install DiDi before flying with international number; use at official rideshare pickup zone.
  • Take airport Metro Line 14 (¥17, 70 min) or shuttle bus (¥25, 60 min) for scam-proof option.
  • If licensed taxi, say 'da biao' (打表) before boarding; Traveler reports confirm ¥120–¥160 range.
  • Don't hand your unlocked phone to any stranger at the airport.
  • Photograph taxi plate number from rear windscreen on entering.

Scam #4
Bell Tower & Drum Tower Area Pickpockets
🔶 Medium
📍 Xi'an Bell Tower and Drum Tower squares, City Wall South Gate tourist crowd, Xi'an Ancient City Wall bike-rental area

Opportunistic pickpockets work the Bell Tower underground passage, Drum Tower's Muslim Quarter entrance, and the City Wall South Gate queue during tour-bus peak hours, lifting phones and wallets from outer bag compartments in the compression of passing crowds.

The Bell Tower underground passage, Drum Tower square, and City Wall South Gate (Yongningmen) draw massive tour-group traffic — especially at 10 AM and 2 PM when buses discharge simultaneously. The four-way pedestrian tunnel under Bell Tower creates the densest compression; the mixed food-crowd and tour-group flow at the Drum Tower's Muslim Quarter south entrance adds another high-risk window.

A stranger asks for directions or photo help while a companion approaches from your blind side, or the crowd compression itself provides cover as someone works your outer bag pocket or back pocket through a narrow passage. At the City Wall bike-rental station, some operators shortchange the deposit return — the published rate is ¥45 for 100 minutes with a deposit — relying on tourists not counting carefully before leaving.

Close the access points before entering the crowd. Wear your bag zipped across your front through the Bell Tower passage and Drum Tower area, and never put your phone in a back pocket or outer backpack compartment. Carry only ¥200 and one card — passport and backup card stay in the hotel safe. Verify your City Wall bike-rental deposit is returned in full before leaving the station.

Red Flags

  • Bell Tower underground passage during 10 AM / 2 PM tour-bus peak — compression moments
  • Drum Tower south entrance to Muslim Quarter where food crowds mix with tour groups
  • City Wall South Gate (Yongningmen) ticket queue with someone standing unusually close
  • Stranger asks for photo help or directions while a companion approaches blind side
  • Bike rental station at City Wall where deposits are handled manually in cash

How to Avoid

  • Wear crossbody bag zipped in front during Bell Tower / Drum Tower / City Wall walks.
  • Never place phone in back pocket or outer backpack compartment.
  • Carry only ¥200 + one card; passport + backup in hotel safe.
  • Bike rental at City Wall is ¥45 for 100 min (published) — verify deposit returned in full.
  • If theft occurs, call 110 and 12315 (English-line consumer protection) within 24 hours.
Scam #5
Jade 'Factory' Forced-Shopping Tour Stops
⚠️ High
📍 Along tour bus routes between Xi'an city center and Terracotta Warriors / Huaqing Hot Spring, 'government jade museums' bundled with Terracotta day trips

Xi'an day-trip bus tours routinely include mandatory 60–120-minute stops at "government-certified jade factories" and TCM clinics where guides earn 20–30% commissions — selling serpentine or dyed quartz as "imperial jade" at 5–20× market prices.

Budget Terracotta Warriors day tours at ¥150–¥250 per person build their economics on shopping commissions: the guide earns 20–30% of every purchase made at "jade museums," silk workshops, and traditional Chinese medicine clinics along the route. The itinerary's "cultural stop at a government-certified jade museum" is marketed as an attraction; the real Terracotta Army visit is compressed to 60–90 minutes to leave time for three or four shopping stops.

At each stop, tourists are held for 60–120 minutes of demonstration and high-pressure sales. The "imperial jade" on display is typically serpentine or dyed quartz sold at 5–20× market value. The "government certification" is a standard business license — every retail operation in China holds one — presented as official endorsement to give the venue false legitimacy.

The only reliable defense is a written guarantee before you pay. Book only tours that state "zero shopping stops" in the written itinerary through licensed operators like Viator, GetYourGuide, or hotel-partner Ctrip — and budget ¥300–¥500 per person, because honest tours without shopping commissions cost more to run. If you end up on a shopping-stop tour, you are not required to buy anything — wait out the 45–60 minute stop and reboard the bus.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥250 per person for full-day Terracotta + surroundings — shopping stops are baked in
  • Itinerary lists 'jade museum,' 'TCM clinic,' 'silk factory,' 'tea ceremony,' or 'Tibetan medicine'
  • Guide presents 'government-certified' credentials of the shopping venue
  • Stop duration is 60–120 minutes with a 'sample' or 'demonstration' before sales begin
  • Pressure to buy 'for your family,' 'for health,' 'once-in-a-lifetime' pricing

How to Avoid

  • Book only tours with 'ZERO shopping stops' verified in writing before paying.
  • Use licensed operators: Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets, or hotel-partner Ctrip tours.
  • Expect ¥300–¥500 per person for honest full-day tours; anything lower includes shopping.
  • If already on a shopping-stop tour, simply don't buy — wait out the 45–60 min stop.
  • Report shopping-stop tours to 12315 (consumer protection English line) for documentation.
Scam #6
Tang Paradise / Huaqing Hot Spring 'Show' Ticket Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Tang Paradise (曲江大唐芙蓉园) evening water show area, Huaqing Palace hot spring evening shows, hotel-concierge Tang Dynasty Show packages

Hotel concierges and street touts mark up Tang Dynasty Show, Huaqing Palace, and Tang Paradise evening tickets from their ¥298–¥498 official rates to ¥600–¥1,200 per person, with "dinner + show" bundles adding a mediocre buffet that pushes the total over ¥1,000.

The Tang Dynasty Show at the Xi'an Hotel, the "Song of Everlasting Sorrow" at Huaqing Palace, and the Tang Paradise evening water show are legitimate cultural performances popular with visitors. Official ticket rates run ¥298–¥498 per adult depending on tier, bookable directly via the venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com. A genuine mid-tier Tang Dynasty Show ticket is ¥298; VIP is ¥398; and the dinner + show bundle with the hotel's own buffet is ¥598.

Hotel concierges offer "exclusive" packages at ¥600–¥1,200 per person for the same seats — the markup is pure commission. Street touts outside the venues sell "VIP" tickets at 1.5–2× face value. "Dinner + show" bundles from third-party sellers add a ¥300–¥400 mediocre buffet that pushes the per-person total over ¥1,000 when the show alone costs ¥298.

Official channels take only a few minutes and save hundreds of yuan. Book the Tang Dynasty Show, Huaqing Palace evening show, or Tang Paradise water show directly via the venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com at published rates — and ignore any hotel concierge package priced above ¥600 per person. Transport: Metro Line 2 to Tang Paradise (¥5); Lintong tourist bus ¥8 plus taxi ¥20 for Huaqing Palace.

Red Flags

  • Hotel-concierge show package over ¥600 per person (official rates ¥298–¥498)
  • Street tout offers 'VIP ticket' at the venue itself
  • 'Dinner + show' bundle over ¥700 per person for a buffet + standard seating
  • Tout's 'package' includes mandatory transportation markup (¥100+ per person)
  • Claim of 'last seats available' or 'English audio included at premium'

How to Avoid

  • Book Tang Dynasty Show or Huaqing Palace show directly via venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com.
  • Legitimate rates: ¥298 standard, ¥398 VIP, ¥598 dinner+show.
  • Ignore hotel-concierge packages over ¥600 per person — all are commission markups.
  • Metro Line 2 serves Tang Paradise area (¥5); Lintong tourist bus ¥8 + taxi ¥20 for Huaqing Palace.
  • For evening return, use DiDi (app-regulated) rather than venue taxi touts.
Scam #7
Xi'an Tea Ceremony / Tea House Invitation
⚠️ High
📍 Bell Tower plaza, Drum Tower approach, Muslim Quarter entrance, around tourist-heavy Yong Ning Gate

Fluent-English speakers at Bell Tower and Drum Tower — sometimes claiming to be Xi'an Jiaotong University students — approach tourists for "Muslim Quarter tours" and steer them within 20 minutes to a side-street tea house with bills of ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person for a 30-minute tasting.

At Bell Tower plaza, the Drum Tower approach, and the Muslim Quarter entrance, young men and women fluent in English — sometimes claiming to study at Xi'an Jiaotong University or Shaanxi Normal University — approach tourists asking to "practice English" or "take a photo together." The opener quickly becomes an offer to "show you around the Muslim Quarter."

Within twenty minutes, a "traditional Shaanxi tea ceremony at a nearby teahouse" invitation appears. The venue is always on a quiet side street north or east of the Drum Tower — never on the main tourist walkway. The tea is real; the bill of ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person for a 30-minute tasting is 30–80× the price at any legitimate tea shop. The script runs identically in Beijing, Shanghai, and Xi'an.

The entire setup depends on following a stranger off the main road. Any person who approaches you at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or the Muslim Quarter entrance with an invitation to tea, a cultural experience, or a "Muslim Quarter tour" is running this scam — walk past with "bù yào, xièxiè" without stopping. For genuine Xi'an tea, visit the Defuxiang tea market south-west of Bell Tower (posted prices, no solicitation) or ask your hotel concierge for a verified teahouse.

Red Flags

  • English-speaking strangers at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter invite you to tea or cultural experience
  • Claim to be students at Xi'an Jiaotong or Shaanxi Normal University
  • Invitation to 'Muslim Quarter tour' that transitions to tea-house proposal
  • Venue is side street north or east of Drum Tower, not on a main walkway
  • No menu or prices shown before serving begins

How to Avoid

  • Walk past any stranger approaching at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter entrance.
  • Never accept 'cultural experience' or 'tea ceremony' invitations from strangers.
  • For genuine Xi'an tea, visit Defuxiang tea market (posted prices) or hotel-verified venues.
  • Pay with credit card for chargeback leverage if trapped.
  • Call 12315 (English-line) and 110 (police) for both scam reports.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Chinese Police (公安局) station. Call 110 (Police) or 120 (Ambulance). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at mps.gov.cn.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Beijing is at No. 55 An Jia Lou Road, Chaoyang District, Beijing 100600. For emergencies: +86 10-8531-3000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
