The 7 Scams

Scam #1
Fake Terracotta Army Site & 'Shortcut' Tour
⚠️ High
📍 Outside Xi'an city center on the road to Lintong; fake 'Terracotta Museum' venues along the route, hotel lobby tour-booth referrals

One of China's most documented tourist scams: a taxi or 'cheap tour' from Xi'an city center pulls up to a venue marked 'Terracotta Warriors' or 'Qin Museum' that is NOT the real Emperor Qin Shi Huang Mausoleum. The real Terracotta Army site is at Lintong, 35 km east of Xi'an, operated by Shaanxi Provincial Bureau of Cultural Heritage. Fake venues on the route show mass-produced warrior replicas, charge ¥100–¥200 entry, and are a complete fraud. confirms: 'there are fake terracotta attractions in xi'an that would scam tourists with the entire thing' — entire faux-archaeological sites built to deceive.

The related scam is the 'all-inclusive' tour: documents the bait-and-switch pattern where tourists book a ¥150 'Terracotta tour' that turns out to be 80% shopping stops at jade factories, tea 'museums,' and silk 'factories' — each paying commission to the guide. The real Terracotta Army visit gets 60–90 minutes before the bus moves to the next shopping stop.com app at ¥120 adult (¥100 low season).

For older travelers, the clean route: (1) take the official Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza to Lintong — ¥8 one-way, 1 hour, departures every 20 minutes; (2) book the Terracotta Warriors entry ticket (¥120 adult) via the official WeChat mini-program 'Qin Shi Huang Di Ling Bowu Yuan' (秦始皇帝陵博物院) or on Trip.com; (3) for a guided experience, book a licensed small-group tour via Viator, GetYourGuide, or your hotel's Ctrip partnership — verify ZERO shopping stops in writing before paying; (4) NEVER accept 'discount Terracotta tour' offers from taxi drivers, hotel-lobby booths under ¥200, or touts at Xi'an Railway Station.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥200 for a full-day Terracotta trip (genuine tours are ¥300–¥500 without shopping)
  • Itinerary includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' 'Tibetan medicine,' or 'tea ceremony' stops
  • Site name is 'Qin Museum,' 'Warriors Heritage Park,' or anything other than 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum'
  • Driver offers to 'take you a different way' for a 'better price'
  • Location is on the Xi'an side of Lintong — the real site is past Lintong town center

How to Avoid

  • Take Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza — ¥8, 1 hour, every 20 min.
  • Book entry ticket (¥120 adult) at the official WeChat mini-program 'Qin Shi Huang Di Ling Bowu Yuan' or Trip.com.
  • For guided tours, use Viator, GetYourGuide, or hotel Ctrip partnership; verify ZERO shopping stops in writing.
  • Reject any tour booth at Xi'an Railway Station or hotel lobby offering 'discount Terracotta tour' under ¥200.
  • Confirm the destination as 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum' — nothing else.
Scam #2
Muslim Quarter Tourist-Menu & 'Famous' Food Stall Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Muslim Quarter (Beiyuanmen / North Gate Market) main street, around Drum Tower south entrance, Huimin Street

The Muslim Quarter's main street (Beiyuanmen, just north of the Drum Tower) is Xi'an's most-photographed food-tourism street. It is also, per the 2025 community forums anchor: 'The famous Muslim Quarter Main Street is crowded and expensive. Eat here inst' ead — meaning locals avoid it. Common overcharges: ¥30–¥50 for a single yangrou paomo (lamb bread soup) that costs ¥15–¥20 one street over; ¥40–¥60 for 'famous' biangbiang noodles that cost ¥15–¥25; ¥35 per skewer of lamb that should be ¥5–¥10.

The Muslim Quarter also hosts a documented 'Xinjiang cake' scam — a dried-fruit-and-nut bar cut from a wheel displayed on a cart. Vendors cut a small-looking piece and weigh it in grams; the 'small piece' turns out to be 200+ grams at ¥12/gram, producing a ¥2,000+ bill for what looked like a snack. Community reports places the Xinjiang cake scam in the short list of 'first-time China trip' experiences to avoid.

For older travelers, the defense: (1) walk one street off Beiyuanmen to Huajue Xiang or Xiyangshi Street where locals eat at posted-price stalls; (2) for specific honest venues, Yongxing Fang (a 2018-opened food court showcasing Shaanxi cuisine with posted prices), Lao Sun Jia (established 1898, yangrou paomo ¥45 per bowl), Jia San Guantang Baozi (¥30 per basket of soup dumplings) are all community-verified; (3) NEVER buy 'Xinjiang cake' from a pushcart vendor — even if you want to try it, buy a pre-packaged version at a supermarket for ¥5–¥15; (4) for all food stalls, ask 'duo shao qian' (how much) and confirm the price before the vendor starts cutting or serving.

Red Flags

  • Vendor pushes 'try a small piece' of Xinjiang cake from a pushcart on Beiyuanmen
  • No posted prices visible; prices quoted verbally after service begins
  • Tout outside restaurant pulls tourists in with English 'menu, menu'
  • Bowl/skewer/noodle prices 2–3x what's listed at residential venues one street over
  • Venue is directly on Beiyuanmen tourist strip with laminated English photo menu

How to Avoid

  • Eat one street off Beiyuanmen: Huajue Xiang, Xiyangshi Street, or Yongxing Fang food court.
  • Community-verified venues: Lao Sun Jia (yangrou paomo ¥45), Jia San (soup dumplings ¥30), Yongxing Fang (posted prices).
  • NEVER buy 'Xinjiang cake' from a pushcart — supermarket pre-packaged versions are ¥5–¥15.
  • Confirm 'duo shao qian' (how much) BEFORE the vendor starts cutting or serving.
  • Reject any tout pulling you toward a restaurant with English-photo menus.
Scam #3
Xi'an Airport (XIY) Taxi Overcharge & Station Touts
⚠️ High
📍 Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY) arrivals, Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza, Xi'an North High-Speed Rail Station

You clear immigration at Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY) at 11 PM after a long-haul flight, and a man in a crisp polo is already at the terminal doors with a rehearsed smile: 'Taxi? Fixed price 400 yuan, very fast.'

XIY is 40 km north-west of the city center and the legitimate metered fare runs ¥120–¥160 for the ~45-minute ride. Unofficial drivers inside the terminal swarm arriving tourists with 'fixed price 300! 400!' and refuse the meter entirely. One traveler wrote: 'They are scams and trying to overcharge you. It's unbearable. Go straight to the real taxi stand' — the rule is universal across Chinese airports and applies at XIY. The Didi platform has s' afety controls that actually detect this — meaning DiDi's GPS-verified routing catches route-padding that licensed street taxis can still get away with.

The alternative transport options at XIY: (1) the airport Metro Line 14 runs to Xi'an Railway Station (¥17, 70 min) — slower but scam-proof; (2) the airport shuttle bus to Xi'an Railway Station (¥25, 60 min) and Longhai Hotel (¥30); (3) DiDi at the official rideshare pickup zone with app-regulated fares (¥120–¥150 estimate). flags a related airport scam: 'One scam thieves do is trick you into using your phone, such as I lost my phone in a taxi, and I need to call' — a stranger at arrivals asks to borrow your phone and either walks off with it or uses it to log into your mobile-banking or WeChat.

For older travelers arriving at XIY after an international flight, the protective playbook: (1) pre-install DiDi with international number before flying; (2) walk past anyone soliciting taxi rides inside the terminal; (3) use the airport Metro Line 14 or shuttle bus if you have time-flexibility; (4) if taking a licensed taxi, insist on 'da biao' (打表) and screenshot a DiDi fare estimate as reference; (5) NEVER hand your unlocked phone to a stranger for any reason — if anyone at the airport asks to 'borrow' your phone, it is a scam.

Red Flags

  • Driver approaches inside terminal offering 'fixed price 300+' to Xi'an center
  • Driver refuses meter ('da biao'); claims it is 'broken' or 'too complicated'
  • Stranger at arrivals asks to 'borrow your phone' for any reason
  • Meter runs oddly fast or switches modes during daytime
  • No fapiao receipt offered on arrival

How to Avoid

  • Install DiDi before flying with international number; use at official rideshare pickup zone.
  • Take airport Metro Line 14 (¥17, 70 min) or shuttle bus (¥25, 60 min) for scam-proof option.
  • If licensed taxi, say 'da biao' (打表) before boarding; Community reports confirm ¥120–¥160 range.
  • NEVER hand your unlocked phone to any stranger at the airport.
  • Photograph taxi plate number from rear windscreen on entering.

Scam #4
Bell Tower & Drum Tower Area Pickpockets
🔶 Medium
📍 Xi'an Bell Tower and Drum Tower squares, City Wall South Gate tourist crowd, Xi'an Ancient City Wall bike-rental area

Bell Tower, Drum Tower, and the Ancient City Wall — draws massive tour-group traffic, particularly in summer peak and during Spring Festival.

Bell Tower, Drum Tower, and the Ancient City Wall — draws massive tour-group traffic, particularly in summer peak and during Spring Festival.

The risk zones: (1) Bell Tower underground passage (the pedestrian tunnel connecting all four sides) during 10 AM and 2 PM tour-bus arrivals; (2) Drum Tower's Muslim Quarter south entrance where food crowds and tour groups mix; (3) City Wall South Gate (Yongningmen) ticket office queue and bike-rental station; (4) Big Wild Goose Pagoda square during evening fountain show. Community reports places Xi'an in the broader Chinese city-wide context of opportunistic theft rather than organized crime.

For older travelers exploring Xi'an's old-city core, the standard defense applies: (1) crossbody zipped bag in front during Bell Tower / Drum Tower / City Wall walks; (2) phone in zipped inner pocket, never back pocket; (3) split valuables — carry only ¥200 and one card, passport and backup card in hotel safe; (4) bike rental on the City Wall is ¥45 for 100 minutes (published price) and includes a deposit — ensure deposit is returned in full on bike return. Xi'an's security is generally better than Beijing or Shanghai for pickpocket crime, but still maintain the Spain-applicable posture.

Red Flags

  • Bell Tower underground passage during 10 AM / 2 PM tour-bus peak — compression moments
  • Drum Tower south entrance to Muslim Quarter where food crowds mix with tour groups
  • City Wall South Gate (Yongningmen) ticket queue with someone standing unusually close
  • Stranger asks for photo help or directions while a companion approaches blind side
  • Bike rental station at City Wall where deposits are handled manually in cash

How to Avoid

  • Wear crossbody bag zipped in front during Bell Tower / Drum Tower / City Wall walks.
  • Never place phone in back pocket or outer backpack compartment.
  • Carry only ¥200 + one card; passport + backup in hotel safe.
  • Bike rental at City Wall is ¥45 for 100 min (published) — verify deposit returned in full.
  • If theft occurs, call 110 and 12315 (English-line consumer protection) within 24 hours.
Scam #5
Jade 'Factory' Forced-Shopping Tour Stops
⚠️ High
📍 Along tour bus routes between Xi'an city center and Terracotta Warriors / Huaqing Hot Spring, 'government jade museums' bundled with Terracotta day trips

Xi'an day-trip bus tours routinely include mandatory stops at 'government-certified jade factories' or 'traditional Chinese medicine museums' where tourists are held for 60–120 minutes of high-pressure sales. The guide's commission on purchases is 20–30%, so the 'free' tour's real revenue model depends on you buying. 'Never buy anything that tour guides recommend, it's a psuedo-scam thing they have going with the shops where t' hey get commission. The jade sold is typically serpentine or dyed quartz sold as 'imperial jade' at 5–20x market prices.

is the canonical PSA: 'We were promised a Chengdu tour in the itinerary, but ended up getting 4 scam spots in 2 days' — the model runs identically in Xi'an, Beijing, Chengdu, and other tourist cities. The 'government' certification is meaningless — the 'museum' is typically a private retail operation with government-issued business licenses (all businesses in China need one) that the guide presents as 'government-approved.'

For older travelers, the only reliable defense is to BOOK TOURS WITH 'ZERO SHOPPING STOPS' IN WRITING before paying. Licensed small-group operators (Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets, and hotel-partnership Ctrip tours) offer this verification. Budget tours at ¥150–¥250 per person for a full-day Terracotta + surrounding sites trip ALWAYS include shopping stops — the economics don't work otherwise. Expect ¥300–¥500 per person for an honest full-day tour with no shopping. If you're already on a tour and it stops at a 'jade factory' or 'TCM clinic,' simply do not buy anything and wait 45–60 minutes for the bus to leave — the guide will be annoyed but has no legal power to compel purchases.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥250 per person for full-day Terracotta + surroundings — shopping stops are baked in
  • Itinerary lists 'jade museum,' 'TCM clinic,' 'silk factory,' 'tea ceremony,' or 'Tibetan medicine'
  • Guide presents 'government-certified' credentials of the shopping venue
  • Stop duration is 60–120 minutes with a 'sample' or 'demonstration' before sales begin
  • Pressure to buy 'for your family,' 'for health,' 'once-in-a-lifetime' pricing

How to Avoid

  • Book only tours with 'ZERO shopping stops' verified in writing before paying.
  • Use licensed operators: Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets, or hotel-partner Ctrip tours.
  • Expect ¥300–¥500 per person for honest full-day tours; anything lower includes shopping.
  • If already on a shopping-stop tour, simply don't buy — wait out the 45–60 min stop.
  • Report shopping-stop tours to 12315 (consumer protection English line) for documentation.
Scam #6
Tang Paradise / Huaqing Hot Spring 'Show' Ticket Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Tang Paradise (曲江大唐芙蓉园) evening water show area, Huaqing Palace hot spring evening shows, hotel-concierge Tang Dynasty Show packages

the Tang Dynasty Show at the Xi'an Hotel, the 'Song of Everlasting Sorrow' musical at Huaqing Palace, and the Tang Paradise evening water show — are popular with older travelers looking for a cultural evening.

the Tang Dynasty Show at the Xi'an Hotel, the 'Song of Everlasting Sorrow' musical at Huaqing Palace, and the Tang Paradise evening water show — are popular with older travelers looking for a cultural evening. The scam variants: (1) hotel concierges push 'exclusive' show packages at ¥600–¥900 per person for shows that cost ¥298–¥498 direct; (2) street flyer touts outside the venues sell 'VIP' tickets that are actually standard seating at 1.5–2x face value; (3) 'Dinner + Show' bundles include a mediocre buffet at ¥400 that takes the total to ¥1,000+ per person when the show alone is ¥298.

The Tang Dynasty Show (at the Xi'an Hotel) is the most-referenced for its packaging-vs-reality gap. A legitimate mid-tier ticket is ¥298 adult; 'VIP' is ¥398; dinner + show bundle is ¥598. Hotel concierge packages at ¥800–¥1,200 per person for the same experience are pure commission markups. references the broader ecosystem of tourist-evening-entertainment overpricing common across Xi'an, Beijing, and Shanghai.

For older travelers, the booking playbook: (1) book Tang Dynasty Show, Huaqing Palace evening show, or Tang Paradise water show directly via the official venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com / Ctrip at published rates (¥298–¥498); (2) ignore hotel-concierge 'exclusive' packages above ¥600 per person; (3) for transportation, Metro Line 2 runs to Yanta area near Tang Paradise (¥5), and Huaqing Palace is reached via the Lintong tourist bus ¥8 + taxi ¥20; (4) verify 'dinner + show' components — a ¥300 buffet rarely delivers better than a ¥100 residential restaurant.

Red Flags

  • Hotel-concierge show package over ¥600 per person (official rates ¥298–¥498)
  • Street tout offers 'VIP ticket' at the venue itself
  • 'Dinner + show' bundle over ¥700 per person for a buffet + standard seating
  • Tout's 'package' includes mandatory transportation markup (¥100+ per person)
  • Claim of 'last seats available' or 'English audio included at premium'

How to Avoid

  • Book Tang Dynasty Show or Huaqing Palace show directly via venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com.
  • Legitimate rates: ¥298 standard, ¥398 VIP, ¥598 dinner+show.
  • Ignore hotel-concierge packages over ¥600 per person — all are commission markups.
  • Metro Line 2 serves Tang Paradise area (¥5); Lintong tourist bus ¥8 + taxi ¥20 for Huaqing Palace.
  • For evening return, use DiDi (app-regulated) rather than venue taxi touts.
Scam #7
Xi'an Tea Ceremony / Tea House Invitation
⚠️ High
📍 Bell Tower plaza, Drum Tower approach, Muslim Quarter entrance, around tourist-heavy Yong Ning Gate

The same Dong Bei criminal ring documented in Beijing and Shanghai tea-house scams extends to Xi'an's tourist core.

The script is identical: attractive English-speaking Chinese women approach tourists at Bell Tower or Drum Tower asking to 'practice English' or 'take a photo together,' then invite the victim to a 'traditional Shaanxi tea ceremony' at a side-street venue. Bill: ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person for a 30-minute tea tasting. flags the Xi'an extension of the Beijing/Shanghai ring: 'You will also want to watch out for the tea house scam and possible variations on it (search the internet) b' ecause it runs in every tier-1 Chinese tourist city. Community reports places the tea-house scam alongside the fake Terracotta Army and airport taxi scams as Xi'an's top three.

The 2025 Xi'an pattern has some distinguishing features: the 'students' sometimes claim to be studying at Xi'an Jiaotong University or Shaanxi Normal University and offer to 'show you around the Muslim Quarter' as the opening move. The subsequent 'traditional tea house' invitation appears within 20 minutes. The venue is always on a quiet side street north or east of the Drum Tower area. The tea itself is real but overpriced 30–80x the residential rate.

For older travelers visiting Xi'an, the rule is absolute: ANY stranger who approaches at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, Muslim Quarter, or Yongningmen (South Gate) with an invitation to 'tea,' 'Muslim Quarter tour,' 'traditional Shaanxi culture,' or 'cultural experience' is running this scam. Walk past with 'bu yao xie xie.' For genuine Xi'an tea culture, visit the Defuxiang tea market (south-west of Bell Tower) with posted prices, or a hotel-concierge-verified teahouse with English-language menus.

Red Flags

  • English-speaking strangers at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter invite you to tea or cultural experience
  • Claim to be students at Xi'an Jiaotong or Shaanxi Normal University
  • Invitation to 'Muslim Quarter tour' that transitions to tea-house proposal
  • Venue is side street north or east of Drum Tower, not on a main walkway
  • No menu or prices shown before serving begins

How to Avoid

  • Walk past any stranger approaching at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter entrance.
  • Never accept 'cultural experience' or 'tea ceremony' invitations from strangers.
  • For genuine Xi'an tea, visit Defuxiang tea market (posted prices) or hotel-verified venues.
  • Pay with credit card for chargeback leverage if trapped.
  • Call 12315 (English-line) and 110 (police) for both scam reports.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Chinese Police (公安局) station. Call 110 (Police) or 120 (Ambulance). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at mps.gov.cn.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Beijing is at No. 55 An Jia Lou Road, Chaoyang District, Beijing 100600. For emergencies: +86 10-8531-3000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
