The 7 Scams

Scam #1
Fake Terracotta Army Site & 'Shortcut' Tour
⚠️ High
📍 Outside Xi'an city center on the road to Lintong; fake 'Terracotta Museum' venues along the route, hotel lobby tour-booth referrals

Taxi drivers and cheap-tour operators divert Xi'an tourists to fake "Terracotta Warrior" replica venues short of the real site — or sell "all-inclusive" tours that spend 80% of the day at commission-paying shopping stops.

You arrange a taxi or book a low-cost tour from Xi'an's city center to see the Terracotta Warriors. The vehicle heads east on the Lintong highway, the driver sounds confident, and a "Terracotta Museum" sign appears roadside well before the 35 km mark.

The venue is a private replica attraction — not the official Emperor Qin Shi Huang Mausoleum site. You pay ¥100–¥200 to view mass-produced warrior replicas and leave without seeing a single authentic figure. In the "all-inclusive tour" variant, a ¥150 package stops at jade factories, tea "museums," and silk "factories" for most of the day, leaving only 60–90 minutes at the real site.

Take the official Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza directly to the real Terracotta Warriors site for ¥8 each way. Buy entrance tickets (¥120 adult) through the official WeChat mini-program or Trip.com. If booking a guided tour, verify in writing that it includes zero shopping stops.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥200 for a full-day Terracotta trip (genuine tours are ¥300–¥500 without shopping)
  • Itinerary includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' 'Tibetan medicine,' or 'tea ceremony' stops
  • Site name is 'Qin Museum,' 'Warriors Heritage Park,' or anything other than 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum'
  • Driver offers to 'take you a different way' for a 'better price'
  • Location is on the Xi'an side of Lintong — the real site is past Lintong town center

How to Avoid

  • Take Tourist Bus Line 5 (游5) from Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza — ¥8, 1 hour, every 20 min.
  • Book entry ticket (¥120 adult) at the official WeChat mini-program 'Qin Shi Huang Di Ling Bowu Yuan' or Trip.com.
  • For guided tours, use Viator, GetYourGuide, or hotel Ctrip partnership; verify ZERO shopping stops in writing.
  • Reject any tour booth at Xi'an Railway Station or hotel lobby offering 'discount Terracotta tour' under ¥200.
  • Confirm the destination as 'Emperor Qin Shi Huang's Mausoleum Site Museum' — nothing else.
Scam #2
Muslim Quarter Tourist-Menu & 'Famous' Food Stall Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Muslim Quarter (Beiyuanmen / North Gate Market) main street, around Drum Tower south entrance, Huimin Street

Stalls on Beiyuanmen — the Muslim Quarter's main tourist street — charge two to three times local prices for yangrou paomo and biangbiang noodles, while "Xinjiang cake" vendors weigh small-looking cuts at ¥12 per gram and produce bills exceeding ¥2,000.

You walk north from the Drum Tower onto Beiyuanmen, Xi'an's most photographed food street. Stalls display biangbiang noodles, lamb skewers, and pomegranate juice. Everything looks authentic and the street is alive with the smells of a working kitchen.

Yangrou paomo (lamb bread soup) costs ¥30–¥50 here versus ¥15–¥20 one street over. A "Xinjiang cake" vendor cuts a small-looking piece from a dried-fruit wheel, weighs it in grams, and the "small piece" turns out to be over 200 grams at ¥12 per gram — a ¥2,000+ bill for what looked like a snack.

Walk one block off Beiyuanmen to Huajue Xiang or Xiyangshi Street, where locals eat at posted-price stalls with no tourist markup. For Xinjiang cake, buy a pre-packaged version at a supermarket for ¥5–¥15. At any food stall, ask "duo shao qian" (how much) and confirm the price before the vendor starts cutting or serving.

Red Flags

  • Vendor pushes 'try a small piece' of Xinjiang cake from a pushcart on Beiyuanmen
  • No posted prices visible; prices quoted verbally after service begins
  • Tout outside restaurant pulls tourists in with English 'menu, menu'
  • Bowl/skewer/noodle prices 2–3x what's listed at residential venues one street over
  • Venue is directly on Beiyuanmen tourist strip with laminated English photo menu

How to Avoid

  • Eat one street off Beiyuanmen: Huajue Xiang, Xiyangshi Street, or Yongxing Fang food court.
  • Community-verified venues: Lao Sun Jia (yangrou paomo ¥45), Jia San (soup dumplings ¥30), Yongxing Fang (posted prices).
  • Don't buy 'Xinjiang cake' from a pushcart — supermarket pre-packaged versions are ¥5–¥15.
  • Confirm 'duo shao qian' (how much) BEFORE the vendor starts cutting or serving.
  • Reject any tout pulling you toward a restaurant with English-photo menus.
Scam #3
Xi'an Airport (XIY) Taxi Overcharge & Station Touts
⚠️ High
📍 Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY) arrivals, Xi'an Railway Station East Plaza, Xi'an North High-Speed Rail Station

Unlicensed drivers inside Xi'an Xianyang Airport quote ¥300–¥400 for a ride that legitimately costs ¥120–¥160, while a second scam at the arrivals exit involves strangers asking to borrow your phone — and walking off with it.

You clear immigration at Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY) after a long-haul flight and head toward the exit. A man in a crisp polo shirt is already at the terminal doors: "Taxi? Fixed price ¥400, very fast." XIY is 40 km from the city center and the legitimate metered fare runs ¥120–¥160 for the 45-minute ride.

Unofficial drivers inside the terminal swarm arriving tourists with "¥300! ¥400!" and refuse the meter. A related scam targets phone fatigue: a stranger at the arrivals gate claims to have lost their phone in a taxi and asks to use yours to "call" — they walk off with the device, or use the unlocked screen to access WeChat Pay or mobile banking.

Walk past anyone soliciting rides inside the terminal and use DiDi from the official rideshare pickup zone, or take Metro Line 14 to Xi'an Railway Station for ¥17. Pre-install DiDi before flying. Never hand your unlocked phone to a stranger at the airport for any reason.

Red Flags

  • Driver approaches inside terminal offering 'fixed price 300+' to Xi'an center
  • Driver refuses meter ('da biao'); claims it is 'broken' or 'too complicated'
  • Stranger at arrivals asks to 'borrow your phone' for any reason
  • Meter runs oddly fast or switches modes during daytime
  • No fapiao receipt offered on arrival

How to Avoid

  • Install DiDi before flying with international number; use at official rideshare pickup zone.
  • Take airport Metro Line 14 (¥17, 70 min) or shuttle bus (¥25, 60 min) for scam-proof option.
  • If licensed taxi, say 'da biao' (打表) before boarding; Traveler reports confirm ¥120–¥160 range.
  • Don't hand your unlocked phone to any stranger at the airport.
  • Photograph taxi plate number from rear windscreen on entering.

Scam #4
Bell Tower & Drum Tower Area Pickpockets
🔶 Medium
📍 Xi'an Bell Tower and Drum Tower squares, City Wall South Gate tourist crowd, Xi'an Ancient City Wall bike-rental area

Pickpockets work the Bell Tower underground passage, Drum Tower south entrance, and City Wall crowds during peak tour-bus arrivals, targeting distracted tourists in the narrow pedestrian tunnels and queue areas.

You are walking through the Bell Tower underground passage at mid-morning, when several tour buses have just disgorged their passengers. The corridor is packed. You have your phone out to check a map and your bag is on your shoulder.

In the press of the crowd, a practiced hand lifts your wallet from a back pocket or unzips the outer compartment of your bag. The Bell Tower tunnel, the Drum Tower south entrance toward the Muslim Quarter, the City Wall South Gate ticket queue, and the Big Wild Goose Pagoda fountain plaza during the evening show are all documented hotspots for this pattern.

Carry your bag zipped and in front of your body through Bell Tower and Drum Tower crowds, and keep your phone in a zipped inner pocket, never a back pocket. Leave your passport and backup card in the hotel safe; carry only ¥200 and one bank card for the day. At the City Wall bike-rental station, confirm your deposit amount before paying and ensure it is returned in full on return.

Red Flags

  • Bell Tower underground passage during 10 AM / 2 PM tour-bus peak — compression moments
  • Drum Tower south entrance to Muslim Quarter where food crowds mix with tour groups
  • City Wall South Gate (Yongningmen) ticket queue with someone standing unusually close
  • Stranger asks for photo help or directions while a companion approaches blind side
  • Bike rental station at City Wall where deposits are handled manually in cash

How to Avoid

  • Wear crossbody bag zipped in front during Bell Tower / Drum Tower / City Wall walks.
  • Never place phone in back pocket or outer backpack compartment.
  • Carry only ¥200 + one card; passport + backup in hotel safe.
  • Bike rental at City Wall is ¥45 for 100 min (published) — verify deposit returned in full.
  • If theft occurs, call 110 and 12315 (English-line consumer protection) within 24 hours.
Scam #5
Jade 'Factory' Forced-Shopping Tour Stops
⚠️ High
📍 Along tour bus routes between Xi'an city center and Terracotta Warriors / Huaqing Hot Spring, 'government jade museums' bundled with Terracotta day trips

Budget day-trip tours from Xi'an include mandatory 60–120-minute stops at "jade factories" and "traditional medicine museums" where guides earn 20–30% commissions — and the "imperial jade" sold is typically serpentine or dyed quartz at five to twenty times market price.

You book a ¥150–¥250 per person full-day tour advertised as covering the Terracotta Warriors and surrounding historical sites. The price sounds reasonable and the itinerary lists all the right landmarks.

The tour stops at a "government-certified jade factory" and a "traditional Chinese medicine museum" for a combined two or more hours of sales presentations. The guide earns 20–30% commission on everything sold; the "imperial jade" is serpentine or dyed quartz priced at five to twenty times market value. The "government certification" the guide shows is simply a standard Chinese business license, which every shop holds.

Book only tours that explicitly state "zero shopping stops" in writing before you pay — licensed operators on Viator or GetYourGuide offer this verification. Expect ¥300–¥500 per person for an honest full-day tour. If you are already on a tour and it stops at a jade factory, simply wait 45–60 minutes without buying anything — the guide cannot compel purchases.

Red Flags

  • Tour price under ¥250 per person for full-day Terracotta + surroundings — shopping stops are baked in
  • Itinerary lists 'jade museum,' 'TCM clinic,' 'silk factory,' 'tea ceremony,' or 'Tibetan medicine'
  • Guide presents 'government-certified' credentials of the shopping venue
  • Stop duration is 60–120 minutes with a 'sample' or 'demonstration' before sales begin
  • Pressure to buy 'for your family,' 'for health,' 'once-in-a-lifetime' pricing

How to Avoid

  • Book only tours with 'ZERO shopping stops' verified in writing before paying.
  • Use licensed operators: Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets, or hotel-partner Ctrip tours.
  • Expect ¥300–¥500 per person for honest full-day tours; anything lower includes shopping.
  • If already on a shopping-stop tour, simply don't buy — wait out the 45–60 min stop.
  • Report shopping-stop tours to 12315 (consumer protection English line) for documentation.
Scam #6
Tang Paradise / Huaqing Hot Spring 'Show' Ticket Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Tang Paradise (曲江大唐芙蓉园) evening water show area, Huaqing Palace hot spring evening shows, hotel-concierge Tang Dynasty Show packages

Hotel concierges and street touts in Xi'an mark up Tang Dynasty Show and Huaqing Palace evening performance tickets by 50–100% above face value, while "dinner + show" bundles pad the total to ¥1,000+ per person for a ¥298 ticket and a mediocre buffet.

You ask the hotel concierge about the Tang Dynasty Show or the "Song of Everlasting Sorrow" musical at Huaqing Palace for a cultural evening. They offer an "exclusive VIP package" at ¥800–¥900 per person, complete with a "special dinner" and "best seats."

A standard adult ticket to the Tang Dynasty Show is ¥298; VIP seating is ¥398; and the official dinner + show bundle is ¥598. Hotel concierge packages charging ¥800–¥1,200 for the same experience are pure commission markups. Street touts outside venues sell "VIP" tickets that are standard seats at 1.5–2× face value, while "dinner + show" bundles add a mediocre buffet to push the total above ¥1,000.

Book the Tang Dynasty Show, Huaqing Palace evening shows, and Tang Paradise directly through the official venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com at published rates of ¥298–¥498. Ignore hotel concierge "exclusive packages" priced above ¥600 per person. Metro Line 2 reaches the Yanta area near Tang Paradise for ¥5.

Red Flags

  • Hotel-concierge show package over ¥600 per person (official rates ¥298–¥498)
  • Street tout offers 'VIP ticket' at the venue itself
  • 'Dinner + show' bundle over ¥700 per person for a buffet + standard seating
  • Tout's 'package' includes mandatory transportation markup (¥100+ per person)
  • Claim of 'last seats available' or 'English audio included at premium'

How to Avoid

  • Book Tang Dynasty Show or Huaqing Palace show directly via venue WeChat mini-program or Trip.com.
  • Legitimate rates: ¥298 standard, ¥398 VIP, ¥598 dinner+show.
  • Ignore hotel-concierge packages over ¥600 per person — all are commission markups.
  • Metro Line 2 serves Tang Paradise area (¥5); Lintong tourist bus ¥8 + taxi ¥20 for Huaqing Palace.
  • For evening return, use DiDi (app-regulated) rather than venue taxi touts.
Scam #7
Xi'an Tea Ceremony / Tea House Invitation
⚠️ High
📍 Bell Tower plaza, Drum Tower approach, Muslim Quarter entrance, around tourist-heavy Yong Ning Gate

English-speaking women near Bell Tower or Drum Tower approach tourists with a request to "practice English," then invite them to a nearby "traditional Shaanxi tea ceremony" — and present bills of ¥2,000–¥5,000 for a 30-minute tasting.

You are near the Bell Tower or browsing the Muslim Quarter when an attractive young woman approaches in fluent English: "I'm a student at Xi'an Jiaotong University — could you help me practice English?" The conversation is friendly and natural. Within twenty minutes she proposes showing you a "traditional tea ceremony" on a side street near the Drum Tower.

Inside the tea house the host serves several varieties of tea over thirty minutes. When the session ends, a bill arrives for ¥2,000–¥5,000 per person. The "student" disappears. Staff block the exit. The same operation runs in Beijing and Shanghai; the Xi'an branch identifies itself by using Xi'an university names and an offer to "tour the Muslim Quarter" as the opening move. The tea is real but priced 30–80 times the residential rate.

Any stranger who approaches at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or the Muslim Quarter with an invitation to tea, a cultural experience, or a "tour of the area" is running this scam — walk past with "bu yao xie xie." For genuine Xi'an tea, visit the Defuxiang tea market southwest of Bell Tower, where prices are posted and no solicitation occurs.

Red Flags

  • English-speaking strangers at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter invite you to tea or cultural experience
  • Claim to be students at Xi'an Jiaotong or Shaanxi Normal University
  • Invitation to 'Muslim Quarter tour' that transitions to tea-house proposal
  • Venue is side street north or east of Drum Tower, not on a main walkway
  • No menu or prices shown before serving begins

How to Avoid

  • Walk past any stranger approaching at Bell Tower, Drum Tower, or Muslim Quarter entrance.
  • Never accept 'cultural experience' or 'tea ceremony' invitations from strangers.
  • For genuine Xi'an tea, visit Defuxiang tea market (posted prices) or hotel-verified venues.
  • Pay with credit card for chargeback leverage if trapped.
  • Call 12315 (English-line) and 110 (police) for both scam reports.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Chinese Police (公安局) station. Call 110 (Police) or 120 (Ambulance). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at mps.gov.cn.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Beijing is at No. 55 An Jia Lou Road, Chaoyang District, Beijing 100600. For emergencies: +86 10-8531-3000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
