7 Tourist Scams in Guilin

Key Takeaways

  • The #1 reported scam is the Li River Cruise Fake-Ticket & 'Upgrade' Scams.
  • 1 of 7 scams are rated high risk.
  • Use app-based ride services or official metered taxis — avoid unmarked vehicles near tourist areas.
  • Never accept unsolicited offers from strangers near tourist sites in Guilin.

⚡ Quick Safety Tips

  • Book the Li River cruise (¥215 tourist class, ¥310 deluxe) via Trip.com / Viator / Ctrip — avoid hotel-lobby 'Li River package' at ¥500+ which are commission markups; Community reports document the overpriced-cruise pattern.
  • At Yangshuo, book bamboo-raft via guesthouse with posted prices (¥150–¥200 per 2-person for 90-min route) — refuse West Street touts and 'cormorant fishing shows.'
  • From Guilin Liangjiang Airport (KWL), take the Airport Shuttle Bus (¥25, 45 min) or DiDi warns about airport-terminal touts across China.
  • For Longji Rice Terraces, book a 1-night guesthouse stay (¥300–¥600/night) via Ctrip/Trip.com + Longji entry (¥80) + bus from Qintan (¥25, 2.5h) — total ~¥400 vs hotel-concierge day trips at ¥400–¥800.
  • NEVER book Guilin day trips via hotel concierge documents the jade/silk/rice-wine shopping-stop ecosystem; use Trip.com, Viator, GetYourGuide with 'ZERO shopping stops' in writing.

The 7 Scams

Scam #1
Li River Cruise Fake-Ticket & 'Upgrade' Scams
⚠️ High
📍 Guilin hotel lobbies, Magu Pier, Zhujiang Pier booking booths, Yangshuo arrival docks

83 km of photogenic peaks between Zhujiang and Yangshuo, 4 hours one-way.

83 km of photogenic peaks between Zhujiang and Yangshuo, 4 hours one-way. The legitimate tourist-class ticket is ¥215 adult via the Guilin Zhujiang Pier or Magu Pier official booking; deluxe/VIP boats cost ¥310–¥450. The scam variants: (1) hotel-lobby booths quote ¥500–¥800 per person for 'standard' cruise + 'private guide' + 'hotel pickup' bundles where the cruise itself is actually ¥215; (2) street touts near Guilin Railway Station sell 'discount tickets' on boats that turn out to be local bamboo-raft boats, not the full Li River cruise; (3) 'upgraded' boats marketed at ¥400+ that are actually the standard boats.

Community reports confirms the 2025 continuation. The legitimate booking channel is the Guilin Lijiang Cruise website via Trip.com / Ctrip / Viator, or the Magu Pier ticket office directly.'

For older travelers, the practical playbook: (1) book the standard 4-hour cruise via Trip.com, Viator, or Ctrip at ¥215–¥310 per person; (2) avoid hotel-lobby 'Li River cruise package' at ¥500+ — all are commission markups; (3) the cruise departs from Magu Pier (40 km south of Guilin) — pre-arranged transfer is ¥50–¥80 per person shared (bus); (4) at Yangshuo arrival, pre-book your hotel pickup rather than accepting touts at the dock; (5) AVOID any 'all-in-one Guilin + Yangshuo tour' under ¥600 per person total — real cost is ¥800–¥1,200 with honest operators.

Red Flags

  • Hotel lobby 'Li River cruise package' at ¥500+ per person (cruise ticket alone is ¥215)
  • Street tout near Guilin Railway Station offering 'discount cruise tickets'
  • 'Upgraded' boat marketed at ¥400+ that turns out to be standard class
  • Bundle includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' or 'tea ceremony' on the shore portions
  • Tout at Yangshuo dock offering 'hotel transfer' or 'bamboo raft extension'

How to Avoid

  • Book standard 4-hour Li River cruise via Trip.com, Viator, or Ctrip at ¥215–¥310 per person.
  • Avoid hotel-lobby packages at ¥500+; buy the cruise ticket only, then pre-book hotel transfer separately.
  • Magu Pier transfer bus from Guilin ~¥50–¥80 per person shared.
  • Pre-book Yangshuo hotel pickup; don't accept touts at Yangshuo dock.
  • Reject any 'all-in-one Guilin+Yangshuo tour' under ¥600 per person — shopping stops guaranteed.
Scam #2
Yangshuo Bamboo Raft & Photo-Trap Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Yulong River Yangshuo bamboo-raft docks, West Street tourist strip, cormorant-fishing 'show' pickup points

¥150–¥200 per 2-person raft for a 90-minute float is the fair rate.

¥150–¥200 per 2-person raft for a 90-minute float is the fair rate. The scam variants: (1) tout at West Street offers 'discount raft ¥80' that turns out to be 30 minutes in a stagnant backwater; (2) 'traditional cormorant fishing' at ¥80 per person is actually a fake cormorant-show where the birds are chained and no real fishing happens; (3) 'photo with fisherman' (the old man with straw hat + two cormorants) on the Li River costs ¥20–¥50 per photo — the fisherman is posed for tourist photos, not actually fishing, and has been documented on as essentially a photo-tout operation.

Community reports document the rural-tourist-scam density: 'in some rural areas (we went to Yangshuo) they tried to scam us a lot even tho we traveled with a Ch' inese friend.

For older travelers, the practical guide: (1) book Yulong River bamboo-raft ride via a guesthouse that has posted prices — ¥150–¥200 per 2-person raft for the full 90-min route is fair; (2) for cormorant fishing, the only 'authentic' experience is a pre-dawn fisherman on the Li River (not staged shows) — most tourists are better off skipping; (3) NEVER accept a tout's 'discount raft' offer on West Street; (4) West Street (Xi Jie) itself has become 85% tourist shops with inflated pricing — walk to the adjacent residential streets for honest food; (5) Yangshuo day-trip from Guilin: train to Guilin North, bus to Yangshuo ¥25 (1.5h) or stay overnight at a guesthouse rather than day-trip tour.

Red Flags

  • Tout on West Street offers bamboo raft at ¥80 (genuine ¥150–¥200 per 2-person)
  • 'Cormorant fishing show' at ¥80 with chained birds in a staged venue
  • Fisherman with cormorants on the Li River demanding ¥20–¥50 for a photo
  • Tout bundles 'raft + dinner + show' at ¥300+ per person
  • Pre-pay demanded for raft route you haven't seen yet

How to Avoid

  • Book Yulong River raft via guesthouse with posted prices (¥150–¥200 per 2-person for full 90-min route).
  • Skip 'cormorant fishing shows' — they are staged performances, not authentic.
  • Refuse 'photo with fisherman' requests on the Li River cruise.
  • Walk one block off West Street for honest food at residential restaurants.
  • Stay overnight in Yangshuo rather than day-trip tour — more authentic and cheaper.
Scam #3
Guilin Airport (KWL) & Railway Station Taxi Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Guilin Liangjiang International Airport (KWL) arrivals, Guilin Railway Station taxi rank, Guilin North Railway Station

Guilin Liangjiang International Airport (KWL) is 30 km south of the city center.

The legitimate metered taxi fare is ¥100–¥130 (45 min). Unofficial drivers at arrivals and Railway Station quote 'fixed prices' of ¥200–¥300. is the 2025 China-wide airport-taxi scam anchor applicable at KWL: 'They are scams and trying to overcharge you. It's unbearable. Go straight to the real taxi stand.'

Community reports confirms the universal rule. DiDi operates at KWL with English interface; the official rideshare pickup is at the marked zone. The Airport Shuttle Bus to Guilin City runs ¥25 per person (45 min). For Guilin Railway Station and Guilin North Railway Station arrivals, taxi touts similarly quote 2–3x metered rates — the real city-center taxi fare from either station is ¥15–¥30.

For older travelers, the practical guide: (1) take the Airport Shuttle Bus (¥25, 45 min) if you arrive during daytime hours; (2) install DiDi before flying for app-regulated fares at the official pickup zone; (3) if taking a licensed taxi, say 'da biao' (打表) and expect ¥100–¥130 KWL-to-center; (4) at Guilin Railway Station, walk past taxi touts to the official outdoor rank; (5) for Yangshuo transfer from KWL, the Guilin Airport bus to Yangshuo runs ¥50 (90 min) — much cheaper than a ¥300+ taxi quote.

Red Flags

  • Driver approaches inside KWL arrivals or Guilin Railway Station offering taxi
  • 'Fixed price' of ¥200+ quoted to Guilin center (legitimate meter ¥100–¥130)
  • Driver refuses 'da biao' (meter) citing 'broken' or 'unnecessary'
  • Fixed price over ¥300 quoted for KWL-to-Yangshuo (airport bus is ¥50)
  • No fapiao (receipt) offered on arrival

How to Avoid

  • Airport Shuttle Bus KWL to Guilin City: ¥25, 45 min.
  • DiDi with international-number sign-up at official rideshare pickup zone.
  • Licensed taxi: 'da biao' (打表) and expect ¥100–¥130 KWL-to-center.
  • KWL-to-Yangshuo airport bus: ¥50, 90 min (scam-free).
  • Photograph taxi plate number from rear windscreen before boarding.

Scam #4
Guilin Tea House & Guiyang Fermented Rice Wine Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Guilin Seven Star Park approach, Two Rivers and Four Lakes waterfront, Elephant Trunk Hill ticket office queue, Guilin old town pedestrian streets

Guilin's compact tourist core around Elephant Trunk Hill, Seven Star Park, and the Two Rivers and Four Lakes promenade has a gentler variant of the nationwide tea-house scam. The approach: fluent-English strangers at Elephant Trunk Hill or the Two Rivers waterfront invite visitors to a 'traditional Guangxi tea ceremony' or 'fermented rice wine tasting' at a side-street venue. Bill: ¥800–¥3,000 per person. The scam is less organized than Beijing/Shanghai but follows the same script. Community reports places the Guilin tea variant in the broader China-wide ring.

Guilin's specific regional version pitches 'Guangxi tea' or 'fermented rice wine' (the Dong and Zhuang ethnic-minority specialities) instead of green tea — the product is real but sold at 20–40x residential rates. Community reports places Guilin on the short list of Chinese tourist cities running the tea-invite pattern.

For older travelers visiting Guilin's old-city scenic areas, the rule: walk past any stranger who approaches in fluent English with invitations to tea, rice wine, ethnic-minority village visits, or cultural experiences. For genuine Guangxi tea, Guilin Tea Research Institute has a visitor center with posted prices. For authentic Dong and Zhuang ethnic-minority cultural experience, the genuine option is a booked overnight trip to Longji Rice Terraces or Ping'an Zhuang village — book via Trip.com or Viator with licensed operators, not via a stranger's side-street invitation.

Red Flags

  • English-speaking strangers at Elephant Trunk Hill, Seven Star Park, or Two Rivers waterfront invite you to tea
  • Pitch switches to 'fermented rice wine' or 'ethnic-minority cultural tasting'
  • Venue is side-street or upper floor, not a main walkway
  • No posted menu; prices quoted only after serving
  • 'Authentic Dong/Zhuang' claim with no licensing or posted certification

How to Avoid

  • Walk past any stranger at scenic areas with 'bu yao xie xie.'
  • For Guangxi tea, visit Guilin Tea Research Institute visitor center (posted prices).
  • For authentic ethnic-minority experience, book Longji Rice Terraces or Ping'an overnight via Trip.com/Viator.
  • Never follow a stranger to a side-street 'tasting' venue.
  • Pay with credit card for chargeback leverage if trapped.
Scam #5
Longji Rice Terraces Minority Village Tour Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Ping'an Zhuang Village, Dazhai Yao Village, Jinkeng Terraces; hotel-concierge 'Longji day-trip' packages

Longji Rice Terraces (90 km north of Guilin) is a genuinely spectacular UNESCO-candidate landscape with Zhuang and Yao ethnic-minority villages. Hotel-concierge day-trip packages charge ¥400–¥800 per person; the self-guided alternative via public bus + entry ticket + lunch costs ¥150–¥250. The scam variants: (1) hotel concierge marks up 2–3x for a tour that includes 'Miao long-hair show' that is staged for tourists; (2) 'ethnic-minority home-stay' packages at ¥600+ per night are modern guesthouses rebranded; (3) 'hand-loom weaving demonstration' includes forced purchase of ¥200–¥500 woven scarves at 3–5x market price.

Community reports confirms 2025 continuation. The authentic Longji experience: spend one night at Ping'an Zhuang or Dazhai, walk the terrace trails at sunrise, eat at the guesthouse, avoid the 'cultural shows.' references the Yangshuo-area scam density that extends north to Longji.

For older travelers who want Longji Rice Terraces: (1) book a 1-night guesthouse at Ping'an Zhuang or Dazhai directly via Ctrip / Trip.com / Booking.com (¥300–¥600 per room); (2) take bus from Guilin Qintan Bus Station to Longji (¥25, 2.5h) or licensed tour van (¥80–¥120); (3) pay Longji entrance ticket ¥80 at the gate; (4) AVOID day-trip tours at ¥400+ — a self-guided overnight costs ¥400 total per person; (5) skip 'cultural shows' and 'hand-loom demonstrations' — they pay commissions to the tour operators who bring you there.

Red Flags

  • Hotel-concierge 'Longji day-trip' package ¥400–¥800 per person
  • Itinerary includes 'Miao long-hair show,' 'hand-loom demonstration,' 'silver-jewelry museum'
  • 'Ethnic home-stay' package ¥600+ per night at modern guesthouses
  • Pressure to buy woven scarves, silver jewelry, or ethnic crafts at 'demonstration' stops
  • Day-trip tour bus makes unexplained stops at shopping venues

How to Avoid

  • Book Ping'an Zhuang or Dazhai guesthouse direct (¥300–¥600/night via Ctrip/Trip.com/Booking).
  • Bus Guilin Qintan to Longji ¥25 (2.5h) or tour van ¥80–¥120.
  • Longji entrance ticket ¥80 at gate.
  • Skip 'cultural shows' and 'hand-loom demonstrations' — all commission-driven.
  • Total self-guided Longji overnight ~¥400 per person all-in vs ¥400+ day-trip tour.
Scam #6
Guilin Night Market & Tourist-Menu Restaurant Overcharge
🟢 Low
📍 Zhengyang Pedestrian Street, Central Square night market, tourist restaurants around Two Rivers and Four Lakes

Guilin's compact tourist core has a modest tourist-menu restaurant problem.

The Zhengyang Pedestrian Street (city center, east of Two Rivers) and the waterfront restaurants around the Two Rivers and Four Lakes use laminated-English-photo menus with prices inflated 50–100% over residential rates. Common overcharge: Guilin rice noodles (Guilin's signature dish, normally ¥8–¥15 per bowl) served at ¥30–¥50 at tourist-strip venues; 'beer fish' (Yangshuo specialty) at ¥180–¥300 at Zhengyang when it costs ¥80–¥150 at Yangshuo residential restaurants; 'Taro taro' dessert at ¥40 when the residential price is ¥10–¥15.

For older travelers, the practical guide: (1) walk one block off Zhengyang Pedestrian Street for honest Guilin rice noodle shops; community favorites: Chong Shan Rice Noodle (¥10), Yi Cun Yi Wei (¥12); (2) for beer fish, wait until Yangshuo and eat at a residential restaurant rather than a Guilin tourist-strip version; (3) avoid the 'night market' tourist stalls with English signs and photos — the same dishes at 2–3x price; (4) any restaurant with a tout outside in English is targeting tourists — the restaurants one street back, with Chinese-only menus, serve locals at fair pricing.

Red Flags

  • Tout outside restaurant on Zhengyang Pedestrian Street calls out in English
  • Laminated English-photo menu with no Chinese chalkboard specials
  • Guilin rice noodle at ¥30+ per bowl (residential rate ¥8–¥15)
  • Beer fish at ¥180+ at Guilin tourist-strip (Yangshuo residential ¥80–¥150)
  • Mandatory 'service charge' or 'tourist tax' added to bill

How to Avoid

  • Walk one block off Zhengyang for honest Guilin rice noodle (¥8–¥15).
  • Eat beer fish in Yangshuo residential restaurants, not Guilin tourist strip.
  • Community favorites: Chong Shan Rice Noodle, Yi Cun Yi Wei.
  • Skip night-market tourist stalls with English signs.
  • Any restaurant with English-speaking tout outside is targeting tourists — walk past.
Scam #7
Guilin Hotel Tour-Desk Commission & 'All-Inclusive' Upsells
🔶 Medium
📍 Guilin hotel lobbies (particularly budget and mid-range), hotel concierge booking desks, partnered tour operator booths

Guilin hotels across all tiers have commission arrangements with local tour operators that pay 20–40% kickback on every booking. The result: 'Li River cruise + Yangshuo + cormorant show' packaged tours sold at hotel lobbies for ¥600–¥1,200 per person cost 2–3x what independent booking via Trip.com or Viator delivers. the operator's tour includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' or 'rice wine distillery' stops where the hotel receives additional commissions on purchases; 20–30% of tour time is shopping.

Community reports confirms this operates unchanged in 2025.

For older travelers, the defensive playbook: (1) NEVER book day trips, cruises, or excursions via hotel concierge or lobby tour booths in Guilin; (2) book direct via Trip.com / Viator / GetYourGuide / Tiqets with operators that have 'zero shopping stops' verified in writing; (3) licensed small-group operators include China Highlights, Viator Guilin, and Ctrip's vetted partners; (4) expect ¥400–¥600 per person honest Li River cruise + Yangshuo + return transport; anything above is hotel-concierge commission markup; (5) if a hotel presents a tour verbally at 'special price,' ask them to show the operator's independent website — if they can't, walk away.

Red Flags

  • Hotel lobby tour desk quotes Li River cruise package at ¥600+ per person
  • 'Special price' only available if you book through the hotel, not direct
  • Itinerary includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' 'rice wine distillery,' or 'cormorant show'
  • Operator not listed on Trip.com, Viator, or major booking platforms
  • Hotel refuses to share operator's direct website for verification

How to Avoid

  • NEVER book day trips via hotel concierge or lobby tour booths.
  • Book direct: Trip.com, Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets with 'zero shopping stops' verified in writing.
  • Licensed operators: China Highlights, Viator Guilin, Ctrip vetted partners.
  • Expect ¥400–¥600 per person honest day trip; above is commission markup.
  • If concierge presents verbal 'special price,' demand the operator's independent website.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Chinese Police (公安局) station. Call 110 (Police) or 120 (Ambulance). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at mps.gov.cn.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Beijing is at No. 55 An Jia Lou Road, Chaoyang District, Beijing 100600. For emergencies: +86 10-8531-3000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
