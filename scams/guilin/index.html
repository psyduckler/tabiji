The 7 Scams

Scam #1
Li River Cruise Fake-Ticket & 'Upgrade' Scams
⚠️ High
📍 Guilin hotel lobbies, Magu Pier, Zhujiang Pier booking booths, Yangshuo arrival docks

Hotel-lobby tour booths and street touts near Guilin Railway Station sell Li River cruise packages at ¥500–¥800 per person by bundling a ¥215 government-priced cruise ticket with worthless add-ons, or substitute short bamboo-raft rides and call them the full cruise — inflating the real cost two to three times.

The Li River cruise between Guilin and Yangshuo is one of China's most celebrated experiences — 83 km of limestone karst peaks reflected in slow green water, with a government-regulated tourist-class ticket priced at ¥215 per adult at the Magu Pier official booking office. The scenic reputation makes it an obvious target: visitors want it, the official price is fixed, and the gap between that price and what a middleman can charge is the entire operating margin of the scam.

Hotel-lobby booking booths present 'Li River cruise packages' at ¥500–¥800 per person that bundle the ¥215 cruise ticket with a 'private guide,' 'hotel pickup,' and 'Yangshuo shore excursion' — commissions that absorb all apparent savings. Street touts near Guilin Railway Station sell 'discount cruise tickets' that turn out to be short bamboo-raft rides in a local backwater, not the 4-hour government cruise. A third variant markets 'upgraded VIP boats' at ¥400+ that are identical to the standard vessel in every material respect.

The official booking channel has a fixed price that removes the entire markup opportunity. Book the Li River cruise only through Trip.com, Ctrip, Viator, or the Magu Pier ticket office directly at ¥215–¥310 per person, and arrange your own transfer to the pier by shuttle bus at ¥50–¥80 rather than paying a hotel-lobby bundle. Any quote above ¥310 for the cruise ticket itself is a commission markup you do not need.

Red Flags

  • Hotel lobby 'Li River cruise package' at ¥500+ per person (cruise ticket alone is ¥215)
  • Street tout near Guilin Railway Station offering 'discount cruise tickets'
  • 'Upgraded' boat marketed at ¥400+ that turns out to be standard class
  • Bundle includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' or 'tea ceremony' on the shore portions
  • Tout at Yangshuo dock offering 'hotel transfer' or 'bamboo raft extension'

How to Avoid

  • Book standard 4-hour Li River cruise via Trip.com, Viator, or Ctrip at ¥215–¥310 per person.
  • Avoid hotel-lobby packages at ¥500+; buy the cruise ticket only, then pre-book hotel transfer separately.
  • Magu Pier transfer bus from Guilin ~¥50–¥80 per person shared.
  • Pre-book Yangshuo hotel pickup; don't accept touts at Yangshuo dock.
  • Reject any 'all-in-one Guilin+Yangshuo tour' under ¥600 per person — shopping stops guaranteed.
Scam #2
Yangshuo Bamboo Raft & Photo-Trap Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Yulong River Yangshuo bamboo-raft docks, West Street tourist strip, cormorant-fishing 'show' pickup points

Touts on Yangshuo's West Street sell ¥80 'discount' bamboo raft rides that turn out to be 30-minute paddles in a stagnant backwater rather than the 90-minute Yulong River route, while staged cormorant-fishing shows and posed fisherman photo demands extract further payments on arrival.

After the Li River cruise arrives in Yangshuo, visitors emerge at the dock into a concentrated zone of touts offering bamboo raft rides on the Yulong River. The Yulong raft is genuinely worthwhile — a quiet 90-minute float through jade-green water and karst hills — with a fair market rate of ¥150–¥200 per 2-person raft through a guesthouse that posts prices. West Street (Xi Jie), Yangshuo's main tourist strip, concentrates the highest density of commission-driven offers in the area and has become roughly 85% tourist shops with inflated pricing.

A tout on West Street offers 'discount raft ¥80.' The actual ride is 30 minutes in a stagnant backwater canal, not the Yulong River route. Cormorant fishing 'shows' at ¥80 per person feature birds chained on a platform in a staged venue with no actual fishing. On the Li River, a fisherman in a traditional straw hat with two trained cormorants charges ¥20–¥50 per photo — a full-time posed tourist photo operation documented extensively as having no connection to traditional livelihood. Traveler reports note the scam density extends beyond West Street into rural areas around Yangshuo where even locals accompanying visitors were targeted.

The safest booking is one where a price is already visible before any conversation begins. Book the Yulong River bamboo raft directly through your guesthouse with a posted rate board, where ¥150–¥200 per 2-person raft for the full 90-minute route is the honest market price, and skip cormorant shows and Li River fisherman photos unless you have already decided the amount you are willing to pay before anyone approaches you.

Red Flags

  • Tout on West Street offers bamboo raft at ¥80 (genuine ¥150–¥200 per 2-person)
  • 'Cormorant fishing show' at ¥80 with chained birds in a staged venue
  • Fisherman with cormorants on the Li River demanding ¥20–¥50 for a photo
  • Tout bundles 'raft + dinner + show' at ¥300+ per person
  • Pre-pay demanded for raft route you haven't seen yet

How to Avoid

  • Book Yulong River raft via guesthouse with posted prices (¥150–¥200 per 2-person for full 90-min route).
  • Skip 'cormorant fishing shows' — they are staged performances, not authentic.
  • Refuse 'photo with fisherman' requests on the Li River cruise.
  • Walk one block off West Street for honest food at residential restaurants.
  • Stay overnight in Yangshuo rather than day-trip tour — more authentic and cheaper.
Scam #3
Guilin Airport (KWL) & Railway Station Taxi Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Guilin Liangjiang International Airport (KWL) arrivals, Guilin Railway Station taxi rank, Guilin North Railway Station

Unofficial drivers at Guilin Liangjiang Airport arrivals and Guilin Railway Station quote ¥200–¥300 fixed fares for routes where the legitimate metered taxi costs ¥15–¥130, with a ¥25 airport shuttle bus available as a direct alternative.

Guilin Liangjiang International Airport sits 30 km south of the city center, and domestic arrivals from Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangzhou step into an unfamiliar environment with luggage and no reference point for local fares. Unofficial drivers position themselves near the arrivals exit before passengers reach the official taxi rank or the airport shuttle bus counter, approaching with the same quiet authority as licensed drivers and quoting flat rates with apparent confidence.

The 'fixed price' they quote — ¥200–¥300 to the city center — is two to three times the ¥100–¥130 metered fare on a licensed cab for the same 45-minute route. At Guilin Railway Station and Guilin North Railway Station, the same pattern applies: touts intercept arriving passengers before they reach the official outdoor taxi rank and quote 2–3x metered rates for city-center trips that should cost ¥15–¥30. A 2025 traveler report on the KWL situation used the bluntly applicable phrase: 'They are scams and trying to overcharge you. It's unbearable. Go straight to the real taxi stand.'

The simplest defense bypasses the taxi system entirely. Take the Airport Shuttle Bus at ¥25 for the 45-minute ride to Guilin city, or use DiDi (installed before traveling) at the official rideshare pickup zone for fully app-regulated fares — never accept a ride from anyone who approaches you inside the arrivals hall. If you take a licensed taxi, say 'da biao' (打表 — run the meter) before the car moves and walk away if the driver refuses.

Red Flags

  • Driver approaches inside KWL arrivals or Guilin Railway Station offering taxi
  • 'Fixed price' of ¥200+ quoted to Guilin center (legitimate meter ¥100–¥130)
  • Driver refuses 'da biao' (meter) citing 'broken' or 'unnecessary'
  • Fixed price over ¥300 quoted for KWL-to-Yangshuo (airport bus is ¥50)
  • No fapiao (receipt) offered on arrival

How to Avoid

  • Airport Shuttle Bus KWL to Guilin City: ¥25, 45 min.
  • DiDi with international-number sign-up at official rideshare pickup zone.
  • Licensed taxi: 'da biao' (打表) and expect ¥100–¥130 KWL-to-center.
  • KWL-to-Yangshuo airport bus: ¥50, 90 min (scam-free).
  • Photograph taxi plate number from rear windscreen before boarding.

Scam #4
Guilin Tea House & Guiyang Fermented Rice Wine Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Guilin Seven Star Park approach, Two Rivers and Four Lakes waterfront, Elephant Trunk Hill ticket office queue, Guilin old town pedestrian streets

Fluent-English strangers at Elephant Trunk Hill, Seven Star Park, and the Two Rivers waterfront invite visitors to 'traditional Guangxi tea ceremonies' or 'fermented rice wine tastings' at side-street venues, then present bills of ¥800–¥3,000 per person for regional products sold at 20–40 times their residential price.

At the ticket queue for Elephant Trunk Hill or walking the Two Rivers and Four Lakes promenade, a fluent-English-speaking local strikes up a friendly conversation about the city, asks where you are from, and mentions that they know a wonderful place for authentic Guangxi tea or Dong ethnic-minority fermented rice wine — a cultural experience they frame as beyond what any guidebook covers. The invitation sounds spontaneous and genuinely local rather than commercial, which is its precise function in the approach.

The venue is a side-street room or upper-floor tea parlor with no posted menu. Products are served, conversation continues, and prices are quoted only after consumption. The bill arrives at ¥800–¥3,000 per person. The Guangxi green tea or Dong minority fermented rice wine is often real — these are genuine regional products — but presented at 20–40 times the residential rate. The Guilin variant is less organized than the equivalent in Beijing or Shanghai, but follows the same script and produces the same outcome. Refusal to pay after consumption leads to physical pressure in an unlicensed venue with no incentive to resolve the dispute fairly.

Any fluent-English stranger at a scenic area who volunteers an invitation to a cultural tasting is operating on commission, regardless of how natural the conversation feels. Walk past with 'bu yao xie xie' (不要谢谢 — no thank you) without slowing down, and visit the Guilin Tea Research Institute visitor center for genuine Guangxi tea at posted prices, or book an overnight trip to Longji Rice Terraces via Viator or Trip.com for authentic ethnic-minority cultural experience without the captive-audience dynamic.

Red Flags

  • English-speaking strangers at Elephant Trunk Hill, Seven Star Park, or Two Rivers waterfront invite you to tea
  • Pitch switches to 'fermented rice wine' or 'ethnic-minority cultural tasting'
  • Venue is side-street or upper floor, not a main walkway
  • No posted menu; prices quoted only after serving
  • 'Authentic Dong/Zhuang' claim with no licensing or posted certification

How to Avoid

  • Walk past any stranger at scenic areas with 'bu yao xie xie.'
  • For Guangxi tea, visit Guilin Tea Research Institute visitor center (posted prices).
  • For authentic ethnic-minority experience, book Longji Rice Terraces or Ping'an overnight via Trip.com/Viator.
  • Never follow a stranger to a side-street 'tasting' venue.
  • Pay with credit card for chargeback leverage if trapped.
Scam #5
Longji Rice Terraces Minority Village Tour Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Ping'an Zhuang Village, Dazhai Yao Village, Jinkeng Terraces; hotel-concierge 'Longji day-trip' packages

Hotel-concierge 'Longji day-trip' packages to the terraced Zhuang and Yao villages north of Guilin cost ¥400–¥800 per person through the lobby desk, while the self-guided alternative via public bus, entry ticket, and a guesthouse overnight totals ¥150–¥400 — with mandatory souvenir stops consuming 20–30% of the commercial tour day.

Longji Rice Terraces, 90 km north of Guilin, is a genuinely spectacular UNESCO-candidate landscape of stacked paddy fields built by Zhuang and Yao ethnic-minority communities. The hotel concierge presents it as a natural next booking after the Li River cruise — a 'Minority Village Cultural Tour' including a 'Miao long-hair show,' 'hand-loom weaving demonstration,' 'traditional home-stay experience,' and transport for ¥400–¥800 per person per day.

The commercial package devotes 20–30% of the tour day to commission stops: a jade shop, a silk factory, a hand-loom demonstration where visitors are pressured to buy woven scarves at ¥200–¥500 each — three to five times market price. The 'ethnic home-stay' is a modern guesthouse rebranded with minority décor. The 'Miao long-hair show' is a staged performance for tourists, not a community event. The self-guided alternative via public bus from Guilin Qintan Bus Station (¥25, 2.5 hours) plus the ¥80 gate entry ticket and an authentic guesthouse overnight in Ping'an Zhuang or Dazhai (¥300–¥600 per room) is both cheaper and a far more genuine experience.

The hotel tour desk sells convenience but delivers a less authentic experience than going independently. Book a one-night guesthouse at Ping'an Zhuang or Dazhai directly via Ctrip, Trip.com, or Booking.com, and take the public bus from Guilin Qintan Bus Station — total cost is roughly equal to the hotel tour package, but you arrive on your own schedule and eat at the guesthouse rather than at a commission-restaurant midway stop.

Red Flags

  • Hotel-concierge 'Longji day-trip' package ¥400–¥800 per person
  • Itinerary includes 'Miao long-hair show,' 'hand-loom demonstration,' 'silver-jewelry museum'
  • 'Ethnic home-stay' package ¥600+ per night at modern guesthouses
  • Pressure to buy woven scarves, silver jewelry, or ethnic crafts at 'demonstration' stops
  • Day-trip tour bus makes unexplained stops at shopping venues

How to Avoid

  • Book Ping'an Zhuang or Dazhai guesthouse direct (¥300–¥600/night via Ctrip/Trip.com/Booking).
  • Bus Guilin Qintan to Longji ¥25 (2.5h) or tour van ¥80–¥120.
  • Longji entrance ticket ¥80 at gate.
  • Skip 'cultural shows' and 'hand-loom demonstrations' — all commission-driven.
  • Total self-guided Longji overnight ~¥400 per person all-in vs ¥400+ day-trip tour.
Scam #6
Guilin Night Market & Tourist-Menu Restaurant Overcharge
🟢 Low
📍 Zhengyang Pedestrian Street, Central Square night market, tourist restaurants around Two Rivers and Four Lakes

Restaurants on Guilin's Zhengyang Pedestrian Street and Two Rivers waterfront serve the city's signature rice noodles at ¥30–¥50 per bowl — three to six times the ¥8–¥15 residential rate — using English-photo menus and street touts to capture tourists before they walk one block to honest local shops.

Guilin's tourist core is compact, and the most prominent dining options on Zhengyang Pedestrian Street and along the Two Rivers and Four Lakes waterfront display laminated English-photo menus featuring the city's famous dishes. Guilin rice noodles — the city's signature food, eaten for breakfast by locals every morning — are one of the defining dishes of southern China, and every visitor has good reason to try them. The tourist-strip restaurants are the most visible and the easiest to walk into.

The prices on those English menus are calibrated for visitors without local reference points. Guilin rice noodles that cost ¥8–¥15 at residential shops appear on Zhengyang at ¥30–¥50. 'Beer fish' — a Yangshuo specialty that runs ¥80–¥150 at residential Yangshuo restaurants — is listed at ¥180–¥300 at Guilin tourist-strip venues. Night market stalls with English signage and photo displays charge 2–3 times the price of identical items at adjacent stalls without tourist-facing signs. Any restaurant with an English-speaking tout at the door is setting prices for visitors who do not know the alternatives.

The tourist pricing only holds if you never walk one block off the main strip. Turn one block off Zhengyang Pedestrian Street and look for shops with handwritten Chinese-only menus and locals eating inside — honest Guilin rice noodles are never more than a two-minute walk from any tourist area. Community-recommended shops include Chong Shan Rice Noodle at ¥10 and Yi Cun Yi Wei at ¥12; for beer fish, save it for Yangshuo and eat at a residential restaurant rather than the Guilin tourist-strip version at double the price.

Red Flags

  • Tout outside restaurant on Zhengyang Pedestrian Street calls out in English
  • Laminated English-photo menu with no Chinese chalkboard specials
  • Guilin rice noodle at ¥30+ per bowl (residential rate ¥8–¥15)
  • Beer fish at ¥180+ at Guilin tourist-strip (Yangshuo residential ¥80–¥150)
  • Mandatory 'service charge' or 'tourist tax' added to bill

How to Avoid

  • Walk one block off Zhengyang for honest Guilin rice noodle (¥8–¥15).
  • Eat beer fish in Yangshuo residential restaurants, not Guilin tourist strip.
  • Community favorites: Chong Shan Rice Noodle, Yi Cun Yi Wei.
  • Skip night-market tourist stalls with English signs.
  • Any restaurant with English-speaking tout outside is targeting tourists — walk past.
Scam #7
Guilin Hotel Tour-Desk Commission & 'All-Inclusive' Upsells
🔶 Medium
📍 Guilin hotel lobbies (particularly budget and mid-range), hotel concierge booking desks, partnered tour operator booths

Guilin hotel tour desks receive 20–40% kickback from partnered local operators, marking up Li River cruise and Yangshuo day-trip packages to ¥600–¥1,200 per person — two to three times the ¥400–¥600 cost of booking the same experiences independently through Trip.com or Viator, with mandatory shopping stops built into the itinerary.

Every hotel in Guilin, from budget hostels to international chains, has a tour-desk arrangement with local operators. The concierge presents a 'Li River Cruise + Yangshuo Day Trip' package during check-in as a seamless extension of helping you get oriented. The price is ¥600–¥1,200 per person. The pitch frames the hotel bundle as convenient — transfers, guide, and timing handled — which makes the markup feel like a service rather than a commission extraction.

The same Li River cruise ticket costs ¥215 at the official pier or ¥215–¥310 through Trip.com. The extras that inflate the hotel price include mandatory stops at a jade museum, silk factory, or rice wine distillery — venues that pay the hotel additional commissions on any purchases visitors make during the stop. Traveler reports confirmed in 2025 that the pattern continues unchanged. Twenty to thirty percent of the booked tour day is spent at shopping stops rather than at the scenic attractions that were the reason for booking.

The hotel tour desk is a commission aggregator, not a travel planner working in your interest. Book the Li River cruise and Yangshuo day trip directly through Trip.com, Viator, or GetYourGuide — look for listings explicitly marked 'zero shopping stops' and confirmed in writing, and expect an honest all-in price of ¥400–¥600 per person for cruise plus Yangshuo transport. If a hotel concierge insists on a verbal 'special price,' ask them to show the operator's independent website — if they cannot, the booking is commission-only.

Red Flags

  • Hotel lobby tour desk quotes Li River cruise package at ¥600+ per person
  • 'Special price' only available if you book through the hotel, not direct
  • Itinerary includes 'jade museum,' 'silk factory,' 'rice wine distillery,' or 'cormorant show'
  • Operator not listed on Trip.com, Viator, or major booking platforms
  • Hotel refuses to share operator's direct website for verification

How to Avoid

  • Don't book day trips via hotel concierge or lobby tour booths.
  • Book direct: Trip.com, Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets with 'zero shopping stops' verified in writing.
  • Licensed operators: China Highlights, Viator Guilin, Ctrip vetted partners.
  • Expect ¥400–¥600 per person honest day trip; above is commission markup.
  • If concierge presents verbal 'special price,' demand the operator's independent website.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Chinese Police (公安局) station. Call 110 (Police) or 120 (Ambulance). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at mps.gov.cn.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Beijing is at No. 55 An Jia Lou Road, Chaoyang District, Beijing 100600. For emergencies: +86 10-8531-3000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
