⚡ Quick Safety Tips

  • From Ngurah Rai Airport (DPS), walk OUT to the designated Grab/Gojek pickup zone ~50m from terminal exit — drivers are NOT allowed inside. Community reports document scammers holding 'Grab' signs INSIDE the terminal at 2–4x app rates.

  • For solo female travelers or drinking at Canggu/Seminyak beach clubs, NEVER leave your drink unattended and refuse free drinks from strangers.

The 7 Scams

Scam #1
Bali Ngurah Rai Airport (DPS) Fake-Grab & Taxi Mafia
⚠️ High
📍 Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS) arrivals hall, official Grab/Gojek pickup point (50m walk from terminal), Kuta-area drop-off zones

Bali's Ngurah Rai Airport has the most-documented 2025 airport-taxi scam ecosystem in Indonesia.

Community reports document the pattern: 'There are a few exceptions like Japan, Singapore but more often then not they are trying to scam you' — meaning the airport taxi queue itself is run by a 'taxi mafia' that blocks Grab/Gojek from pickups inside the terminal perimeter and quotes 'fixed prices' 2–4x the legitimate app rate.

One traveler wrote: 'They had signs in their hands with Grab on them and aggressively asking Grab sir, Grab baby and' — meaning scammers hold Grab-branded signs INSIDE the arrivals hall to fool tourists. gives the Vietnam-parallel answer: 'Yes. They overcharge tourists by a lot. Use Grab. There are also many scams around the temples.' documents the cross-island pattern where legitimate Grab drivers are forced by local mafia to transfer tourists to higher-priced local drivers at ferry ports. The legitimate fare from DPS to Kuta is 60,000–100,000 IDR (~$4–$6 USD) via Grab/Gojek app; to Seminyak 80K–140K; to Ubud 300K–400K (1-hour drive). Unofficial 'fixed prices' quoted by airport touts run 250K–500K for Kuta, 600K–1M for Ubud.

For older travelers arriving in Bali, the practical defense: (1) walk OUT of the arrivals hall and cross to the designated Grab/Gojek pickup zone (signposted, ~50m from terminal exit) — drivers are NOT allowed inside; (2) book Grab or Gojek yourself on airport Wi-Fi AFTER luggage — verify license plate matches app; (3) ignore any 'Grab' sign-holder inside the terminal — all are unauthorized; (4) for the Ubud transfer, pre-book via Klook at 250K–350K IDR or ask your hotel for a free transfer (many include it); (5) NEVER let a 'friendly local' walk you to a 'cheaper taxi' — this is the Padang Bai pattern adapted to the airport.

Red Flags

  • Person inside terminal holding a 'Grab' sign offering to drive you
  • 'Fixed price' quote 300K+ IDR to Kuta or 600K+ to Ubud
  • Driver claims Grab 'doesn't work at the airport' (untrue — official pickup zone exists)
  • Pressure to pay cash before confirming Grab/Gojek app booking
  • Meter 'broken' at ferry ports (Padang Bai, Sanur)

How to Avoid

  • Walk OUT to the designated Grab/Gojek pickup zone ~50m from terminal exit.
  • Book Grab/Gojek on airport Wi-Fi AFTER luggage; verify license plate.
  • Typical fare: 60K–100K IDR DPS-Kuta, 80K–140K Seminyak, 300K–400K Ubud.
  • For Ubud, pre-book Klook transfer 250K–350K IDR or use hotel free pickup.
  • IGNORE every 'Grab' sign-holder inside the arrivals hall.
Scam #2
Bali ATM Skimmer Fraud & Card Cloning
⚠️ High
📍 Free-standing ATMs at tourist areas (Kuta, Seminyak, Uluwatu, Ubud), non-bank-branch ATMs in convenience stores, hotel-lobby ATMs without CCTV

You pull rupiah from a free-standing ATM near Uluwatu, pocket the cash, and walk the 200 meters back to your villa — and inside 20 minutes, a fraud alert pings your phone while a cloned copy of your card spends your money somewhere you've never been. Bali is one of Asia's most-documented ATM-skimming destinations.

One traveler wrote: 'Just had to freeze our wise cards, got cash out of an ATM near Uluwatu. Not even 20 minutes later, my card had been' cloned and used...'

Community reports document a related variant: 'I get Fraud alert on my cards. I paid cash. My cards were in my wallet within reach while I was get' ting a massage — meaning cards sitting in a wallet at a spa or hotel room can be cloned remotely via RFID skimmers, not just physical ATM skimmers. The specific ATM scam patterns: (1) physical card-reader overlay on non-bank ATMs that captures magnetic stripe data; (2) pinhole camera capturing PIN entry above the keypad; (3) 'cash trap' where money is held back mechanically and claimed 'dispensed' by the bank's records; (4) RFID-skimmer variants that clone contactless cards at close range.

For older travelers, the practical defense: (1) use ONLY ATMs INSIDE bank branches during business hours (BCA, Mandiri, BNI, BRI) — never freestanding convenience-store ATMs; (2) cover the keypad with your other hand when entering PIN; (3) inspect the card slot for loose overlays before inserting (wiggle it — real slots don't move); (4) use a RFID-blocking wallet or sleeve for contactless cards; (5) set low daily withdrawal limits and transaction alerts on your bank app; (6) withdraw larger amounts less frequently rather than small amounts often; (7) if skimmed, file a denuncia with Polda Bali (+62 361 227 274) within 24 hours for credit card chargeback documentation.

Red Flags

  • Free-standing ATM in a convenience store, hotel lobby, or street corner without CCTV
  • Card slot feels loose or wiggles when touched
  • Keypad looks thicker than normal or has slight color difference
  • Pinhole or plastic trim above the keypad
  • Transaction says 'cash dispensed' but no cash emerges

How to Avoid

  • Use ONLY bank-branch ATMs during business hours (BCA, Mandiri, BNI, BRI).
  • Cover keypad with your other hand when entering PIN.
  • Wiggle the card slot before inserting — real slots don't move.
  • Use RFID-blocking wallet for contactless cards.
  • Set low daily withdrawal limits and transaction alerts on your bank app.
Scam #3
Money Changer Sleight-of-Hand & 'Best Rate' Trap
⚠️ High
📍 Independent money changer stalls with 'BEST RATE' signs (Kuta, Legian, Ubud center, Seminyak), beachfront changers in tourist strips

Bali's money-changer scam is so well-documented that it has its own 2025 PSA thread.

One traveler wrote: 'This scam has been going on since tourism started in Bali… Like decades. The higher the exchange ra' te advertised, the more sleight-of-hand is used during counting. is another 2025 first-person anchor: 'Went just outside Ubud and popped into a money exchange. So many red flags, I should have known, but' the rate was too good to resist.

The scam mechanics: (1) advertised rate is 200–500 IDR per USD HIGHER than the real market rate (bait); (2) changer counts out stacks of 50K or 100K notes quickly in Indonesian numerals while hiding part of the stack; (3) when tourists count their own stack, it's 10–30% short; (4) if challenged, changer re-counts while palming notes back; (5) changer argues the calculation to confuse; (6) some changers intermix torn or expired notes into the stack. Typical loss: 500K–3M IDR ($30–$200) per transaction.

For older travelers, the practical defense: (1) use ONLY authorized PT. Central Kuta Money Exchange (bright blue/white branding, numerous locations, transparent glass counting) or PVA ASSOCIATE money changers with the proper authorisation sticker from Bank Indonesia; (2) reject any 'BEST RATE' stall — rates 100+ IDR above Google rate are a scam; (3) count the full stack YOURSELF before leaving the counter; (4) bring a calculator and do the math in front of the changer; (5) never exchange more than $200 at once; (6) alternative: use a BCA, Mandiri, or CIMB Niaga ATM to withdraw IDR directly from your home-country card at the interbank rate.

Red Flags

  • 'BEST RATE' or 'HIGHEST RATE' sign at a street-corner stall (NOT authorized PT. Central Kuta)
  • Rate 200+ IDR/USD higher than the Google interbank rate
  • Changer counts quickly in Indonesian numerals while partially hiding stack
  • Changer 're-counts' by palming notes back into their drawer
  • Changer uses calculator to 'verify' and shows confused arithmetic

How to Avoid

  • Use ONLY PT. Central Kuta Money Exchange (blue/white branding) or Bank Indonesia-authorized PVA.
  • Reject 'BEST RATE' stalls — rates above market are the scam bait.
  • Count the full stack YOURSELF before leaving the counter.
  • Bring your own calculator and do the math in front of the changer.
  • Alternative: ATM withdrawal at BCA, Mandiri, or CIMB Niaga bank branches for interbank rate.

Scooter Rental 'Spare Key Theft' & Pre-Damage Claim Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Kuta, Legian, Canggu, Ubud scooter rental shops; hotel-doorstep scooter rentals; street-corner rental stands with no paperwork

Bali's scooter-rental economy runs on a 300K–500K IDR/week model — but has two documented scam variants.

The second variant is pre-existing-damage-claim: the rental shop photographs the scooter in specific angles that don't reveal scratches, hands it to the renter, and at return claims the scratches as new damage demanding 500K–2M IDR...4M IDR/month is 'fair,' 50-80K IDR/day is typical daily.

For older travelers considering a scooter (IMPORTANT: scooter driving in Bali requires confidence in heavy traffic and a valid international driving permit with motorcycle class — most older travelers should skip scooter rental entirely and use Grab/GoCar), the protective playbook: (1) rent ONLY from community-vetted shops: Bali Scooter Rental, Rental Scooter Ubud, Jaya Bike Rentals — each with Google reviews mentioning 'no damage scam' outcomes; (2) video a walk-around narrating all visible scratches BEFORE leaving; (3) photograph the scooter, the keys (to verify only ONE key handed over), and your license exchange; (4) NEVER leave passport as deposit — cash deposit maximum 500K IDR; (5) wear a helmet (legally required; refusing to wear one creates bribe leverage for police); (6) if the scooter is 'stolen' during the rental, call Polda Bali immediately (+62 361 227 274) — the report documents legitimate theft for insurance.

Red Flags

  • Rental shop takes passport as deposit (never surrender it)
  • Only ONE key handed over when renters should receive both (spare-key theft setup)
  • No walk-around damage documentation before rental
  • Rental demanded in cash with no written contract
  • Daily rate under 50K IDR is typically a tourist-scam operator

How to Avoid

  • For older travelers: SKIP scooter rental entirely; use Grab/GoCar.
  • Rent only from community-vetted shops (Bali Scooter Rental, Rental Scooter Ubud, Jaya Bike Rentals).
  • Video walk-around narrating scratches BEFORE leaving.
  • Verify BOTH keys are handed over; photograph them.
  • NEVER leave passport as deposit — cash only (max 500K IDR).
Scam #5
Traffic Police 'Bribe Shakedown' for Scooter Riders
🔶 Medium
📍 Kuta, Legian, Seminyak police checkpoints targeting tourist scooter riders; morning and late-evening enforcement windows

Bali's traffic police have a long-documented pattern of stopping tourist scooter riders for 'violations' (no international driving permit, no helmet, wrong road, expired tax) and demanding an on-the-spot 'fine' payable in cash with no receipt. One traveler wrote: 'police station, its idr 150,000. Either way you won't be contributing to Bali's corruption.' documents the veteran response: 'The cops are corrupt af but also you should've known better. Wear helmet and get an IDP so you don't' give them leverage.

50k IDR was more than enough to get through wi' thout incident. The 2025 escalation is more systematic — some checkpoints now process 20–30 tourists per morning, typically demanding 200K–500K IDR per stop. The scam mechanics: (1) checkpoint set up on known tourist routes (Jalan Raya Kuta, Jalan Sunset Road, near Tanah Lot); (2) police officer waves scooter to pull over; (3) officer cites violation (helmet angle, passport not in hand, 'wrong lane'); (4) officer quotes 1M–2M IDR 'official fine'; (5) officer 'sympathetically' suggests 200K–500K 'pay here' alternative as 'faster'; (6) some officers photograph your passport and license before returning them, creating data-harvest risk.

For older travelers (who should not be riding scooters in Bali anyway), the defensive rules: (1) get an International Driving Permit WITH motorcycle endorsement before travel — not having one is the single most common excuse; (2) wear helmet and ensure passenger wears one too; (3) keep passport + IDP in waterproof bag on body; (4) if stopped, insist on paying the 'official fine' at the police station (about 150K IDR); officers lose interest when cash-in-hand isn't the outcome; (5) do NOT escalate or argue — stay calm and professional; (6) if you're riding pillion with a local Grab driver, you're not at risk — the scam targets self-drive tourist riders.

Red Flags

  • Police checkpoint in tourist-heavy scooter area (Kuta, Legian, Seminyak, Tanah Lot)
  • Officer cites vague 'violation' (helmet angle, 'wrong lane')
  • Officer quotes 1M–2M IDR 'official fine' then suggests 200K–500K 'pay here'
  • Officer wants cash without issuing a written ticket (tilang)
  • Officer photographs your passport and license without clear reason

How to Avoid

  • Get International Driving Permit WITH motorcycle endorsement before travel.
  • Wear helmet (including passenger); keep passport + IDP in body-worn waterproof bag.
  • If stopped, INSIST on paying 'official fine' at police station (~150K IDR).
  • Stay calm; do not escalate or argue.
  • Use Grab/GoRide for older travelers — you're not a target as a passenger.

Scam #7
Organized Drink Spiking at Bali Beach Clubs & Nightlife
⚠️ High
📍 Kuta and Legian nightlife strip bars, Seminyak beach clubs (Potato Head, Finns), Canggu beach bars, Gili Islands nightclub cross-reference

Bali has a 2025 documented escalation in organized drink spiking targeting female tourists at beach clubs and nightlife venues. One traveler wrote: 'What I witnessed around us was organized, it looked coordinated. A group of three foreign men were g' roup-drugging tourist women at a named venue.

Community reports document the scopolamine variant: 'It kills hundreds every year in Indonesia, including some in Bali and the Gilis. It is legal to dist' ribute. Scopolamine is a deliriant that renders victims compliant and unable to recall events — commonly used in Latin American scams but documented in Bali and Gili nightlife since at least 2018. The specific 2025 patterns: (1) coordinated group of foreign men at Seminyak/Canggu beach clubs identifying solo or small-group female tourists; (2) drinks spiked in exchange moments (returning from bathroom, dancing); (3) victim becomes cooperative and compliant rather than incapacitated — removed from venue to hotel or elsewhere; (4) robbery, sexual assault, or both follow; (5) memory blackout typical.

For older travelers and all solo female travelers, the defensive playbook: (1) NEVER leave a drink unattended — not even for 30 seconds; (2) order bottled drinks where possible and open them yourself (beer from unopened bottle is safest); (3) at beach clubs, stay with trusted companions and set buddy check-in times; (4) refuse drinks you didn't personally order from the bartender; (5) if you feel unexpectedly disoriented, ask bar staff for help IMMEDIATELY — reputable Bali venues have trained protocols; (6) notify Polda Bali (+62 361 227 274) immediately if spiking suspected; (7) stay at hotels with proper security staff and taxi-desks rather than hostels or budget villas; (8) consider Ubud (calmer, yoga/retreat focused) over Kuta/Canggu for older traveler itineraries.

Red Flags

  • Drink left unattended at a beach club or nightclub
  • Coordinated group of foreign men positioning near solo or small-group female tourists
  • Drink 'gifted' by a stranger rather than delivered from the bartender
  • Unusual taste, color, or temperature in a drink
  • Sudden disorientation disproportionate to alcohol consumed

How to Avoid

  • NEVER leave a drink unattended — even for 30 seconds.
  • Order bottled drinks where possible and open them yourself.
  • Stay with trusted companions; set buddy check-in times at beach clubs.
  • Refuse drinks you didn't personally order from bartender.
  • If disoriented, ask bar staff IMMEDIATELY; call Polda Bali (+62 361 227 274).

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Indonesian National Police (Polri) station. Call 110 (Police) or 112 (Emergency). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at polri.go.id.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Jakarta is at Jl. Merdeka Selatan No. 3-5, Jakarta 10110. For emergencies: +62 21-5083-1000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
