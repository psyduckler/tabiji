Scam #1
Hoi An Tailor Scam & 'Exclusive' Fabric Markup
🔶 Medium
📍 Hoi An Ancient Town tailor shops (300+ operating), hotel-recommended tailor 'partners,' touts approaching tourists with 'free tea and design' offers, fake 'Worst Tailor' copycat branding

300+ tailor shops competing for the suit-and-dress custom of cruise-day and multi-night visitors.

300+ tailor shops competing for the suit-and-dress custom of cruise-day and multi-night visitors. Most are legitimate, but a distinct scam ecosystem operates around them. One traveler wrote: 'Went to Hoi An to find a good tailor and ended up getting scammed... 90% of tailor mad' e clothing uses fabric 2-3x cheaper than advertised, with rushed construction and no follow-up fittings.

The specific scam patterns: (1) 'free tea and design consultation' tout leads tourists to a specific shop where 'partner' commission arrangements inflate prices 30–50%; (2) 'premium fabric' upsell at 3x market rates — fabric that should be $30/metre sold as 'Italian wool' at $90/metre; (3) 'rushed 24-hour delivery' yields low-quality construction that doesn't survive one wash; (4) 'first fitting free, but alterations cost extra' when alterations should be included; (5) hotel-concierge 'partner tailor' that operates a kickback arrangement at the tourist's expense.

For older travelers considering a Hoi An tailored piece, the practical playbook: (1) use only community-verified tailors with 4.5+ Google ratings and long review histories — Yaly Couture, Bebe Tailor, and A Dong Silk are the three most-consistently-recommended; (2) allow 2–3 days minimum for a legitimate order (quality construction, 2–3 fittings); (3) bring a reference garment for fit — tailors match it; (4) REFUSE hotel 'partner tailor' recommendations; (5) verify fabric composition by touch and burn-test before purchase; (6) expect $150–$250 for a quality suit, $80–$150 for a tailored dress; $40 for a 'cheap shirt' is likely a scam; (7) pay 50% deposit, 50% on final fitting — NEVER pay 100% upfront; (8) for older travelers, skip the 1-day cruise-excursion rush entirely — tailoring needs time.

Red Flags

  • Tout offers 'free tea and design consultation' leading to a specific tailor
  • Hotel concierge pushes a 'partner tailor' — kickback arrangement
  • 'Premium fabric' upsell 3x market rate ('Italian wool,' 'Japanese silk')
  • Tailor promises 24-hour turnaround with no follow-up fittings
  • Pricing below $40 for a shirt or $150 for a suit (quality can't be achieved)

How to Avoid

  • Use community-verified tailors: Yaly Couture, Bebe Tailor, A Dong Silk (4.5+ Google ratings).
  • Allow 2–3 days minimum with 2–3 fittings; bring a reference garment for fit matching.
  • REFUSE hotel 'partner tailor' recommendations — kickback arrangement.
  • Pay 50% deposit, 50% on final fitting — NEVER 100% upfront.
  • Expected: $150–$250 suit, $80–$150 dress; below this signals quality issues.

Scam #3
Hoi An Ancient Town Ticket & 'Fake Ticket Checker' Confusion
🔶 Medium
📍 Hoi An Ancient Town entry points (all main-street entries), UNESCO heritage monument access checkpoints, tourist-trap 'ticket-selling' stands outside the legitimate booths

Hoi An's UNESCO Ancient Town charges a single entry fee that provides access to 5 of 20+ heritage monuments.

The ticket policy has generated confusion and a minor scam ecosystem. The money will' fund heritage preservation.

The scam variants that have emerged: (1) unofficial 'ticket checkers' at secondary entry points demanding cash for tickets already purchased; (2) 'combo ticket' touts outside the official booth selling 3x-markup tickets with 'extra access' claims that don't exist; (3) confusion about which monuments your ticket covers (it covers 5 of your choice, not all); (4) entry through back alleys attempted to avoid the fee — but checkers now patrol these routes.

For older travelers, the practical playbook: (1) buy Ancient Town tickets at OFFICIAL booths at main entrance points — 120,000 VND adults (2025); the ticket gives access to 5 monuments of your choice from a list of 20+; (2) plan your 5-monument choice: Japanese Covered Bridge, Phung Hung Ancient House, Hai Nan Assembly Hall, Tran Family Chapel, Quan Cong Temple are most community-recommended; (3) keep your ticket visible when walking the Ancient Town — checkers occasionally verify; (4) REFUSE 'ticket checkers' demanding additional cash if you already have a ticket; (5) for older travelers with mobility concerns, the Ancient Town is walkable in 3–4 hours with 2–3 monument stops plus lunch; (6) the 'full moon festival' (monthly, 14th day of lunar month) is free with your Ancient Town ticket — no additional fee.

Red Flags

  • Unofficial 'ticket checker' demanding additional cash at secondary entry point
  • 'Combo ticket' tout outside official booth selling 3x-markup tickets
  • Claim that 'regular' ticket doesn't cover monuments you want to visit
  • Touts promoting 'back alley' routes to 'avoid the fee'
  • Ticket prices above 120,000 VND for adult Ancient Town entry

How to Avoid

  • Buy at OFFICIAL booths at main Ancient Town entrance — 120K VND adults (2025).
  • Plan 5-monument choice: Japanese Covered Bridge, Phung Hung, Hai Nan Hall, Tran Family, Quan Cong.
  • Keep ticket visible when walking Ancient Town — checkers occasionally verify.
  • REFUSE 'ticket checkers' demanding additional cash if you already have a ticket.
  • Full-moon festival is FREE with Ancient Town ticket — no additional fee.

Scam #4
Hoi An 'Fruit Ladies' Photo Trap & Forced Weight Overcharge
🟢 Low
📍 Hoi An Ancient Town streets (Nguyen Thai Hoc, Tran Phu), Hoi An Central Market perimeter, photo-opportunity corners with 'authentic local' women carrying fruit baskets

A specific 2025 Hoi An scam targets tourists seeking 'authentic' photos of Vietnamese women carrying bamboo poles with fruit baskets (gánh hàng rong).

The scam sequence: (1) a woman in traditional dress with fruit baskets positions herself at a photogenic corner; (2) she offers to let tourists 'try on the hat and baskets for a photo'; (3) once the photo is taken, she demands payment — typically 200K–500K VND, sometimes with 'forced purchase' of fruit at 100K VND for what should cost 10K; (4) if the tourist refuses, she becomes loud and attracts a crowd, escalating social pressure.

Community reports captures the broader 2025 traveler sentiment: 'Out of all of that Hoi An was our least favorite. Feels like the motto is Cram them in a nd extract every dong.' The fruit-lady scam is part of a broader commercialization pattern where every photo opportunity has been monetised.

For older travelers, the practical defense: (1) NEVER accept offers to 'try on' a fruit-basket hat or pose with a stranger's props for a photo; (2) if you want an 'authentic' photo, politely ask from a distance WITHOUT touching props — agree a price (typically 20K–50K VND) before taking the photo; (3) buy fruit ONLY from fixed-stall vendors with posted-price signs, not from mobile fruit-lady carts; (4) typical fruit prices: 20K VND for a pineapple, 30K for 500g rambutan, 40K for a dragon fruit; (5) if caught in a photo-demand, pay 50K VND maximum (already above fair rate) and walk away — scammers rarely escalate beyond shouting; (6) document the experience for the traveler community forum so others are warned.

Red Flags

  • Woman in traditional dress positioned at photogenic corner offering to 'try on' her hat
  • English-speaking 'friendly local' approaching with props and camera invitation
  • Price only mentioned after photo is taken
  • Forced fruit purchase at 10x market rate accompanies photo demand
  • Raised voice or crowd escalation when you refuse to pay

How to Avoid

  • NEVER try on a fruit-basket hat or pose with stranger's props.
  • For 'authentic' photos, politely ask from a distance WITHOUT touching — agree 20K–50K VND before photo.
  • Buy fruit ONLY from fixed-stall vendors with posted prices (20K pineapple, 30K rambutan 500g).
  • If caught in a photo-demand, pay 50K VND maximum and walk away.
  • Scammers rarely escalate beyond shouting — don't let crowd pressure coerce you.
Scam #5
Hoi An Cooking Class / Tour Booking Kickback Fraud
🔶 Medium
📍 Hotel-concierge 'partner' cooking class and tour bookings, Hoi An Ancient Town tout-driven bookings, online bookings via unfamiliar aggregators

Hoi An's cooking-class economy and Cham Island/My Son day-trip bookings have a specific kickback-and-overcharge pattern.

Totally times... Scam after scam' — kickback-driven recommendations from hotels and touts inflate prices 30–100% above direct-booking rates. Feels like the motto is Cram them in' — commercialized tour volume amplifies kickback opportunities.

Community reports document the generic-tour-operator issue that applies in Hoi An. The scam variants: (1) hotel concierge recommends 'partner' cooking class at $50/person when the direct-booking rate at the same venue is $30/person; (2) tout at Ancient Town offers 'discount cooking class' at $35 but it's a lower-quality operator than advertised; (3) 'day trip to My Son temples' quoted at $60 per person via hotel when Klook/GetYourGuide direct is $25; (4) 'Cham Island snorkelling day trip' at $80 via tout when direct is $45; (5) online bookings via unfamiliar aggregators that upcharge 50%.

For older travelers considering a Hoi An cooking class or day tour, the protective playbook: (1) book cooking classes directly via the venue's own website — community-recommended: Red Bridge Cooking School (redbridgecookingschool.com), Morning Glory Cooking School (facebook.com/morningglorycooking), Vy's Market Restaurant Cooking Class; expect $25–$40 per person for 4-hour class including market visit; (2) for My Son temples, book via Klook/GetYourGuide at $20–$30 — half-day tour including guide; (3) for Cham Island snorkelling, book via Klook or direct at the An Bang Beach jetty at $40–$55 per person; (4) REFUSE hotel 'partner' recommendations and Ancient Town touts; (5) verify any online booking is via a platform with cancellation protection (Klook, GetYourGuide, Viator) — never wire transfer.

Red Flags

  • Hotel concierge pushes a 'partner cooking class' at $50+ per person
  • Ancient Town tout offers 'discount' cooking class at $30–$35
  • 'My Son day trip' quoted at $60+ via hotel or tout
  • Booking via unfamiliar aggregator website asking for wire transfer
  • Online booking with no cancellation protection

How to Avoid

  • Book cooking classes DIRECT: Red Bridge, Morning Glory, Vy's Market ($25–$40/person).
  • For My Son temples, book via Klook/GetYourGuide ($20–$30 half-day).
  • For Cham Island snorkelling, book Klook or An Bang jetty direct ($40–$55).
  • REFUSE hotel 'partner' recommendations and Ancient Town touts.
  • Only use platforms with cancellation protection (Klook, GetYourGuide, Viator) — never wire transfer.
Scam #6
Hoi An Beach Bicycle & Motorbike Rental Damage Claims
🔶 Medium
📍 Hoi An An Bang Beach bicycle rental shops, hotel-rental bicycles with 'damage deposit' at return, motorbike rentals at $5–$10/day operators

Hoi An's bike-rental economy serves the daily commute to An Bang Beach (5 km east of Ancient Town).

The scam: rental shops take a 'deposit' (cash, passport, or both) and at return claim minor scratches or tire issues to retain the deposit. I' — the rental + police shakedown combination is documented.

The scam variants: (1) bicycle rental shop takes 200K VND deposit, claims 'scratch damage' at return demanding 150K+ VND; (2) passport taken as deposit and withheld until additional 'cleaning fee' is paid; (3) motorbike rental at $5–$10/day shop claims 'helmet damage' or 'fuel shortage' at return; (4) hotel-provided 'free bicycles' charged 300K+ VND at checkout for 'damage' that wasn't documented. gives the Southeast Asia-wide passport-deposit warning: 'It's not a scam. Every time we travel to the islands in Thailand they take passports as de' posit — but the cross-Asia concern about passport-retention is real in Hoi An too.

For older travelers, the practical defense: (1) use hotel-provided bicycles where possible — most 3-4 star Hoi An hotels include bikes free with the stay; (2) if renting independently, use established operators (Mr. Hai, The Pedal Collective, Long Life Bikes) with clear written policies; (3) NEVER leave your passport as a deposit — offer cash deposit only (200K VND is standard); (4) photograph the bike from all angles BEFORE riding away; (5) for motorbikes, only use Motorvina/Tigit-quality operators with English paperwork; (6) at return, video the bike/motorbike showing no new damage; (7) if the shop claims damage you didn't cause, dispute with photographic evidence and contact Hoi An Tourist Police (Hoang Dieu Street, +84 235 3861 234) if escalated.

Red Flags

  • Rental shop demands passport as deposit (never leave your passport)
  • Deposit over 500K VND for a bicycle (200K is standard)
  • No written rental agreement or receipt offered
  • Shop doesn't offer walk-around inspection before you ride away
  • At return, shop claims minor scratches you didn't cause

How to Avoid

  • Use hotel-provided bicycles where possible (most 3-4 star Hoi An hotels include them).
  • For independent rental, use Mr. Hai, The Pedal Collective, or Long Life Bikes.
  • NEVER leave passport as deposit — cash deposit 200K VND is standard.
  • Photograph bike from all angles BEFORE riding away.
  • At return, video the bike showing no new damage.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Vietnamese Police (Công An) station. Call 113. Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at hanoi.gov.vn.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy is at 7 Lang Ha Street, Ba Dinh District, Hanoi. For emergencies: +84 24 3850-5000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
