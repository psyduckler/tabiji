The 4 Scams

Scam #1
The Nara Park Deer Antler & Bite Injury
⚠️ High
📍 Nara Park, Todai-ji approach, Kasuga Taisha approach

You buy a ¥200 pack of shika senbei near Todai-ji and within seconds five or six deer surround you — nosing pockets, tugging your map, one nipping your thigh when you hesitate. Most encounters end with a bruise or bite; roughly a third involve antlers and puncture wounds.

Nara Park deer are genuinely wild animals (国の天然記念物 / national natural monuments) that have learned tourists carry food. Nara Prefectural Government logged 159 injury cases in fiscal 2024, of which 111 involved tourists — rising every year since FY2021. Fiscal 2018 saw an all-time peak of 217 incidents. A Japanese academic study in Acute Medicine & Surgery analyzed 49 ambulance-transported cases, finding male children were the highest-risk victim group and that direct-contact trauma was more common than bites. During the September–October mating season (rut), male deer with un-clipped antlers become significantly more aggressive — September 2024 alone accounted for 35 injuries, 10 of them requiring hospitalization, a seven-fold jump from the year before. Foreign tourists account for more than half of incidents (historically over 80% in 2017, with 116 Chinese nationals among 171 cases that year). An Asahi Shimbun report in November 2024 described an emergency transport case where a stag's antler went through a tourist's thigh. This isn't a scam in the classical sense, but it's the dominant tourist-harm pattern in Nara and visitors routinely arrive believing the deer are petting-zoo tame when they are not — and Japan's Nihon Keizai Shimbun notes the deer have lived as wild animals in Nara for over 1,000 years, with no one legally responsible for injuries.

Red Flags

  • Deer lowers its head and stomps a front hoof — this is the warning to charge, not a bow
  • Deer follows you persistently after you've run out of crackers — they expect more and will nip
  • You're near a stag with intact antlers between late August and early November (mating season)
  • Small child feeding deer alone without an adult buffer — kids are the highest-risk group in the academic trauma study
  • You're teasing or withholding crackers for photos — teasing is the #1 documented bite trigger

How to Avoid

  • Buy only one pack of shika senbei at a time and hand them all out fast — delay/teasing is the #1 cited cause of bites per the Deer Preservation Foundation
  • Raise both hands empty and palm-out when out of crackers so deer see you have nothing — a Reddit commenter confirms "worked most of the time"
  • Stay away from stags with full antlers in September–October; park staff clip 10–15 antlers per day but cannot keep up
  • Do not let small children feed deer alone — the Wiley trauma study found male children were the most common victims
  • Read the multilingual (Japanese/English/Chinese) warning pamphlets distributed by the prefectural government and Shika Sodan-shitsu (Deer Consultation Room, shikasapo.jp)
  • If bitten, wash the wound with tap water and apply disinfectant — Japan has had no rabies cases in 50+ years, but call the Nara Deer Preservation Foundation at +81-742-22-2388 for serious injuries
Scam #2
The Sanjo-dori / Higashimuki-dori Souvenir Shakedown
🟢 Low
📍 Sanjo-dori shopping street, Higashimuki-dori, Todai-ji Nanmon approach

Between Kintetsu Nara Station and Todai-ji runs roughly 1 km of souvenir shops selling generic "Nara deer" keychains, fans, and sweet-bean cakes at 2–3× department-store prices. The bigger problem is rude treatment — shopkeepers shout at, and even spray water at, foreign tourists who linger out front.

At 奈良みやげ百貨商店 (Nara-miyage-hyakka-shoten), an April 10, 2026 Google Maps review describes the elderly male shopkeeper shouting at foreign tourists stopped in front to feed deer, with customers saying "please don't shout at us" in English. A 9-month-old 1★ review: "People at this store are so rude. They use shoot water on you without telling you just because you stand in front of the store for a while. And we are even with a kid." At なら和み館 (Nara Nagomikan), multiple 1★ reviews describe individual travelers being turned away because the shop prioritizes bus tours for group kickbacks — one reviewer was told by a parking attendant "no general cars, buses only" despite an entirely empty lot. At 春日野 restaurant/shop (Kasugano, 3.6★), foreign visitors describe being "rudely pushed out" and one traveler was forced back into heavy rain after taking shelter under the awning. An industry survey covered by atpress found 1 in 5 inbound tourists to Japan report overcharging, concentrated in restaurants and souvenir shops — and a TBS News Dig investigation flagged Nara as having one of Japan's lowest per-tourist inbound-spending figures, creating pressure on park-adjacent shops to extract maximum value from each visitor.

Red Flags

  • Prices not clearly labeled on individual items; no total price list visible
  • Shop staff shout at or spray water at tourists standing outside the store
  • Staff speak only Japanese to foreigners and use dismissive hand gestures
  • You are told "group bookings only" despite an empty shop / empty parking lot
  • Shop is unusually large and parking-lot-oriented — a sign it's a bus-tour captive

How to Avoid

  • Skip the Sanjo-dori/Higashimuki-dori souvenir strips and buy identical items at Don Quijote, Kintetsu Nara department store, or Mahoroba Plaza for 30–50% less
  • For real Nara crafts, visit branded specialists (中川政七商店 main store is 4.2★/601 reviews) rather than generic omiyage shops
  • Avoid bus-tour captive shops like なら和み館 — individual walk-ins are explicitly deprioritized per multiple 1★ reviews
  • If you encounter a rude or aggressive shopkeeper, Nara's Tourist Information Center (Kintetsu Nara Station 2F) accepts complaints in English
  • Check the venue's Google Maps rating before entering — anything under 3.8★ for a tourist-adjacent souvenir shop in Nara is a reliable red flag
Scam #3
The Nara Ryokan Bait-and-Switch
🔶 Medium
📍 Nara city hotels near Kintetsu Nara Station and Todai-ji

You book a "traditional Nara ryokan experience" from beautiful photos of a tatami room with a private outdoor bath. On the day of arrival the hotel switches you to a lower-grade room "for our reasons," the outdoor bath is cold water the next morning, or the "ryokan" turns out to be a renovated 1970s apartment building.

At 和鹿彩別邸 / Wakasa Bettei and sister property Hotel New Wakasa (Nara Park-adjacent, 4.2–4.3★ overall ratings concealing clustered 1★ complaints), guests describe the experience using the Japanese phrase "おとり広告" (bait-and-switch advertising). A 2023 1★ review: "On the day of arrival, staff told me 'we've closed the annex for our reasons, we've upgraded you to the main building' with no reason given... felt like bait-and-switch advertising." Another: "The in-room outdoor bath — when I tried to use it the next morning, it was basically cold water and I was shocked." A 9-year-old 1★ review from Wakasa Bettei states bluntly: "It was a fake and modern ryokan style room... I want to warn the next customers going in there it's a scam." More structurally, a 3-month-old review documents a double-billing / cancellation mishandling: guest re-booked the same day via web, double charge resulted, the elderly front-desk man said "we'll cancel it here, don't worry" — but nothing was processed and no refund came through months of follow-up. Another 2-year-old review describes the hotel refusing a refund after a family medical emergency and threatening lawyer involvement. Nara's ryokan market is unusually small — locals openly say "奈良には良い宿がない" (there are no good inns in Nara) — which has combined with low-season desperation to produce several operators playing fast-and-loose with bookings.

Red Flags

  • Room "upgrade" or "change" announced at check-in without prior notification
  • Photos on the booking site show features (outdoor bath, tatami view) that aren't in your actual room
  • The hotel answers differently to the same question depending on which staff member you call
  • Cancellation request not acknowledged by a human email reply despite an automated receipt
  • Double booking produced by booking both directly and via a third-party site — hotel refuses to refund one

How to Avoid

  • Only book Nara accommodations with 50+ English-language reviews and 4.2+ ratings (weight the most recent 20 reviews most heavily)
  • Screenshot the exact room and amenity photos you booked; photograph bills at check-in and bring those screenshots if a "change" is announced
  • Always get written (human) email confirmation of cancellations — the auto-reply alone is not proof the cancel was processed
  • Do not double-book the same dates on direct + third-party platforms to avoid the "ダブルブッキング" dispute pattern documented at Hotel New Wakasa
  • Prefer large chain hotels (Nara Royal Hotel, Kasuga Hotel) or Japan Ryokan Association-certified properties over boutique "concept ryokans" with fewer than 100 reviews
  • Report booking-platform fraud to Booking.com, Rakuten Travel, or Jalan immediately; Japan's Minpaku Law (民泊法) requires short-term rentals to be licensed
Scam #4
The Nara Taxi Long-Route Detour
🟢 Low
📍 Kintetsu Nara Station / JR Nara Station taxi stands

You need a quick taxi from Kintetsu Nara Station to Todai-ji or your hotel 2 km away. The driver asks "Shall I take Yasuragi-no-michi?" and you say yes because you recognize the name. He then routes via Prefectural Route 751 + Route 104 — a 6.8 km detour that takes 18–35 minutes and inflates the fare.

A Google Maps 1★ review of 奈良近鉄タクシー (Nara Kintetsu Taxi, the dominant dispatcher) documents this exact pattern with taxi number 1446: the customer's Google Maps navigation would have taken the short Narayama-dori route, but the driver chose the prefectural-route alternate instead. The dispatcher sits at 2.5★ / 62 reviews overall with clustered complaints about drivers "running the meter," dispatch confusion between east and west station exits, and rude call-center behavior — one reviewer quotes a driver snapping "メーター上がるので早くしてくださいよ" (hurry up because the meter is running) with an arrogant attitude. The same parent company's sister brand 大和交通 / Kai-Nara Taxi sits at 1.9–2.0★ across 16–30 reviews. Nara isn't Tokyo-style taxi culture: the fleet is small, English is limited, and reviewers explicitly note "タクシー配車アプリGOのほうがスムーズに予約とれます" — the GO app is smoother than calling dispatch because it shows route and estimated fare before you board. Separately, OhaJapan and Japan's Ministry of Land Transport warn about unmetered "unofficial taxis" at airports and stations — legal taxis in Japan always carry green license plates (緑ナンバー); white-plate cars offering paid rides are illegal.

Red Flags

  • Driver asks you to confirm a route name rather than typing your destination into the meter GPS
  • Taxi queue has a long wait but dispatch says no cars are available when you call
  • Meter starts running before you're seated and given a door-closing signal
  • Driver takes prefectural-route alternates when a direct city street runs to your destination
  • The route distance on your Google Maps estimate (before booking) is >30% shorter than the taxi's actual route

How to Avoid

  • Use the GO taxi app for Nara Kintetsu Taxi — it shows route and estimated fare before you board, and reviewers explicitly prefer it over calling dispatch
  • Show the driver Google Maps with the route highlighted and decline any route alternatives offered verbally
  • Walk instead for any distance under 2 km — Nara central attractions are walkable (Kintetsu Nara Station to Todai-ji is about 20 minutes on foot)
  • Use the Nara City Loop Bus or Kintetsu/JR rail for station-to-station hops (far cheaper than a taxi)
  • Prefer higher-rated taxi stands: 近鉄奈良駅前観光タクシー乗り場 (4.0★) and 近鉄奈良駅前中型タクシー乗り場 (5.0★) rather than 奈良駅東口タクシー乗り場 (2.9★)
  • Only use green-plate licensed taxis — white-plate cars offering paid rides are illegal under Japanese transport law

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Japanese Police (Keisatsu) station or koban. Call 110. Get an official crime report — you'll need it for insurance and chargebacks. For deer injuries specifically, contact the Nara Deer Preservation Foundation at +81-742-22-2388.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block suspicious transactions before anyone uses your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The nearest US consulate to Nara is the US Consulate General Osaka-Kobe at 2-11-5 Nishitenma, Kita-ku, Osaka 530-8543. For emergencies: +81 6-6315-5900.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
