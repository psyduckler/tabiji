6 Tourist Scams in Fukuoka

Documented from Japanese police reports, Nishinippon Shimbun, Yomiuri investigations, and hundreds of traveler reviews.

Key Takeaways

  • Nakasu girls-bar overcharging is the most dangerous scam — documented cases of ¥1,000,000 billed for three hours, with yakuza-linked operators
  • 4 of 6 scams are rated high risk — Fukuoka's scam profile is harder-edged than Tokyo's or Osaka's, concentrated in Nakasu and Hakata Station's Tsukushi-guchi exit
  • Never follow a street tout (kyakuhiki) into any bar in Nakasu or at Hakata Station — Fukuoka Prefecture's anti-tout ordinance exists precisely because this happens daily
  • At Hakata Station, use the Hakata-guchi (west) exit instead of Tsukushi-guchi (east) to avoid the two organized tout groups Yomiuri documented in 2024

⚡ Quick Safety Tips

  • Only enter bars and izakayas you found on Google Maps or Tabelog — never one a stranger led you to
  • If a Tinder or Bumble match picks the venue in Nakasu or Tenjin, unmatch and leave — this is a documented scam lure
  • At Fukuoka Airport and Hakata Station, only take taxis from the official queue with green license plates
  • Never pay short-term rental hosts outside Booking.com or Airbnb — off-platform payment requests are always a scam
  • If presented with a shocking bill, stay calm, ask for an itemized receipt, and call 110 — Fukuoka Police actively prosecute bottakuri cases

The 6 Scams

Scam #1
The Nakasu Girls Bar ¥1,000,000 Overcharge
⚠️ High
📍 Nakasu entertainment district, Fukuoka

A young woman with a signboard reading "60分 3000円" or a tout promising "cute girls, cheap drinks" lures you into a Nakasu girls bar. Three hours later the bill is ¥400,000 — sometimes over ¥1,000,000.

Inside, hostesses keep topping up your glass without ever quoting prices. When the bill arrives, bouncers appear at the door. A March 2024 Nishinippon Shimbun investigation documented one victim billed ¥1,000,000 for three hours at a Nakasu girls bar, with Fukuoka Prefectural Police reporting a surge of similar consultations as post-COVID tourism recovered. A January 2025 Yomiuri case saw a customer charged ¥710,000 at a Nakasu snack bar — the dispute turned violent and the customer was arrested for assaulting the employee. Some of these venues have yakuza ties: Yomiuri reported in April 2023 that the Kudo-kai-affiliated girls bar VIBLISS had operated without a license since 2018, earning an estimated several hundred million yen that funded organized crime. Japanese-language forum Bakusai documents a specific case at the now-defunct Nakasu Secret: 8 people, 2 hours, ¥526,500.

Red Flags

  • Female signboard workers standing on Nakasu Central Street with low hourly prices (60 min / 3000 yen, etc.)
  • A tout or matching-app contact steers you toward a specific bar instead of one you chose
  • No visible menu or pricing once you are seated
  • Hostesses keep ordering champagne or bottles without asking price or confirming with you
  • Door staff physically block your exit when you question the bill

How to Avoid

  • Never follow any street tout (kyakuhiki) into any Nakasu venue — it is illegal under Fukuoka Prefecture's ordinance and almost always leads to a bottakuri bar
  • Stick to venues with 4.5+ stars on Google Maps and 50+ English-language reviews
  • If you are seated without a printed menu with prices, stand up and leave before ordering anything
  • Fukuoka Prefecture has a formal Bottakuri Prevention Ordinance (ぼったくり防止条例) — call 110 and cite it if staff pressure you
  • If you choose to visit a girls bar, go only to licensed venues advertising transparent pricing like "明朗会計" on their own signage
Scam #2
The Nakasu Yatai Foreign-Tourist Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Nakasu riverside yatai (food stalls) along the Naka River

You cross to the Nakasu riverside for Fukuoka's famous yatai experience. You ordered roughly ¥3,200 of food. The bill is ¥5,600 — and reading reviews later, you learn this happens almost exclusively to foreigners.

A detailed March 2025 Google Maps review of 博多屋台 中洲 十番, written in Taiwanese, documents paying ¥5,600 for a ¥3,200 order and notes: "This is not an isolated case. Almost all victims are foreign tourists. Even though I could communicate in Japanese, I still got this treatment." A Nishinippon Shimbun reporter's price survey confirmed Nakasu yatai are systematically more expensive than the Tenjin yatai that Fukuoka locals actually use. Japanese 1-star reviews tell the same story: "I was ripped off. After a big argument I got a partial refund. My hotel said 'that happens often.' Locals don't go here." Another English-language review calls it "a rather unhygienic roadside stall charging five-star restaurant prices (around ¥8,000 for 2 people)." Multiple Yahoo! 知恵袋 threads warn Japanese travelers away from Nakasu's tourist-facing stalls.

Red Flags

  • A tout at the stall actively calls out to you from the street — locals are not recruited this way
  • The menu is not visible from your seat, or prices are not listed at all
  • Staff speak limited Japanese; "point at the menu" becomes the only way to communicate
  • Your bill is not itemized and doesn't match what you ordered
  • A one-drink-one-dish minimum is enforced only after you sit down, not posted at the entrance

How to Avoid

  • Eat at Tenjin yatai (especially Nagahama) instead — cheaper, same atmosphere, actual locals
  • If you visit Nakasu yatai, photograph the menu before sitting and keep a running tally on your phone
  • Refuse any stall where a tout pulled you in from the street
  • Japan law lets you demand an itemized receipt before paying — do it
  • On Google Maps, stalls with 4.5+ stars from Japanese reviewers are safe; 3.5 stars or lower is where the bottakuri complaints cluster
Scam #3
The Hakata Station Tsukushi-guchi Tout Shakedown
⚠️ High
📍 JR Hakata Station Tsukushi-guchi (east) exit

You exit Hakata Station's east side with your luggage. Within seconds, a man in a suit steps into your path: "Beer? 3000 yen, all-you-can-drink, very good restaurant." That "3000 yen" becomes a ¥15,000–30,000 bill.

An April 2024 Yomiuri Shimbun investigation documented that Hakata Station touts are organized into two major groups at the Tsukushi-guchi east exit, earn 10–20% of each customer's spend as commission, and top earners pull in ¥500,000 per month exclusively catching tourists. Fukuoka Police's Nakasu Special Investigation Unit arrested 8 touts between September 2023 and January 2024 under the Prefecture's Nuisance Prevention Ordinance. A July 2024 "disbandment ceremony" was held at Hakata Police Station — but a follow-up Yomiuri report confirmed the same groups have been operating again. Once inside the izakaya, appetizers (otoshi) you didn't order appear at ¥500–1000 each, a "service charge" gets added, and staff turn aggressive when you question the bill. A January 2026 arrest added to the pattern: a 20-something cabaret manager was charged for paying another man to bring in customers at a Nakasu venue.

Red Flags

  • Man in a business suit blocks your path immediately as you exit Tsukushi-guchi
  • He quotes a specific low price ("3000 yen all-you-can-drink") but won't show a menu up front
  • He walks you multiple blocks to a venue you can't see from the station
  • The izakaya sends out appetizers you didn't order, each charged separately
  • The final bill adds a "service charge" or "seat charge" not mentioned when you sat down

How to Avoid

  • Use the Hakata-guchi (west) exit — touts are concentrated on the Tsukushi-guchi (east) side
  • Ignore all street touts — do not stop, do not make eye contact, do not say "no thanks" (they read engagement as an opening)
  • Only go to restaurants you selected on Google Maps or Tabelog before arriving
  • If pressured, call 110 — Fukuoka Police's Nakasu Special Investigation Unit actively prosecutes this pattern
  • Fukuoka City has posted anti-tout warning signage at Tsukushi-guchi since 2020 — it's not a rumor, it's a city-acknowledged problem
Scam #4
The Tinder / Matching App Bar Lure
⚠️ High
📍 Nakasu and Tenjin bars introduced via dating apps

You match on Tinder or Bumble with a friendly local woman. She suggests meeting at "this nice bar I know" in Nakasu. One drink each, a small plate — and the bill is ¥40,000 to ¥80,000. Her job was to deliver you.

These "sakura" (plant) matches are explicitly documented on Japanese local forum Bakusai, which names a specific active operation: PIGLET bar on the 3rd floor of the Yamamoto Building near Nakasukawabata Station (next to the Don Quijote). The warning is blunt: "If a woman from a matching app takes you there, it's a bottakuri bar — watch out." On r/fukuoka, a local commenter noted that a friend "just lost ¥50,000 on this scam in Tenjin." The woman is paid per delivered customer and typically vanishes to the bathroom when the bill is about to arrive. Both the UK Foreign Office and US State Department warn in their Japan travel advisories about this broader pattern, including specific warnings that "U.S. citizens have reported being threatened with guns or knives in such venues" and that British travelers have been arrested over disputes with bar staff over excessive bills.

Red Flags

  • She suggests the venue — not you, not from Google Maps
  • The bar is in a multi-tenant building, often on an upper floor with no street-facing sign
  • She pushes to meet "right now" instead of suggesting coffee or a walk first
  • She seems to know the staff, who greet her by name as you arrive
  • She conveniently goes to the bathroom right before the bill is presented

How to Avoid

  • If matching on an app in Fukuoka, insist on a venue you chose from Google Maps with visible street frontage and 4.5+ reviews
  • If she refuses your venue suggestion, unmatch — no exceptions
  • Suggest daytime coffee or a walk before drinks; genuine interest survives a daytime meeting
  • If warning signs hit at the bar entrance (upper floor, no sign, she's familiar with staff), leave before ordering
  • Screenshot the Bakusai warning for PIGLET (Yamamoto Bldg 3F near Nakasukawabata Don Quijote) — knowing specific named venues helps you pattern-match
Scam #5
The Booking.com "Cash in the Fridge" Rental Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Short-term rentals near Hakata Station (south side)

You book a budget apartment on Booking.com for ¥8,000/night, 500m from Hakata Station. Minutes later, the host messages: "Photograph ¥50,500 in cash and leave it inside the refrigerator." You already paid in full.

A documented October 2025 r/fukuoka report names the property exactly: 駅南うちやまビル 201 (Eki-minami Uchiyama Building, unit 201) at 812-0016 Fukuoka, roughly 1,500 feet from Hakata Station. The host demanded ¥50,500 cash be placed inside the fridge despite a ¥8,000 pre-paid booking through Booking.com. When the guest refused and asked for a refund, the host denied it, and the guest initiated a PayPal chargeback. The pattern of off-platform payment demands from Japanese short-term rental "hosts" is spreading as unregulated Airbnb-style units proliferate around tourist zones — the Minpaku Law requires a license, and unlicensed operations are illegal. Booking.com and Airbnb will delist a property the moment you report this, and your credit card issuer will almost always process the chargeback.

Red Flags

  • Host messages you directly (outside Booking.com's chat system) demanding additional payment
  • Payment request is for cash or an unusual method ("leave it in the fridge")
  • Amount requested is 3–10× your paid booking
  • The property is in an obscure multi-tenant building with no obvious reception
  • Host refuses a refund once you object to the off-platform payment

How to Avoid

  • Report any off-platform payment request to Booking.com or Airbnb immediately so they can delist the property
  • Never pay cash outside a booking platform — legitimate hosts never need this
  • Initiate a credit card chargeback with your issuer if any unauthorized charge appears
  • Report to Fukuoka Police (Hakata-ku division) — Japan's Minpaku Law makes unlicensed rentals prosecutable
  • For Hakata stays, prefer listings at real hotels with 50+ reviews over apartment-style rentals
Scam #6
The Hakata Station Phishing Approach
🔶 Medium
📍 Hakata Station and Fukuoka Airport taxi stands

A friendly stranger approaches: "Sorry, are you visiting Fukuoka? I work for the tourism office — can I take a quick photo of your passport for a welcome survey?" Or at the airport, an "unofficial taxi" offers ¥8,000 to Hakata — the real metered fare is ¥1,500.

A 2025 r/TokyoTravel thread titled "Strange encounter at Fukuoka Station" was answered by a long-time Japan resident identifying the pattern: "It is a common phishing scam targeting foreigners in Japan, especially around transit hubs or touristy places." The airport unofficial-taxi pattern (kyakuhiki at airports) is explicitly warned about by Japanese travel safety sites, which note that real Japanese taxis all have green license plates and that Fukuoka Airport displays official warning signage about this scam. The "tourism survey" approach harvests passport data and hotel location for follow-up fraud — fake hotel-charge emails, identity theft, or targeted bar-scam outreach at your hotel. A detour-taxi variant is also documented on r/japanlife: drivers taking long routes from Fukuoka Airport to downtown, turning a ¥1,500 fare into ¥4,000+.

Red Flags

  • A stranger asks for your passport, phone number, or hotel name at a station or airport
  • Someone approaches you offering "help" you didn't request
  • The person claims to be from a "tourism office" or "government" but has no ID or uniform
  • An "unofficial taxi" driver at the airport quotes a flat price — real Japanese taxis always use meters
  • The taxi does not have green license plates (the legal marker of a Japanese taxi)

How to Avoid

  • Only use the official taxi queue at Fukuoka Airport and Hakata Station — look for green license plates
  • Never share passport or hotel details with strangers who approach you unsolicited
  • If you need directions, ask uniformed station staff at the information counter
  • From Fukuoka Airport, take the Subway Namboku Line (¥270) or the Airport Shuttle Bus (¥470) instead of any taxi — cheaper and scam-proof
  • Download Google Maps offline before arrival so you never need to accept "help" from a stranger

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Japanese Police (Keisatsu) station or koban. Call 110. Fukuoka Police's Nakasu Special Investigation Unit actively prosecutes bottakuri and kyakuhiki cases. Get an official crime report — you'll need it for insurance and chargebacks.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block suspicious transactions before they use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Consulate Fukuoka is at 5-26 Ohori 2-chome, Chuo-ku, Fukuoka 810-0052. For emergencies: +81 92-751-9331.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
