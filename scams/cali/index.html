The 6 Scams

Scam #1
Scopolamine Drink Spiking (Devil's Breath)
⚠️ High
📍 Salsa clubs in Juanchito, bars in Menga and Granada neighborhoods, Parque del Perro area, and dating app meetups

Attractive strangers — sourced via dating apps, hotel bars, or Cali nightlife districts — drug visitors with scopolamine (burundanga / 'devil's breath'), which renders victims compliant or unconscious for up to 24 hours while accomplices drain bank accounts and steal electronics; losses range from $500 to complete ATM drainage.

Scopolamine is extracted from the Borrachero tree (Brugmansia) that grows wild throughout the Colombian Andes. It eliminates short-term memory and renders victims chemically compliant — unable to resist instructions. In Cali, the drug is most commonly deployed via dating apps: an attractive match agrees to meet at a bar in Granada or Parque del Perro, steers the visitor toward a round of drinks, and doses the glass while the victim steps away to the bathroom. Hotel bar encounters and 'friendly local' introductions at salsa clubs are secondary vectors.

The victim wakes hours later — typically in their hotel room — with phone, wallet, watch, and cash missing. Bank accounts have been drained at multiple ATMs during the hours of compliance. The perpetrator is unreachable. Because the victim has no memory of what followed the drugged drink, reconstructing the timeline for a police report is nearly impossible, and consular assistance is limited to providing a crime-reference number for insurance purposes.

Never leave your drink unattended and never accept a drink from a stranger — order a fresh drink when returning from the bathroom, watch it being poured, and cover the glass with your hand between sips — meet any dating app contact only in a busy public place and inform someone of the meeting with the person's profile details before going.

Red Flags

  • A stranger — especially an unusually attractive one — initiates contact and is extremely eager to meet at a bar or come to your room
  • Your date insists on ordering drinks for you or handles your drink while you are not watching
  • You feel unusually drowsy or disoriented after consuming a normal amount of alcohol
  • Your date suggests leaving the bar quickly or going somewhere more private after you have been drinking
  • The person you matched with online looks different from their photos or avoids being photographed together

How to Avoid

  • Never leave your drink unattended — take it with you to the bathroom or order a fresh one when you return.
  • Meet dating app matches only in busy public places and tell a friend or hotel reception your plans with the person's details.
  • Do not accept drinks from strangers, food samples from street vendors, or cigarettes from anyone you do not know.
  • Watch your drink being prepared and served — scopolamine can be slipped into drinks, applied to paper, or even blown in your face in powdered form.
  • If you suddenly feel very dizzy or disoriented, immediately tell the bartender or bouncer — do not wait or try to walk home alone.
Scam #2
The Motorcycle Phone Snatch
⚠️ High
📍 Major intersections throughout Cali, especially along Avenida Sexta, Avenida Roosevelt, near Parque del Perro, and the San Antonio neighborhood

Two-rider motorcycles operating throughout Cali — concentrated on Avenida Sexta, Avenida Roosevelt, near Parque del Perro, and in San Antonio — snatch phones from pedestrians' hands in passing; the phone disappears into traffic in under two seconds with no opportunity to intervene or capture a plate number.

Cali's street layout — major avenidas carrying fast motorcycle traffic through neighborhood commercial zones — creates ideal conditions for grab-and-run theft. The technique uses two riders: the driver maintains speed near the curb while the passenger reaches out to grab phones, necklaces, or bags. The action takes less than two seconds. Tourist neighborhoods including San Antonio, Granada, and Parque del Perro are particularly targeted because the foot-traffic density creates frequent opportunities while adjacent streets allow instant escape.

The loss is immediate and irreversible. The phone is typically stripped and resold same-day through an established second-hand electronics market. Any data not protected by biometrics is accessible. Because the incident is over before the victim registers what happened, police reports rarely produce recovery. The same tactic applies to gold necklaces, watches, and cameras — any high-value item worn or held visibly near a road is a viable target.

Never use your phone while walking near any road in Cali — step into a shop doorway or against a building wall before checking your screen, keep the phone in a front zippered pocket, and use earbuds for navigation audio so the phone stays out of sight — remove visible gold jewelry and watches for the duration of any outdoor time in the city.

Red Flags

  • A motorcycle with two riders slows down near you — the passenger is the one who will grab while the driver accelerates
  • You are standing near a busy road with your phone in your hand, especially at intersections where motorcycles stop
  • Someone on foot seems to be watching you use your phone and looking toward the street — they may be spotting for a motorcycle team
  • You hear a motorcycle engine revving or approaching from behind while you are walking near the curb
  • The area is transitional — between tourist zones and residential areas — where police presence is lighter

How to Avoid

  • Never use your phone while walking near any road in Cali — step inside a shop, cafe, or doorway if you need to check your phone.
  • Walk on the side of the sidewalk furthest from the street, keeping buildings between you and traffic.
  • Keep your phone in a front zippered pocket, not in your hand, and use earbuds for navigation so you do not need to look at the screen.
  • Do not wear visible gold chains, expensive watches, or flashy jewelry in any part of Cali.
  • If you must carry your phone visible, use a phone lanyard clipped to your belt or bag strap so it cannot be snatched cleanly.
Scam #3
Salsa Club Pickpocketing
🔶 Medium
📍 Salsa clubs in Juanchito, Tin Tin Deo, La Topa Tolondra, Zaperoco, and Menga nightlife district

Cali's salsa clubs in Juanchito and the Menga nightlife district are targeted by pickpocket teams who use the close physical contact of salsa dancing as cover for wallet and phone theft — losses of $100–$400 in cash and cards per incident are typical, with no recourse once the theft is complete.

Cali is the world capital of salsa, and clubs in Juanchito, Tin Tin Deo, La Topa Tolondra, Zaperoco, and Menga are a core part of the visitor experience. The same characteristics that make salsa dancing distinctive — close physical contact, frequent spinning and dipping, packed floors — create ideal conditions for skilled pickpockets. Working in teams, one member initiates a dance invitation while a partner works the edges of the crowd. Bags and valuables left on tables are vulnerable during the 20–30 minutes a visitor is on the dance floor.

The theft is discovered at end-of-night when a visitor reaches for their wallet to pay for drinks and finds it gone. Perpetrators have long since left. Because the clubs are crowded and CCTV coverage is limited, police reports produce no outcome. Losses include cash — typically 200,000–500,000 COP — plus any cards carried. Replacing bank cards from Colombia requires international courier delivery, typically 5–10 days.

Carry only the cash you plan to spend that night in a front trouser pocket — leave all cards, your passport, and additional cash in the hotel safe before going out — never put a phone or bag on a table while dancing, and use a club with a locker or coat-check service (Tin Tin Deo has both) for anything that can't stay on your body.

Red Flags

  • A stranger is unusually insistent on dancing with you despite a visible language barrier or skill gap — they may be creating a distraction
  • You notice someone's hands near your pockets during a close dance move, especially during spins or dips
  • Your dance partner steers you toward a crowded area of the floor where physical contact with others is unavoidable
  • Items left on tables are moved by club staff to a 'safer' area — check that they are actually there
  • A group takes the table next to yours and one member stays behind while the others dance — watching bags

How to Avoid

  • Leave your wallet, passport, and cards at the hotel — bring only enough cash for the night in a front pocket.
  • Use a slim money belt or neck wallet under your shirt for your cash and phone.
  • Never leave your phone, camera, or bag unattended on a table — keep them on your body or leave them at the hotel.
  • Go to salsa clubs with a group rather than alone — one person can always watch the table and belongings.
  • Choose reputable clubs with coat check or locker services (like Tin Tin Deo) where you can secure valuables before dancing.

Scam #4
Fake Police ID Shakedown
⚠️ High
📍 Streets near Parque del Perro, Barrio San Antonio, areas outside hostels and tourist accommodations, quiet streets at night

Plain-clothes scammers in Cali flash fake police ID cards on quiet streets, claim to be conducting drug or counterfeit-money investigations, and 'inspect' wallets — palming cash during the search; losses of 300,000–800,000 COP ($75–$200 USD) per incident are typical.

Colombian police do conduct plainclothes operations, which gives the scam its surface credibility. The setup works on quiet streets in San Antonio, near hostels, and around Parque del Perro at night: two men in civilian clothing approach a tourist, produce a badge or ID card flashed too quickly to read, and claim to be investigating drug trafficking, counterfeit pesos, or an immigration irregularity. A second person positions behind the tourist to limit the exit. The urgency of the claimed investigation is used to justify immediate inspection of the wallet, bag, or pockets.

Once the wallet is in the scammer's hands for 'inspection,' cash is palmed and the wallet returned. The victim — nervous throughout and uncertain whether these were real officers — often doesn't discover the loss until later. In escalated versions, the scammer 'finds' a suspicious-looking bill, declares it counterfeit, and confiscates the entire wallet for 'processing.' Real Colombian police never ask to inspect cash on the street, never request inspection of a tourist's wallet contents, and never collect any payment of any kind.

Never hand your wallet or passport to anyone who stops you on a street claiming to be police — say 'Vamos a la estación de policía' (Let's go to the police station) clearly, and walk toward the nearest busy area or open shop — legitimate plainclothes officers will accompany you without objection, and scammers will back off rather than enter a real station.

Red Flags

  • Plain-clothes individuals approach you flashing a badge too quickly for you to read the details
  • They ask to see your wallet, inspect your money, or search your bag — real police never need to check your cash
  • The encounter happens on a quiet street rather than a checkpoint or official area
  • They create urgency — claiming drug operations, counterfeiting investigations, or immigration checks
  • A second person positions behind you or blocks your exit route

How to Avoid

  • Never hand over your wallet, cash, or passport to anyone on the street — offer to walk to the nearest police station instead.
  • Carry only a photocopy of your passport (the data page and entry stamp) — leave the original at the hotel safe.
  • If confronted, say loudly 'Vamos a la estación de policía' (Let's go to the police station) — scammers will back off.
  • Real Colombian police carry a standardized ID card with a photo, rank, and badge number — ask to see it and photograph it.
  • Walk toward a crowded area, enter a shop, or flag down a passing taxi if the interaction feels threatening.
Scam #5
The Millionaire's Taxi Ride (Express Kidnapping)
⚠️ High
📍 Street-hailed taxis throughout Cali, especially outside nightlife districts, at night, and near bus terminals

Street-hailed taxis in Cali — particularly outside nightlife districts at night — are used for 'paseo millonario' (millionaire's ride) express kidnappings: a driver picks up an armed accomplice mid-route, holds the tourist for 2–4 hours, and forces ATM withdrawals at consecutive machines until daily limits are exhausted, with total losses of $400–$2,000 USD typical.

The paseo millonario operates exclusively via street-hailed taxis. The driver appears legitimate, quotes a reasonable fare, and begins the route normally. Partway through — typically 5–10 minutes in, after leaving the busy zone where a quick exit would be possible — the driver stops to 'pick up a friend' who turns out to be an armed accomplice. The victim is now in a moving vehicle under control with no safe exit option. This is one of Colombia's most well-documented crimes against tourists, and every major government travel advisory — US State Department, UK Foreign Office, Canadian Global Affairs — specifically warns about it in Colombian cities.

The taxi circuit typically visits three to five different ATMs, with the victim forced to withdraw the daily maximum at each one. Banks have daily ATM limits — typically $200–$400 USD equivalent — so kidnappers visit multiple machines across different networks to maximize extraction. Electronics, jewelry, and any cash are taken directly. Victims are dropped in an unfamiliar neighborhood — rarely physically harmed if compliant — but left without phone, cards, or cash. Bank account recovery is possible but card cancellation during the event is not.

Never hail a taxi from the street in Cali — use InDrive, Uber, or DiDi exclusively, where the driver is identified, the route is tracked, and a record of the trip exists — if apps are unavailable, have your hotel or restaurant call a registered company; set daily ATM withdrawal limits to the minimum viable amount in your banking app before arriving.

Red Flags

  • You hail a taxi from the street rather than using an app — this is the single biggest risk factor
  • The taxi does not match the registration details visible on the dashboard or has no visible registration at all
  • The driver picks up another passenger after you are already in the vehicle
  • The route deviates from what your phone shows — the driver is heading to an area you do not recognize
  • The driver asks which bank you use or if you have cash in your hotel room

How to Avoid

  • Don't hail a taxi from the street in Cali — use InDrive, Uber, or DiDi exclusively, where rides are tracked and drivers are identified.
  • If ride-hailing apps are unavailable, have your hotel or restaurant call a registered taxi company for you.
  • Share your live location with a friend or family member before getting into any vehicle.
  • Set low daily ATM withdrawal limits through your banking app — this limits what criminals can extract even in a worst-case scenario.
  • Keep most of your cash, cards, and your passport in the hotel safe — carry only what you need for the outing.
Scam #6
Nightclub Drink Overcharging Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Bars and clubs in Menga, La Sexta, Parque del Perro area, and Juanchito strip

Bars and clubs near Parque del Perro, Menga, La Sexta, and the Juanchito strip pad tourist tabs with phantom drinks — unordered rounds, undisclosed foreigner-rate pricing, and bell-ring traditions applied without participation — with bills arriving at 2–3 times the expected amount being common.

Cali's nightlife operates on an informal tab system at many venues: a bartender tracks orders on a napkin or notepad and presents a total at closing. In venues catering to tourists, the tab system is exploited to inflate the count — drinks ordered for others that were never delivered, a bar bell tradition (ringing the bell buys everyone a round) charged without the tourist's participation, or a separate foreigner price per drink applied without disclosure. Friendly strangers who buy rounds and then 'add it to your tab' are also a documented pattern in the Granada and Parque del Perro bar zones.

The inflated bill is presented at closing time when the tourist is tired and often impaired. Staff produce a napkin tally the tourist cannot verify against their actual order history. Disputing the bill produces a bouncer rather than a manager. Most tourists pay — the amount ($50–$150 USD) isn't worth a confrontation in a foreign country — and the venue resets for the next tourist. The only reliable protection is pay-per-round: if each drink was paid for individually as it arrived, there's nothing to dispute at closing.

Pay for each round individually by card tap as drinks arrive and never open a running tab at a Cali nightclub or bar — ask the price per drink before ordering, keep a count on your phone's notes app, and choose only well-reviewed venues with electronic POS systems rather than napkin-tally operations.

Red Flags

  • The bar does not display prices or has no menu — you are expected to just order and find out the price on the bill
  • The bartender keeps a manual tally (napkin, notepad) instead of an electronic tab system
  • Drinks seem to arrive at your table that you did not order — 'sent by someone' or 'complimentary'
  • A friendly stranger insists on buying you drinks all night and adds them to YOUR tab
  • The bill at the end of the night is significantly higher than what you calculate based on the drinks you remember ordering

How to Avoid

  • Pay for each round as you go rather than running a tab — this makes it impossible to add phantom drinks.
  • Ask the price of drinks before ordering and keep a running count on your phone's notes app.
  • Do not ring any bells, accept unsolicited drinks, or agree to buy rounds for groups of people you just met.
  • Stick to established, well-reviewed bars and clubs rather than places suggested by strangers.
  • If the bill seems wrong, calmly ask for an itemized breakdown and dispute specific charges — most bars will adjust rather than lose the tourist.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Colombian National Police (Policía Nacional) station. Call 123 (Emergency) or 112. Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at policia.gov.co.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Bogotá is at Calle 24 Bis No. 48-50, Bogotá. For emergencies: +57 1-275-2000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
