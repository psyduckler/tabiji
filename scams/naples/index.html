The 8 Scams

Scam #1
The Scippatori Scooter Bag & Watch Snatch
⚠️ High
📍 Corso Arnaldo Lucci (near Napoli Centrale), Via Toledo, Via Partenope / Lungomare, Mergellina, Chiaia, Piazza dei Martiri, Corso Umberto

Two men on a scooter or motorcycle ride up alongside a pedestrian — often a tourist with a visible handbag or luxury watch — the passenger rips the bag or watch off in one motion and they vanish into the vicoli. This is the textbook Naples crime; the city gave the Italian language the word "scippo."

A Napolitoday cronaca report documents a tourist "aggredito da due ragazzi in scooter" on Corso Arnaldo Lucci — the arterial running southeast from Napoli Centrale — losing his Rolex, with the pair fleeing into the rione delle Case Nuove. The MO scales internationally: on November 15, 2024, Naples Polizia and Spanish police arrested five Naples-linked suspects (ages 18–52, including one woman who identified victims) for a Rolex-robbery ring operating in Málaga, Marbella and Puerto Banús — one documented theft was a €50,000 Rolex ripped from a British tourist's wrist by a passenger on a motorcycle (per internapoli.it). A July 2025 blitz in the Casertano province indicted 22 and arrested 9 more for thefts targeting "vip in vacanza" totaling approximately €374,000 in luxury watches (per edizionecaserta). Within Naples itself, a Napolitoday report titled "Scippa la borsa ad una turista: giovane inseguito e arrestato" documents a 52-year-old Catanese with prior property-crime obligations arrested for a tourist bag-snatch in the city. The vicoli (narrow alley network) of Forcella, Quartieri Spagnoli and Sanità are the classic escape routes — a moped disappears in them in under 30 seconds.

Red Flags

  • A scooter or motorcycle with two riders slows to your walking pace alongside you, especially if you're on Corso Umberto, Via Toledo, the Lungomare, or anywhere near Napoli Centrale
  • You're wearing a visible luxury watch (Rolex, Patek, Audemars), a gold chain, or a designer handbag on the street side of your body
  • Your handbag strap is on the street-side shoulder, not the inside (wall-side) shoulder
  • You just exited a taxi or a high-end hotel (Grand Hotel Vesuvio, Royal Continental, Romeo) and are standing on the curb checking your phone
  • A scooter has circled your block twice — the riders are scouting

How to Avoid

  • Leave the Rolex in the hotel safe; wear a cheap watch. This is the single highest-leverage change you can make in Naples
  • Carry bags crossbody on the inside (wall-side) shoulder with the strap over your head — not on the street-side shoulder
  • Stay at least one metre from the curb when walking arteries like Via Toledo or Corso Umberto; if you hear a scooter slowing behind you, stop, face it, and put your hand firmly on your bag
  • If a bag or watch is snatched, do not chase — the scooter will outrun you in ten seconds. Call 112 and file a denuncia at the Questura di Napoli, Via Medina 75
  • Travel-insurance claims and credit-card chargebacks for the watch require the denuncia — get it in the first 24 hours
Scam #2
Napoli Centrale & Piazza Garibaldi Pickpocket Rings
⚠️ High
📍 Napoli Centrale station concourse, Piazza Garibaldi, Corso Umberto, Corso Garibaldi, Metro Linea 1 and Circumvesuviana platforms, Piazza Principe Umberto

Piazza Garibaldi and the Napoli Centrale concourse are the densest pickpocket zone in southern Italy. Coordinated crews work the arrivals board, metro entrance, taxi queue and the 200-metre sidewalk exiting the station — targeting tourists with fresh luggage and jetlag.

A Napolitoday investigation, "Il centro città nelle mani dei borseggiatori: agiscono in…," quotes citizens: "Nel mirino soprattutto i turisti, bersagli facili di un sistema criminale che sfrutta il caos e la confusione dell'area." An October 2024 r/ItalyTravel firsthand post, "Almost Robbed at Napoli Centrale (Tuesday Night)," documents a 69-year-old American woman chasing down a late-20s thief who lifted a lilac backpack off her rolling suitcase while she sat under the departures board around 22:00 waiting for a night train to Palermo — her husband spotted the lift from the corner of his eye. A 2023 r/solotravel post with 1,300 upvotes documents a 32-year-old female tourist having her phone lifted from her pocket on the crowded sidewalk outside the station in broad daylight; she chased and screamed until the thief dropped it. A Napolitoday report, "Borseggiatori a bordo dei treni della Metro Linea 1," documents plainclothes agents catching a thief who had just pickpocketed a German tourist on the metro — the thief returned the loot and was arrested for furto aggravato. The r/napoli FAQ is blunt: "Is the train station area sketchy? Short answer? Yeah, a bit. But… The piazza Garibaldi area has high police and carabinieri presence."

Red Flags

  • Someone clusters near you as trains pull in or board — three or more people suddenly close on the same carriage door
  • A stranger offers unsolicited "help" at a ticket machine, FastTicket kiosk, or metro turnstile
  • You're sitting under the arrivals/departures board with luggage at your feet, phone out, or looking up at the board
  • A child or teenager brushes past or "trips" near you — this is a classic distraction front
  • Someone bumps into you aggressively on the station sidewalk — the r/ItalyTravel account describes this as the probable distraction front where onlookers get pickpocketed

How to Avoid

  • Treat the 500 metres around Piazza Garibaldi like Rome Termini. Wear a crossbody bag with zippers facing your body; use a zipper pin or mini-padlock
  • Keep a hand on your backpack or roller at all times; never set a backpack on top of a roller handle and look away
  • Exit to the taxi rank via the main station atrium — not the side exits onto Corso Novara or Piazza Principe Umberto, which are more isolated
  • Buy tickets only on the Trenitalia or Italo app or their logo-marked machines; refuse any "helper" who touches your card
  • If pickpocketed, yell "ladro!" loudly — the r/solotravel 1,300-score story confirms yelling recovers the item; thieves flee attention
Scam #3
The Tre Campanelle (Three-Bells) Shell Game
⚠️ High
📍 Piazza Garibaldi (especially the pedestrian side near Napoli Centrale), Corso Umberto, Piazza Dante, Via Toledo crowd chokepoints

A portable cardboard stand runs the classic tre campanelle shell game — three shiny bells and a hidden ball. Shills posing as tourists "win" to lure you in, the conductor palms the ball the whole time, and lookouts signal when police approach. Nobody who isn't a shill ever wins.

In September 2024 the Carabinieri Compagnia Stella arrested three men running the game on Piazza Garibaldi and seized the mobile bench after "numerose segnalazioni" from citizens and tourists (per Cronache della Campania and Il Mattino, which headlined its coverage "giro di vite contro truffatori e banchetti spenna-turisti"). Fanpage's coverage captured the durability of the con bluntly: "dopo 50 anni possibile che ci caschino ancora?" An Ottopagine Napoli report documents the scam "miete ancora vittime" on the same piazza. The gang composition is textbook: a conductor who manages the bells, three to five "winners" who pose as tourists and pretend to win, and one or two lookouts. The ball is palmed — it never actually sits under a bell. Tourists typically lose €100–€500 in a single session before realizing every "winning" bet was a shill and every "losing" bet was themselves. The Il Mattino coverage notes that beyond the headline arrests, repeated enforcement sweeps keep dispersing the stands; new crews return within days.

Red Flags

  • A cardboard or folding table on Piazza Garibaldi, Piazza Dante, or Via Toledo with three cups, three bells, or three matchboxes — this is never a legitimate street game
  • A tight crowd around the table with people "winning" easy €100 rounds — these are shills; real winners are statistically impossible
  • A friendly "tourist" in the crowd urges you to try, promising an easy win
  • The operator glances up the street every 10–15 seconds — he's watching lookouts for approaching police
  • The stand folds and disappears in under 30 seconds when someone raises a phone camera or police walk toward it

How to Avoid

  • Walk past without stopping — do not stop even "to watch" because lookouts mark interested viewers for pickpocket follow-up
  • Do not accept a "free try" — the first bet is a shill win designed to hook you; every bet after is the real game and you will lose every time
  • Physically move your wallet or phone to a front pocket before you cross Piazza Garibaldi — the crowd around the stand is a secondary pickpocket field
  • If you've already lost money, report at the Polizia Municipale office inside Napoli Centrale or call 112 — the September 2024 Carabinieri Stella operation was triggered specifically by tourist complaints
  • Never try to "win back" what you lost — the game's mechanics guarantee you cannot
Scam #4
The Circumvesuviana (Pompeii-Bound) Pickpocket Gang
⚠️ High
📍 Circumvesuviana line — Napoli Porta Nolana, Napoli Piazza Garibaldi, Ercolano Scavi, Pompei Scavi Villa dei Misteri, Sorrento

The Circumvesuviana commuter rail — the crowded, often un-air-conditioned line that carries tourists from Naples to Pompei, Ercolano and Sorrento — is worked by an identified pickpocket crew. Tourists with daypacks and Pompeii tickets are the primary targets.

A Napolitoday cronaca report, "Stanata la banda di borseggiatori della Circumvesuviana," documents the arrest of a crew caught mid-lift: "nel corso del viaggio, uno degli indagati avrebbe tentato il borseggio del portafogli di un turista, scena a cui aveva assistito un altro agente" — an undercover officer on the same train saw the attempt and made the arrest. r/ItalyTravel regulars describe the line as "one of Italy's worst commuter rail lines by punctuality" and note that Pompei-bound and Sorrento-bound runs are packed standing-room-only at peak tourist hours, which is exactly the density a pickpocket team needs to hand off wallets between accomplices. A related Napolitoday report documents pickpocket arrests on Metro Linea 1 targeting a German tourist. The r/ItalyTravel and r/travel threads on Circumvesuviana safety converge on the same advice: "Pretend you're on the subway in someplace like NY or Chicago and you'll be fine" and "If you take the normal precautions and send a message that you are aware, they don't even try anything. They move on." The Campania Express is an upgraded service (€15 extra, reserved seats, fewer stops) that materially reduces exposure by removing the standing crush.

Red Flags

  • You boarded at Napoli Porta Nolana or Piazza Garibaldi and are standing in a packed carriage with a daypack on your back
  • Two or three people cluster around you as the train approaches Ercolano or Pompei Scavi — the major tourist stops
  • Someone "trips" or drops something next to you — this is a distraction front while a second crew member works pockets
  • You have a wallet or phone in a back trouser pocket, an outer backpack pocket, or a jacket side pocket
  • You're heading inbound to Naples from Pompei in the afternoon — the peak-tourist-return window is the crew's richest hour

How to Avoid

  • Book the Campania Express for Pompei or Sorrento at peak hours — the €15 reserved seat removes the standing crush where theft happens
  • Wear a money belt under your shirt for passport, cards, and main cash; carry only €40–€60 in a front-pocket day wallet
  • Sit rather than stand whenever possible; if you must stand, position yourself with your back to a wall or the carriage end
  • Wear backpacks on the front (belly) when the carriage is packed — it looks silly and works
  • If you spot a lift, yell "ladro!" — the Napolitoday arrest came from exactly this kind of public reaction plus undercover backup
Scam #5
The Capodichino Taxi Overcharge
⚠️ High
📍 Capodichino airport taxi rank, Napoli Centrale rank, Piazza Municipio / Beverello port rank, hotel-door hails in Chiaia and Santa Lucia

Capodichino drivers refuse the posted tariffa predeterminata, switch the meter to a long-distance tariff, or simply quote double the fixed rate hoping tourists don't know the numbers. The Comune di Napoli's 2024–2025 tariffe predeterminate are €21 airport → Piazza Garibaldi, €24 → Molo Beverello / Piazza Municipio, €28.50 → Via Partenope / Mergellina — all inclusive.

The Comune di Napoli fixed-fare schedule is published by both the Comune and Aeroporti di Napoli (Gesac). Per SicurAuto and Il Mattino reporting on the 2024 update, the rates are all-inclusive of luggage, night, holiday, and airport supplements; the driver is legally required to provide a receipt with the route and the amount before departure. Reality on the rank is another matter. On r/napoli, a tourist reports a driver demanding €55 from Napoli Centrale to the ferry port at Molo Beverello — a 4-km ride that should be €10–€15 on the meter or a single fixed fare from the airport. A 2024 r/napoli thread documents even the FreeNow app being gamed: an estimated €15.38 ride was edited post-drop to €28.01; FreeNow issued a partial refund after an in-app complaint. Il Mattino's "tassista truffa il consigliere Paipais" report documents a driver who not only overcharged a Naples comune council member but handed him counterfeit change; the Il Mattino "sfida dei B&B ai tassisti senza regole" feature describes Naples B&Bs distributing a vademecum instructing guests to photograph the driver's licence number on the dashboard. Il Mattino's interview with Prefect Antonio De Iesu confirms: "I turisti truffati devono rivolgersi ai vigili urbani."

Red Flags

  • The driver refuses or dodges a direct request for the "tariffa predeterminata" by destination name
  • The driver quotes over €24 for Piazza Municipio/Beverello, over €21 for Piazza Garibaldi, or over €29 for Via Partenope from Capodichino — these exceed the 2024–2025 Comune fixed tariff
  • The meter is already running, or started above the base, before you close the door
  • The "credit card reader is broken" and you're steered to an ATM — a universal taxi-scam tell used in Naples too
  • The vehicle is not an official white taxi with a roof "TAXI" sign and licence number on the rear — it's an unlicensed NCC/abusivo

How to Avoid

  • Say it before closing the door: "Tariffa predeterminata per Piazza Garibaldi, ventuno euro" (or your destination and rate). The driver must accept and provide a receipt before departure
  • Use the Alibus shuttle from Capodichino — €5, ~20 minutes to Piazza Garibaldi and Molo Beverello, buy at the airport newsagent. It removes taxi negotiation entirely
  • For in-city rides, use FreeNow or NoxNo and compare the final charge to the app estimate; dispute via in-app support if the driver edits it — FreeNow refunded within minutes per r/napoli reports
  • Photograph the licence number on the rear of the vehicle (and on the dashboard) before getting in — Naples B&Bs distribute this advice as a pre-emptive vademecum
  • Refuse any ATM detour for "broken" card readers; pay cash for the posted fare only, keep the driver's number, and file a complaint with the Polizia Municipale on Via Toledo or via the Comune di Napoli portal
Scam #6
The Via Toledo "Salt & Corno" Gift Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Via Toledo (entire length), Spaccanapoli / Via dei Tribunali, Piazza del Plebiscito, Galleria Umberto I, Piazza Dante

Operators work Via Toledo and the Centro Storico carrying a paper bag of salt and a handful of small red cornicelli (lucky-horn charms). They chat you up, press a corno into your hand as a "gift," then demand €10–€50 and turn aggressive if refused.

A July 2025 r/napoli post with 121 upvotes titled "Turisti, fate attenzione!" — posted by a Naples local specifically to warn visitors — lays out the mechanism: "Via Toledo è invasa da truffatori. Li riconosci perché hanno in mano un pacco di sale e dei piccoli corni rossi (🌶️). Inizieranno a chiacchierare con voi e poi vi assilleranno per ricevere dei soldi. Tirate dritto e non fermatevi. Sono arroganti e aggressivi." Top commenters describe the same "salt + charm" combo and compare it to Times Square tourist hustlers. The r/napoli FAQ notes the pattern alongside the "Brad Pitt / Angelina Jolie" catcalling ritual that locals use to flag tourists for hustle attention. It is the Naples twin of Rome's "friendship bracelet" scam and operates on the same instinct reflex: the moment the trinket is in your hand, the aggressor treats it as yours and demands payment. The r/napoli post is blunt about the operators' demeanour — "arroganti e aggressivi" — which is why refusal escalations can become loud, public, and persistent until a bystander intervenes or the operator gives up.

Red Flags

  • Someone on Via Toledo or Spaccanapoli approaches you with something small and red in their hand — a corno, a ribbon, or what looks like a trinket
  • They carry a small paper bag or sachet — often salt — and start a friendly "chat" in English
  • They call you "Brad Pitt," "Angelina Jolie," "bella" or similar — the r/napoli FAQ flags this as a hustle opener ("they do that to everybody")
  • They try to press, drop, or tuck something into your hand, wrist, or jacket pocket
  • The approach happens in the first 10 metres after you step out of a hotel, restaurant, or funicolare exit

How to Avoid

  • Keep your hands in your pockets or closed at your sides when any stranger approaches with something in their hands — do not open your palm
  • If anything is pressed into your hand, drop it on the ground immediately and walk away; the aggressor will pick it up rather than lose inventory
  • Reply to any "un regalo!" or "per te, gratis!" opener with a firm "no, grazie" and keep moving — do not engage, do not explain
  • Do not stop for photographs or "blessings" from street operators; legitimate Naples vendors operate from licensed carts or shopfronts with posted prices
  • Report persistent aggressive operators to the Polizia Municipale office near Via Toledo or call 112
Scam #7
The Centro Storico Tourist-Menu & Coperto Trap
🔶 Medium
📍 Spaccanapoli, Via dei Tribunali, Via San Gregorio Armeno, Quartieri Spagnoli, Piazza Bellini, Via Toledo restaurant alcoves

Restaurants clustered on Spaccanapoli, Via dei Tribunali, Via San Gregorio Armeno and the lower Quartieri Spagnoli add high coperto (€2–€4 per head), automatic 10–15% servizio, unrequested pane/acqua/limoncello "gifts" billed on the check, and seafood priced "al etto" that arrives at 4–6 times the expected portion.

Even famous venues surface coperto complaints: a documented Gino Sorbillo receipt lists "Servizio 10% €1.33" plus a coperto, which is legal under Italian law if disclosed on the menu but often surprises travelers used to the Roman convention. The Il Mattino interview with Naples Prefect Antonio De Iesu tells overcharged tourists to file complaints with the vigili urbani, confirming the problem is common enough to be an official policy line. A 2024 r/napoli FAQ names the Quartieri Spagnoli as "a sketchy area now turned tourist nightlife hot spot" — meaning restaurant density has exploded there and quality is wildly uneven, with many new venues optimized for tourist turnover rather than locals. The Rick Steves travel forum on "Service charges added onto bills in Italy" documents the tourist pattern: coperto and servizio are legal when disclosed, but many Naples places bury them or add "gift" drinks that show up priced on the bill. The shakedowns are mechanical, not personal: pane and acqua arrive unrequested; antipasti you didn't order appear; the waiter offers a "small digestivo" that turns out to be €8 on the bill.

Red Flags

  • A "barker" stands outside the restaurant on Spaccanapoli or Via dei Tribunali calling to passing tourists — locals flag this as the clearest tell of a trap
  • The menu is in four or more languages with glossy photos but no version includes per-item pricing for fish or steak
  • Pane, acqua, antipasti, or limoncello arrive without you ordering them — all will appear on the check
  • The coperto line on the menu is in small print, buried at the bottom, and higher than €3 per head
  • The waiter mentions "pesce fresco al etto" without showing the piece before cooking

How to Avoid

  • Photograph the posted menu from outside before sitting — Italian law requires prices to be displayed; if no prices are visible, walk away
  • Ask at seating: "mi conferma il coperto e se c'è servizio?" Get the numbers verbally, not at the bill
  • For fish or steak priced "al etto," ask the waiter to weigh it and confirm the total before it goes to the kitchen
  • Walk 3–5 minutes off the main tourist arteries — Vomero, Sanità, Chiaia back-streets, and Porta Capuana typically have local prices and local clientele
  • Sort Google Maps reviews by Italian language, Most Recent — a venue rated 4.5+ by Italian reviewers and 3.5 by English is usually authentic; reversed, it's usually a trap
Scam #8
The Fantasma (Ghost) Apartment Rental Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Apartment listings in the Centro Storico, Chiaia, Vomero, Sanità, and near Napoli Centrale; also outlying Ischia, Sorrento Peninsula, and Cilento listings sold as "Naples base"

Fraudulent "hosts" post apartments on Airbnb, Booking, Facebook Marketplace and Subito.it, collect deposits off-platform, and vanish before check-in. The address is real but the flat isn't theirs, or the address doesn't exist at all.

Il Mattino's "Ischia case vacanze fantasma" report documents a 38-year-old from Marano arrested by carabinieri for running a web-based maxi-truffa of affittanze fasulle. A separate Il Mattino Salerno report documents a €500 ghost-rental sting in Battipaglia; an Il Mattino Naples report on a Roccaraso snow-holiday scam documents eight young people from Castellammare and Santa Maria la Carità losing €1,500 to a nonexistent casa in via Pedemontana. An August 2025 ANSA Campania report warns specifically of case vacanze scams in Cilento as tourist demand spikes: "La domanda turistica nel Cilento è in forte crescita e questo, purtroppo, attira anche malintenzionati pronti a sfruttare l'occasione." The Il Mattino Booking report documents an unusual cross-border pattern — scammers listed somebody else's real London house using stolen photos from a different city entirely, and real tourists showed up to a private home that had never been for rent. A 2024 r/ItalyTravel thread, "Airbnb situation in may 2024," documents ongoing confusion from travelers about which listings are legitimate after a rash of cancellations. Naples has a €5/night tourism tax on rooms >€120/night; a host who adds it "in cash on arrival" without a receipt is often using it as cover to pocket extra.

Red Flags

  • The host contacts you outside the booking platform (direct email, WhatsApp, personal mobile) and asks to move payment off-platform
  • You're asked to "wire a deposit" or "pay in cash on arrival" on top of the platform charge
  • The property has a name but no physical presence on Google Street View at the address given
  • The listing photos reverse-image-search to other Italian cities or other countries (the Il Mattino Booking London case)
  • The host pressures you to book fast "before someone else grabs it" and dodges requests for documentation

How to Avoid

  • Book only through Airbnb, Booking.com, Vrbo or a platform with a pay-through-platform guarantee — never wire or pay cash off-platform for a Naples rental
  • Check the listing address on Google Street View before arrival; confirm the building matches the photos
  • Keep all communication in the platform chat system — this preserves your refund rights if the listing turns out to be fake
  • Reverse-image-search the listing photos — if they appear on other sites in other cities, it's a stolen-photo scam
  • Budget for Naples's €5/night tourism tax (rooms over €120/night only); refuse cash "city fee" surprises without a receipt and legal justification

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Carabinieri / Polizia di Stato station — Questura di Napoli is at Via Medina 75. Call 112 (Carabinieri) or 113 (Polizia). Get an official denuncia — you'll need it for insurance claims, travel insurance and any card chargebacks. You can also report online at poliziadistato.it.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately and use in-app blocks. Naples bag-snatches often result in ATM cash-outs within 3–10 minutes of the theft — freeze cards in under two minutes.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Naples has its own US Consulate General: Piazza della Repubblica, 80122 Naples. Emergency phone: +39 081-583-8111. For non-US citizens, contact your country's nearest consulate — most have offices in Naples or Rome.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Do not confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
