The 12 Scams

Scam #1
Pickpocketing
⚠️ High
📍 Vieux Lyon, Place Bellecour, Part-Dieu station, Perrache station, Confluence Tram stop, Metro Line D, T2 tram line, Basilica of Notre-Dame de Fourvière

Pickpockets in Lyon operate in tourist-heavy areas and on crowded public transport.

They often work in teams using distraction techniques - one person might ask for directions, bump into you, or create a commotion while an accomplice steals your wallet or phone. At the Confluence Tram stop, groups of women wearing large bags have been observed surrounding tourists to access backpacks. On metro Line D and the T2 tram, pickpockets are most active during rush hour boarding and exiting when movement is chaotic.

Red Flags

  • Being surrounded by strangers asking questions
  • Someone bumping into you repeatedly
  • Groups of people getting unusually close
  • Chaotic boarding situations on public transport

How to Avoid

  • Wear bags across your body with zippers facing inward.
  • Keep only minimal cash and one card on you.
  • Use money belts or hidden pouches.
  • Stay alert during metro boarding and exiting.
Scam #2
Fake Metro Ticket Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Part-Dieu station, Perrache station

Ticket touts approach tourists who appear lost or confused at major Lyon train stations.

They offer to sell metro tickets at seemingly reasonable prices, but the tickets are either fake, expired, or already used. The scammer may claim they have 'extra' tickets or offer a 'discount' compared to the official ticket machines. When you try to use the ticket, it won't work at the turnstile.

Red Flags

  • Strangers approaching you near ticket machines
  • Offers of discounted tickets
  • Sellers not in official TCL uniforms

How to Avoid

  • Only buy tickets from official TCL machines or counters.
  • Use the TCL mobile app for digital tickets.
  • Ignore anyone offering to sell tickets outside official channels.
Scam #3
Fake Charity/Petition Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Rue de la République, Part-Dieu station, Perrache station, Place Bellecour

Someone approaches with a clipboard asking 'Do you speak English?' and requests you sign a petition for a charity supporting deaf-mute individuals. After signing, they flip the clipboard to reveal a donation section and pressure you to give money. While you're distracted with the clipboard, an accomplice may pickpocket you from behind.

Red Flags

  • Petition only in English in a French-speaking city
  • Immediate request for cash donation after signing
  • Others hovering nearby while you interact

How to Avoid

  • Simply say 'Non, merci' and keep walking.
  • Never sign anything handed to you on the street.
  • Legitimate charities in France only ask for email addresses, not cash.

Scam #4
Restaurant Overcharging
🔶 Medium
📍 Vieux Lyon (Old Town), Presqu'île, Rue Mercière

Tourist-centric restaurants near Lyon's historic areas may present inflated bills.

Tactics include: adding items you didn't order, charging for bread and water that appeared 'free,' using 'tourist menus' with elevated prices. Some establishments trade on Lyon's reputation as France's gastronomic capital to justify excessive prices for mediocre food.

Red Flags

  • No prices listed on menus outside
  • Menus only in French with no translations
  • Aggressive touts outside trying to pull you in

How to Avoid

  • Check prices before sitting down.
  • Ask for itemized bills and review carefully.
  • Eat where locals eat, away from main tourist streets.
Scam #5
Shell Game / Three-Card Monte
⚠️ High
📍 Busy tourist areas, Near major attractions, Metro exits

A scammer sets up a makeshift table with three cups or cards.

They invite you to bet on guessing the ball's location. 'Winners' in the crowd are accomplices who pretend to win to draw you in. The game is completely rigged through sleight of hand - you cannot win.

Red Flags

  • Crowd of 'players' who seem unusually lucky
  • High-pressure atmosphere encouraging large bets
  • Game operators who can pack up in seconds

How to Avoid

  • Never play street gambling games - they are always rigged.
  • Do not stop to watch.
  • Remember that bystanders are likely accomplices.
Scam #6
Gold Ring Scam
🟢 Low
📍 Tourist walkways, Near bridges, Pedestrian areas

A person 'finds' a gold ring on the ground near you and asks if you dropped it.

When you say no, they show you a marking claiming it's real gold and offer to sell it for a 'good price.' The ring is worthless brass. They may also ask for money 'for food' or 'for their children.'

Red Flags

  • Someone 'finding' something valuable right next to you
  • Claims of precious metal markings
  • Emotional appeals about needing money for family

How to Avoid

  • Don't engage - say 'Non' and keep walking.
  • Never accept items 'found' on the street.
Scam #7
Friendship Bracelet Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Tourist attractions, Near churches, Pedestrian areas

A vendor approaches and grabs your hand, quickly weaving a colorful friendship bracelet around your wrist.

Once tied on with a difficult knot, they demand payment of 10-20 euros. If you refuse, they become aggressive or an accomplice uses the distraction to pickpocket you.

Red Flags

  • Anyone reaching for your hand unexpectedly
  • Offers of 'free' gifts or demonstrations
  • String or craft materials visible

How to Avoid

  • Keep your hands in your pockets when approached.
  • Say 'Non' firmly and keep walking.
  • Don't let anyone touch you or attach anything.
Scam #8
Rose/Flower Scam
🟢 Low
📍 Restaurant terraces, Romantic spots, Evening entertainment areas

A seller approaches couples dining outdoors, offering a rose with 'Flower for the pretty lady!' The moment you touch or accept the flower, they demand 5-10 euros. If you try to return it, they refuse and become aggressive until you pay out of embarrassment.

Red Flags

  • Anyone approaching your table with flowers
  • Flowers being pushed into your hands

How to Avoid

  • Don't touch or accept any flowers offered.
  • A simple 'Non, merci' and wave sends them away.
  • Ask restaurant staff to intervene if needed.
Scam #9
Fake Police Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Metro stations, Tourist areas, Near bars at night

Criminals pose as police officers, quickly flashing fake badges.

They claim they need to check your wallet for counterfeit bills or 'drug money.' While examining your wallet, they steal cash. Sometimes a 'tourist' accomplice first asks you to break a large bill, then the fake officer arrives.

Red Flags

  • Anyone stopping you to exchange money just before 'police' arrive
  • Officers in plain clothes rather than uniform
  • Quick badge flashes without allowing inspection

How to Avoid

  • Know that real police never ask to see your wallet - only ID documents.
  • Suggest going to the nearest police station to verify their identity.
  • Call 17 if unsure.
Scam #10
Taxi Overcharging
🔶 Medium
📍 Lyon-Saint Exupéry Airport, Part-Dieu station, Perrache station, Outside nightclubs

Unscrupulous taxi drivers target tourists unfamiliar with Lyon's layout.

They may take long routes to inflate the fare, 'forget' to start the meter, or select the higher night/weekend rate during regular hours. Airport runs are particularly vulnerable - the fare from Lyon-Saint Exupéry to the city center should be around €50-70.

Red Flags

  • Driver not starting the meter
  • Meter showing night rate during daytime
  • Driver taking unfamiliar routes

How to Avoid

  • Only use taxis from official ranks.
  • Ensure the meter is zeroed before departure.
  • Use GPS on your phone to follow the route.
  • Consider using Uber or Bolt.
Scam #11
ATM Skimming / Private ATM Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Private ATMs in tourist areas, ATMs on busy streets, Near train stations

Criminals attach skimming devices to ATMs that steal your card data and PIN.

Private ATM companies like Euronet place machines that charge excessive fees and poor exchange rates - up to 4x more than bank ATMs. They may offer Dynamic Currency Conversion at terrible rates.

Red Flags

  • ATMs that look modified or have loose parts
  • ATMs branded with non-bank names (Euronet, Travelex)
  • Prompts offering to convert to your home currency

How to Avoid

  • Use ATMs inside bank branches only.
  • Look for major French banks: BNP Paribas, Société Générale, LCL.
  • Always cover keypad when entering PIN.
  • Decline dynamic currency conversion.
Scam #12
Broken Camera Scam
🟢 Low
📍 Tourist attractions, Photo-worthy spots

Someone asks you to take their photo using their camera.

After you return the camera, they claim you broke it and demand money for repairs. The 'camera' was already broken - they use it repeatedly for this scam.

Red Flags

  • Insistence on using their camera rather than phone
  • Old or unusual looking camera equipment
  • Aggressive reaction that seems disproportionate

How to Avoid

  • Politely decline to take photos with others' cameras.
  • If accused of damage, suggest filing a police report together - scammers will refuse.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Police Nationale / SAMU station. Call 17 (Police) or 15 (SAMU medical). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at pre-plainte-en-ligne.interieur.gouv.fr.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Paris is at 2 Avenue Gabriel, 75008 Paris. For emergencies: +33 1 43-12-22-22.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
