The 6 Scams

Scam #1
Hartsfield-Jackson (ATL) Airport Rideshare & Taxi Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL) arrivals, North/South Terminal pickup zones, MARTA station

ATL is the world's busiest airport by passenger volume (2019-2023).

The legitimate Uber/Lyft ride to downtown Atlanta runs $20–$35 depending on surge; licensed taxi is approximately $30–$45 to downtown with the meter. Scam variants: (1) drivers soliciting at baggage claim offering 'flat $60' to downtown — unlicensed; (2) 'limo' or 'black car' touts quoting $80–$120 for standard-distance trips; (3) Uber/Lyft drivers who accept the ride then insist on additional 'tip' or 'airport surcharge' in cash at drop-off; (4) fake MARTA 'ticket agents' in the airport selling overpriced transit passes.

The MARTA train runs from ATL to downtown (Five Points station) for $2.50 — scam-proof, 20 minutes, runs every 10–15 minutes.

For older travelers arriving at ATL, the protective playbook: (1) take MARTA Red or Gold line from the ATL station to downtown: $2.50, 20 min, scam-proof; (2) use Uber or Lyft from the designated rideshare pickup zones (North Terminal: North Economy Parking; South Terminal: South Economy Parking) — screenshot fare estimate; (3) if licensed taxi, ensure meter is running and expect $30–$45 to downtown; (4) never engage drivers soliciting at baggage claim or outside terminal doors; (5) ignore 'MARTA ticket agents' — buy Breeze Card at the official MARTA station kiosk for $2.50.

Red Flags

  • Driver solicits at baggage claim offering 'flat $60' to downtown
  • 'Limo' or 'black car' tout quotes $80–$120 for standard trip
  • Uber/Lyft driver demands cash tip or 'airport surcharge' beyond app total
  • Fake 'MARTA ticket agent' sells overpriced transit passes
  • No meter running on licensed taxi

How to Avoid

  • MARTA Red/Gold Line from ATL to Five Points downtown: $2.50, 20 min.
  • Uber/Lyft at designated rideshare pickup (North or South Terminal Economy Parking).
  • Licensed taxi with meter running: $30–$45 to downtown.
  • Never engage drivers soliciting at baggage claim.
  • Buy Breeze Card at official MARTA kiosk for $2.50.
Scam #2
Georgia Aquarium & Coca-Cola Museum Reseller Scams
🔶 Medium
📍 Online — third-party reseller sites for Georgia Aquarium, World of Coca-Cola, CNN Center tickets; touts at Pemberton Place

Atlanta's three main tourist attractions are clustered at Pemberton Place: Georgia Aquarium, World of Coca-Cola, and CNN Center. Legitimate prices: Georgia Aquarium adult $49.95 via georgiaaquarium.org; World of Coca-Cola adult $22 via worldofcoca-cola.com; CNN Center tours $18 via cnn.com (tour discontinued in 2023 — current offerings vary). Scam variants: (1) third-party reseller websites sell Georgia Aquarium at $75–$120 per person — 50–150% markup; (2) 'CityPASS bundles' at $95 per person — legitimate CityPASS exists at citypass.com ($85-$95 depending on dates) but fake resellers sell similar-looking bundles at inflated rates; (3) touts at Centennial Olympic Park offer 'combo tickets' to Aquarium+Coca-Cola at $120+ vs $72 direct.

For older travelers visiting Atlanta, the practical playbook: (1) Georgia Aquarium tickets direct at georgiaaquarium.org — adult $49.95; (2) World of Coca-Cola direct at worldofcoca-cola.com — adult $22; (3) genuine Atlanta CityPASS at citypass.com — $85-$95 for 5 attractions including Georgia Aquarium + World of Coca-Cola + Zoo Atlanta + Fernbank Museum + College Football Hall of Fame, only buy from citypass.com directly; (4) reject touts at Centennial Olympic Park offering 'combo tickets'; (5) avoid Google ads for 'Atlanta Aquarium tickets' — they lead to resellers.

Red Flags

  • Third-party reseller charges $75+ for Georgia Aquarium (direct $49.95)
  • World of Coca-Cola tickets at $40+ (direct $22)
  • 'Atlanta CityPASS' at $120+ (legitimate $85-$95 at citypass.com)
  • Tout at Centennial Olympic Park offers combo tickets at inflated prices
  • Google ad leads to unfamiliar reseller site

How to Avoid

  • Georgia Aquarium direct: georgiaaquarium.org, adult $49.95.
  • World of Coca-Cola direct: worldofcoca-cola.com, adult $22.
  • Atlanta CityPASS direct: citypass.com, $85-$95 for 5 attractions.
  • Reject touts at Centennial Olympic Park.
  • Avoid Google ads for 'Atlanta tickets' — scroll past sponsored results.
Scam #3
'US Customs and Border Protection' Phone Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Phone calls to travelers visiting Atlanta or transiting through ATL airport, claiming to be from US Customs/ATL area code (404)

A 2025 Atlanta-area phone scam specifically targets international travelers and visitors to Atlanta.

One traveler wrote: 'scammers spoof a 404-area-code phone number, claim to be from US Customs and Border Protection, and tell the victim a 'package containing illegal substances has been seized at Hartsfield-Jackson' addressed to them — they must pay a 'fine' of $1,000–$5,000 via Zelle, gift cards, or wire transfer to 'avoid arrest.'

The scam pattern includes: (1) caller ID spoofed to show '(404) xxx-xxxx' Atlanta area code; (2) caller claims to be CBP or Homeland Security officer; (3) demands immediate payment to 'clear the warrant'; (4) threatens arrest at the airport if victim doesn't comply; (5) sometimes transfers the call to another 'supervisor' for added legitimacy.

For older travelers (who are disproportionately targeted by this scam), the absolute rules: (1) US Customs and Border Protection Don't calls travelers demanding payment by phone — all communications are in writing via official USPS mail; (2) CBP never accepts Zelle, Venmo, wire transfer, gift cards, or cryptocurrency for any legitimate payment; (3) hang up immediately on any such call — do not engage, do not confirm your name, do not say 'yes' to anything; (4) report the scam to the Federal Trade Commission at reportfraud.ftc.gov or call the FTC at 1-877-382-4357; (5) if you're genuinely concerned, call CBP directly at 1-877-CBP-5511 (CBP's real number, easily verified).

Red Flags

  • Caller claims to be US Customs, CBP, or Homeland Security
  • Caller ID shows a 404 Atlanta area code
  • Claim of 'seized package' or 'warrant' at ATL airport
  • Demand for immediate payment by Zelle, Venmo, wire, gift cards, or crypto
  • Threat of arrest if you don't pay immediately

How to Avoid

  • Hang up immediately — CBP never calls demanding payment by phone.
  • Never confirm your name or say 'yes' to a suspicious caller.
  • Report to Federal Trade Commission: reportfraud.ftc.gov.
  • Verify by calling CBP directly: 1-877-CBP-5511 (official number).
  • CBP communicates legitimate matters via USPS mail only, never Zelle/gift cards.

Scam #4
MLK Center & Sweet Auburn Tour Touts
🟢 Low
📍 Martin Luther King Jr. National Historical Park, Sweet Auburn Historic District, Ebenezer Baptist Church approach, King Center tours

The Martin Luther King Jr.

National Historical Park (MLK Center, birthplace home, Ebenezer Baptist Church, King Center) is free to visit. The birthplace home tour requires a free reservation via the National Park Service (nps.gov/malu). Scam variants: (1) street touts outside the Ebenezer Baptist Church offer '$30 private tour' of the MLK sites — everything is free and self-guided; (2) 'MLK Walking Tour' packages at $80+ per person marketed by third-party operators when the free ranger-led tour is comprehensive; (3) 'Sweet Auburn historic walking tour' bundles at $100+ — the Sweet Auburn district is a free pedestrian heritage zone.

For older travelers, the MLK Historical Park is a genuinely moving free experience: (1) reserve birthplace home tour at recreation.gov (free, limited daily spots); (2) MLK Center (the tomb and reflection pool) is always free and accessible; (3) Ebenezer Baptist Church is free (historical site); (4) the King Center across the street is also free; (5) skip all paid 'walking tours' — the NPS ranger-led tours are higher quality and free.

Red Flags

  • Street tout outside Ebenezer Baptist Church offers '$30 private tour'
  • 'MLK Walking Tour' package at $80+ per person
  • 'Sweet Auburn historic tour' bundle at $100+
  • Claim that MLK birthplace home is 'paid admission'
  • Tout selling 'skip-the-line' tickets (there is no line system)

How to Avoid

  • Reserve MLK birthplace home tour at recreation.gov (free).
  • MLK Center, Ebenezer Baptist, King Center — all free, walk-in.
  • NPS ranger-led tour is free and higher quality than paid tours.
  • Sweet Auburn historic district is a free self-guided walk.
  • Skip all paid 'MLK walking tours.'
Scam #5
Ponce City Market & Beltline Tourist-Menu Overcharge
🟢 Low
📍 Ponce City Market food hall, Atlanta BeltLine (Eastside Trail) restaurant strips, Buckhead tourist-facing venues

Ponce City Market (the former Sears, Roebuck building converted to food hall) is Atlanta's signature food destination.

It's a legitimate venue with quality vendors, but some stalls are tourist-priced (e.g. $20+ sandwiches, $18+ hot dogs). The adjacent Atlanta BeltLine's Eastside Trail is lined with restaurants and bars charging 40–60% above residential Atlanta pricing. Buckhead tourist-facing venues (near the Mall at Lenox) similarly inflate for expense-account and tourist traffic.

For older travelers, the practical guide: (1) Ponce City Market is worth visiting — but eat at mid-tier vendors with posted prices ($12–$18 per meal): Holeman and Finch, El Super Pan, King of Pops; (2) for authentic Atlanta food at residential pricing, go to Virginia-Highland (The Nook, La Fonda), Grant Park (Grant Central Pizza), or Cabbagetown (Agave); (3) BeltLine Eastside Trail restaurants are drink-tourism venues — $14 cocktails and $18 small plates common; (4) Buckhead tourist venues are high-end expense-account territory, not tourist-friendly value; (5) for Southern soul food, Mary Mac's Tea Room ($18–$28 per person — institutional), Paschal's (Westside, $22–$35), or Busy Bee Cafe (Westside, $15–$22).

Red Flags

  • Ponce City Market vendor at $20+ per sandwich with laminated photo menu
  • BeltLine Eastside restaurant at $18+ small plates with 'American' vibes
  • Buckhead tourist restaurant near Mall at Lenox at $30+ entrees for basic cuisine
  • Mandatory 20% service charge on small parties at tourist venues
  • Claim of 'BeltLine exclusive' pricing

How to Avoid

  • Ponce City Market mid-tier: Holeman and Finch, El Super Pan, King of Pops ($12–$18).
  • Virginia-Highland or Grant Park for residential-priced Atlanta food.
  • Mary Mac's Tea Room ($18–$28), Paschal's ($22–$35), Busy Bee Cafe ($15–$22) for soul food.
  • Avoid BeltLine Eastside Trail drink tourism (overpriced).
  • Check Yelp/Google for Atlanta restaurants with 4.5+ stars at residential prices.
Scam #6
Atlanta Airbnb & Convention-Season Short-Term Rental Fraud
⚠️ High
📍 Online — Airbnb listings for Midtown, Downtown, Buckhead during Atlanta convention season (January, March, May, October); Facebook Marketplace short-term rentals

Atlanta hosts major conventions and events year-round (Dragon Con, Music Midtown, Atlanta Falcons/Hawks games, SEC championships) that create tight lodging markets and fertile ground for short-term rental fraud. The scam: a listing on Airbnb, Facebook Marketplace, or Craigslist advertises a Midtown or Downtown apartment at 30–50% below the convention-week hotel price; the 'host' demands deposit via Zelle, Venmo, or wire transfer; listing is either fraudulent or double-booked with multiple victims for the same dates.

The high-risk weeks when Atlanta hotels are scarcest: Dragon Con (late August/early September), Music Midtown (September), Falcons/Hawks home games in fall/winter, SEC Football Championship (December), National Black Arts Festival, and Atlanta Dogwood Festival.

For older travelers considering Atlanta accommodation, especially during convention weeks: (1) book only through Airbnb, VRBO, or Booking.com with platform-verified payment and cancellation protection; (2) refuse Zelle, Venmo, or bank transfer payment requests from any 'host'; (3) verify listings with 20+ reviews from last 12 months; (4) licensed hotels with posted prices: Four Seasons Atlanta, Ritz-Carlton Atlanta, Loews Atlanta, W Atlanta Midtown, Kimpton Sylvan Hotel — all book via Booking.com or the hotel's own site; (5) during convention season, book 3+ months ahead to avoid the scam-vulnerable last-minute market.

Red Flags

  • Airbnb listing at 30–50% below comparable hotel rates during convention week
  • 'Host' asks for Zelle, Venmo, or bank transfer payment
  • Off-platform ID-scan or credit-card-photo request
  • New listing with fewer than 10 reviews for high-demand dates
  • Pressure to 'secure' booking immediately because 'other bookings coming in'

How to Avoid

  • Book only through Airbnb, VRBO, or Booking.com with platform-verified payment.
  • Refuse Zelle/Venmo/bank transfer from 'hosts.'
  • Verify listings with 20+ reviews from last 12 months.
  • Licensed hotels: Four Seasons, Ritz-Carlton, Loews, W, Kimpton Sylvan.
  • Book 3+ months ahead for convention weeks to avoid scam-vulnerable last-minute market.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Local Police Department station. Call 911. Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at usa.gov/crimes.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Visit the nearest US Passport Agency. For international visitors, contact your country's consulate or embassy directly. US State Department emergency line: +1-888-407-4747 (from US) or +1-202-501-4444 (international).

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
