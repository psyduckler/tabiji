The 6 Scams

Scam #1
Disneyland 'Parrot Scammer' & Downtown Disney Photo Hustle
🟢 Low
📍 Downtown Disney esplanade, Disneyland park entrance plaza, Disney California Adventure plaza, Disneyland Hotel walkway

Men with parrots, iguanas, or snakes work the Downtown Disney entrance area, placing animals on tourists' shoulders for "photo ops" then demanding $20–$40 per person — they have no Disney affiliation and operate on the public sidewalk outside the park gate.

You are walking toward the Downtown Disney entrance with your family when a man approaches carrying a brightly colored parrot. He places it on your child's shoulder — or on yours — and says "how about a photo?" The interaction feels spontaneous and fun.

When the photo is taken, the man demands $20–$40 per person as a "photo fee." Variants include a street artist offering a "free sketch" then demanding $40, and a person in a character costume hugging children and then demanding a tip. None of these operators are Disney cast members; they work on the public sidewalk outside the gate, outside Disney's jurisdiction.

Decline any unsolicited photo with animals, costumes, or street artists in the Downtown Disney entrance zone — Disney character meet-and-greets are inside the parks and free with admission. If an animal is placed on your shoulder without asking, say "no thank you" and walk away without paying. Report aggressive hustling to Anaheim PD non-emergency at (714) 765-1900.

Red Flags

  • Man with parrot/iguana/snake on Downtown Disney sidewalk outside park gate
  • Animal placed on your shoulder or child's shoulder unsolicited
  • 'Artist' offering 'free sketch' on public sidewalk
  • Character-costume person hugging children then demanding tips
  • 'Fortune teller' booth on Downtown Disney perimeter sidewalk

How to Avoid

  • Refuse all unsolicited 'photo op' solicitations outside park gates.
  • Disney's actual character meets are INSIDE parks (free with admission).
  • Book Disney PhotoPass ($79 Memory Maker) for unlimited pro photos.
  • Walk away firmly if animal placed on shoulder — do NOT pay.
  • Report aggressive hustling: Anaheim PD 714-765-1900.
Scam #2
Disneyland Ticket OfferUp / Craigslist Resale Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Online marketplaces (OfferUp, Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace, eBay) targeting Disneyland buyers, Anaheim hotel-lobby 'ticket broker' desks

Third-party sellers on OfferUp, Craigslist, and Anaheim hotel-lobby "ticket broker" desks sell Disneyland tickets at $50–$80 below Disney's direct price — the QR codes are already-scanned one-time tickets or revoked Magic Keys that fail at the gate.

You find a Disneyland ticket seller on OfferUp or Facebook Marketplace offering tickets for $80–$100 below the Disney direct price of $154–$206 per adult day. The seller sends a professional-looking PDF with a QR code.

At the Disneyland gate, the ticket scans as "already used" or "revoked." The QR code was from a one-time-use ticket already scanned, or from a Magic Key account Disney revoked for unauthorized transfer. The Zelle or Venmo payment is non-refundable. Anaheim hotel-lobby "ticket broker" desks sell identical fraudulent tickets with printed receipts that look official.

Buy Disneyland tickets exclusively at disneyland.com, the on-site ticket booth, or authorized resellers Get Away Today and Undercover Tourist — never from OfferUp, Craigslist, or hotel-lobby brokers. If any deal is $50 or more below the disneyland.com price, it is a scam. Pay by credit card (not Zelle or Venmo) to preserve chargeback rights.

Red Flags

  • Disney tickets at $50–$80 below disneyland.com direct price
  • Seller sends PDF ticket via OfferUp, Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace
  • Payment demanded via Zelle, Venmo, Cash App, or wire transfer
  • Anaheim hotel-lobby 'ticket broker' desk offering 'package deals'
  • 'Magic Key + hotel + $499 total' package offer

How to Avoid

  • Buy ONLY at disneyland.com, Disneyland ticket booth, Get Away Today, or Undercover Tourist.
  • Don't buy from OfferUp, Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace, eBay.
  • Avoid Anaheim hotel-lobby 'ticket broker' desks.
  • If $50+ below disneyland.com, it IS a scam — no authorized discounts go that low.
  • Pay by credit card for chargeback leverage.
Scam #3
Anaheim Disney-Area Parking & Off-Disney Shuttle Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Off-Disney parking lots on Harbor Blvd, Katella Ave, Disneyland Dr perimeter, 'Disney Good Neighbor Hotel' shuttle touts

Private lots on Harbor Boulevard advertise "discount Disney parking" at $15–$20/day with "free shuttles" that run every 90 minutes — while fake parking attendants on residential side streets sell non-existent street parking permits for cash.

You drive to Anaheim for Disneyland and see signs for "discount Disney parking — $15, free shuttle" on private lots along Harbor Boulevard. Disney's official Mickey & Friends Parking Structure charges $35/day, and the private lot price looks like a real saving.

The private lot's shuttle runs every 90 minutes and the lot is over a mile from the parks — the wait and walk erase the saving. A separate scam operates on residential side streets: men posing as parking attendants sell "street parking permits" for $10–$20; the permits are fake and cars are towed. Windshield tickets claiming to be from "Anaheim Public Works" with non-government payment URLs are also reported.

Use Disney-operated parking ($35/day at Mickey & Friends or Toy Story Area) or book a Disney Good Neighbor Hotel within walking distance and use the hotel's included shuttle. If you receive a parking citation, verify it at anaheim.net before paying — pay only at anaheim.net or in person at Municipal Court.

Red Flags

  • Off-Disney private lot at $15–$20/day with '90-min shuttle'
  • 'Parking attendant' on residential Harbor Blvd selling 'street permits'
  • 'Disney Good Neighbor Hotel shuttle' tout at $15–$25 one-way
  • Fake 'Anaheim Public Works' citation URL on windshield
  • 'Downtown Disney parking' offer with no Disney validation

How to Avoid

  • Disney-operated lots: Mickey & Friends or Toy Story at $35/day.
  • Disney Good Neighbor Hotels (Camelot, Tropicana, Fairfield) often include free shuttle.
  • Refuse private '$15 parking' lots advertising 'free shuttle.'
  • Pay parking tickets ONLY at anaheim.net or Municipal Court.
  • Downtown Disney Monorail Lot: $10 with 3-hour purchase validation.

Scam #4
Disneyland Magic Key & 'Lightning Lane' Fast-Pass Upsell Confusion
🟢 Low
📍 Disneyland ticket upgrade kiosks, Disney Genie+ / Lightning Lane in-park pricing displays, Disney-area hotel concierges selling 'VIP' passes

Hotel concierges and walk-up sellers near Disneyland offer "Lightning Lane Unlimited" passes and "VIP tour discounts" that do not exist — Disney discontinued free FastPass in 2021 and all add-ons must be purchased only through the Disney app or disneyland.com.

You check into your Anaheim hotel and the concierge offers a "Lightning Lane Unlimited package" at $80 per person or a "VIP tour" at $200 per person. The add-ons sound like they will solve the long queues you have read about.

Disney does not sell "Lightning Lane Unlimited." Lightning Lane Multi Pass is $30–$39 per person per day, purchased only through the Disney app or disneyland.com. The "VIP Tour" concierge special at $200 is fabricated; the legitimate Disney VIP Tour costs $450+ per hour with an 8-hour minimum, booked only at disneyland.com. Magic Keys purchased on the secondary market are routinely revoked when the transfer is detected.

Buy all Disneyland add-ons — Lightning Lane, special experiences, annual passes — exclusively at disneyland.com or in the Disney app. For a 1–2 day visit on a budget, use Disney's free Virtual Queue for high-demand rides and plan ride order strategically: Peter Pan and Fantasyland first, then Tomorrowland. No hotel concierge has cheaper or exclusive access to Disney products.

Red Flags

  • Hotel-concierge 'Disneyland VIP Tour' at $200+ (legit is $450+/hr)
  • 'Lightning Lane Unlimited' pass (Disney does not offer this)
  • 'Skip-the-line package' at $150+ (actually just Lightning Lane)
  • Magic Key purchased on secondary market (risk of revocation)
  • Pressure to buy 'today only' Disney upgrade at Anaheim hotel

How to Avoid

  • Skip Lightning Lane for 1-2 day visit — use free Virtual Queue + strategic ride order.
  • Buy Lightning Lane ($30–$39/day) ONLY at disneyland.com or Disney app.
  • Legitimate Disney VIP Tour: $450+/hr via disneyland.com only.
  • Avoid secondary-market Magic Keys — revocation risk.
  • Veterans: verify discount programs at disneyland.com.
Scam #5
Anaheim Disney-Area Hotel Resort Fee & STR Off-Platform Booking Fraud
🔶 Medium
📍 Disneyland-perimeter hotels (Harbor Blvd, Katella Ave), 'Disney Good Neighbor' properties, Anaheim short-term rentals

Anaheim hotels advertising "from $159" add resort fees of $20–$45 per night and parking charges of $25–$45 at check-in — while fake "Disney-area Airbnb" listings on Facebook request Zelle payment and vanish before arrival.

You book an Anaheim hotel online at $159 per night — advertised as a "Disney Good Neighbor Hotel" with shuttle included. Or you respond to a Facebook listing offering a vacation rental near Disneyland at $100 below comparable rates, with payment by Zelle.

At check-in, the hotel presents a "resort fee" of $20–$45 per night for "WiFi, pool, and gym access," plus a $25–$45 nightly parking charge not visible during booking. The shuttle, advertised as "included," costs $5–$12 per person each way upon boarding. The Facebook Airbnb listing takes your Zelle payment and disappears before your arrival date.

Use resortfeechecker.com to calculate the all-in nightly cost before booking, adding resort fee and parking to the headline rate. Book short-term rentals only through Airbnb, VRBO, or Booking.com platform payment — never by Zelle or bank transfer. If a resort fee was not disclosed during booking, dispute it at check-in and file a chargeback with your credit card company.

Red Flags

  • 'Resort fee' $20–$45/night not disclosed in online booking
  • 'Free parking' hotel charging $25–$45/night parking at check-in
  • 'Disney shuttle fee' $5–$12 per person each way (if advertised 'included')
  • STR with 'mandatory resort fee' (not a real Airbnb concept)
  • 'Walk to Disneyland' listing that's 1.5+ miles away

How to Avoid

  • Add resort + parking + shuttle fees to headline hotel rate.
  • Check resortfeechecker.com for all-in costs.
  • Book STRs ONLY via Airbnb/VRBO/Booking.com platform payment.
  • Free-shuttle hotels: Tropicana, Camelot, Fairfield, Candy Cane, Howard Johnson.
  • Dispute undisclosed resort fees at check-in + credit card.
Scam #6
Anaheim Timeshare Presentation Near Disneyland
🔶 Medium
📍 Anaheim-area timeshare sales offices, Westgate Park City Resort promoter booths, Disney-area hotel-lobby 'concierge' desks

Hotel-lobby and mall-kiosk promoters near Disneyland offer "free Disney tickets" in exchange for a "90-minute presentation" that runs 3–5 hours and ends in a $15,000–$50,000 "vacation ownership" pitch — the complimentary ticket is typically a single-park, restricted-date pass worth far less than a full-day admission.

You are in the lobby of your Anaheim hotel when a representative approaches: "Free Disneyland ticket for you and your grandchildren if you attend a 90-minute resort presentation — just your time, no obligation." The offer sounds like a way to cut the trip's cost.

The "90-minute presentation" runs three to five hours with high-pressure sales tactics for vacation ownership packages at $15,000–$50,000, plus opaque annual maintenance fees. The "free Disney ticket" issued at the end is often a restricted single-park pass worth far less than a full-day ticket at $154–$206 per person. A second scam preys on existing timeshare owners: "exit companies" charge $5,000–$15,000 upfront to cancel existing contracts and then deliver nothing.

Never accept "free ticket" offers that require attending a sales presentation — three to five hours under high pressure is not worth any complimentary admission. California offers a seven-day right of rescission for timeshare contracts — cancel within seven days by certified mail. For rescission assistance, contact the California Attorney General at oag.ca.gov, not exit companies that charge thousands upfront.

Red Flags

  • 'Free Disney tickets' offered in exchange for 90-minute presentation
  • 'Anaheim vacation club' membership at $10,000–$25,000
  • Presentation runs past 2 hours
  • Westgate Park City Resort promoter at Anaheim hotel lobby
  • Exit company charges $5,000+ upfront to cancel existing timeshare

How to Avoid

  • Refuse 'Free Disney ticket' offers requiring presentation attendance.
  • If attending, bring 'I will not sign anything today' rule and stick to it.
  • Use California 7-day right of rescission.
  • Report to California AG Consumer Protection: oag.ca.gov.
  • Buy Disney tickets direct at disneyland.com ($154–$206/day).

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Local Police Department station. Call 911. Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at usa.gov/crimes.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Visit the nearest US Passport Agency. For international visitors, contact your country's consulate or embassy directly. US State Department emergency line: +1-888-407-4747 (from US) or +1-202-501-4444 (international).

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
