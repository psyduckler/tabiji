The 5 Scams

Scam #1
Airport Taxi Overcharge
⚠️ High
📍 Almaty Airport (ALA) arrivals curb, terminal taxi line, downtown hotel drop-offs

A driver outside Almaty Airport's international arrivals quotes KZT 18,000–KZT 30,000 for the ride downtown.

The legitimate Yandex Go fare for the same 17-kilometer trip is KZT 3,000–KZT 5,000. The pitch starts at the curb the moment your luggage clears the carousel: a man in a dark jacket asks where you are headed, then gestures toward an unmarked sedan parked in the official-looking taxi line. There is no kiosk, no posted tariff, no airport authority queue marshal. The car door opens before you can check your phone for fares.

The driver may show a meter that mimics a ride-hailing app — a fake QR code, a generic logo, a screen counting up too fast — and quotes a flat fare before pulling away from the curb. Travelers landing at 1:00 AM and skipping the app step report being charged KZT 30,000 for a ride that costs locals about KZT 4,000. If you protest at the hotel, two or three other drivers walk over from the line and crowd the door. Your bags are still in the trunk.

The mechanism is a **classic information-asymmetry play**: Almaty Airport has no formal taxi rank with posted rates, so unmetered freelancers fill the curb and quote whatever a tired tourist will pay. Yandex Go and inDrive both work at ALA and post a fixed price before you confirm — about KZT 3,000–KZT 5,000 to the city. community threads from 2025 and 2026 document the same play daily, with foreigners charged four to ten times the legitimate fare. **The defensive move is to install Yandex Go before you land, ignore curb-side offers, and walk to the marked app pick-up zone.**

Red Flags

  • Driver approaches you inside the arrivals hall instead of the marked app zone
  • No posted tariff, no kiosk, no queue marshal at the taxi line
  • Quoted price four or more times the Yandex Go in-app fare
  • Meter app shows a generic logo with no driver name or license number
  • Driver refuses to accept Kaspi Pay or card and demands cash only

How to Avoid

  • INSTALL Yandex Go and inDrive before landing and order the ride from inside arrivals.
  • WALK past the curb-side touts to the marked app pick-up zone outside the doors.
  • CONFIRM the in-app fare and license plate match the car before you sit down.
  • REFUSE every meter-only quote and never let the driver start without a fixed price.
  • PHOTOGRAPH the dashboard and license plate before getting in if no app is available.
Scam #2
Lounge Bar Honey-Trap
⚠️ High
📍 Lounge bars off Panfilov Street, late-night Arbat venues, taxi-hail spots near Kok-Tobe

A friendly stranger at a Panfilov Street lounge bar nudges your round to a spiked cocktail, then offers a ride to a club where the bill or the robbery is waiting.

The opener is warm and English-friendly, often about your trip, your camera, your country. Within twenty minutes she has waved a server over for two more drinks and called a taxi. Her hand brushes the rim of the glass. You are smiling. The drink lands.

What lands next is either a four- or five-figure tab at a bar nobody warned you about, or a blank gap in memory and an empty wallet at a hotel concierge desk. An community threads from 2024 documents a tourist being slipped a date-rape drug in a roadside taxi after drinks, hallucinating, then waking with belongings gone and the driver untraceable. Other threads catalog the lounge version: an attractive stranger talks for an hour, the bill arrives at KZT 200,000–KZT 400,000, and two men block the doorway when you object.

The mechanism is **alcohol plus social warmth doing the work your phone usually does**: in an unfamiliar city with a foreign menu and a bar with no posted prices, flattery substitutes for a tab sanity check. Recent Almaty trip reports on community forums, community forums, and community forums from 2025 and 2026 repeat the warning — solo tourists, lounge bars, surprise bills or worse. **The defensive move is to never accept a drink poured out of sight, never leave with someone met at the bar, and order the taxi yourself through Yandex Go before standing up.**

Red Flags

  • Stranger initiates conversation in fluent English at a quiet lounge bar
  • Suggested off-the-menu cocktail or back-room drink with no posted price
  • Bar has no menu in view and no card terminal at the door
  • Sudden suggestion of a taxi or club nearby that they will arrange
  • Two large men positioned near the exit when the bill arrives

How to Avoid

  • NEVER accept a drink poured or carried to the table out of your sight.
  • ASK for the drinks menu with prices before ordering anything off-suggestion.
  • ORDER your own taxi through Yandex Go and leave the bar alone if anything feels wrong.
  • PHOTOGRAPH the bill and refuse to pay any line item that is not on the printed menu.
  • CALL 102 (police) or the US Consulate at +7 727-250-4802 if you are surrounded or extorted.
Scam #3
Krisha Apartment Rental Scam
🟢 Low
📍 Krisha.kz listings, WhatsApp follow-ups, short-stay flats in Almaly and Medeu districts

A Krisha.kz short-term flat at KZT 12,000–KZT 25,000 per night moves to WhatsApp on first contact and demands the first night plus a deposit before you see the keys.

The photos look professional and the rate undercuts Booking.com for the same district. The host writes confident English, says they live abroad, that building registration is in progress, and that a cleaner will meet you. They send bank details for Kaspi or a foreign card. The transfer goes through. The cleaner does not show.

The pivot is a stall — ten more minutes, thirty more minutes — while you stand on a curb in Almaty with luggage and a paid taxi meter ticking. A 2024 community threads describes losing USD 40 on a Krisha rental after the host blocked the chat, sending the renter to chase taxi rides between addresses. Other 2025 Almaty trip-planning threads warn that Krisha postings often demand a brokerage fee of KZT 100,000 or higher with no service rendered. The keys never arrive, and the WhatsApp number stays online for the next victim.

The mechanism is **time pressure plus prepayment-only platforms**: Krisha.kz is the legitimate Kazakh listings hub, but it has no escrow and no platform-side dispute system, so a scam host who collects a wire vanishes. Local advice on community forums from 2024 and 2025 is consistent — pay only on arrival, only at the door, with keys in hand. **The defensive move is to book through Booking.com or Airbnb for the first night, treat any Krisha listing as cash-on-arrival only, and walk away the moment a host asks for a wire.**

Red Flags

  • Listing well below Booking.com or Airbnb for the same district
  • Host moves the conversation to WhatsApp on first message
  • Demand for full first-night payment plus deposit before key handover
  • Host claims to live abroad or be out of town for the handoff
  • Brokerage fee added at signing with no listed service or contract

How to Avoid

  • BOOK your first night through Booking.com or Airbnb so you have a fallback if the deal fails.
  • REFUSE every wire transfer request and pay only at the apartment with keys in hand.
  • VERIFY the host through Kaspi Pay registration — unregistered phone numbers are almost always scammers.
  • DEMAND a written rental contract with the address, dates, and total price before any payment.
  • REPORT a suspected Krisha scam to police at 102 — phone numbers are traceable in Kazakhstan.

Scam #4
Phone Bank Impersonation
🟢 Low
📍 Calls from +7 727 numbers, WhatsApp messages, OLX and HeadHunter listings

A caller posing as Kaspi Bank, the National Bank, or the KNB security service tells you that your tenge deposit is at risk and must be moved to a so-called secure account immediately.

The number looks Kazakh — a +7 727 prefix or a Kaspi-branded sender ID — and the script names a real bank employee, a real branch, even a real ID badge number. They speak in fluent local Russian, address you by name, and stay on the line while you walk to the ATM.

A second pivot lands in the marketplace apps: an OLX or HeadHunter listing for a part-time remote job paid in euros, or a buyer who insists on switching to WhatsApp and asks you to courier an item to a stranger. A 2025 community threads tracks scammers cycling Kazakh, Ukrainian, and Russian SIM cards to call families across the country, while a 2026 HeadHunter post offered euros for a sales-manager role with no interview required. The 2025 OLX thread describes a buyer asking for a courier handoff and refusing to use a Kaspi-registered number.

The mechanism is **authority plus app fluency**: Kazakhstan runs a tightly integrated digital economy on Kaspi Pay and a few platforms, so a caller who knows the bank phone tree, branch IDs, and Kaspi sender format reads as legitimate. community threads from 2025 and 2026 catalog the same patterns — KNB-impersonator phone scripts, OLX courier baits, HeadHunter euro-paid remote roles. **The defensive move is to hang up on any unsolicited call about your account, never share an SMS code, and verify by calling Kaspi at the number printed on your card.**

Red Flags

  • Caller claims to be from KNB, Kaspi, or the National Bank with urgency
  • Sender ID matches a Kazakh prefix but the script asks for codes or transfers
  • Marketplace buyer insists on WhatsApp and refuses Kaspi-registered phone numbers
  • Job listing pays in euros for a remote role with no interview
  • Caller asks you to walk to an ATM while staying on the line

How to Avoid

  • HANG UP on any unsolicited call claiming to be from Kaspi, the National Bank, or KNB.
  • VERIFY by calling Kaspi at the number printed on your card, never the caller's callback line.
  • NEVER share an SMS code, OTP, card number, or password with anyone over the phone.
  • CHECK an OLX or HeadHunter buyer's WhatsApp number against a Kaspi registration before any deal.
  • REPORT phone fraud to police at 102 and to your bank's anti-fraud team within the hour.
Scam #5
Bazaar and Bus Pickpocket
🟢 Low
📍 Green Bazaar aisles, city buses on Tole Bi and Abay, Arbat pedestrian street, Kok-Tobe cable car queue

A pickpocket on a packed Almaty city bus or in the narrow aisles of the Green Bazaar bumps your bag, slips a phone or wallet, and steps off at the next stop before you notice.

The buses on Tole Bi and Abay during morning and evening rush are the standard hunting ground, with two or three operators working as a team — one to crowd, one to lift, one to receive the handoff. The Arbat pedestrian strip and the Kok-Tobe cable car queue see daytime variants. Your hand goes for an unzipped pocket. The phone is gone.

The pivot is rarely violent. Almaty has low rates of armed robbery and the city is generally safe to walk after dark, but the Green Bazaar and the bus network are the two well-documented exceptions. A 2025 community threads on mugging notes pickpocketing in packed buses during rush hour and lock-picking in apartment blocks during work hours. Recent 2025 and 2026 trip reports across community forums, community forums, and community forums praise Almaty's overall safety yet still warn solo travelers to keep wallets, phones, and passports on inside pockets in crowded markets and on public transport.

The mechanism is **the gap between Almaty's low-violence reputation and the foot-traffic chokepoints where opportunist theft concentrates**: travelers who feel safe stop watching the bag, and the bag is what disappears. Kazakhstan's police can be reached at 102 and the US Consulate in Almaty at +7 727-250-4802 for stolen-passport help. **The defensive move is to wear a crossbody bag with the zipper forward, keep your phone deep in an inside pocket on every bus and at the bazaar, and use Yandex Go when carrying anything irreplaceable.**

Red Flags

  • Two or three people crowding you in a packed bus aisle
  • Sudden bump or push in the Green Bazaar narrow lanes
  • Outer pocket of your backpack feels lighter after rush-hour transit
  • Cable car queue at Kok-Tobe with strangers pressing in close
  • Buses on Tole Bi or Abay packed at morning and evening rush

How to Avoid

  • WEAR a crossbody bag with the zipper turned to your front, never to the back.
  • KEEP your phone, wallet, and passport in inside pockets on every bus and at every bazaar.
  • AVOID rush-hour buses on Tole Bi and Abay if you are carrying valuables or luggage.
  • USE Yandex Go or inDrive for the cable car run to Kok-Tobe and back.
  • REPORT stolen items to police at 102 and to the US Consulate at +7 727-250-4802 within the day.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Kazakhstan Police station. Call 102 (Police) or 103 (Ambulance). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at gov.kz.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact the US Embassy in Astana or Consulate in Almaty at 97 Zholdasbekov Street, Almaty 050010. For emergencies: +7 727-250-4802.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
