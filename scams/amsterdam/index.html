The 8 Scams

Scam #1
The Centraal Station Taxi Gang
⚠️ High
📍 Amsterdam Centraal train station

You step out of Centraal station with your luggage and a man immediately offers you a taxi.

He seems legitimate, but 15 minutes later you're at your hotel with a bill for €80 for what should have been a €15 ride. A gang of unlicensed taxi operators used to operate systematically at Centraal, threatening tourists who refused to pay and in some cases getting physically aggressive — they ended up in court for it. Even after crackdowns they keep returning.

Red Flags

  • Driver approaches you at the station exit rather than waiting at a rank
  • No meter running, or meter starts at an unusually high number
  • Quote sounds high but driver says 'fixed rate'
  • Luggage loaded before you agree on price

How to Avoid

  • Use the official taxi rank (TCA taxis) or pre-book through the Uber app
  • Bolt and Uber both operate in Amsterdam at metered rates
  • Official taxis are metered by law and must provide receipts — insist on both
  • Use the official Schiphol Taxi or TCA taxi stand marked with signs rather than accepting rides from drivers who approach you
Scam #2
The Fake / Stepped-On Weed Dealer
🔶 Medium
📍 Leidseplein, Red Light District, near coffeeshops

You're near the Red Light District and someone quietly offers to sell you 'better weed, cheaper ...

You're near the Red Light District and someone quietly offers to sell you 'better weed, cheaper than the coffeeshops.' It looks like cannabis. You buy it in a back alley transaction and later realize you've paid €30 for a mix of dried herbs, tobacco, or something entirely unknown. Street drug dealers near coffeeshops are illegal, usually selling substandard products, and occasionally associated with other crimes.

Red Flags

  • Unsolicited quiet approach near coffeeshop areas
  • Price significantly below coffeeshop prices
  • Transaction in a side street or alley
  • Dealer is nervous or watching for police

How to Avoid

  • Buy exclusively from licensed coffeeshops — they're everywhere and perfectly legal
  • Street dealers have zero accountability and zero quality control
  • Report street dealers to police at 0900-8844 if they approach aggressively or threaten you
  • Walk away immediately without engaging in conversation or negotiation
Scam #3
The Red Light District Tourist Trap Restaurant
🟢 Low
📍 Red Light District (De Wallen), Damrak strip

You choose a restaurant along the Damrak tourist strip because it has a laminated photo menu ...

You choose a restaurant along the Damrak tourist strip because it has a laminated photo menu outside and a smiling host waving you in. An hour later you've paid €55 for two mediocre pasta dishes and two beers that were €6 each in the fine print on the back of the menu. These restaurants survive entirely on one-time tourist traffic and have no reason to provide value.

Red Flags

  • Host stands outside actively waving tourists in
  • Laminated photo menu with suspiciously appealing images
  • Location is directly on a major tourist strip
  • The restaurant has a staff member outside actively soliciting passersby to come inside

How to Avoid

  • Walk 2-3 blocks off the main tourist corridors for dramatically better value
  • Apps like Google Maps filtered for 4+ stars from >200 reviews work well
  • Avoid anywhere a host is actively recruiting customers
  • Check Google Maps reviews before entering any restaurant in the Red Light District area

Scam #4
The Bicycle Rental Damage Deposit Trap
🔶 Medium
📍 Bicycle rental shops near Centraal, tourist areas

You rent a bicycle from a small shop near Centraal for €15/day, paying €50 cash deposit.

When you return it with not a scratch more than it had before, the shop owner produces a previously unnoticed chip in the paint and demands your entire €50 deposit to fix it. You don't have a timestamp photo of the pre-existing damage, and you're leaving tomorrow. Some sketchy rental shops around Centraal specifically exploit tourists on this model.

Red Flags

  • Shop refuses to document existing damage before rental
  • Deposit is high and cash-only
  • No written rental agreement provided
  • Shop owner examines bike very carefully on return

How to Avoid

  • Take dated photos of every scratch before leaving the shop
  • Use the city's official OV-fiets bike share scheme instead
  • Rent from established shops with hundreds of Google reviews
  • Photograph the bicycle from every angle before riding away and email the photos to yourself as timestamped proof
Scam #5
The Fake Hash / Drug Offer Near Coffeeshop
🔶 Medium
📍 Leidseplein and surrounding streets

You've just left a coffeeshop near Leidseplein when a man falls into step beside you and offers ...

You've just left a coffeeshop near Leidseplein when a man falls into step beside you and offers hashish, MDMA, or cocaine at 'tourist friendly prices.' Even if the product is real, buying from a street dealer is illegal in the Netherlands, and possession of quantities above personal use can result in arrest. Some dealers also add robbery or 'rolling' to the interaction — you pay, they leave; or they take your wallet as a 'bonus.'

Red Flags

  • Unsolicited offer of harder drugs outside coffeeshops
  • Person walks alongside you specifically
  • Very low prices for the product offered
  • The seller is lurking outside a coffeeshop rather than working inside one

How to Avoid

  • Ignore all street drug approaches entirely
  • Coffeeshops are legal and regulated — use them exclusively for cannabis
  • Only purchase cannabis from licensed coffeeshops that display the green-and-white sticker
  • Report persistent street dealers to the Amsterdam police non-emergency line
Scam #6
The Museum Queue Pickpocket Team
🔶 Medium
📍 Anne Frank House queue along Prinsengracht, Van Gogh Museum entrance line, Rijksmuseum main entrance, Heineken Experience street queue

You have been standing in the Anne Frank House queue for forty minutes when someone bumps into you from behind.

They apologize in English and step aside. You think nothing of it until you reach the ticket counter and discover your wallet is missing from your jacket pocket. Amsterdam's museum queues are hunting grounds for pickpocket teams who work in pairs. One creates physical contact or a distraction, while the other lifts your wallet or phone within seconds. The European Pickpocketing Index 2024 ranks the Netherlands at 100 mentions per million visitors, with Amsterdam's museum district and Red Light District as primary hotspots.

Red Flags

  • Someone in the queue bumps into you, drops something near your feet, or creates any unexpected physical contact
  • A person asks you a question or shows you a map while standing unusually close to your bag or pockets
  • You notice the same person has been near you in the queue without seeming to move forward themselves
  • Someone creates a commotion or argument nearby that draws everyone's attention
  • A group shuffles around you as the queue moves, with one person pressing close to your side

How to Avoid

  • Keep your wallet in a zipped inside jacket pocket or a cross-body bag worn in front during all museum queues
  • Buy timed-entry tickets online in advance to avoid long outdoor queues entirely, especially for the Anne Frank House
  • Never keep your phone in a back pocket or open bag pocket while standing in a crowd
  • Stay aware of people who get unusually close in a queue and create space by shifting your bag to your front
  • If someone bumps you or creates a distraction, immediately check your pockets and bag zippers before engaging
Scam #7
The Street Drug Dealer Bait
⚠️ High
📍 Leidseplein area, Rembrandtplein side streets, alleys near Damrak and Dam Square, streets adjacent to the Red Light District

A man on a side street near Leidseplein offers you cocaine, ecstasy, or pre-rolled joints at what ...

A man on a side street near Leidseplein offers you cocaine, ecstasy, or pre-rolled joints at what seems like a bargain price. The drugs are fake: the cocaine is baking soda, the ecstasy is candy, and the joints are stuffed with herbs. If you hand over 50 euros, you get nothing of value. Worse, some dealers work with pickpocket teams. While you are focused on the transaction, an accomplice lifts your wallet or phone. In the most dangerous variation, the dealer leads you into a dark alley where you are robbed at knifepoint. Amsterdam police and DutchAmsterdam.nl warn specifically about these fake drug sellers who target tourists, noting that legitimate coffeeshops make street dealers entirely unnecessary.

Red Flags

  • Someone on the street offers to sell you drugs rather than you going to a licensed coffeeshop
  • The price seems unusually cheap compared to coffeeshop rates
  • The seller is on a side street or alley rather than a main pedestrian area
  • They pressure you to decide quickly or follow them somewhere more private
  • Another person is lingering nearby watching the interaction

How to Avoid

  • Only purchase cannabis from licensed coffeeshops that display the official green-and-white sticker; all other street drug sales are illegal and dangerous
  • Never follow a stranger into a side street or alley for any transaction
  • Walk away immediately if anyone offers drugs on the street; do not engage in conversation or negotiation
  • Keep valuables secure in front pockets or cross-body bags when walking through Leidseplein and Rembrandtplein at night
  • If you feel threatened, walk toward the nearest busy street or enter a shop and ask staff to call 112
Scam #8
The Ticket Machine Coin Trap
🟢 Low
📍 Public transport ticket machines at Amsterdam Centraal Station, Leidseplein tram stops, Dam Square metro entrance, Museumplein tram stops

You approach a public transport ticket machine at Amsterdam Centraal or any busy tram stop and ...

You approach a public transport ticket machine at Amsterdam Centraal or any busy tram stop and insert your coins or card, but the machine appears to malfunction. Reddit users on r/Netherlands describe a specific technique: scammers place stickers over the coin return slot or card reader. When you abandon the transaction in frustration, the scammer returns to collect the coins trapped behind the sticker, or approaches you offering to 'help' while an accomplice lifts your wallet or bag. A variation involves someone standing nearby who offers to sell you a 'cheaper' ticket they claim is still valid — these are used tickets or expired day passes picked up from the ground. The thread on r/Netherlands specifically warns about people blocking the machine with stickers and returning to collect trapped coins. In busy stations, this can net scammers 50-100 EUR per day from confused tourists.

Red Flags

  • The ticket machine appears to jam or refuse your payment despite seemingly working for previous users
  • A helpful stranger immediately offers to assist you with the machine or sell you a ticket at a 'discount'
  • The coin return slot or card insertion slot looks different from machines at other stops — check for stickers or tape
  • Someone is loitering near the machines without appearing to use them
  • The ticket offered by a stranger has creases, stains, or a date that has passed

How to Avoid

  • Buy an OV-chipkaart or use a contactless debit/credit card for all GVB transport — avoid coin-operated machines entirely
  • Download the GVB or NS app to purchase digital tickets before approaching any machine
  • If a machine malfunctions, report it to the GVB service desk inside the station rather than accepting help from strangers
  • Never purchase tickets from individuals on the street — legitimate tickets are only sold at machines, service desks, and the GVB app
  • Keep your bag zipped and in front of you while using any machine in a crowded station

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Dutch Police (Politie) station. Call 0900-8844 (non-emergency) or 112 (emergency). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at politie.nl.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Consulate Amsterdam is at Museumplein 19, 1071 DJ Amsterdam. For emergencies: +31 70 310 2209.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
