The 6 Scams

Scam #1
The Costa Verde R$350 Shuttle
🔶 Medium
📍 Rio Novo Rio bus terminal (Costa Verde routes), São Paulo Tietê bus terminal, Costa Verde BR-101 highway, GIG / GRU airport tour-desk transfer counters, Paraty Rodoviária

A GIG airport tour-desk quotes R$2,500 for the four-hour drive to your Paraty pousada. The Costa Verde Transportes coach from Novo Rio Rodoviária is R$95–R$140 in an air-conditioned bus with reclining seats; a real licensed private door-to-door is R$600–R$900. You'd be paying triple to a desk taking a 30% kickback.

Paraty has no airport. Every visitor arrives by road — 250 kilometers southwest of Rio (a four-hour coach), 340 kilometers northeast of São Paulo (six hours), along a famously winding two-lane highway with 250-plus curves between Angra dos Reis and Paraty itself. That single-route transfer is the lever for 2026's most consistent Paraty markup, worked from Rio's GIG and São Paulo's GRU airport tour-desks, from hotel concierges in both cities, and from WhatsApp "private apartment owner" contacts who recommend their cousin as the driver.

The right anchor is the public coach. Costa Verde Transportes runs Rio Novo Rio Rodoviária to Paraty at R$95–R$140 in air-conditioned coaches with reclining seats and an onboard restroom — four to six daily departures, bookable at viacaocostaverde.com.br. Reunidas Paulista runs São Paulo Tietê to Paraty at R$130–R$180 over six hours. A legitimate private transfer through BR Transfers, Carioca Transfers, or Litoral Transfers is R$600–R$900 per car (1–4 passengers) Rio–Paraty and R$900–R$1,200 São Paulo–Paraty. The trap quotes start at R$1,800–R$3,000 from a GIG or GRU tour-desk for a "private transfer" — two to three times the real licensed rate. They drop to R$1,500 for a hotel-concierge "recommended driver" kickback. Then a R$350-per-person "shared shuttle van" that's identical to the R$95 Costa Verde bus at three times the price. And a R$2,000 "door-to-door" WhatsApp driver from your Airbnb host. r/Brazil's Paraty threads document each of these running side by side.

At Paraty Rodoviária, the same desk economy stages a R$80 "taxi to your pousada" quote for what's a five-minute walk into Centro Histórico or a R$20–R$30 metered ride. Take Costa Verde Transportes from Rio Novo Rio Rodoviária at R$95–R$140 or Reunidas Paulista from São Paulo Tietê at R$130–R$180 directly to Paraty (book one to two weeks ahead in high season), use a R$60 Uber from GIG to Novo Rio if you're flying into Rio, and refuse every airport tour-desk "private transfer R$1,800–R$3,000," every hotel-concierge "recommended driver R$1,500," and every "shared shuttle van R$350 per person." If you genuinely want a door-to-door private, book BR Transfers, Carioca Transfers, or Litoral Transfers directly at R$600–R$900 per car. Take Dramamine 30 minutes before boarding for the winding road, plan for daytime travel only (the BR-101 has poor lighting and a serious nighttime accident record), and refuse any "toll surcharge R$80" since actual round-trip tolls are R$25–R$40.

Red Flags

  • A GIG or GRU tour-desk quoting R$1,800–R$3,000 for a private transfer to Paraty (real licensed rate R$600–R$900).
  • A hotel-concierge "recommended driver" at R$1,500 — that's a kickback on top of the real R$600–R$900.
  • A "shared shuttle van R$350 per person" — the Costa Verde coach is R$95–R$140 for the same route.
  • A WhatsApp "door-to-door driver R$2,000" from your apartment owner.
  • A "Costa Verde toll surcharge R$80" tacked onto a private quote — actual round-trip BR-101 tolls are R$25–R$40.

How to Avoid

  • Take Costa Verde Transportes from Novo Rio Rodoviária to Paraty at R$95–R$140 (book at viacaocostaverde.com.br).
  • From GIG, Uber to Novo Rio is R$60; total Rio–Paraty under R$200.
  • For door-to-door, book BR Transfers, Carioca Transfers, or Litoral Transfers at R$600–R$900 per car.
  • At Paraty Rodoviária, walk five to ten minutes into Centro Histórico — it's a small UNESCO town.
  • Take Dramamine 30 minutes before boarding; the Costa Verde road has 250 curves.
Scam #2
The Cais do Cabral VIP Schooner
🔶 Medium
📍 Paraty Marina (Largo do Rosário), Praia do Pontal pier, Cais do Cabral schooner pier, Centro Histórico tour-booking touts, hotel-concierge boat-tour desks

A Centro Histórico tour-tout pitches a "private schooner" Paraty Bay tour at R$500 per person; the reputable group tour with the same four-to-six-beach route, snorkel gear, and lunch is R$70–R$120 with operators like Saveiros Soberanos or Escuna Mar Aberto. Many of the "private" boats run without life jackets, VHF radio, or Marinha registration.

The Passeio de Escuna — a 5–6 hour wooden-schooner cruise visiting four to six of Paraty Bay's island beaches with snorkeling stops — is the destination's signature excursion and the single biggest visitor activity in the bay. Three reputable operators with consistent Google 4.5+ ratings work the route from Cais do Cabral, the Marina, and Pier Largo do Rosário: Saveiros Soberanos, Escuna Mar Aberto, and Escuna Saveiros. The standard product is a 5–6 hour bay loop hitting Lagoa Azul, Praia Vermelha, Ilha Comprida, and Praia da Lula with included snorkel gear, buffet lunch, and soft drinks for R$70–R$120 per person.

Centro Histórico tour-booking touts work the obvious confusion. The most common pitch is "private schooner R$500 per person" for what's a R$70–R$120 group experience — and the "private" boat often turns out to be the same group schooner with extra travelers added. The "VIP exclusive route to Lagoa Azul + Praia Vermelha R$800" is fictional; both are standard stops on the public itinerary. The "all-inclusive open bar premium R$280" is just the standard tour with two extra beers. The "private islands access R$600" is fiction — Paraty Bay's islands are 95% public-access. Layered on top: R$300 "photo and drone video packages," a R$50 "mandatory life-jacket rental" on tours where jackets are required-to-be-included safety equipment, and a R$250-per-person "speedboat to Trindade" from Cais do Cabral when the real Trindade route is a R$8 local bus from Paraty Rodoviária plus a R$30–R$60 mini-schooner from Trindade pier. The genuine risk sits at the unlicensed boats: operators advertising "cheap schooner R$50 today only" lack life jackets, VHF radios, and Marinha do Brasil registration, and Paraty Bay has documented serious safety incidents during summer afternoon storms.

Book schooner tours only through Google 4.5+ rated operators with verified Marinha do Brasil registration — Saveiros Soberanos at the Marina, Escuna Mar Aberto at Cais do Cabral, or Escuna Saveiros at Pier Largo do Rosário — at R$70–R$120 per person for a 5–6 hour cruise with four to six beach stops, snorkel gear, and lunch included. Refuse every dock-front "private schooner R$500," "VIP exclusive route R$800," "mid-tour drink package R$200," and "life-jacket rental R$50" upsell as either fictional or a violation of safety regulations. Verify life jackets for every passenger before boarding and look for the Marinha registration sticker on the boat. Pre-book one to two days ahead online in the December–February and July high seasons. Bring biodegradable sunscreen only (Paraty Bay has reef rules), take Dramamine 30 minutes before for motion sickness and sit center-boat, and skip the pier-front R$250 "speedboat to Trindade" — the right route is a R$8 bus plus a R$30–R$60 mini-schooner.

Red Flags

  • A Centro Histórico tout selling "private schooner R$500 per person" — the real group tour runs R$70–R$120.
  • A "VIP exclusive Lagoa Azul + Praia Vermelha R$800" pitch — both are standard schooner stops.
  • A boat without visible life jackets, a VHF radio, or a Marinha registration sticker.
  • A "private islands access R$600" upsell — Paraty Bay's islands are 95% public access.
  • A "mandatory life-jacket rental R$50" — jackets must be included on licensed tours.

How to Avoid

  • Book Google 4.5+ Marinha-registered operators: Saveiros Soberanos, Escuna Mar Aberto, or Escuna Saveiros at R$70–R$120 per person.
  • Verify life jackets for everyone and the Marinha registration sticker before boarding.
  • Refuse every "private," "VIP," or "premium" upsell — the group tour visits the same four to six beaches.
  • Pre-book one to two days ahead in high season (December–February, July).
  • For Trindade, take the R$8 local bus, not the R$250 pier-front "speedboat."
Scam #3
The Centro Histórico Couvert
🔶 Medium
📍 Centro Histórico cobblestone street restaurants, Praia do Pontal waterfront dining, Rua da Lapa tourist-menu strip, Rua Comendador José Luiz dining row

Paraty Centro Histórico waterfront restaurants on Rua da Lapa post USD-only menus at 2–3× local prices, layer R$60–R$180 "couvert artístico" for a single guitarist (legitimate is R$15–R$35), present 10% serviço as mandatory when Brazilian Lei 13.419/2017 makes it optional, and quote market-price lobster at R$800–R$1,400 per kg without weight disclosed. Eat one block off the strip and the same dish costs half.

Paraty's Centro Histórico is a compact 8×6-block UNESCO cobblestone old town, almost entirely pedestrian, dining-dense, and built around Rua da Lapa and Rua Comendador José Luiz where every package-tour Brazilian visitor and every Rio day-tripper is funneled through the same restaurant strip. That density supports a tourist-menu bill-padding pattern that runs around two consumer-protection facts most foreign visitors don't know: Brazilian law (Lei 13.419/2017) makes the 10% serviço charge optional, and INMETRO regulates pricing disclosure on menus and weighed items.

The Paraty trap menu starts with USD-only menus at the waterfront restaurants — Brazilian moqueca quoted at US$30+ when the same dish is R$70–R$110 at non-tourist spots, with the dollar conversion adding 10–15% on top of the existing markup. "Couvert artístico" is layered at R$60–R$180 per person for a single guitarist when legitimate couvert for a real performer is R$15–R$35 disclosed before seating. Bottled "água mineral" at R$22 vs the real R$6–R$12, undisclosed credit-card surcharges of 5–10% added at payment, and the bill-switcheroo where the printed receipt total doesn't match the card-charge amount round out the standard padding. The most expensive single play is "lagosta peso variável" — a market-price lobster quoted at R$800–R$1,400 per kg with no weight shown before cooking, where a 1.5-kg lobster lands as a R$1,800 line item. Cachaça shots at R$80 are sold from house bottles when reputable artisanal cachaça runs R$25–R$50 at proper distilleries, and a R$600 "Paraty seafood platter for 2" typically delivers R$250 worth of fish padded with bread, palm hearts, and a couvert charge.

Eat at authentic Paraty spots — Caminho do Ouro on the waterfront for traditional Caiçara cuisine (Google 4.7+, R$80–R$180), Banana da Terra on Rua Dr Samuel Costa for regional Brazilian (R$90–R$200), Quintal das Letras for vegetarian-friendly patio dining (R$60–R$140), or Restaurante Sereia do Mar at Praia do Pontal for fish (R$70–R$150). Before sitting, photograph the menu, confirm the couvert artístico amount per person and the credit-card surcharge in writing, and refuse any unordered couvert (bread, olive oil, palm hearts) since Brazilian Lei 13.419/2017 makes serviço optional and you can decline a 10% charge for poor service. Order "água sem gás copo" (free tap water) at most places. For weighed seafood, agree the weight in writing before cooking — fair 2026 prices are R$400–R$700 per kg for lobster and R$80–R$130 per kg for white fish. For cachaça, the proper Paraty experience is at the distilleries 5 km from Centro at R$8–R$25 a shot, not the R$80 tourist-restaurant pour. The Sunday afternoon Feira do Pescador fishmonger market at Praia do Pontal sells fresh-grilled fish meals at R$35–R$60 and is the budget locals' answer to the waterfront strip.

Red Flags

  • A Rua da Lapa or Rua Comendador José Luiz restaurant with a USD-only menu at 2–3× local R$ prices.
  • A "couvert artístico" line at R$60–R$180 per person for a single guitarist (real couvert R$15–R$35).
  • "Serviço 10%" presented as mandatory — under Brazilian law (Lei 13.419/2017) it's optional.
  • A "lagosta peso variável" verbal quote with no weight shown before cooking.
  • A "Paraty seafood platter for 2 R$600" line item — typically R$250 worth padded with sides.

How to Avoid

  • Eat at Caminho do Ouro, Banana da Terra, Quintal das Letras, or Sereia do Mar at R$60–R$200 per meal.
  • Before sitting, photograph the menu and confirm couvert, serviço, and card-surcharge in writing.
  • "Serviço 10%" is optional under Lei 13.419/2017 — refuse if the service was poor.
  • For lobster, agree weight × R$400–R$700 per kg in writing before the cooking starts.
  • Sunday's Feira do Pescador at Praia do Pontal sells fresh-grilled fish meals at R$35–R$60.

Scam #4
The Cachaça Master's Flight
🟢 Low
📍 Cachaça distillery tour stops outside Paraty Centro (Engenho d'Ouro, Pedra Branca, Maria Izabel), Centro Histórico cachaça shops, hotel-concierge cachaça-tour bookings

Paraty's cachaça distilleries (Engenho d'Ouro, Pedra Branca, Maria Izabel, Coqueiro) charge R$25–R$70 entry including tour and four to six tastings — but Centro hotel concierges bundle "distillery tour combos" at R$350 per person, Centro tourist shops sell "limited-edition Paraty cachaça US$80 per 200ml" for what's R$60–R$150 at the producer's own door, and "master cachaça 12-year aged R$1,500" bottles inflate against R$400–R$800 reputable pricing.

Paraty is Brazil's historic center for artisanal cachaça production, with multiple working distilleries dating to the Portuguese colonial era and IBRAC (Brazilian Cachaça Institute) certification on the country's most respected producers. Distillery visits are one of the destination's signature half-day cultural experiences, and the four reputable open-to-visitors stops — Engenho d'Ouro 5 km from Centro, Pedra Branca 8 km, Maria Izabel, and Coqueiro — are the right answer for both tasting and bottle purchases. The 2025 commission-tour ecosystem exploits visitor confusion between "cachaça artesanal" (hand-distilled in copper alembics with fresh cane juice — the good stuff) and "cachaça industrial" (column-distilled, skip), and between distillery-direct prices and Centro Histórico tourist-shop markups.

Real distillery prices are simple: Engenho d'Ouro R$30–R$60 entry (tour plus four to six tastings), Pedra Branca R$25–R$50, Maria Izabel R$35–R$70. Quality artisanal Paraty bottles run R$60–R$120 for Coqueiro 500ml, R$80–R$180 for Pedra Branca, R$150–R$300 for Engenho d'Ouro envelhecida (aged), and R$250–R$500 for top-shelf 5+ year amburana or bálsamo wood-aged. The trap menu starts with the hotel-concierge "cachaça distillery tour combo R$350 per person" against a real DIY of R$50 round-trip Uber plus R$30–R$60 entry (R$80–R$110 per person). At the distillery itself, "premium tasting flight R$200" duplicates what's already included in the standard entry. "Master cachaça 12-year aged R$1,500 per bottle" inflates against legitimate R$400–R$800 from reputable producers, and the vintage-and-barrel-wood claims often don't survive a quick check. Centro Histórico tourist shops sell "limited-edition Paraty cachaça US$80 per 200ml" for what's R$60–R$150 at the same producer's distillery counter. Layered on top: "mandatory shipping fee R$200" for take-home bottles when Brazilian Correios is R$30–R$60 for one to two bottles, "private chauffeur to 3 distilleries R$800" against R$60–R$80 Uber per leg, and "cachaça master class R$400 per person" that turns out to be a 30-minute Centro talk.

Take Uber from Centro to Engenho d'Ouro 5 km (R$50 round-trip with wait), Pedra Branca 8 km (R$80 round-trip), or Maria Izabel directly — pay R$25–R$70 entry that includes the tour and four to six tastings, and buy bottles at the distillery counter at R$60–R$300. Refuse every hotel-concierge "distillery combo R$350 per person," every "premium tasting flight R$200" (already included in entry), every "master cachaça 12-year R$1,500 bottle" against the R$400–R$800 reputable price, every Centro tourist-shop "limited-edition US$80 per 200ml," and every "mandatory shipping R$200" fee against the R$30–R$60 Correios reality. Stick to IBRAC-certified producers — Coqueiro, Pedra Branca, Engenho d'Ouro, Maria Izabel — and look for "cachaça artesanal" or "cachaça de alambique" on the label rather than industrial column-distilled. Pedra Branca is the most photogenic working distillery for travelers wanting to see the alembics in operation. Plan lunch at the Engenho d'Ouro restaurant (R$80–R$150) for traditional Caiçara cuisine — the half-day cultural visit is one of the better-value Paraty experiences when booked DIY.

Red Flags

  • A hotel-concierge "cachaça distillery tour combo R$350 per person" — the DIY is R$80–R$110.
  • A "premium tasting flight R$200" at the distillery — the standard tasting is already included in the R$25–R$70 entry.
  • A "master cachaça 12-year aged R$1,500 per bottle" — legitimate aged Paraty bottles run R$400–R$800.
  • A "mandatory shipping fee R$200" for take-home bottles — Brazilian Correios is R$30–R$60 for one to two bottles.
  • A Centro tourist-shop "limited-edition Paraty cachaça US$80 per 200ml" sold at twice the distillery price.

How to Avoid

  • Take Uber direct to Engenho d'Ouro at R$50 round-trip; entry R$30–R$60 includes the tasting flight.
  • Buy bottles at the distillery counter — 30–50% cheaper than Centro tourist shops.
  • Reputable IBRAC-certified producers: Coqueiro, Pedra Branca, Engenho d'Ouro, Maria Izabel.
  • Look for "cachaça artesanal" or "de alambique" on the label — skip industrial column-distilled.
  • For international shipping, Correios is R$30–R$60 for one to two bottles; declare value honestly.
Scam #5
The Trindade R$600 Combo
🔶 Medium
📍 Centro Histórico tour-booking touts, hotel-concierge day-trip desks, Trindade beach village (35 km south of Paraty Centro), Angra dos Reis schooner connections, BR-101 highway day-trip routes

Centro Histórico tour-desks bundle "Trindade + Angra combined day-trip R$600 per person" against a R$80–R$160 DIY total via R$8 local bus and R$25–R$40 ferry. The "private speedboat to Ilha Grande R$1,800" and "Lagoa Azul exclusive access R$250" are pure fiction layered on a free public route.

Paraty sits on the Costa Verde almost exactly between two of the region's signature day-trip targets. Trindade — a tiny Caiçara beach village 35 km south, reachable on a R$8 local bus in 50 minutes — has four walk-from-center beaches (Praia dos Ranchos, Cepilho, Praia do Cachadaço, Lagoa Azul) and the free Cachoeira do Tobogã natural-slide waterfall five minutes from the village. Angra dos Reis 50 km north on the Auto Viação Costa Verde bus is the ferry hub for Ilha Grande, the largest island on Brazil's southeast coast. Both are easy DIY days, and that's exactly the gap Centro Histórico tour-desks paper over with bundled commission-kickback packages.

Real prices keep the DIY anchor honest. Trindade is R$8 each way on the local bus (50 minutes, every one to two hours from Paraty Rodoviária) or R$80 each way by Uber, plus R$30–R$60 for a beach-hopping mini-schooner from Trindade pier if you want the offshore beaches. Angra is R$15–R$30 each way on the Auto Viação Costa Verde bus (50 km, one hour) plus a R$25–R$40 ferry to Ilha Grande, totalling R$80–R$140 round-trip Paraty-to-Ilha-Grande and best done as a two-day rather than a same-day trip. The trap menu starts with Centro Histórico touts selling "Trindade + Angra combined day-trip R$600 per person" against a real DIY of R$80–R$160. The "recommended driver + tour combo R$800" from your pousada concierge is the kickback variant. "Private speedboat to Ilha Grande R$1,800 per person" is fictional against the legitimate R$25–R$40 ferry. "Lagoa Azul exclusive Trindade access R$250" charges for what's already part of the standard Trindade beach-hopping route. "Cachoeira do Tobogã private guide R$150" charges for a free public waterfall five minutes from Trindade center. "Mandatory ferry skip-the-line R$80" at Angra is fiction — no skip-the-line exists. Layered on top: a commission-restaurant lunch stop at R$200 per person added silently to the route, and tour-bus drivers detouring to commission cachaça shops on the BR-101 corridor where bottles cost two to three times the distillery price.

For Trindade, take the local bus from Paraty Rodoviária at R$8 each way (or Uber R$80) and walk to the four village-center beaches — Praia dos Ranchos, Cepilho, Praia do Cachadaço, Lagoa Azul — using a R$30–R$60 mini-schooner from Trindade pier if you want the offshore route; for Angra, take Costa Verde bus at R$15–R$30 plus the R$25–R$40 ferry to Ilha Grande as a two-day trip. Refuse every "Trindade + Angra combined R$600," every "private speedboat to Ilha Grande R$1,800," every "Lagoa Azul exclusive R$250," every "Cachoeira do Tobogã private guide R$150," and every "mandatory ferry skip-the-line R$80" as either commission stacking or outright fiction. Eat in Trindade at Restaurante Olho de Boi in the village center (fresh fish, R$50–R$100) or Restaurante 7 Estrelas at Praia do Cepilho (R$60–R$120) rather than the "commission-restaurant lunch stop" on a tour route. Buy your Angra ferry ticket at the terminal box office on the day at R$25–R$40 — there is no skip-the-line product, only legitimate boarding. If a tour-bus driver detours to a "recommended cachaça shop" on the BR-101, refuse to enter; the real distillery purchases happen at Engenho d'Ouro and Pedra Branca closer to Paraty Centro at half the price.

Red Flags

  • A "Trindade + Angra combined day-trip R$600 per person" bundle — DIY total is R$80–R$160.
  • A hotel-concierge "recommended driver + tour combo R$800" — that's a kickback markup.
  • A "private speedboat to Ilha Grande R$1,800 per person" — the real ferry from Angra is R$25–R$40.
  • A "Cachoeira do Tobogã private guide R$150" — the slide is a free public attraction.
  • A "Lagoa Azul exclusive Trindade access R$250" — Lagoa Azul is on the standard schooner route.

How to Avoid

  • Take the local bus from Paraty Rodoviária to Trindade at R$8 each way (50 minutes).
  • At Trindade, walk to the four village-center beaches (5 minutes) or take a mini-schooner R$30–R$60.
  • For Ilha Grande, Costa Verde bus + Angra ferry totals R$80–R$140 round-trip — best as a two-day trip.
  • Cachoeira do Tobogã is a free public waterfall — refuse any "private guide R$150" charge.
  • Eat in Trindade at Restaurante Olho de Boi or 7 Estrelas at R$50–R$120 per meal.
Scam #6
The FLIP Pousada Cancel
⚠️ High
📍 Paraty pousada Booking.com / Airbnb listings, Carnaval (Feb–Mar) booking window, FLIP literary festival (late July) accommodations, WhatsApp 'private apartment' Centro Histórico offers

Paraty accommodation rates spike four to eight times during Carnaval (late February or early March) and FLIP (the Festa Literária Internacional de Paraty in late July, 30,000+ attendees), drawing Booking.com cancel-and-relist plays where R$450/night confirmed bookings get cancelled 30–60 days out and re-listed at R$1,800–R$3,500. WhatsApp "private Centro apartment" sellers demand 100% PIX upfront for properties that don't exist.

Paraty's two peak booking windows — Carnaval in late February or early March and FLIP, the Festa Literária Internacional de Paraty in late July — concentrate Brazil's domestic and literary tourism into a single UNESCO town with limited Centro Histórico stock. FLIP alone draws 30,000+ attendees as Brazil's premier literary festival, and accommodation rates run four to eight times normal across both windows. That pricing pressure on a small inventory creates exactly the cancel-and-relist conditions seen in Rio, Búzios, and Ouro Preto Carnaval, plus the WhatsApp PIX fraud pattern that targets last-minute international bookers.

The mechanic spans the booking funnel and the on-arrival door. Booking.com and Airbnb "phantom listings" use stolen photos of Centro Histórico colonial pousadas — you pay in advance, arrive, and find no property at the address. Confirmed-booking cancellations land 30–60 days before Carnaval or FLIP, with the host citing a "pousada emergency" before re-listing the same unit at three to five times the original price (the platform refunds the original payment, but you're stuck a month from travel with prices doubled). WhatsApp "private Centro Histórico apartment for FLIP R$3,500/night" offers demand 100% PIX upfront — Brazilian PIX is irreversible once cleared, and the listing disappears the moment payment confirms. A "colonial Centro pousada" booking turns out to be one to two kilometers outside Centro in suburban Paraty (always verify on Google Maps before paying).
