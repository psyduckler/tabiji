The 6 Scams

Scam #1
Salvador (SSA) Airport Taxi Mafia & Late-Night Uber Confusion
⚠️ High
📍 Salvador Bahia Airport (SSA) arrivals halls (Terminals 1 & 2), 'Special Taxi' kiosks, Cocobambu / Coopataxi counters, Barra / Pelourinho / Itapuã drop-offs

Salvador Bahia Airport (SSA, 28 km from Pelourinho) 'Special Taxi' and Coopataxi kiosks inside arrivals quote R$200–R$400 to Barra, Pelourinho, or Rio Vermelho for what's R$70–R$130 on Uber, 99, or InDriver — sign-holding 'pousada transfer agents' intercept tourists, post-22:00 Uber driver supply is thinner so unauthorized drivers approach cancelled-ride passengers with cash quotes, and credit-card cloning happens at unofficial taxi-cooperative counters via 'manual entry' card machines.

Salvador Bahia (SSA) is North-East Brazil's second-busiest airport after Recife, sitting 28 kilometres north of Pelourinho via the BR-324 and BA-526 access roads. The airport has Uber, 99, and InDriver coverage with reasonable supply during the day but documented thinner driver availability after 22:00 — and that thinness is the lever for the most consistent SSA arrivals trap, where unauthorized drivers wait near the Aplicativos pickup zone for travellers whose Uber matches drop and offer 'cash only' rides at three to five times the app rate. The trap economy follows the Brazilian-airport template: 'Special Taxi' kiosks selling fixed-rate quotes well above metered fare, sign-holders posing as 'pousada transfer' agents inside arrivals, and credit-card cloning at unofficial cooperative counters.

The trap menu has seven recurring mechanics. 'Special Taxi' or Coopataxi kiosks inside SSA arrivals quote R$200–R$400 to Barra, Pelourinho, or Rio Vermelho against an Uber rate of R$70–R$130. Sign-holders posing as 'pousada transfer' agents intercept tourists in the baggage hall (legitimate hotel transfers wait outside arrivals with a printed name). Uber cancel-and-recontract works the post-22:00 thin-supply window — the in-app driver cancels, an unauthorized driver appears with a cash quote, and the traveller defaults to that out of fatigue. 'Meter broken' yellow-taxi demands materialise at hotel arrival with a R$250+ flat-rate cash demand. 'Overnight surcharge R$80' added unannounced after late-night arrivals isn't a legitimate fee. Credit-card cloning at unofficial cooperative counters via 'manual entry' card-machines is the most damaging variant — the card details are skimmed during a transaction that itself isn't fraud. During Carnaval (February–March), legitimate prices spike 3–5× and trap quotes spike correspondingly. Real fares anchor at R$80–R$130 to Pelourinho, R$70–R$110 to Barra, R$70–R$120 to Rio Vermelho, with 35–60 minute travel times depending on traffic.

For older travellers arriving at SSA, the defense is to book the app on airport Wi-Fi and meet the driver at the signposted Aplicativos zone. Book Uber, 99, or InDriver on airport Wi-Fi after collecting luggage — expect R$80–R$130 to Pelourinho, R$70–R$110 to Barra, R$70–R$120 to Rio Vermelho — meet your driver at the signposted Aplicativos pickup zone at the terminal exit, and refuse every 'Special Taxi' kiosk quoting R$180+, every sign-holding 'pousada transfer agent' inside arrivals, every 'meter broken' R$250 cash demand at the hotel, every 'overnight surcharge R$80' against the regulated rate, and every 'manual entry' credit-card transaction at unofficial cooperative counters. For late-night arrivals after 22:00, the R$15–R$30 surcharge for Uber Comfort or 99 Top buys a more reliable driver and is worth it; allow 10–20 minutes of wait time when supply is thin. Pay in-app or cash only — never hand a credit card to any taxi driver. For Carnaval period (February–March), budget 3–5× normal taxi rates and book Uber Black or pre-arrange hotel transfer. Get a Brazilian eSIM (Vivo, Claro, TIM) for guaranteed connectivity before relying on Uber. Save DEATUR Salvador +55 71 3116 6817 (English available, Praça da Sé) and US Consulate Recife +55 81 3416 3050 (covers Bahia) before arrival.

Red Flags

  • 'Special Taxi' / Coopataxi kiosk inside SSA quoting R$200–R$400 to Pelourinho
  • Sign-holder posing as 'pousada transfer' for hotel without shuttle
  • Uber driver canceling at curb post-22:00 and re-quoting R$200+ cash
  • Yellow taxi 'meter broken' with R$250+ flat-rate demand at hotel
  • 'Overnight surcharge R$80' added after late-night arrival (fake)

How to Avoid

  • Book Uber/99/InDriver on airport Wi-Fi: SSA-Pelourinho R$80–R$130.
  • Late-night (post-22:00): Uber Comfort/99 Top R$15–R$30 surcharge.
  • Ignore 'Special Taxi' kiosks quoting above R$180.
  • Don't hand credit card to any taxi driver.
  • DEATUR Salvador +55 71 3116 6817 (English available).
Scam #2
Pelourinho 'Fita do Senhor do Bonfim' Ribbon-Tying Forced-Tip Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Pelourinho cobblestone streets (Largo do Pelourinho, Terreiro de Jesus), Igreja do Senhor do Bonfim church area (Itapagipe peninsula), Mercado Modelo, walking-tour pickup zones

Pelourinho's 'free Fita do Senhor do Bonfim' ribbon scam ties a colourful three-knot ribbon on your wrist as a 'three wishes' blessing, then demands R$20–R$100 'donation' for what's a R$0.50–R$2 ribbon at the actual Bonfim church gift shop — variants extract R$50 'cultural fund' donations after unsolicited Bahiana photos, R$50 'capoeira roda donations' after street performances, and R$150 'frame + delivery' on R$30 caricatures.

The 'Fita do Senhor do Bonfim' (Bonfim ribbon) is a genuine Bahian Catholic-Candomblé blessing ribbon — colourful printed silk strips sold at the actual Igreja do Senhor do Bonfim church on Praia do Bonfim and at every Pelourinho gift shop for R$0.50–R$2 each, often in packs of 12 for R$6–R$18. The ribbon is tied around the wrist with three knots, each representing a wish that comes true when the ribbon eventually breaks. That genuine tradition is the cover story for the most common Pelourinho forced-tip scam, which targets obvious tourists (cameras, English-speaking, hotel-zone clothes) on the cobblestone streets between Largo do Pelourinho and Praça da Sé.

The mechanic has four stages and several variants. Stage one: a friendly local approaches with 'free fita do Bonfim, three wishes' framing on the cobblestones. Stage two: before the traveller can decline meaningfully, the ribbon is tied with three knots around the wrist. Stage three: a R$20–R$100 'donation' demand once the ribbon is on, against the R$0.50–R$2 church-shop price. Stage four: refusal escalates to verbal pressure, accusations of disrespect, or 'bad luck if you remove the blessing.' The same coordinated forced-tip ecosystem runs three other scripts. The Bahiana-photo variant: a costumed white-dress Baiana woman appears in the line of sight, the traveller takes an unsolicited photo, then a R$50 'photo for charity' or 'Pelourinho cultural fund' demand lands. The capoeira-roda variant: tourists watch a 5-minute street capoeira performance, then face a R$50 demand when legitimate street rodas are tip-based at R$5–R$20 voluntary. The street-artist variant: a 'quick caricature R$30' becomes R$150 'frame plus delivery' once the drawing is done. All four variants target the same impulse compassion and the same English-language vulnerability.

For older travellers walking Pelourinho, the defense is to refuse every 'free' offer and buy the ribbon at the actual Bonfim church if you want one. Refuse every 'free fita' or 'free blessing' offer on the Pelourinho cobblestones — there is no legitimate free fita, and the R$20–R$100 'donation' that follows is the trap — and if you want a Bonfim ribbon, buy direct at the Igreja do Senhor do Bonfim gift shop on Praia do Bonfim at R$0.50–R$2 each (or packs of 12 for R$6–R$18); say 'não, obrigado' firmly to every 'free' photo with a Bahiana, every street capoeira approach, and every 'quick caricature' offer in Pelourinho. The legitimate paid capoeira show is at Forte da Capoeira on Largo do Santo Antônio at R$30–R$50 with no surprise charges, and street rodas are tip-based at R$5–R$20 voluntary. For photos with the iconic white-dress Baiana figures, agree the price before the photo (R$5–R$10 per photo is fair for the professional models who pose for tourists). Pelourinho is broadly safe for daytime sightseeing, but the cobblestone narrow streets concentrate the forced-tip touts — visit 9 AM–5 PM on weekdays and avoid evenings when vendors are more aggressive. DEATUR Salvador +55 71 3116 6817 sits at Praça da Sé for incident reports.

Red Flags

  • Friendly local offering 'free fita do Senhor do Bonfim' on Pelourinho
  • Ribbon tied around your wrist with three knots before you agree on price
  • 'Donation R$20–R$100' demand after the ribbon is tied
  • 'Photo with costumed Bahiana R$50' demanded after unsolicited photo
  • 'Capoeira roda donation R$50' demand after watching street performance

How to Avoid

  • Buy fita at OFFICIAL Igreja do Senhor do Bonfim gift shop R$0.50–R$2.
  • Firm 'não obrigado' to every Pelourinho 'free' offer.
  • Capoeira: Forte da Capoeira paid show R$30–R$50; voluntary tip otherwise.
  • Bahiana photos: agree R$5–R$10 BEFORE photo.
  • Pelourinho daytime only (9 AM–5 PM weekdays); DEATUR +55 71 3116 6817.
Scam #3
Pelourinho & Centro Histórico Pickpocket & Phone-Snatch Ring
⚠️ High
📍 Pelourinho cobblestone streets (Largo do Pelourinho, Terreiro de Jesus, Largo do Cruzeiro de São Francisco), Mercado Modelo plaza, Elevador Lacerda upper plaza, Praça da Sé / Cathedral approach

Pelourinho (Salvador's UNESCO colonial old town) hosts the city's highest-density pickpocket ring — organized 3–5 member teams target photographers around São Francisco Church and Largo do Pelourinho, the Mercado Modelo and Elevador Lacerda boarding queues funnel tourists into team setups, capoeira-roda crowds get worked from the edges, and 'Olafemi Tours' is community-flagged for poor security awareness — Pelourinho is daytime-only (9 AM–5 PM weekdays), Uber to and from your hotel, never walk after 19:00.

Pelourinho is Salvador's UNESCO colonial old town and the cultural heart of Afro-Brazilian Bahia — unmissable for tourists drawn by the cobblestone streets, the São Francisco Church gilded interior, the Largo do Pelourinho UNESCO core, the Casa de Jorge Amado, and the Pelourinho Carnival Museum. That same density of camera-ready visitors makes it the city's highest-density pickpocket-team operating zone, with organized 3–5 member crews working the same template flagged in São Paulo's Centro pickpocket reports and Salvador's own 2025 traveller-safety threads. The pattern is heavy enough that a community warning has surfaced about specific tour operators (notably Olafemi Tours) booking Pelourinho walks with insufficient security awareness.

There are nine specific risk zones with predictable mechanics. Pelourinho organized teams target tourists photographing cobblestone streets, São Francisco Church, and Largo do Pelourinho with distraction-theft templates. Mercado Modelo plaza absorbs phone-snatches from outdoor café tables. The Elevador Lacerda art-deco lift connecting Cidade Baixa to Cidade Alta has narrow boarding queues that funnel tourists into team-pickpocket setups. Praça da Sé and the Cathedral approach see 'helpful local' direction-givers paired with bag-watchers. Capoeira-roda crowds get worked at the watching-tourist edge of the circle during performances. The Mercado Modelo artisan-market interior runs distraction-team brush-pasts in the narrow stall aisles. Sunset on Praça da Sé sees phone-snatches with sprinting into the descending Pelourinho streets where chasing is impossible. The 'are you Spanish? confusion' approach is the conversational variant — a stranger asks about a country you didn't claim, while the partner moves in. The Olafemi Tours warning specifically flags walking-tour operators who don't enforce body-positioned-bag posture among guests. Night-time Pelourinho post-19:00 is significantly less safe than daytime, and most documented theft happens in the after-dark colonial-zone narrow streets.

For older travellers visiting Pelourinho, the defense is daytime-only weekday visits with body-positioned bags and Ubers between every leg. Visit Pelourinho daytime only (9 AM–5 PM, weekdays preferred over weekends), wear a zipped crossbody bag in front and keep your phone in a zipped inner pocket between uses, take Uber or 99 between Pelourinho and your hotel rather than walking, avoid Pelourinho after 19:00 unless going directly to a reserved-table restaurant via Uber drop-off, and refuse every 'helpful local' direction approach, every 'are you Spanish?' conversational opener, and every photo-help offer in cobblestone-street density. Bring nothing you can't afford to lose — leave passport, jewellery, and valuable watches in the hotel safe. Use the phone selfie timer or ask security guards / hotel staff for photos rather than handing your phone to a stranger. Book walking tours only through Google 4.5+ operators with 50+ reviews (Cláudio's Walking Tour Pelourinho, Salvador Bahia Tours, Free Walker Tour Salvador are reputable) and avoid Olafemi Tours per the 2025 community warning. DEATUR Salvador +55 71 3116 6817 sits at Praça da Sé inside Pelourinho — file a Boletim de Ocorrência within 24 hours of any incident for insurance and chargeback evidence.

Red Flags

  • Team of 3–5 approaching while you photograph Largo do Pelourinho
  • 'Helpful local' offering directions while watching your bag at Praça da Sé
  • Phone on outdoor café table at Mercado Modelo plaza
  • Phone-snatch sprinting into descending Pelourinho narrow streets
  • Olafemi Tours operator (community-flagged per 1ncgwa4, 2025)

How to Avoid

  • Pelourinho daytime only (9 AM–5 PM weekdays).
  • Leave valuables in hotel safe; money belt + crossbody bag with zipper.
  • Phone in zipped front pocket; never on outdoor café table.
  • Uber/99 BETWEEN Pelourinho and hotel; avoid post-19:00 walking.
  • Reputable tours: Cláudio's Walking Tour, Salvador Bahia Tours, Free Walker.

Scam #4
Salvador Morro de São Paulo / Tinharé Schooner Day-Trip Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Salvador Mercado Modelo / Comércio fast-boat pier (Catamarã do Morro), Pelourinho tour-booking touts, hotel-concierge day-trip desks, Morro de São Paulo arrival pier

Morro de São Paulo (Bahia's flagship car-free island village 60 km south of Salvador) costs R$130–R$220 round-trip via Biotur / Ilha Bela / Cassi Tour catamarãs from Mercado Modelo pier (2 hours each way) plus R$35 island conservation tax — but Pelourinho touts sell 'private speedboat R$800 per person,' hotel concierges bundle 'Morro day-trip + lunch + return R$600' against a R$325–R$415 DIY total, and Olafemi Tours' Morro packages are community-flagged for poor logistics.

Morro de São Paulo is Bahia's signature beach day-trip and overnight escape — a car-free island village 60 kilometres south of Salvador, reached only by sea via two-hour fast-boat from Mercado Modelo / Comércio pier in Salvador. Three reputable catamarã operators with Marinha registration run the route: Biotur, Ilha Bela, and Cassi Tour, with three to four daily departures and round-trip pricing of R$130–R$220 per person. The island village itself is walkable end-to-end in 20 minutes, with four numbered praias (Primeira through Quarta and an outer Quinta Praia) reachable on foot or via beach-hopping mini-schooners from the main pier at R$50–R$100. A R$35 environmental conservation tax is mandatory and legitimate at island arrival; everything above that is a markup target.

The trap menu has eight recurring mechanics. Pelourinho tour-booking touts sell 'private speedboat to Morro R$800 per person' against the legitimate catamarã round-trip at R$130–R$220 — the 'private' speedboat usually doesn't materialise as advertised. Hotel-concierge 'all-inclusive Morro day-trip + lunch + return R$600 per person' against a real DIY of R$325–R$415 (catamarã R$130–R$220 + R$35 tax + R$80 lunch + R$80 schooner). 'VIP exclusive route to Quarta Praia + Quinta Praia R$1,200' charges for stops already on every standard beach-hopping itinerary. 'Mandatory island guide R$200' on Morro is fictional — the village is small enough to walk in 20 minutes and no guide is required anywhere. Unlicensed catamarãs without life jackets or Marinha registration carry real safety risk on the Bahia coast in summer-storm season, and the 'cheap R$80 catamarã today' offer at the pier is the warning sign. The conservation tax gets inflated from R$35 to R$100+ at island arrival by counterfeit booth operators (always pay at the official booth and keep the receipt). Olafemi Tours surfaces here too with poorly logistical Morro packages. The 'Boipeba island add-on R$400' bundles a separate 2-hour boat trip south of Morro into the Salvador-side package when Boipeba's actual day-trip from Morro pier is R$80–R$150.

For older travellers planning Morro de São Paulo, the defense is to book the catamarã directly at the pier and treat the trip as an overnight rather than a same-day return. Book the catamarã direct at the Mercado Modelo / Comércio pier ticket window (Biotur, Ilha Bela, Cassi Tour) at R$130–R$220 round-trip per person, verify life jackets and the Marinha registration sticker before boarding, pay the legitimate R$35 environmental conservation tax at the official island booth on arrival (keep the receipt, refuse any R$100+ demand), book beach-hopping schooners directly at the Morro main pier at R$50–R$100, and refuse every 'private speedboat R$800,' 'all-inclusive day-trip R$600,' 'VIP exclusive route R$1,200,' 'mandatory island guide R$200,' and 'Boipeba add-on R$400' upsell from Salvador-side operators. Overnighting in Morro for one to two nights is much better than a same-day day-trip — same-day return is rushed at four hours of boats plus four to five hours on the island. Reputable Morro pousadas include Pousada Vila do Mar, Anima Hotel, and Pousada Catavento. The village has no cars, so for travellers with mobility concerns the 'tractor taxi' (open-air tractor-pulled cart) is R$10–R$20 to most pousadas. Take Dramamine 30 minutes before catamarã departure — the open ocean can be rough December–February. Avoid Olafemi Tours per the community warning; DEATUR Salvador +55 71 3116 6817 takes incident reports.

Red Flags

  • Pelourinho tout 'private speedboat to Morro R$800/person' (catamarã R$130–R$220)
  • Hotel-concierge 'all-inclusive Morro day-trip R$600/person' (DIY R$325–R$415)
  • 'Environmental conservation tax R$100' at island arrival (real R$35)
  • 'VIP exclusive route to Quarta + Quinta Praia R$1,200' (standard stops)
  • Olafemi Tours operator per 2025 community warning (1ncgwa4)

How to Avoid

  • Book catamarã direct at Mercado Modelo pier: Biotur, Ilha Bela, Cassi R$130–R$220.
  • Verify life jackets + Marinha registration BEFORE boarding.
  • Pay R$35 conservation tax at official island booth; KEEP RECEIPT.
  • Beach-hopping schooner direct from Morro main pier R$50–R$100.
  • OVERNIGHT 1–2 nights in Morro (better than day-trip); Pousada Vila do Mar.
Scam #5
Salvador Pelourinho & Rio Vermelho Restaurant Couvert + Serviço Padding
🔶 Medium
📍 Pelourinho restaurant strip (Largo do Pelourinho, Rua das Laranjeiras), Rio Vermelho bohemian dining strip (Largo da Mariquita), Barra waterfront restaurants, Mercado Modelo upstairs restaurants

Pelourinho cobblestone-strip restaurants in Salvador post USD-only menus at 2–3× local prices (Bahian moqueca de peixe US$35+ vs R$80–R$130 at non-tourist spots), layer R$25–R$80 'couvert artístico' for a single guitarist or 5-minute capoeira show, charge 'serviço 13–15%' against the optional 10% standard, and run 'live samba serenade R$100 per person' surcharges in Rio Vermelho — Brazilian Lei 13.419/2017 makes serviço optional and you can refuse.

Salvador's three main dining strips — the Pelourinho colonial cobblestone, the Rio Vermelho bohemian district, and the Barra waterfront — concentrate the city's tourist-facing restaurant scene and run the standard Brazilian-coastal-city couvert-and-serviço bill-padding pattern flagged in Recife, Maceió, and Fortaleza. Brazilian consumer law (Lei 13.419/2017) makes the 10% serviço charge explicitly optional, INMETRO regulates pricing disclosure, and PROCON Bahia enforces against double-charging — but the disclosure obligation is routinely buried in fine print at tourist-strip venues, especially the Pelourinho cobblestone restaurants pitched at cruise-day visitors and Carnaval-week travellers.

The trap menu has ten recurring mechanics. USD-only menus on the Pelourinho cobblestone strip price Bahian moqueca de peixe at US$35+ when the same dish is R$80–R$130 at non-tourist spots. 'Couvert artístico' is layered at R$25–R$80 per person for a single guitarist or a 5-minute capoeira show when legitimate couvert for a real performer is R$10–R$25 disclosed before seating. 'Serviço 13–15%' replaces the standard optional 10%. 'Taxa de couvert + serviço' double-charging is illegal per PROCON BA but appears regularly. Bottled 'água mineral R$18' replaces the real R$5–R$10. 'Caipirinha de cachaça artesanal premium R$50' is poured from house cachaça at R$18–R$30. 'Lagosta peso variável' market-price lobster at R$800–R$1,400 per kg without disclosed weight is the most expensive single line item. Bill-switcheroo where the printed receipt differs from the card-charge by R$50–R$200 is the closing variant. Mercado Modelo upstairs tourist-restaurants serve touristy adaptations of Bahian classics at 2× working-class prices. Rio Vermelho 'live samba serenade' surcharges land at R$100 per person added during the meal without prior disclosure.

For older travellers eating in Salvador, the defense is to eat at authentic Bahian venues with menus in reais and to know the consumer-protection rules before sitting. Eat at authentic Bahian spots — Restaurante Maria Mata Mouro on Largo do Cruzeiro de São Francisco (Google 4.6+, R$100–R$250), Casa de Tereza in Rio Vermelho (traditional Bahian, R$90–R$200), Mistura in Rio Vermelho (contemporary Bahian, R$120–R$280), Boteco do França on Largo do Pelourinho (casual Bahian, R$60–R$140), or licensed Baiana street vendors for acarajé at R$12–R$25 — and before sitting, photograph the menu, confirm couvert artístico amount per person and serviço policy and credit-card surcharge in writing, refuse every unordered couvert with 'eu não pedi,' agree weight in writing for any 'lagosta peso variável' or fish platter (fair lobster R$400–R$700 per kg), and refuse the 10% serviço if service is poor since Lei 13.419/2017 makes it optional. Order 'água sem gás copo' (free tap water in a glass) rather than the bottled R$18 markup. Caipirinhas should be R$18–R$30 at reputable Bahian spots — anything above R$50 is tourist-trap pricing. Moqueca's fair price at authentic spots is R$80–R$180 per person; R$250+ is the tourist-trap mark. Acarajé from licensed Baiana street vendors (with the cebola painted on the stand) at Rio Vermelho or Largo da Mariquita is the right answer at R$12–R$25 each, not the R$50 'tourist menu' version. Refuse the R$100 'live samba serenade' surcharge if it wasn't disclosed before. PROCON BA can be contacted via 151 or procon.ba.gov.br for any abusive bill.

Red Flags

  • Pelourinho USD-only menu at 2–3× local prices
  • 'Couvert artístico R$25–R$80/person' for single guitarist or 5-min capoeira
  • 'Serviço 13–15%' instead of standard 10% (often padded)
  • 'Taxa de couvert + serviço' double-charging (illegal per PROCON BA)
  • 'Live samba serenade R$100/person' added unannounced during meal

How to Avoid

  • Eat: Maria Mata Mouro, Casa de Tereza, Mistura, Boteco do França.
  • Acarajé da Cira (Rio Vermelho street vendor) R$12–R$25.
  • BEFORE sitting confirm couvert + serviço + card surcharge in writing.
  • 'Serviço' OPTIONAL (Lei 13.419/2017); refuse if poor service.
  • PROCON BA: 151 or procon.ba.gov.br for abusive bills.
Scam #6
Salvador Carnaval Booking Fraud & Camarote / Trio Elétrico Ticket Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Salvador Carnaval circuits (Barra-Ondina, Campo Grande, Pelourinho), Camarote VIP venues, online ticket resellers, hotel-concierge Carnaval-package bookings, WhatsApp 'private apartment' Carnaval rentals

Salvador Carnaval (the world's largest street party with 2.5 million daily participants over 4–7 days in February) drives accommodation and ticket prices 5–10× normal and draws Booking.com cancel-and-relist plays, WhatsApp 'private Barra apartment R$6,000/night' PIX-upfront fraud, R$4,000 'Camarote VIP ticket' resellers (real official prices R$1,500–R$5,000 from Camarote Salvador, Expresso 2222, and Andar do Mar websites), and counterfeit 'Abadá' bloco T-shirts at R$600–R$2,000 against R$400–R$2,500 from each bloco's own site.

Salvador hosts the world's largest Carnaval street party — a four-to-seven-day February event with 2.5 million daily participants spread across three trio elétrico circuits (Barra–Ondina, Campo Grande–Praça Castro Alves, and Pelourinho), running on a scale that drives accommodation rates and Camarote (elevated VIP balcony) tickets five to ten times normal pricing. That scale and price pressure creates exactly the same cancel-and-relist booking fraud, WhatsApp PIX-upfront apartment scam, and counterfeit-ticket reselling pattern that mirrors Rio Carnaval and Recife/Olinda — plus a Salvador-specific 'Abadá' (bloco T-shirt) counterfeit market for the colour-coded shirts that grant access to a specific bloco's roped-off circle on the trio elétrico route.

The trap menu has eight recurring patterns. Booking.com and Airbnb phantom listings use stolen photos of properties that don't exist or are already booked; the traveller pays in advance and arrives to find nothing.
