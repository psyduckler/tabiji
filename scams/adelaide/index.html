6 Tourist Scams in Adelaide

Six Adelaide scams — sourced from traveler reports with real incidents. Taxi overcharge, DiDi cancel-distance trick, fake wine tours, and rental fraud. Know before you go.

By Bernard Huang
Editor, tabiji.ai
📍 Adelaide, Australia 📅 Updated April 2026 💬 6 scams documented ⭐ Reddit-sourced & verified

Key Takeaways

Adelaide is physically one of the safest state capitals in Australia, but three financial scam clusters hit tourists hard: (1) taxi and DiDi/Uber overcharging on short CBD routes (traveler reports 118wkpo calls taxis 'a scam'), (2) fake Barossa / McLaren Vale wine tour bookings from cloned sites, and (3) short-stay rental fraud on Facebook Marketplace. International students attending Adelaide colleges also face dedicated visa/education scams worth knowing about for anyone traveling long-term.

⚡ Quick Safety Tips

  • At Adelaide Airport use Uber or DiDi from the rideshare pickup zone — taxis have been charging inflated short-run fares documented across traveler reports.
  • Check every DiDi receipt for 'driver canceled mid-trip' phantom charges — documented in traveler reports 1r2hxxy.
  • Book Barossa, Clare and McLaren Vale wine tours only with operators on the South Australian Tourism Commission register.
  • Never pay a rental deposit before in-person viewing — traveler reports 1k2rdvc documents current fake-Airbnb invoice scams.
  • Use Adelaide Metro's Metrocard or contactless on trams and buses — never pay 'transport officers' directly.
  • International students should verify any 'visa consultant' against the MARA register at mara.gov.au before paying.

The 6 Scams

Scam #1
Adelaide Taxi Short-Run Overcharge
⚠️ High
📍 Adelaide Airport taxi rank, CBD (King William, Rundle Mall), North Terrace hotel ranks

Adelaide taxi drivers add undisclosed fuel surcharges, airport fees, and tampered meter increments to runs from ADL Airport to the CBD, turning a legitimate $28–$35 fare into a $65–$99 bill — international and interstate visitors who don't know the expected range are the primary targets.

You arrive at Adelaide Airport and follow the taxi sign rather than looking for the rideshare bay. The CBD is only 7 kilometers from the terminal — a short run that should cost $28–$35 by meter. The driver does not quote a price before you get in, and nothing about the process looks unusual until you arrive.

During the ride the meter ticks faster than the legal increment — $0.40 per click instead of the authorized $0.35 or smaller. At drop-off the driver adds a "fuel surcharge," an "airport levy," and sometimes a "late-night rate," none of which were displayed on the in-cab rate card before the trip. The total comes to $65, $80, or as high as $99 for a 15-minute run. Cash-only demands when the card reader "breaks" add a second layer — no receipt means no complaint trail.

Use Uber or DiDi from the Adelaide Airport rideshare bay — CBD fares run $25–$40 with digital receipts and no opportunity for undisclosed surcharges. The JetExpress buses (J1, J2, J7, J8) run airport to CBD for $5.40 with Metrocard, the cleanest option of all. If you take a taxi, photograph the driver ID and plate before the car moves, insist on the meter, and pay by card on the in-car terminal — you need a receipt to report overcharging to the South Australian Public Transport Authority at adelaidemetro.com.au.

Red Flags

  • Meter clicks up faster than the legal rate of $0.35 or smaller increments
  • 'Fuel surcharge' or 'airport surcharge' not displayed inside the cab
  • 'Card reader is broken' cash-only demand
  • Flat rate quoted above $55 for a CBD run from ADL
  • Driver takes circuitous route via West Terrace or Port Road

How to Avoid

  • Use Uber or DiDi from Adelaide Airport rideshare pickup — fare $25–$40 to CBD.
  • Take JetExpress bus (J1/J2) to CBD — $5.40 with Metrocard.
  • Pay by card on the in-car terminal and screenshot the receipt.
  • Photograph taxi plate before the ride.
  • Report overcharging via adelaidemetro.com.au.
Scam #2
DiDi / Uber 'Cancel Mid-Trip' Charge Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Adelaide CBD, DiDi and Uber platform

DiDi drivers in Adelaide's CBD pull up to the pickup point, move the car 100 meters down the road, then end the ride in the app — collecting a $15 "part-trip fare" or cancellation fee while the passenger is left standing at the original location with no transport.

You book a DiDi from Rundle Mall to the Adelaide Oval for a $9 fare. The driver accepts the trip, the app shows them nearby, and they pull up to your location. You approach the car. The driver waits about 30 seconds while you walk the last few meters or look for the right vehicle among others on the street.

The driver moves the car 100 meters down the road and ends the trip in the app before you are inside. Your card is charged $15 immediately — a combination of part-trip fare and cancellation fee that the platform structures to compensate drivers for genuinely interrupted pickups, which scammers exploit by pretending a pickup happened and ending the ride. A variant involves the driver calling to claim an "app issue" and asking for cash directly — a signal to cancel the trip yourself and rebook with a different driver.

Screenshot the in-app map the moment you realize a trip has been ended early, then immediately request a refund in-app with the screenshot attached — the GPS record shows the car never reached your location. DiDi's support is slower than Uber's; if the platform denies the refund, escalate via credit-card chargeback. Never cancel at the driver's request — cancelling shifts the fee onto you and removes your refund leverage.

Red Flags

  • Driver sits at pickup spot then 'moves' the car 100–500m and ends the trip
  • Receipt shows a fare disproportionate to the trip distance (e.g. $15 for 100m)
  • Driver claims 'app issue' and wants cash or direct bank payment
  • Multiple cancel-charges on your account from different drivers
  • Driver calls asking you to 'cancel for me' so they get a cancellation fee

How to Avoid

  • Never cancel at the driver's request — they're seeking a cancellation fee.
  • Screenshot the in-app map the moment you realize the ride is being ended early.
  • Request an in-app refund with the screenshot attached.
  • Chargeback on your credit card if the platform denies the refund.
  • Prefer Uber over DiDi in Adelaide for cleaner customer service.
Scam #3
Fake Barossa / McLaren Vale Wine Tour Booking
🔶 Medium
📍 Online via Google Ads, Facebook Ads, cloned tour booking sites

Cloned tour booking sites and Facebook ads target Barossa and McLaren Vale searches with full-day wine tours priced at $79–$99, then request bank transfer "to avoid card fees" — on tour day no vehicle arrives, the phone number disconnects, and the website vanishes within 24 hours.

You search "Barossa wine tour from Adelaide" and click one of the top Google ad results. The site is polished: hotel pickup, four named wineries, lunch included, $79 per person. The reviews look good and the price is below what established operators charge. The booking form requests bank transfer or PayPal Friends & Family "to avoid platform fees" — a concession that sounds plausible for a small local operator.

You transfer $79, $158, or more depending on your party size. A confirmation email arrives with Barossa branding and a plausible itinerary. On tour morning, no minibus appears at the hotel. You call — it disconnects. You email — no reply. Cloned sites using Google Ads have been documented outcompeting legitimate Barossa operators in search results for competitive query terms, making the fake sites hard to distinguish from real ones without checking the ABN.

Book with Groovy Grape Tours, Taste the Barossa, Barossa Daimler, or Adelaide Wine Tours — all have Adelaide offices and verifiable ABNs at abn.business.gov.au — and pay by credit card for chargeback protection. Refusal to accept credit card is the scam tell: no legitimate Australian tour operator declines cards. Read the 50 most recent TripAdvisor reviews and check that dates are spread across months rather than clustered, which signals fake review generation.

Red Flags

  • Price is 30–40% below comparable Barossa full-day tours with lunch
  • Operator asks for bank transfer or PayPal F&F to 'avoid card fees'
  • Website has no physical Adelaide office address or ABN
  • Confirmation email doesn't name specific wineries on the itinerary
  • Google ad leads to a site with.com.co domain (not.com.au)

How to Avoid

  • Book with Groovy Grape, Taste the Barossa, Barossa Daimler or Adelaide Wine Tours.
  • Verify ABN at abn.business.gov.au.
  • Pay by credit card for chargeback protection.
  • Read 50 most recent TripAdvisor reviews — fake operators have clumped review dates.
  • Confirm exact winery names in the itinerary before paying.
Scam #4
Adelaide Fake Rental / Facebook Airbnb Invoice Scam
⚠️ High
📍 CBD, North Adelaide, Unley, Prospect — short-stay and sharehouse market

Scammers on Facebook Marketplace send forged Airbnb-branded PDF invoices for Adelaide short-stays and request bank transfer — Airbnb has no record of the booking, and the "host" disappears with the full $840 or more the moment the transfer clears.

You find a one-bedroom in North Adelaide on Facebook Marketplace for $120 per night — below market and well-photographed. The host responds quickly, seems friendly, and explains they manage the listing "outside Airbnb to save on fees." They send a PDF invoice with Airbnb's logo, a booking reference number, and a nightly rate plus service fee breakdown. The email uses Airbnb fonts and colors closely enough that most people do not question it.

You transfer the full amount — $840 for a week, or more. There is no record of the booking in the Airbnb app because the booking was never made through Airbnb. Airbnb uses only in-app payments and never sends PDF invoices requesting external bank transfers; the "invoice" is a fabricated document. As soon as the transfer clears, the host goes offline. Recovering a domestic bank transfer from a scammer depends on how quickly your bank acts and whether the destination account is still active.

A real Airbnb booking is confirmed entirely within the Airbnb app and never involves an external PDF invoice or bank transfer — any "Airbnb invoice" arriving by email requesting outside payment is a scam without exception. Book only through the Airbnb app or a bookmarked Airbnb URL. If you have already transferred money, call your bank within 24 hours and request a recall. Report the listing to both Airbnb and Facebook Marketplace immediately.

Red Flags

  • 'Airbnb invoice' received as PDF via email rather than inside the Airbnb app
  • Host requests bank transfer, Wise, or crypto instead of in-app payment
  • Listing is on Facebook Marketplace or Gumtree with photos that look too professional
  • Price is 30–50% below comparable Adelaide short-stays
  • Host refuses a 1-minute video call to show the property live

How to Avoid

  • Book only through the Airbnb or Booking.com app — never from an email link.
  • A real Airbnb payment runs entirely in-app; external invoices are always fake.
  • Never pay by bank transfer outside the platform.
  • Reverse image-search listing photos in Google Images.
  • Report scam listings to Airbnb and Facebook Marketplace immediately.
Scam #5
International Student Visa / Education Agent Fraud
⚠️ High
📍 Online / WeChat / WhatsApp ads targeting South Asian and Southeast Asian students

Fake migration agents in Adelaide advertise $8,000–$13,000 PR or student-visa "pathways" via WeChat and Facebook, enrol victims in unaccredited or non-existent colleges, and take cash upfront — the visa pathway does not exist and the money is unrecoverable.

You are a prospective international student or a recent graduate on a temporary visa looking for a PR pathway. A "migration agent" appears in a South Asian or Southeast Asian community group on WeChat or Facebook: "Guaranteed PR pathway via Adelaide college — fast processing, limited spots." The price is $8,000–$13,000 and the agent claims professional credentials. Adelaide's lower-cost international education market has attracted a disproportionate concentration of these schemes compared to Sydney and Melbourne.

The agent takes cash or an untraced transfer upfront. They enrol you in a college that either does not exist, is not registered on the CRICOS database, or is registered but has no pathway to the visa subclass the agent described. The "guaranteed PR" promise is fraudulent in all cases — no legitimate migration agent can legally guarantee visa outcomes. When the visa application fails or the college turns out to be a shell, the agent is unreachable and the cash is gone.

Verify any migration agent against the MARA register at mara.gov.au and any institution against CRICOS at cricos.education.gov.au before paying anything — both are public, searchable, and free, and any agent or college not listed is operating outside the Australian regulatory framework. Never pay cash or untraceable transfers for visa services; pay only by card or bank transfer to a registered ABN with a receipt. Any "guaranteed PR" promise from any agent is an illegal misrepresentation under Australian migration law.

Red Flags

  • Agent promises 'guaranteed PR' or '100% visa approval' impossible
  • Payment requested in cash with no receipt
  • Institution isn't on the CRICOS register (cricos.education.gov.au)
  • Agent has no MARA Migration Agents Registration Authority number
  • Recruitment is via WeChat, WhatsApp, or Facebook without a physical Adelaide office

How to Avoid

  • Use only MARA-registered agents — check mara.gov.au public register.
  • Enrol only in CRICOS-registered institutions — verify at cricos.education.gov.au.
  • Pay by card or bank transfer with receipt — never cash.
  • Verify agent's ABN at abn.business.gov.au.
  • Report suspected fraud to the Department of Home Affairs at immi.gov.au.
Scam #6
Fake 'SA Tourism Voucher' / Booking Voucher Scam
🟡 Low
📍 Email / SMS / Facebook ads claiming South Australian government tourism vouchers

Phishing emails and SMS messages impersonating the South Australian Tourism Commission offer $100–$150 hotel vouchers, then harvest card data on a cloned payment page — no voucher materializes and small test transactions begin appearing on the compromised card within hours.

An email or SMS arrives claiming to be from the "South Australian Tourism Commission": "You've received a $100 winter hotel voucher — valid for 48 hours." The message has SA Tourism branding, a professional layout, and an urgent expiry countdown. The legitimate SA Tourism Commission has run real voucher programs in the past — the Great State Voucher scheme during 2020–2022 was genuine — which gives the scam just enough plausibility to generate clicks from people who remember hearing about it.

Clicking the link opens a site that looks like an official SA government page. It asks for name, email, and card details to "verify and activate your voucher." You enter your card. The voucher never arrives. Small test transactions of $1–$5 begin appearing on your card within hours as the scammers verify the card number is live before using it for larger purchases or selling it. The scam is volume-based — small per-victim amounts generate large aggregated returns across thousands of phishing sends.

Never click a voucher link in an unsolicited email or SMS — go directly to southaustralia.com or tourism.sa.gov.au to check whether any current government voucher program exists. Real SA government programs are listed on these official sites before appearing anywhere else. If you have already entered card details on a suspicious site, call your bank immediately and request a card reissue. Report phishing to ScamWatch at scamwatch.gov.au.

Red Flags

  • Unsolicited email or SMS claiming an SA government tourism voucher
  • Link goes to a site that isn't southaustralia.com or tourism.sa.gov.au
  • Voucher 'verification' requires your card details
  • Urgency — 'voucher expires in 24 hours'
  • Sender domain is similar to but not exactly an SA government domain

How to Avoid

  • Verify any voucher program directly at southaustralia.com or tourism.sa.gov.au.
  • Never click voucher links in unsolicited emails.
  • Book hotels via Booking.com, Stayz, or calling hotels directly.
  • If you've entered card details into a suspect site, reissue your card.
  • Report phishing to ScamWatch at scamwatch.gov.au.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest South Australia Police station. Call 000 (emergency) or 131 444 (non-emergency). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at police.sa.gov.au.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The closest US Consulate is in Melbourne: 553 St Kilda Road, Melbourne VIC 3004 (+61 3-9526-5900). The UK High Commission is in Canberra (+61 2-6270-6666). Report scams to Consumer and Business Services SA or ScamWatch at scamwatch.gov.au.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
