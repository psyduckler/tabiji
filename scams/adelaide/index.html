6 Tourist Scams in Adelaide

Six Adelaide scams — sourced from traveler reports with real incidents. Taxi overcharge, DiDi cancel-distance trick, fake wine tours, and rental fraud. Know before you go.

By Bernard Huang
Editor, tabiji.ai
📍 Adelaide, Australia 📅 Updated April 2026 💬 6 scams documented ⭐ Reddit-sourced & verified

Key Takeaways

Adelaide is physically one of the safest state capitals in Australia, but three financial scam clusters hit tourists hard: (1) taxi and DiDi/Uber overcharging on short CBD routes (traveler reports 118wkpo calls taxis 'a scam'), (2) fake Barossa / McLaren Vale wine tour bookings from cloned sites, and (3) short-stay rental fraud on Facebook Marketplace. International students attending Adelaide colleges also face dedicated visa/education scams worth knowing about for anyone traveling long-term.

⚡ Quick Safety Tips

  • At Adelaide Airport use Uber or DiDi from the rideshare pickup zone — taxis have been charging inflated short-run fares documented across traveler reports.
  • Check every DiDi receipt for 'driver canceled mid-trip' phantom charges — documented in traveler reports 1r2hxxy.
  • Book Barossa, Clare and McLaren Vale wine tours only with operators on the South Australian Tourism Commission register.
  • Never pay a rental deposit before in-person viewing — traveler reports 1k2rdvc documents current fake-Airbnb invoice scams.
  • Use Adelaide Metro's Metrocard or contactless on trams and buses — never pay 'transport officers' directly.
  • International students should verify any 'visa consultant' against the MARA register at mara.gov.au before paying.

The 6 Scams

Scam #1
Adelaide Taxi Short-Run Overcharge
⚠️ High
📍 Adelaide Airport taxi rank, CBD (King William, Rundle Mall), North Terrace hotel ranks

Adelaide taxis targeting tourists at Adelaide Airport and CBD hotel ranks inflate fares with undisclosed fuel surcharges, airport fees, and meters clicking in $0.40 increments instead of the legal $0.35 — turning a $28 rideshare-quoted trip to North Terrace hotels into a demand for $65 or more at drop-off.

You land at Adelaide Airport and join the taxi rank for the 7-km trip to your North Terrace hotel. The driver loads your bags, the meter starts, and the journey appears normal. You are tired after the flight and not watching the fare display closely.

At drop-off the driver quotes $65. The meter advanced in $0.40 increments rather than the legal $0.35 steps. The total includes an 'airport surcharge' not displayed inside the cab and a fuel levy not disclosed before you got in. Uber quoted $28 for the same route. The 13CABS yellow taxi fleet is the most frequently documented offender in traveler complaints, alongside independent operators at hotel ranks in the CBD.

Use Uber or DiDi from the Adelaide Airport rideshare bay — typical CBD fare is $25–$40 — or take the JetExpress bus (J1 or J2) to the CBD for $5.40 with a Metrocard, which is the most scam-proof option available. If you do take a taxi, watch the meter for $0.40 increments and insist the driver accept card payment via the in-car terminal. Photograph the taxi plate and driver ID on boarding in case you need to file a complaint with the South Australian Public Transport Authority.

Red Flags

  • Meter clicks up faster than the legal rate of $0.35 or smaller increments
  • 'Fuel surcharge' or 'airport surcharge' not displayed inside the cab
  • 'Card reader is broken' cash-only demand
  • Flat rate quoted above $55 for a CBD run from ADL
  • Driver takes circuitous route via West Terrace or Port Road

How to Avoid

  • Use Uber or DiDi from Adelaide Airport rideshare pickup — fare $25–$40 to CBD.
  • Take JetExpress bus (J1/J2) to CBD — $5.40 with Metrocard.
  • Pay by card on the in-car terminal and screenshot the receipt.
  • Photograph taxi plate before the ride.
  • Report overcharging via adelaidemetro.com.au.
Scam #2
DiDi / Uber 'Cancel Mid-Trip' Charge Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Adelaide CBD, DiDi and Uber platform

DiDi drivers in Adelaide CBD pull up to a booked pickup, wait briefly, then drive 100 meters and end the trip in the app — collecting a 'part-trip fare' of $15 or more for what should have been a $9 ride — exploiting the platform's cancellation-fee structure designed to compensate drivers for genuinely interrupted trips.

You book a DiDi from Rundle Mall to the Adelaide Oval for a $9 fare. The app confirms a driver is two minutes away. The car arrives near the pickup marker and you walk toward it. The driver appears to acknowledge the booking through the app.

Without you boarding, the driver moves the car 100 meters down the road and ends the trip in the app. Your card is charged $15 for a 'part-trip fare' and 'cancellation fee.' The driver collects payment for a journey that never happened and accepts the next booking immediately. In a related variant, the driver calls and claims an app issue, requesting you pay cash directly — a sign to bail and rebook with another driver rather than comply.

Screenshot the in-app ride map the moment you realize the trip has been ended early, then submit a refund request in-app with the screenshot attached — do not cancel the ride yourself, as that triggers a fee that benefits the driver. If the platform denies the refund, initiate a chargeback through your credit card. Prefer Uber over DiDi in the Adelaide CBD — Uber's customer service resolves these disputes more reliably.

Red Flags

  • Driver sits at pickup spot then 'moves' the car 100–500m and ends the trip
  • Receipt shows a fare disproportionate to the trip distance (e.g. $15 for 100m)
  • Driver claims 'app issue' and wants cash or direct bank payment
  • Multiple cancel-charges on your account from different drivers
  • Driver calls asking you to 'cancel for me' so they get a cancellation fee

How to Avoid

  • Never cancel at the driver's request — they're seeking a cancellation fee.
  • Screenshot the in-app map the moment you realize the ride is being ended early.
  • Request an in-app refund with the screenshot attached.
  • Chargeback on your credit card if the platform denies the refund.
  • Prefer Uber over DiDi in Adelaide for cleaner customer service.
Scam #3
Fake Barossa / McLaren Vale Wine Tour Booking
🔶 Medium
📍 Online via Google Ads, Facebook Ads, cloned tour booking sites

Fake tour operators using Google Ads and cloned booking sites offer full-day Barossa or McLaren Vale wine tours from Adelaide for $79 — 30–40% below legitimate market prices — then request payment by bank transfer, deliver no tour on the day, and disconnect their phone number, leaving victims with no chargeback route since bank-transfer funds are unrecoverable.

You search 'Barossa wine tour from Adelaide' and a polished website appears near the top of the results via a Google ad. The site offers a full-day tour for $79 per person — pickup from your hotel, four wineries, lunch included. The photos look authentic and the testimonials are glowing. You begin the booking process.

At checkout the operator requests payment by bank transfer to 'avoid card processing fees,' promising a confirmation once funds clear. A confirmation email arrives with Barossa Valley imagery but no specific winery names on the itinerary. On tour day, no minibus appears. The phone number is disconnected and the website redirects to a blank page. Bank-transfer funds cannot be recalled without the recipient's cooperation, and the sender is usually overseas or untraceable.

Book only with verified Barossa and McLaren Vale operators who have a physical Adelaide office, a listed ABN at abn.business.gov.au, and verifiable TripAdvisor reviews — and pay by credit card for chargeback protection, never bank transfer. Legitimate operators include Groovy Grape Tours, Taste the Barossa, and Barossa Daimler. Any tour priced 30–40% below the market rate with a bank-transfer payment instruction is a fake booking.

Red Flags

  • Price is 30–40% below comparable Barossa full-day tours with lunch
  • Operator asks for bank transfer or PayPal F&F to 'avoid card fees'
  • Website has no physical Adelaide office address or ABN
  • Confirmation email doesn't name specific wineries on the itinerary
  • Google ad leads to a site with.com.co domain (not.com.au)

How to Avoid

  • Book with Groovy Grape, Taste the Barossa, Barossa Daimler or Adelaide Wine Tours.
  • Verify ABN at abn.business.gov.au.
  • Pay by credit card for chargeback protection.
  • Read 50 most recent TripAdvisor reviews — fake operators have clumped review dates.
  • Confirm exact winery names in the itinerary before paying.
Scam #4
Adelaide Fake Rental / Facebook Airbnb Invoice Scam
⚠️ High
📍 CBD, North Adelaide, Unley, Prospect — short-stay and sharehouse market

Scammers on Facebook Marketplace advertise Adelaide short-stay rentals at 30–50% below market, then send a fake Airbnb-branded PDF invoice and request payment by bank transfer — collecting $840 or more before the victim discovers that Airbnb has no record of the booking and the funds are unrecoverable.

You find a one-bedroom apartment in North Adelaide on Facebook Marketplace for $120 per night — a reasonable price. The host responds quickly, the photos look professional, and they appear genuinely helpful. They say they prefer to process the booking through Airbnb and will send you an invoice to complete the reservation.

A PDF arrives by email with realistic Airbnb branding — the correct logo, color scheme, and layout. The document requests a bank transfer of $840 to confirm the booking. You pay and receive a 'confirmation.' When you log into the Airbnb app ahead of your arrival date, the booking does not exist. The host's messages stop. Airbnb's support team confirms no record of the property or the transaction.

A genuine Airbnb booking exists entirely inside the Airbnb app — any PDF invoice sent by email requesting a bank transfer is a scam, regardless of how convincing the branding looks. Book only through the official Airbnb app or website and pay through the in-app payment system. If you have already transferred money, call your bank within 24 hours to request a recall and report the listing to both Facebook Marketplace and Airbnb.

Red Flags

  • 'Airbnb invoice' received as PDF via email rather than inside the Airbnb app
  • Host requests bank transfer, Wise, or crypto instead of in-app payment
  • Listing is on Facebook Marketplace or Gumtree with photos that look too professional
  • Price is 30–50% below comparable Adelaide short-stays
  • Host refuses a 1-minute video call to show the property live

How to Avoid

  • Book only through the Airbnb or Booking.com app — never from an email link.
  • A real Airbnb payment runs entirely in-app; external invoices are always fake.
  • Never pay by bank transfer outside the platform.
  • Reverse image-search listing photos in Google Images.
  • Report scam listings to Airbnb and Facebook Marketplace immediately.
Scam #5
International Student Visa / Education Agent Fraud
⚠️ High
📍 Online / WeChat / WhatsApp ads targeting South Asian and Southeast Asian students

Unregistered migration agents in Adelaide — often recruiting via WeChat and WhatsApp — charge prospective international students $8,000–$13,000 upfront for visa pathways or permanent residency sponsorship through fake or unaccredited colleges, then enroll victims in courses that generate no valid immigration outcome and disappear once payment is received.

You are a prospective international student or a recent graduate in Adelaide on a temporary visa. A migration agent advertises on social media or through a community contact: $8,000 for a student visa pathway, $13,000 for a permanent residency sponsorship through an Adelaide-based college. The agent has a professional-looking website and testimonials in your language.

The agent requests cash payment, explaining that card transactions create a paper trail that complicates visa applications. You pay. The enrollment confirmation arrives for a college that is unaccredited, absent from the CRICOS register, or entirely fictitious. The immigration pathway described does not exist under Australian law. Months later, your application is refused, the agent is unreachable, and the college no longer responds. Adelaide's lower-cost international education market has attracted a concentrated cluster of these operations targeting South Asian and Southeast Asian students.

Only use MARA-registered migration agents — check the public register at mara.gov.au — and only enroll in CRICOS-registered institutions, verified at cricos.education.gov.au. Pay by credit card or traceable bank transfer with a receipt to an entity with a verifiable ABN; never pay cash. Any agent who promises 'guaranteed PR' or '100% visa approval' is lying — no legitimate agent can guarantee immigration outcomes. Report suspected fraud to the Department of Home Affairs at immi.gov.au.

Red Flags

  • Agent promises 'guaranteed PR' or '100% visa approval' impossible
  • Payment requested in cash with no receipt
  • Institution isn't on the CRICOS register (cricos.education.gov.au)
  • Agent has no MARA Migration Agents Registration Authority number
  • Recruitment is via WeChat, WhatsApp, or Facebook without a physical Adelaide office

How to Avoid

  • Use only MARA-registered agents — check mara.gov.au public register.
  • Enrol only in CRICOS-registered institutions — verify at cricos.education.gov.au.
  • Pay by card or bank transfer with receipt — never cash.
  • Verify agent's ABN at abn.business.gov.au.
  • Report suspected fraud to the Department of Home Affairs at immi.gov.au.
Scam #6
Fake 'SA Tourism Voucher' / Booking Voucher Scam
🟡 Low
📍 Email / SMS / Facebook ads claiming South Australian government tourism vouchers

Phishing emails and SMS messages impersonating the South Australian Tourism Commission offer $100 hotel vouchers — cloning the branding of real government programs like the Great State Voucher — then direct victims to fake booking sites that harvest card details and run small unauthorized test transactions before larger charges follow.

You receive an email claiming a 'South Australian Tourism Commission' voucher: $100 off any hotel stay this winter. The email has the SA government's logo, color scheme, and official tone. A link takes you to a site that looks legitimate, with address fields pre-filled with your name. It asks for your card details to 'verify your eligibility' before the voucher activates.

You enter your card information. The voucher confirmation never arrives. Instead, small test transactions of $1 or $2 appear on your statement within hours, followed by larger charges days later once monitoring has relaxed. The scam works because South Australia ran a legitimate Great State Voucher program during 2020–2022 tourism recovery, giving fake emails plausible context. Phishing sites use domains similar to — but not exactly matching — the official SA government domain.

Never click a voucher link in an unsolicited email or SMS — verify any SA government voucher program by navigating directly to southaustralia.com or tourism.sa.gov.au yourself rather than following a link. Legitimate government incentives never require card details for 'verification.' If you have already entered card details on a suspicious site, call your bank immediately and request a card reissue. Report phishing attempts to ScamWatch at scamwatch.gov.au.

Red Flags

  • Unsolicited email or SMS claiming an SA government tourism voucher
  • Link goes to a site that isn't southaustralia.com or tourism.sa.gov.au
  • Voucher 'verification' requires your card details
  • Urgency — 'voucher expires in 24 hours'
  • Sender domain is similar to but not exactly an SA government domain

How to Avoid

  • Verify any voucher program directly at southaustralia.com or tourism.sa.gov.au.
  • Never click voucher links in unsolicited emails.
  • Book hotels via Booking.com, Stayz, or calling hotels directly.
  • If you've entered card details into a suspect site, reissue your card.
  • Report phishing to ScamWatch at scamwatch.gov.au.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest South Australia Police station. Call 000 (emergency) or 131 444 (non-emergency). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at police.sa.gov.au.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The closest US Consulate is in Melbourne: 553 St Kilda Road, Melbourne VIC 3004 (+61 3-9526-5900). The UK High Commission is in Canberra (+61 2-6270-6666). Report scams to Consumer and Business Services SA or ScamWatch at scamwatch.gov.au.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
