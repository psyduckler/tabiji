The 10 Scams

Scam #1
Lakeside Pickpocketing
🔶 Medium
📍 Lake Annecy promenade, Palais de l'Ile, Old Town (Vieille Ville), popular markets

Pickpockets operate around Lake Annecy, the Palais de l'Ile, and other popular tourist spots.

They often work in pairs or groups, using distraction techniques while an accomplice rifles through bags and pockets. Crowded lakefront promenades during summer are particularly targeted.

Red Flags

  • Groups of people crowding you
  • Someone creating a distraction
  • People watching you take out valuables
  • Bump and grab tactics

How to Avoid

  • Use a cross-body bag with zipped compartments.
  • Keep valuables in front pockets or inside jacket pockets.
  • Stay alert in crowded areas.
  • Never leave bags on chairs or the ground in restaurants.
Scam #2
Beach Belongings Theft
🔶 Medium
📍 Plage d'Albigny, Plage des Marquisats, Plage de Talloires, all lakeside beaches

Thieves patrol the beaches around Lake Annecy watching for swimmers who leave belongings unattended on the shore.

They wait until targets are in the water or facing away, then quickly grab valuables and disappear. Summer months see increased incidents at popular swimming spots.

Red Flags

  • Lone individuals watching beach-goers rather than swimming
  • People walking slowly near unattended bags
  • Someone asking you questions while you're away from belongings

How to Avoid

  • Never bring valuables to the beach.
  • Leave jewelry and passports at your hotel.
  • Use a waterproof pouch for essentials.
  • Take turns swimming with companions so someone watches belongings.
Scam #3
Dual Menu Restaurant Scam
🟢 Low
📍 Old Town restaurants near Palais de l'Ile, lakefront terraces, areas near major attractions

Some restaurants in tourist areas maintain two sets of menus - one for locals with standard prices and another for tourists with inflated prices. Additionally, menus may lack prices entirely, only revealing exorbitant costs when the bill arrives. Hidden charges for bread, water, or cover fees may appear.

Red Flags

  • Menus without prices displayed
  • Different menus given to different tables
  • Aggressive steering toward expensive items
  • Hidden 'service' or 'cover' charges

How to Avoid

  • French law requires restaurants to display menus with prices outside.
  • Ask for a menu with prices before ordering.
  • Walk away from restaurants without posted prices.
  • Verify the bill matches menu prices.

Scam #4
Fake Petition and Charity Clipboard Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Old Town pedestrian areas, near major attractions, market areas, lakefront promenade

Scammers approach tourists with clipboards asking them to sign petitions for supposed charitable causes.

After signing, they demand donations of 10-20 euros. The clipboard distracts victims while accomplices pickpocket bags. The petitions feature photocopied logos from legitimate charities but are entirely fraudulent.

Red Flags

  • Clipboard petitioners in English rather than French
  • Pressure for immediate cash donations
  • Multiple people working the same area
  • Someone standing too close behind you

How to Avoid

  • Never stop for anyone with a clipboard.
  • Say 'Non merci' firmly and keep walking.
  • Keep hands on your belongings if approached.
  • Legitimate charities do not solicit on streets with petitions.
Scam #5
The Gold Ring Trick
🟢 Low
📍 Pedestrian areas, lakefront, near tourist attractions

A scammer 'finds' a gold ring on the ground near a tourist and asks if they dropped it.

When the tourist says no, the scammer offers to sell it as a 'lucky find' for a significant sum, claiming it is valuable gold. The ring is worthless metal. This scam, common in Paris, occasionally appears in smaller French tourist towns.

Red Flags

  • Someone dramatically 'finding' jewelry near you
  • Ring looks too shiny for something found on ground
  • Sob story follows the 'discovery'
  • Request for money as a reward

How to Avoid

  • Never engage with anyone claiming to find valuables near you.
  • Say 'Non' and walk away immediately.
  • Do not touch or examine anything offered.
Scam #6
Friendship Bracelet Scam
🟢 Low
📍 Pedestrian zones, Old Town, near popular photo spots

Scammers aggressively approach tourists and begin tying a 'friendship bracelet' onto their wrist without permission.

Once the bracelet is tied, they demand payment, often 10-20 euros or more, becoming aggressive if refused. While attempting to remove the bracelet, accomplices may pickpocket the distracted victim.

Red Flags

  • Someone approaching with string or yarn
  • Attempts to touch your hand or wrist
  • Overly friendly strangers offering free gifts
  • Groups working together

How to Avoid

  • Keep your hands in pockets or crossed when walking.
  • Never let strangers touch your hands or wrists.
  • Walk away immediately saying 'Non merci' firmly.
Scam #7
Rental Car Break-ins
⚠️ High
📍 Trailhead parking areas, scenic viewpoints around the lake, unstaffed parking lots, street parking

Thieves target rental cars which are easy to identify by license plates, lack of floor mats, and sterile interiors.

They smash windows to steal luggage, electronics, and valuables, sometimes dropping rear seats to access trunks. Parking areas near trailheads and scenic viewpoints are frequently targeted.

Red Flags

  • Parking areas with broken glass on ground
  • Isolated parking with no surveillance
  • Anyone watching you load or unload your car

How to Avoid

  • Never leave any valuables visible in your car.
  • Remove all items including charging cables.
  • Use staffed parking lots when available.
  • Consider sedan rentals with proper trunks rather than hatchbacks.
Scam #8
Vacation Rental Fraud
⚠️ High
📍 Online - affects pre-trip booking for Annecy accommodations

Scammers create fake vacation rental listings for properties in Annecy that either do not exist or belong to someone else. Victims pay deposits or full amounts via bank transfer only to arrive and find no accommodation. Some scammers create sophisticated fake booking websites mimicking legitimate platforms.

Red Flags

  • Prices significantly below market rate
  • Requests to pay outside official platforms
  • Poor communication or urgency to book
  • Empty booking calendars during peak season

How to Avoid

  • Only book through official platforms like Airbnb or Booking.com.
  • Never pay via bank transfer or cryptocurrency.
  • Read reviews carefully.
  • Verify property addresses on Google Maps.
Scam #9
ATM Skimming and Distraction
⚠️ High
📍 Standalone ATMs, tourist area cash machines, train station ticket machines

Criminals install skimming devices on ATMs to capture card data and PINs.

Others pose as helpful locals at ATMs or ticket machines, offering assistance while memorizing PIN codes or switching cards. An international ring was busted in 2025 after stealing over 580,000 euros across Western Europe.

Red Flags

  • Loose or unusual attachments on card slots
  • Strangers offering to help at ATMs
  • Someone standing too close behind you
  • Card getting stuck in machine

How to Avoid

  • Use ATMs inside banks during business hours.
  • Cover the keypad completely when entering PINs.
  • Decline help from strangers at ATMs.
  • Check card slots for loose attachments before inserting cards.
Scam #10
Distraction Theft by Groups
🔶 Medium
📍 Old Town, lakefront, popular photo spots, market areas, near the canal

Thieves work in organized groups using distraction techniques.

One person creates a diversion - asking for directions, showing a map, dropping items, or staging an argument - while accomplices pickpocket the distracted tourist. They particularly target visitors taking photos or consulting phones and maps.

Red Flags

  • Multiple people approaching simultaneously
  • Someone creating any kind of scene near you
  • Person asking for help while others stand nearby
  • Maps or items thrust in front of you

How to Avoid

  • Be immediately suspicious if approached by strangers, especially in groups.
  • Secure your belongings first if someone creates a scene.
  • Keep your phone and wallet in front pockets or secured pouches.
  • Travel with a companion who can watch your back.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Police Nationale / SAMU station. Call 17 (Police) or 15 (SAMU medical). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at pre-plainte-en-ligne.interieur.gouv.fr.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Paris is at 2 Avenue Gabriel, 75008 Paris. For emergencies: +33 1 43-12-22-22.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
