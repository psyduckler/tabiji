The 4 Scams

Scam #1
The Adventure Photo Upsell
🟢 Low
📍 Paragliding and skydiving operators along Hohematte park and Hauptstrasse

You book a tandem paragliding flight over the Swiss Alps for 170 CHF -- the listed price on the operator's website.

The flight is breathtaking and your pilot takes photos and video the entire time. But back on the ground, you're told the photo and video package is an additional 60 to 100 CHF, and without it, you get nothing. Your pilot has already uploaded the best shots to their device, and there's no option to use your own camera during the flight. Swiss travel forums and TripAdvisor reviews note that some Interlaken adventure operators deliberately keep photo pricing vague during booking so the post-flight upsell catches you when you're still buzzing with adrenaline.

Red Flags

  • The activity price is quoted without mentioning photo and video costs separately
  • You're told you cannot bring your own GoPro or camera for 'safety reasons'
  • The photo package price is only revealed after the activity is complete
  • The pilot takes extensive photos during the activity, creating expectation that they're included
  • The operator's website shows stunning photos but doesn't clarify whether media is included

How to Avoid

  • Ask specifically about photo and video package costs before booking any adventure activity.
  • Some operators include media in the price -- compare total costs including photos across companies.
  • Ask in advance whether you can bring and mount your own GoPro or action camera.
  • Book through established operators like Paragliding Interlaken or Skywings that clearly list all fees.
  • Budget an extra 40-80 CHF for media when planning adventure activities in Interlaken.
Scam #2
The Souvenir Shop Tourist Tax
🟢 Low
📍 Souvenir shops along Hoheweg and near Interlaken Ost station

You pop into a souvenir shop on Hoheweg to buy a Swiss army knife and some chocolate for gifts back home.

The prices aren't displayed on the items. The shopkeeper quotes 65 CHF for a Victorinox knife that retails for 30 CHF at Coop or Migros, and 25 CHF for a box of Lindt chocolate available for 9 CHF at the supermarket. As travelers on traveler reports and Swiss locals on Threads have observed, Interlaken's Hoheweg strip has some of the highest souvenir markups in Switzerland, with shops targeting day-trippers and tour groups who don't know local retail prices.

Red Flags

  • Items in the shop have no visible price tags -- you have to ask for each price
  • Prices are dramatically higher than what you've seen for the same brands elsewhere in Switzerland
  • The shop is directly on Hoheweg or adjacent to a tour bus parking area
  • Staff steer you toward expensive items and discourage browsing cheaper options
  • The shop primarily caters to tour groups arriving by bus

How to Avoid

  • Buy Swiss chocolate and knives at Coop or Migros supermarkets for standard Swiss retail prices.
  • Check Victorinox.com for official retail prices so you recognize markups.
  • Walk five minutes from Hoheweg to find shops with honest pricing for locals and tourists alike.
  • The Coop and Migros stores near Interlaken West station carry all the popular Swiss souvenirs.
  • Compare prices on your phone before purchasing anything from tourist-facing shops.
Scam #3
The Jungfrau Region Ticket Confusion
🔶 Medium
📍 Interlaken Ost station ticket counters and third-party booking offices

You arrive at Interlaken Ost planning to ride up to Jungfraujoch, the 'Top of Europe.' A person outside the station offers to help you navigate the complex ticket system and save money with a 'local discount.' They sell you a ticket for 180 CHF -- which sounds great compared to the listed 247 CHF. But the ticket only covers the train to Kleine Scheidegg, not the final segment to Jungfraujoch itself. You discover this halfway up the mountain when a conductor tells you to pay an additional 120 CHF or get off at the next stop. Swiss travel forums document that unauthorized ticket sellers and confusing third-party booking offices around Interlaken Ost exploit the genuinely complex Jungfrau railway pricing structure.

Red Flags

  • Someone outside the official station ticket windows offers to sell you train tickets at a discount
  • The ticket price is significantly below the official published fare on jungfrau.ch
  • The ticket doesn't clearly state 'Jungfraujoch' as the destination -- it may only go partway
  • A third-party booking office near the station charges a 'service fee' on top of the ticket price
  • They rush you to purchase without letting you compare prices or check the official website

How to Avoid

  • Buy Jungfrau region tickets only at official Jungfrau Railway counters inside Interlaken Ost or on jungfrau.ch.
  • Check if your Swiss Travel Pass or Half Fare Card provides a significant discount before buying.
  • The official Jungfraujoch return fare from Interlaken Ost is around 247 CHF (2024/25) -- anything much lower is suspicious.
  • Book the 'Good Morning Ticket' on the official site for genuine early-bird savings of up to 30 percent.
  • Download the Jungfrau Railways app for real-time pricing and schedule information.
Scam #4
The Rental Car Speed Trap Fine
🟢 Low
📍 Highways and village roads between Interlaken and Grindelwald or Lauterbrunnen

You rent a car to explore the Lauterbrunnen valley at your own pace.

The scenic roads are beautiful, and speed limits seem to change frequently between 30, 50, and 80 km/h zones with small signs that are easy to miss. Three weeks after returning home, your credit card is charged 250 CHF by the rental company for a speeding fine, plus a 50 CHF 'administrative fee' they tack on for processing. As TripAdvisor's Interlaken forum and traveler reports warn, Swiss speed cameras are among the most aggressive in Europe, and rental companies profit from the processing fees they charge on top of the actual fines. Exceeding the limit by even 1 km/h triggers a ticket.

Red Flags

  • Speed limits change frequently on mountain roads, often dropping to 30 km/h through villages
  • Speed cameras are small, well-hidden, and often placed just after a speed limit reduction sign
  • Your rental agreement includes a 30-80 CHF administrative fee per fine processed
  • You receive no notification until weeks later when the rental company charges your card
  • The fine increases dramatically with speed -- going 6-10 km/h over costs 120 CHF, 11-15 over costs 250 CHF

How to Avoid

  • Use cruise control and GPS navigation that displays speed limits on the route.
  • Pay special attention to 30 km/h zones in villages -- cameras are almost always placed there.
  • Consider using trains and the PostBus network instead of renting a car in the Jungfrau region.
  • If you must drive, note that Swiss speed tolerances are near-zero -- stay 5 km/h under the limit.
  • Check your rental agreement for fine processing fees and dispute any unreasonable admin charges.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Swiss Cantonal Police (Kantonspolizei) station. Call 117 (Police) or 112 (Emergency). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at police.ch.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact the US Embassy in Bern at Sulgeneckstrasse 19. For emergencies: +41 31-357-7011.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
