The 6 Scams

Scam #1
São Paulo Guarulhos Airport (GRU) Taxi Mafia & Late-Night Uber Confusion
⚠️ High
📍 Guarulhos International Airport (GRU) Terminals 1, 2, 3 arrivals halls; Congonhas Airport (CGH) domestic taxi queue; Airport Bus Service stops; Vila Olímpia / Itaim Bibi / Jardins drop-offs

São Paulo's two airports.

GRU (international, 25 km from city center, R$90–R$180 by Uber) and CGH (domestic, downtown waterfront, R$30–R$70) — host one of South America's most-documented 2025 taxi-overcharge ecosystems. Community reports document the broader pattern.

The 2025 scams: (a) 'Special Taxi' kiosks inside GRU arrivals quote R$250–R$500 to Vila Olímpia / Jardins / Paulista (real Uber R$90–R$180); (b) sign-holders posing as 'hotel transfer' agents intercepting tourists whose hotels do NOT include shuttle; (c) Uber driver cancel-and-recontract scam — driver accepts the in-app ride, cancels at the curb, then quotes cash R$250+; (d) 'meter broken' yellow taxi demands at hotel arrival with R$350+ flat-rate cash demand; (e) GRU-to-CGH 'connecting transfer R$400' sold by touts (real Uber R$80–R$130 for the 35-km cross-city ride); (f) credit-card cloning at unofficial taxi-cooperative counters via 'manual entry' card-machines; (g) 'security escort fee R$150' added unannounced after late-night arrivals.

For travelers: (1) book Uber, 99, or Cabify on airport Wi-Fi AFTER luggage — typical GRU to Paulista / Vila Olímpia R$90–R$180, 35–60 min depending on traffic; meet driver at the designated 'Aplicativos' (apps) pickup zone signposted at each terminal exit; (2) the official Airport Bus Service (airportbusservice.com.br) connects GRU to Tietê / Barra Funda / Congonhas / Praça da República at R$56 per person — comfortable air-conditioned coach with luggage hold, runs every 30 min 5 AM–midnight; (3) IGNORE every 'Special Taxi' kiosk inside the terminal quoting above R$200; (4) for CGH domestic arrivals, Uber is even cheaper — R$30–R$70 to Paulista; (5) NEVER hand a credit card to any taxi driver — pay in-app or cash only; (6) for late-night arrivals (post-22:00), Uber Comfort or Uber Black is worth the R$30–R$50 surcharge for vetted drivers; (7) save Polícia Turística São Paulo (DEATUR) +55 11 3120 4447 (English available, R. Sá e Souza, 99) and US Consulate São Paulo +55 11 3250 5000.

Red Flags

  • 'Special Taxi' kiosk inside GRU arrivals quoting R$250–R$500 to center
  • Sign-holder posing as 'hotel transfer' for hotel that doesn't include shuttle
  • Uber driver canceling at curb and re-quoting R$250+ cash
  • Yellow taxi 'meter broken' with R$350+ flat-rate demand at hotel
  • 'Security escort fee R$150' added unannounced after late-night ride

How to Avoid

  • Book Uber/99/Cabify on airport Wi-Fi: GRU-Paulista R$90–R$180.
  • Meet driver at 'Aplicativos' signposted pickup zone; CGH R$30–R$70.
  • OR Airport Bus Service R$56 per person (every 30 min, 5 AM–midnight).
  • NEVER hand credit card to any taxi driver.
  • Late-night: Uber Comfort/Black R$30–R$50 surcharge for vetted drivers.
Scam #2
Paulista Avenue 'Clowns' Distraction Pickpocket Ring
⚠️ High
📍 Avenida Paulista (full 2.8-km strip, Trianon-MASP and Consolação metro corridors most-targeted), Estação Trianon-MASP, Avenida Paulista weekend cyclovia, MASP underpass

Avenida Paulista.

São Paulo's main commercial spine, daily foot traffic 1.5 million — hosts the city's CANONICAL 2025 organized distraction-pickpocket ring nicknamed 'os palhaços' (the clowns).

The 2025 scam pattern: (a) team of 3–5 men, often near the MASP underpass / Trianon-MASP metro exit / Consolação metro exit, work in coordination; (b) one approaches the tourist saying 'cuidado, caiu algo' ('careful, something fell') pointing at the tourist's shoes / pocket area; (c) tourist looks down, second member lifts wallet / phone from back pocket / open bag; (d) third member picks up dropped item (a peanut shell, coin) and 'returns' it to the tourist; (e) team disperses by the time tourist realizes items missing; (f) variant: 'mustard / sauce / bird-poop on jacket' distraction at café tables on Paulista; (g) variant: 'aren't you the friend of [random Brazilian first name]?' confusion approach. The ring rotates members every few weeks — community vigilante exposure documented in.

For travelers: (1) on Paulista, KEEP YOUR PHONE IN A ZIPPED FRONT-POCKET OR CROSSBODY BAG — never back pocket, never on outdoor café tables; (2) if anyone says 'cuidado, caiu algo' ('careful something fell') — DO NOT LOOK DOWN; immediately step back, hand on wallet, walk away; this is the canonical 'clowns' opening; (3) if 'mustard' or 'sauce' lands on you, do NOT let strangers help clean — that IS the distraction; walk into nearest bank / hotel / Uber to clean; (4) refuse 'aren't you the friend of [name]?' approaches — confusion-distraction template; (5) at MASP underpass, the metro exit funnels people through narrow passageways — tighten bag straps and stay alert; (6) Paulista IS broadly safe for daytime sightseeing — the clowns target inattention, not violence; (7) Sunday cyclovia (avenue closed to cars 7 AM – 4 PM) is the safest browsing time; (8) DEATUR Tourist Police +55 11 3120 4447; file Boletim de Ocorrência (BO) within 24 hours for insurance + chargebacks.

Red Flags

  • Stranger saying 'cuidado, caiu algo' ('careful something fell') near MASP
  • Team of 3–5 men coordinated near Trianon-MASP / Consolação metro exits
  • 'Mustard / sauce / bird-poop on jacket' offer to help clean
  • 'Aren't you the friend of [name]?' confusion approach
  • Phone on outdoor café table on Paulista (instant snatch target)

How to Avoid

  • Phone in zipped FRONT-POCKET or crossbody bag; never back pocket.
  • If anyone says 'caiu algo': DO NOT LOOK DOWN; step back, walk away.
  • If 'mustard' lands on you, do NOT let strangers clean; walk to bank/hotel.
  • Refuse 'aren't you friend of [name]?' confusion approach.
  • Sunday cyclovia (7 AM–4 PM) is safest browsing time.

The 2025 scam pattern: (a) a well-dressed older woman (50s–70s, often with a small medical bag or appearing dressed-as-nurse) approaches a tourist on Paulista or near MASP; (b) opening line in English: 'Excuse me, you don't speak Portuguese? Scam #4
São Paulo Centro Histórico Pickpocket & Phone-Snatch Ring (Sé / Anhangabaú / Estação da Luz)
⚠️ High
📍 Catedral da Sé / Praça da Sé, Vale do Anhangabaú, Estação da Luz / Pinacoteca approach, Estação Júlio Prestes, República metro corridor, 25 de Março shopping street

São Paulo's Centro Histórico.

Sé, Anhangabaú, Luz, República, 25 de Março — contains the city's most-photographed colonial-era sights AND its highest tourist-pickpocket density.

The 2025 scams: (a) Praça da Sé organized pickpocket teams targeting visitors photographing the Cathedral — distraction-theft templates similar to Paulista 'clowns'; (b) Estação da Luz / Pinacoteca approach — narrow pedestrian corridors funnel visitors into team-pickpocket setups; (c) 25 de Março street (wholesale shopping district, 400,000+ daily foot traffic) — pickpockets exploit dense crowd density; tourists with cameras are obvious targets; (d) Vale do Anhangabaú (recently-renovated public plaza) — phone-snatch from outdoor seating; (e) República metro corridor — late-night phone-snatch sprints onto departing trains; (f) 'helpful local' offering to take photo with YOUR phone at Sé Cathedral, then running; (g) outdoor café tables in Centro Velho (historic center) — phone-on-table snatch by sprinting youth.

For travelers: (1) Centro Histórico is WORTH visiting for the colonial architecture (Sé Cathedral, Mosteiro de São Bento, Pinacoteca, Estação da Luz, Mercadão) — but go ONLY DURING DAYTIME (8 AM–5 PM) on weekdays for maximum safety; weekends are quieter (less foot traffic, less pickpocket cover); (2) bring NOTHING you can't afford to lose — leave passport, jewelry, valuable watch in hotel safe; (3) wear a money belt OR crossbody bag with zipper; no back-pocket wallets; (4) keep phone in zipped front pocket between uses; NEVER on outdoor café table; (5) for photographs at Sé Cathedral / Pinacoteca, use phone-timer or ask SECURITY GUARDS / hotel staff (not random strangers); (6) take Uber/99 BETWEEN sights rather than walking long stretches in Centro; (7) refuse 'helpful local' photo offers; (8) if you sense pickpocket-team setup forming, walk INTO a bank / hotel lobby / large shop until they disperse; (9) AVOID Centro after dark (post-18:00); take Uber back to Paulista / Vila Olímpia / Jardins for evening; (10) DEATUR São Paulo +55 11 3120 4447 for any theft.

Red Flags

  • Praça da Sé team approach while you photograph the Cathedral
  • 'Helpful local' offering to take photo with YOUR phone
  • Phone on outdoor café table in Centro Velho
  • Late-night République metro pickpocket sprints to departing trains
  • Centro pedestrian-only after dark (post-18:00) carries elevated risk

How to Avoid

  • Centro daytime only (8 AM–5 PM weekdays preferred).
  • Leave valuables in hotel safe; money belt + crossbody bag with zipper.
  • Phone in zipped front pocket; never on outdoor café table.
  • Take Uber/99 BETWEEN sights, not long walks in Centro.
  • Photos: use timer or ask security/hotel staff, not random strangers.
Scam #5
São Paulo Restaurant 'Couvert Artístico' & Service-Charge Padding Fraud
🔶 Medium
📍 Vila Madalena bar strip (Aspicuelta / Mourato Coelho), Itaim Bibi tourist restaurants, Jardins fine-dining strip, Pinheiros boutique restaurants, Centro tourist-menu spots

São Paulo's restaurant industry has a documented 2025 'abusive practices' problem around couvert.

Artístico (artistic cover for live music) and serviço (service charge) padding.

The 2025 scam patterns: (a) Vila Madalena bars adding 'couvert artístico R$30–R$80 per person' for live music that's a single guitarist for 30 min — legitimate couvert is R$10–R$25 disclosed BEFORE seating; (b) Itaim / Jardins restaurants adding 'serviço 13–15%' instead of standard 10% (Brazilian Lei 13.419/2017 makes serviço OPTIONAL — you can refuse); (c) 'taxa de couvert + serviço' double-charging where both are mandatory (illegal practice per PROCON SP); (d) USD-priced menus at tourist-zone restaurants padding 10–15% exchange vs MX$; (e) 'agua mineral R$22' (real R$6–R$12); (f) 'cachaça artesanal premium R$80 shot' from house cachaça (real R$25–R$45); (g) bill-switcheroo where R$ amount on receipt differs from what's charged to card by R$50–R$200; (h) Centro tourist-menu spots with 'pratos típicos para turistas' menus at 2–3× the local-language menu prices.

For travelers: (1) BEFORE sitting at any São Paulo restaurant, ASK for menu and CONFIRM (a) couvert artístico R$ amount per person (legitimate R$10–R$25), (b) serviço % policy (10% standard, OPTIONAL by Brazilian law), (c) credit-card surcharge — photograph the menu; (2) reputable Vila Madalena: A Casa do Porco (R$200–R$350 per person, no couvert), Bar Brahma (Centro, classic R$80–R$150, no couvert padding), Boteco São Bento (Itaim, R$80–R$150); (3) reputable Jardins: D.O.M. Maní, Kinoshita — fine dining R$350–R$800 with transparent menu pricing; (4) for budget eats, the 'Zona do Sul até 300 reais por 2' thread lists Google 4.5+ spots with no couvert padding at R$30–R$80 per person; (5) 'serviço' is OPTIONAL by Brazilian Lei 13.419/2017 — tip 10% if happy, refuse if poor service or if you're being padded; (6) for water, order 'água sem gás copo' (tap water glass, free) at most spots OR bring your own bottled water; (7) USD-priced menus at tourist-zone spots pad 10–15% — request real R$ amount; (8) if charged abusive couvert + serviço, request the official PROCON SP complaint form (the restaurant must provide it under Brazilian consumer law).

Red Flags

  • Vila Madalena 'couvert artístico R$30–R$80/person' for one guitarist
  • 'Serviço 13–15%' instead of standard 10% (often padded)
  • 'Taxa de couvert + serviço' double-charging (illegal per PROCON SP)
  • 'Agua mineral R$22' (real R$6–R$12)
  • 'Pratos típicos para turistas' English menu at 2–3× Portuguese-menu prices

How to Avoid

  • BEFORE sitting confirm couvert + serviço + card surcharge in writing.
  • Reputable Vila Madalena: A Casa do Porco, Bar Brahma, Boteco São Bento.
  • 'Serviço' OPTIONAL (Lei 13.419/2017); refuse if poor service or padded.
  • Order 'água sem gás copo' (free tap water) instead of R$22 mineral.
  • Request official PROCON SP complaint form if charged abusive couvert.
Scam #6
São Paulo Uber / 99 Off-App Cancel-and-Recontract Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Vila Olímpia / Itaim Bibi pickup zones, Paulista nightlife corridor late-night, Vila Madalena bar-strip evenings, GRU airport departure curb, Allianz Parque concert exits

São Paulo's rideshare ecosystem (Uber, 99, Cabify, Uber Moto, 99 Moto) is excellent and broadly safer than street taxis.

But a 2025 driver-side off-app fraud layer mirrors the CDMX / Lima pattern....

The 2025 scams: (a) driver accepts your in-app ride then messages 'app travado, vamos no WhatsApp' ('app frozen, let's go via WhatsApp') and cancels — quoting cash 2–3× the app fare on arrival; (b) 'long route' gaming via 99 — driver deliberately takes a 45-min route for a 25-min trip claiming 'muito trânsito' and the in-app fare rises accordingly; (c) 'preço errado, pague mais' ('price wrong, pay more') demand mid-trip; (d) post-event (Allianz Parque concert, Anhembi expo, Sambódromo Carnaval) surge-pricing exploitation where unauthorized 'Uber substitute' drivers approach with cash-only quotes; (e) Uber Moto / 99 Moto safety variance — moto-rideshare is fast through traffic but operators sometimes drive recklessly, and Mayor Nunes's 2025 attempt to block moto-rideshare reflects the safety concern; (f) credit-card cloning when tourists hand cards for 'manual entry' in unofficial taxis claiming 'I substitute for your Uber'.

For travelers: (1) pay in-app ONLY — link credit card to Uber / 99 / Cabify account BEFORE travel; refuse any driver requesting WhatsApp / cancellation / cash; (2) verify the license plate matches the app BEFORE getting in; (3) if driver messages 'app travado' (app frozen) or requests cancellation, simply cancel the ride yourself — Uber/99 do not charge cancellation for driver-initiated issues — and book again; (4) for travelers, AVOID Uber Moto / 99 Moto — the moto rideshare service is for fit younger riders, not safe for 60+; (5) watch route on phone during ride — if driver deviates significantly, ask 'por que essa rota?' and refuse unjustified detours; (6) for post-event pickups (Allianz Parque, Anhembi), walk 200–500 m AWAY from venue exit before booking — surge prices and predatory drivers cluster at exits; (7) for late-night Paulista / Vila Madalena returns to hotel, Uber Comfort or 99 Top is worth the R$10–R$25 surcharge for vetted drivers; (8) the 'safest app' varies by neighborhood — Uber and 99 are both reliable in Zona Sul (Paulista / Vila Olímpia / Itaim); 99 is sometimes cheaper for short rides.

Red Flags

  • Driver messaging 'app travado, vamos no WhatsApp' (app frozen)
  • Mid-trip demand 'preço errado, pague mais' for cash
  • Driver deliberately taking long 'muito trânsito' route
  • Unauthorized 'Uber substitute' approaching after concert / event
  • Demand for credit card 'manual entry' in unofficial taxi

How to Avoid

  • Pay in-app ONLY — never WhatsApp, cash, off-app.
  • Verify license plate matches app BEFORE boarding.
  • Cancel driver-initiated 'app travado' rides; rebook (no charge).
  • AVOID Uber Moto / 99 Moto for travelers (safety variance).
  • Post-event: walk 200–500 m from venue exit before booking.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Civil Police (Polícia Civil) station. Call 190 (emergency) or 197 (civil police). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at delegaciaonline.rj.gov.br.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Consulate General is at Av. Presidente Wilson, 147, Centro, Rio de Janeiro. For emergencies: +55 21 3823-2000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
