The 4 Scams

Scam #1
The Cathedral Petition Ring
🔶 Medium
📍 Place de la Cathédrale and surrounding streets near Strasbourg Cathedral

You are admiring the astronomical clock inside Strasbourg Cathedral when a group of young women ...

You are admiring the astronomical clock inside Strasbourg Cathedral when a group of young women approaches outside, waving clipboards. One pushes a petition in front of you for 'deaf-mute children' and points urgently for you to sign and donate. While you write your name, her partners crowd close around you. When you walk away, your wallet or phone is gone from your bag. As travelers on r/france and Strasbourg safety guides warn, the petition scam is a year-round fixture around the cathedral, but intensifies during the Christmas market season when dense crowds provide perfect cover for pickpocketing accomplices.

Red Flags

  • Young women approach with clipboards near the cathedral entrance
  • They claim to collect for deaf-mute children and point urgently rather than speaking
  • Multiple people crowd around you while you look at the clipboard
  • They ask for a cash donation immediately after you sign
  • The petition is in a language different from the one they use to approach you

How to Avoid

  • Say 'Non, merci' firmly and keep walking without stopping
  • Keep your bag zipped and held in front of you when near the cathedral
  • Do not sign anything or hand over cash on the street to petition holders
  • Be especially alert during the Christmas market season from late November through December
  • Report aggressive petition groups to the police station near Place Kléber
Scam #2
The Christmas Market Pickpocket
🔶 Medium
📍 Christkindelsmärik stalls around Place Kléber and Place Broglie

You are wandering through Europe's oldest Christmas market, warming your hands around a mug of vin chaud.

The crowds are thick at the most popular stalls along Place Kléber. You feel a jostle from behind -- normal in this crowd. Two hours later, you reach for your wallet and it is gone, stolen from your zipped crossbody purse without you noticing. As a TripAdvisor reviewer recounted after visiting Strasbourg's 2024 Christkindelsmärik, they had to spend the remainder of their trip at the police station filing a report. Professional pickpocket teams specifically target Strasbourg's markets during the November-December season.

Red Flags

  • Someone bumps into you or presses unusually close in a crowd that is not that tight
  • A person drops something in front of you, causing you to stop while others press from behind
  • You feel a light tug or movement at your bag even through the crowd
  • Groups of two or three move together through the crowd without buying anything
  • A distraction occurs nearby such as a loud argument or someone falling

How to Avoid

  • Carry a cross-body bag with a sturdy zipper and keep one hand on it at all times in the market
  • Leave your passport and extra cash in your hotel safe -- carry only what you need for the day
  • Visit the Christmas markets during off-peak hours such as late morning on weekdays for smaller crowds
  • Keep your phone in a front zipped pocket rather than in your coat or back pocket
  • Consider using a money belt under your clothing for cash and cards during peak market hours
Scam #3
The Overpriced Boat Tour Upgrade
🟢 Low
📍 Batorama boat tour departure points along the Ill River near Palais Rohan

You arrive at the Batorama dock to take the famous Strasbourg boat tour through Petite France and the European Quarter.

A hustler outside offers tickets at the same price but for a 'premium experience' with better seats and a longer route. You pay 18 euros -- the same as the standard tour -- but are directed to an unmarked boat with no audio guide, fewer covered seats, and a shortened route that skips the European institutions. As warned on Strasbourg tourism forums, unofficial boat operators near the Batorama dock sell fake or inferior tours by mimicking official pricing while delivering a fraction of the experience.

Red Flags

  • Someone sells boat tickets outside the official Batorama ticket window
  • The tickets do not have the official Batorama logo or barcode
  • You are directed to a dock or boat that is separate from the main Batorama fleet
  • The seller promises a premium upgrade at no extra cost
  • The boat lacks audio equipment, a glass roof, or the distinctive Batorama green branding

How to Avoid

  • Buy Batorama tickets only at the official kiosk near the Palais Rohan or online at batorama.com
  • The standard Batorama tour costs 15-18 euros and lasts 70 minutes with multilingual audio guides
  • Ignore hustlers near the dock and walk directly to the official ticket counter
  • Check that your boat has the Batorama name and branding before boarding
  • Book online in advance to guarantee your preferred time slot and avoid dock-side pressure
Scam #4
The Restaurant Menu Switch
🟢 Low
📍 Tourist restaurants along Rue du Maroquin and Petite France

You sit down at a charming restaurant on Rue du Maroquin, attracted by a chalkboard outside ...

You sit down at a charming restaurant on Rue du Maroquin, attracted by a chalkboard outside advertising a plat du jour for 14 euros. You order it along with a glass of Riesling. When the bill arrives, the plat du jour is 22 euros and the wine is 9 euros instead of the 6 euros shown on the menu you saw. The waiter explains that the outside board is for lunch only, and the evening menu has different prices. As Strasbourg dining tips on r/strasbourg suggest, some tourist-area restaurants use deceptive pricing where the attractive chalkboard price differs from the actual menu price once you are seated.

Red Flags

  • The restaurant has different prices on the chalkboard outside and the menu at your table
  • Menus are only in French with no prices visible until after you are seated
  • The waiter quotes prices verbally rather than showing you the written menu
  • The restaurant is on a heavily touristed street with staff standing outside recruiting diners
  • No locals are eating there despite it being a meal time

How to Avoid

  • Always ask for the written menu with prices before ordering and confirm the price of each item
  • Photograph the chalkboard outside and compare it to the table menu to catch discrepancies
  • Eat at winstubs (traditional Alsatian taverns) a block or two away from the main tourist streets for honest pricing
  • Check Google Reviews or TripAdvisor ratings before sitting down at any restaurant in the Petite France area
  • If the bill does not match the menu, politely dispute it and ask to see the manager before paying

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Police Nationale / SAMU station. Call 17 (Police) or 15 (SAMU medical). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at pre-plainte-en-ligne.interieur.gouv.fr.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Paris is at 2 Avenue Gabriel, 75008 Paris. For emergencies: +33 1 43-12-22-22.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
