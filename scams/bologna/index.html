Scam #1
The Friendship Bracelet Pickpocket
🔶 Medium
📍 Piazza Maggiore, Via dell'Indipendenza, near Due Torri

A vendor near Bologna's Two Towers ties a "friendship bracelet" on your wrist while engaging you in cheerful chatter — providing cover for an unseen accomplice to lift your wallet or phone from an outer pocket or bag.

Bologna's Le Due Torri area draws steady tourist foot traffic, making it a reliable hunting ground for bracelet teams that operate across northern Italian cities. A smiling man approaches, compliments you, and begins weaving coloured cord onto your wrist before you've agreed to anything — the physical act happening faster than a polite refusal.

The bracelet tying occupies both your hands and your attention, which is the entire point. While the vendor keeps eye contact and conversation going, a second operator closes in from a blind side and removes whatever is accessible — wallet in a rear pocket, phone in a jacket pocket, or any item left loose in a bag. By the time the vendor asks for payment for the bracelet, the accomplice is already gone.

Keep both hands free and your bag against your front any time a stranger initiates physical contact in a tourist area, and walk away before a bracelet reaches your wrist. Saying "no" once and immediately — without softening it — is far easier than trying to remove the bracelet mid-tying under social pressure. If you are approached, step back, break physical contact, and move toward a group of other people.

Red Flags

  • Someone approaches to tie something on your wrist
  • Overly friendly physical contact from a stranger
  • A second person hovers nearby
  • Happens near major landmarks and bottleneck walkways

How to Avoid

  • Keep your hands at your sides — don't let anyone tie anything on you.
  • Step back and say 'No grazie' firmly.
  • Keep valuables in front pockets or a money belt.
  • This scam operates in Rome, Florence, Bologna, Milan, and Venice.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Carabinieri / Polizia di Stato station. Call 112 (Carabinieri) or 113 (Polizia). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at poliziadistato.it.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Rome is at Via Vittorio Veneto 121, 00187 Rome. For emergencies: +39 06-4674-1.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
