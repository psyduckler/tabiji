Key Takeaways

  • The #1 reported scam is the The Bellagio Water Taxi Gouge
  • Most scams in Lake Como are low-to-medium risk
  • Use app-based ride services (Uber, Bolt) or official metered taxis instead of unmarked vehicles
  • Never accept unsolicited offers from strangers near tourist sites in Lake Como

⚡ Quick Safety Tips

  • Book tours and tickets through verified operators with online reviews

The 3 Scams

Scam #1
The Bellagio Water Taxi Gouge
🔶 Medium
📍 Bellagio, Varenna, Menaggio docks

A water taxi captain at Bellagio offers to take you across the lake to Villa del Balbianello for €80.

The regular public ferry costs €5. Some charge per person rather than per boat — a couple ends up paying €160 for what should be a €10 ferry ride.

Red Flags

  • Price quoted without clarifying per person or per boat
  • Captain approaches you at the dock
  • Claims the public ferry isn't running today

How to Avoid

  • Use the public ferries operated by Navigazione Laghi
  • A single ferry ticket between towns is €3-7
  • Buy a one-day ferry pass for €15 and hop between all lake towns
  • If using a water taxi, agree on total price before boarding
Scam #2
The Villa Garden Entry Confusion
🟢 Low
📍 Villas around the lake

You visit Villa Carlotta and pay the €12 entrance.

Then you learn the separate garden section is another €8. Then the art gallery inside is a third ticket. Individual fees are legitimate but rarely presented clearly upfront.

Red Flags

  • Multiple separate tickets for what seems like one attraction
  • Prices not clearly listed at the main entrance
  • Trust your instincts — if something feels off, walk away

How to Avoid

  • Check villa websites before visiting for full pricing
  • Villa del Balbianello: €10 gardens only, €20 gardens + villa, plus €7 boat from Lenno
  • Villa Carlotta: €12 includes everything
  • Budget €15-25 per villa visit
Scam #3
The Overpriced Lakeside Restaurant
🟢 Low
📍 Restaurants on Bellagio waterfront, Varenna harbor, Como town center

You sit down at a restaurant on the Bellagio waterfront, drawn by the view.

The menu shows reasonable pasta prices around 14 euros, but the waiter recommends the daily fish special without mentioning the price. It arrives beautifully plated. The bill shows the fish at 45 euros, plus a coperto (cover charge) of 4 euros per person and a 15% service charge that was not listed on the menu. Some waterfront restaurants in Como's tourist towns operate with dual pricing systems and push expensive specials on tourists. The practice is legal in Italy but catches visitors unaware.

Red Flags

  • Waiter pushes daily specials without stating the price
  • Menu does not clearly list coperto or service charges
  • Restaurant has no local customers despite being in a prime location
  • No prices are shown for market-priced fish or seafood dishes
  • Bill includes charges not visible on the menu

How to Avoid

  • Always ask the price of daily specials before ordering
  • Check the menu for coperto and service charge disclosures
  • Walk one or two streets back from the waterfront for better value restaurants
  • Read recent Google or TripAdvisor reviews mentioning bill surprises
  • Ask for the bill itemized and question any charges you do not recognize

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Carabinieri / Polizia di Stato station. Call 112 (Carabinieri) or 113 (Polizia). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at poliziadistato.it.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Rome is at Via Vittorio Veneto 121, 00187 Rome. For emergencies: +39 06-4674-1.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
