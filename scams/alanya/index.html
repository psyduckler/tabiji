  • Don't follow an unsolicited 'friendly local' to any bar — Traveler reports documents a card-skimming pattern where charges of €800–€2,500 land within hours; pay cash for drinks at small bills.
  • From AYT (Antalya Airport, 130 km), use TUI/Jet2/Tez Tour included transfer or Havaş bus (₺250, 2.5 hr) — taxi 'fixed prices' over €100 are overcharges (legitimate metered ₺2,000–₺2,500).

  • Avoid hotel-concierge excursions over €60/person — book Manavgat/Side/Aspendos via GetYourGuide or Viator with 'no shopping stops' filter and TÜRSAB licensing verified.
  • Damlataş Cave (₺120), Alanya Castle (₺240), Red Tower (₺120) — all walk-up at official prices; decline 'skip-the-line' offers from touts (these attractions rarely have queues).

The 6 Scams

Scam #1
Alanya 'Buy Drinks' Card-Skimming Bar Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Alanya bars on Bar Street (Damlataş Caddesi) and Cleopatra Beach promenade, side-street venues off the main strip, late-night returns from Karaalioğlu Park area

A friendly 'local' approaches you on Bar Street and invites you to a 'nice bar nearby.' You buy four drinks.

The card terminal is brought to your table. The waiter taps the screen multiple times saying it 'didn't go through.' By the next morning you discover your card has been charged €800–€2,500 in additional 'transactions' to bars you never entered. The card was skimmed: while pretending to retry the payment, the staff captured your card data and made multiple high-value charges through the same terminal or sister venues.

all document the social-engineering opening: a fluent-English 'local' who initiates conversation specifically to steer tourists toward a flagged venue. Traveler reports captures the broader 2025 community framing: 'Just came back after being in Alanya. Fantastic place with amazing people! But yeah, def many tourist traps wh' ich include both bar-card scams and overcharge bars.

For older travelers, particularly solo male travelers, the protective playbook: (1) Don't follow an unsolicited 'friendly local' to a bar you haven't chosen yourself; (2) at any bar, pay cash for drinks (small bills only — never ₺500 notes); (3) if you must pay by card, watch the terminal stay at your table and refuse 'didn't go through, let me try again' retries; (4) if asked to 'sign here' on a paper receipt, photograph the amount before signing; (5) check your bank app immediately after each transaction; (6) if you suspect skimming, freeze the card via your bank app within 60 seconds and call your bank's fraud line; (7) report the venue to Tourism Police 155 with venue name and address.

Red Flags

  • 'Friendly local' approaches you unsolicited on Bar Street and offers to 'show you a nice bar'
  • Bar has no posted prices at the entrance or on the menu
  • Waiter tries to take your card to a back-room terminal rather than bringing the terminal to you
  • Multiple 'didn't go through' retries on the card terminal
  • Aggressive escalation if you refuse to pay an inflated bill

How to Avoid

  • Don't follow an unsolicited 'friendly local' to any bar in Alanya.
  • Pay cash for drinks at bars (small bills, never ₺500 notes).
  • If using card, watch the terminal stay at your table; refuse 'didn't go through' retries.
  • Photograph the amount on any signed paper receipt BEFORE signing.
  • Freeze card via banking app within 60 seconds of suspected skimming.
Scam #2
Cleopatra Beach Taxi & Boat Excursion Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Cleopatra Beach taxi rank, Damlataş Caddesi pickup zones, Alanya Marina boat-tour booking offices, Portacall (Liman) ferry transfers

2 km of fine sand named after the legend that Mark Antony gifted it to Cleopatra.

2 km of fine sand named after the legend that Mark Antony gifted it to Cleopatra. The legitimate licensed taxi fare from Alanya center to Cleopatra Beach is approximately ₺140–₺200 (€3.50–€5) on the meter. They don't' want to run the meter and quote 'fixed prices' of ₺500–₺800 instead.

For boat excursions, the legitimate Alanya 'pirate boat' day-tour with 5–6 swim stops costs ₺600–₺900 per person (€15–€22) including lunch through vetted operators. Below that tier, beach touts sell €10–€12 'special boat tours' that turn out to be 2-hour shorter trips with hidden ₺200 'mandatory tip' demands at end. Traveler reports documents the broader excursion-reseller pattern: 'are we being scammed and if yes, by whom?' is the recurring community question. Traveler reports confirms the 2024 escalation in beach-tout aggression.

For older package-holiday travelers, the protective playbook: (1) for taxis, insist on the meter and confirm ₺140–₺200 range to Cleopatra Beach before boarding; (2) use BiTaksi app for app-regulated fares with digital receipts; (3) for boat tours, book through your hotel concierge or vetted operators (Alanya Pirate Boat with posted prices) at ₺600–₺900 per person; (4) decline beach-tout offers under €15 — the math forces shortcuts; (5) confirm in writing: lunch included, all drinks at posted prices, no 'mandatory tip,' specific number of swim stops; (6) for the short walk back from Cleopatra Beach to Alanya center (1.5 km), the coastal promenade is flat and pleasant — older travelers can walk in 25 min and skip the taxi.

Red Flags

  • Cleopatra Beach taxi quotes 'fixed price' over ₺400 for the 1.5 km trip
  • Driver refuses to run the meter or claims it is 'broken'
  • Boat tour priced under €15 per person for a 'full day' excursion
  • On-board drink prices €15–€20 each (legitimate operators charge €3–€5)
  • Hidden 'mandatory tip' demand at end of boat tour

How to Avoid

  • Insist on meter for taxis; Traveler reports confirm ₺140–₺200 range to Cleopatra Beach before boarding.
  • Use BiTaksi app for app-regulated fares.
  • Book boat tours via hotel concierge or vetted operator at ₺600–₺900/person.
  • Decline beach-tout 'special tours' under €15 — the math forces shortcuts and tip demands.
  • Walk the 1.5 km coastal promenade back to Alanya center (25 min) instead of taxi.
Scam #3
Restaurant Pricing Inflation & 'Tourist Menu' Doubling
🔶 Medium
📍 Restaurants on Bar Street (Damlataş Caddesi), Cleopatra Beach restaurant strip, Alanya Old Town tourist alleys, Mahmutlar all-inclusive resort area exterior

Alanya's restaurant strip operates on the standard Turkish tourist-belt model: English-language laminated menus with photos and no prices, touts on the sidewalk recruiting passing tourists, drinks priced 4–5x local rates, and bills that arrive 30–60% above the menu prices that were shown. is the canonical 2024–2025 traveler thread: 'Prices have actually tripled or even quadrupled over the last 2 years. Compared to when I first came to Turkey' the basic dining math has shifted.

The specific Alanya patterns: (1) 'menü' boards in English with photos but no prices; (2) bread, olives, meze 'complimentary' that cost ₺50–₺100 per item on the bill; (3) 'fish of the day' priced 'per kilo' with weight inflated; (4) cover charges (kuver) of ₺50–₺80 per person not disclosed at seating; (5) 'tourist set menu' at ₺800–₺1,200 for what local restaurants charge ₺300–₺450. gives the single-line rule: 'If a restaurant has a dude out front who comes up even before you look at the menu, keep walking.'

For older travelers, the practical playbook: (1) avoid Bar Street and Cleopatra Beach strip restaurants with English-only photo menus; (2) walk one block back from the seafront to find restaurants where Turkish residents eat — community-recommended Alanya names: Köşem Restaurant (Atatürk Caddesi, Turkish home cooking), Sofra (Old Town, regional specialties), Mahmutlar Manti House (Mahmutlar, posted prices); (3) for fish, ask to see the fish before ordering AND have it weighed in your presence, get the per-kg price IN WRITING; (4) refuse complimentary bread/olives unless prices are confirmed; (5) check the bill line-by-line and dispute any item not ordered; (6) bargaining is expected and not rude — 30–40% off the first quote is reasonable in tourist-tier shops.

Red Flags

  • Tout positioned outside the restaurant actively recruiting passing tourists
  • Menu in English with photos but no posted prices
  • Bread, olives, meze appear on table 'complimentary' before you order
  • 'Fish per kilo' pricing without weighing in your presence
  • Bill includes kuver (cover), terrace, or service charge not on menu

How to Avoid

  • Walk one block off Bar Street/Cleopatra strip to find restaurants for Turkish residents.
  • Community-recommended: Köşem Restaurant (Atatürk Caddesi), Sofra (Old Town), Mahmutlar Manti House.
  • For fish, see it weighed in your presence and get per-kg price in writing.
  • Refuse complimentary bread/olives unless prices are confirmed.
  • Check bill line-by-line; dispute any item not ordered.

Scam #4
Antalya (AYT) & Gazipaşa (GZP) Airport Transfer Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Antalya Airport (AYT) taxi rank, Gazipaşa-Alanya Airport (GZP) taxi rank, hotel-concierge transfers, late-night returns from Bar Street nightlife

Alanya is served by two airports: Antalya (AYT) 130 km west and Gazipaşa-Alanya (GZP) 40 km east.

Most package-holiday travelers arrive via AYT — the legitimate licensed taxi fare from AYT to Alanya is approximately ₺2,000–₺2,500 (€50–€63) on the meter for the 130 km journey. From GZP, the fare to central Alanya is ₺600–₺900 (€15–€22). Unofficial operators at AYT quote 'fixed prices' of €100–€150 for the same trip; from GZP €40–€60.

gives the Turkey-wide baseline: 'Biggest scam are taxis, they'll charge you 2x 3x as soon as they notice you are a foreigner.' recommends BiTaksi for app-regulated fares. Most package-tour operators (TUI, Jet2, easyJet Holidays, Tez Tour, Anex Tour) include free coach transfer from AYT — the cheapest and most overcharge-proof option for older travelers with luggage.

For independent travelers, the practical playbook: (1) confirm whether your tour operator includes free coach transfer BEFORE arrival and wait at the designated meeting point; (2) for AYT, the Havaş airport bus serves Alanya otogar at ₺250 per person (~€6.50, 2.5 hr) — the budget-friendly independent option; (3) if taking a metered taxi from AYT, confirm the ₺2,000–₺2,500 range upfront and insist on Tarife 1 (day rate); (4) for GZP, the airport is small and the metered fare to Alanya is straightforward — refuse 'fixed price' quotes over €30; (5) book Welcome Pickups in advance for AYT-to-Alanya transfer (€60–€80 per car for up to 4 people) — vetted operators with fixed prices; (6) Uber does NOT operate in Alanya; only BiTaksi and licensed taxis are legitimate.

Red Flags

  • AYT driver refuses meter, quoting 'fixed price' over €100 for Alanya trip
  • GZP driver quotes over €30 for the 40-km Gazipaşa-to-Alanya trip
  • Hotel-concierge 'partner' transfer at €80+ from AYT
  • Driver claims meter is 'broken' or 'closed for the night'
  • Late-night Bar Street return quoted at €25+ for short in-town rides

How to Avoid

  • Confirm package-tour transfer is included with TUI/Jet2/Tez Tour/Anex Tour before arrival.
  • From AYT, take Havaş bus to Alanya otogar (₺250, 2.5 hr) for budget independent travel.
  • If using metered taxi from AYT, confirm ₺2,000–₺2,500 range and insist on Tarife 1.
  • Book Welcome Pickups for AYT-to-Alanya (€60–€80 per car for 4).
  • Pre-book late-night Bar Street returns through hotel reception.
Scam #5
All-Inclusive Resort Excursion Reseller & Hotel Tout Markups
🔶 Medium
📍 All-inclusive hotel concierge desks (Mahmutlar, Avsallar, Konaklı), beach-promenade tour booking offices, hotel-arranged 'special excursion' packages

Alanya is one of Europe's largest German, Russian, and Scandinavian package-tour markets, with a dense ecosystem of all-inclusive resorts on the Mahmutlar–Avsallar–Konaklı strip. The standard scam: hotel concierges and beach-promenade tour offices sell €60–€120 'all-inclusive day excursions' to nearby attractions (Manavgat Waterfall, Side Ancient City, Aspendos Theatre, Alanya Castle) that bundle into the price 2–3 'demonstration' or 'cooperative' shopping stops — onyx jewelry, Turkish delight, leather, carpets — each paying the operator a 30–40% commission.

For older travelers on package holidays, the practical playbook: (1) avoid hotel-concierge excursion sales — these are commission-driven referrals; (2) for legitimate excursions, book directly via GetYourGuide or Viator with 'no shopping stops' filter active and verify TÜRSAB Turkish Ministry of Culture licensing; (3) Manavgat Waterfall + Side day-trip should cost €30–€50 per person via vetted small-group operators; (4) for Aspendos Theatre, book a private driver (€80–€120 round-trip from Alanya for up to 4 people) instead of bus tours that include shopping; (5) if a tour stops at a 'demonstration' or 'cooperative,' stay on the bus and refuse to enter; (6) for Alanya Castle and Cleopatra Beach, no excursion is needed — these are walkable from central Alanya hotels.

Red Flags

  • Hotel-concierge excursion sells over €60 per person for a half-day local trip
  • Itinerary includes 'onyx workshop,' 'leather show,' 'Turkish delight cooperative,' or 'silk demonstration'
  • Operator unwilling to confirm 'no shopping stops' in writing
  • TripAdvisor reviews mention 'carpet shop,' 'shopping stop,' or 'demonstration visit'
  • Cash-only payment demand at hotel concierge desk

How to Avoid

  • Avoid hotel-concierge excursion sales (commission-driven referrals).
  • Book directly via GetYourGuide or Viator with 'no shopping stops' filter and TÜRSAB licensing verified.
  • Manavgat + Side day-trip should be €30–€50 per person at vetted operators.
  • For Aspendos, hire a private driver (€80–€120 round-trip for up to 4) instead of bus tours.
  • If tour stops at 'demonstration' or 'cooperative,' stay on the bus and refuse to enter.
Scam #6
Damlataş Cave & Castle 'Skip-the-Line' Tout Scams
🟢 Low
📍 Damlataş Cave entrance area, Alanya Castle (Alanya Kalesi) approach paths, cable-car queue, Red Tower (Kızıl Kule) ticket area

Alanya Castle and Damlataş Cave are the city's headline cultural attractions.

Damlataş Cave entry is ₺120 (~€3); Alanya Castle entry is ₺240 (~€6); Red Tower is ₺120 (~€3). The cable car to the castle is ₺200 round-trip (~€5). All four are inexpensive walk-up attractions with rare queues. The scam: touts at the entrance approach foreigners offering 'skip-the-line' tickets at €15–€25 per person, claiming the official queue is 'closed today' or that a 'special bundle pass' is required.

(comments) gives the broader Antalya-region rule: 'If a shop doesn't have price tags, it's likely a tourist scam. If it's located in a touristy area, it's a tour' ist-tier extraction. Traveler reports confirms the broader Alanya tout ecosystem operating around all the major attractions. Traveler reports captures the broader 2025 region context.

For older travelers, the practical playbook: (1) walk past all touts to the official ticket booth at each attraction; (2) Damlataş Cave: ₺120 (~€3), useful for 15-min visit, indoor and accessible; (3) Alanya Castle: ₺240 (~€6) — the cable car (₺200) is recommended for older travelers given the steep walk; (4) Red Tower: ₺120 (~€3); (5) for a Castle + Cable Car + Damlataş Cave combo experience, the total is under €15 per person paid at official booths; (6) decline 'skip-the-line' offers — none of these attractions has queues except briefly during cruise mornings; (7) buy water, snacks, and evil-eye souvenirs at Migros or supermarket stalls before the climb — the castle vendors charge 3x.

Red Flags

  • Tout offers 'skip-the-line' Castle/Cave/Red Tower tickets at €15–€25 per person
  • Tout claims official queue is 'closed today' or 'special bundle' is required
  • Bundle priced over €30 for combined Castle + Cave + Tower entry
  • Vendor pressure to buy water/snacks/souvenirs at castle summit at 3x supermarket prices
  • Cable-car ticket reseller offering 'discount' off the official ₺200 round-trip price

How to Avoid

  • Walk past all touts to the official ticket booth at each attraction.
  • Damlataş Cave ₺120, Alanya Castle ₺240, Red Tower ₺120, Cable Car ₺200 round-trip — all official prices under €6.
  • Combined experience under €15 per person paid at official booths.
  • Decline 'skip-the-line' offers — these attractions rarely have queues.
  • Buy water, snacks, evil-eye souvenirs at Migros before the climb.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Turkish National Police (Emniyet) station. Call 155 (Police) or 112 (Emergency). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at egm.gov.tr.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Consulate General in Istanbul is at Kaplicalar Mevkii No. 2, İstinye, 34460 Istanbul. For emergencies: +90 212-335-9000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
