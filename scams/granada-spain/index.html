The 7 Scams

Scam #1
Fake Alhambra Ticket Websites
⚠️ High
📍 Online — tiskets-alhambra-palace.com, alhambra.org, alhambratickets.com, various resellers; the official site is tickets.alhambra-patronato.es

You are planning your Granada trip from home.

You search 'Alhambra tickets' and a dozen results appear — all with similar Nasrid-tile logos, 'official' language, and authoritative-looking booking interfaces. You pay. The confirmation email arrives with Arabic calligraphy and PDF attachments. Then you show up at the Alhambra gate, and the staff tell you the ticket is not recognized in their system. You have been scammed, and by then your actual time-slot has sold out.

This is the single most-documented Spanish tourist scam on Reddit for 2024–2025. is the canonical first-person account: 'I was repeatedly told that my ticket was fraudulent, a scam! Having invested time, interest, and a significant sum of money, I stood my ground.' is the locals-only PSA: 'This is NOT the official website. It is both more expensive than official tickets and it is either actually a scam or just very poorly run.' documents the specific markup: '€261 for €60 tickets.' is another 2025 victim account.alhambra-patronato.es.

The Alhambra does sell out months in advance during peak season — that scarcity is exactly what fake sites exploit. For older travelers on cruise excursions or Madrid-based day trips, the risk is amplified: your entry window is inflexible, the gate staff cannot accept a fake ticket, and you will have to leave without seeing the palace. Book the General Visit ticket (€19.09) at tickets.alhambra-patronato.es as soon as your dates are fixed — ideally 3+ months out for June–October visits. If official tickets are sold out, only GetYourGuide, Viator, and Tiqets are licensed third-party resellers with buyer protection; any site charging €60+ per ticket is either a markup or outright fraud.

Red Flags

  • URL is not tickets.alhambra-patronato.es — variants like alhambra.org, alhambratickets.com, tiskets-alhambra-palace.com are confirmed fake
  • Ticket price is significantly above the official €19.09 General Visit rate (sometimes €60–€261 documented on community forums)
  • Site asks for passport details at booking — the official site does too, but so do many scam clones, so the request alone is not a guarantee
  • Google ads for 'Alhambra tickets' routinely lead to resellers — scroll past all sponsored results to the official page
  • Booking confirmation email lacks the Patronato de la Alhambra logo or Alhambra Nasrid-tile pattern and QR format

How to Avoid

  • Bookmark the official URL before your trip: tickets.alhambra-patronato.es — the only legitimate primary booking site.
  • Book the General Visit ticket (€19.09) 2–3 months ahead for June–October visits; peak dates sell out quickly.
  • If the official site is sold out, use only GetYourGuide, Viator, or Tiqets — licensed resellers with money-back guarantees.
  • Type the URL manually rather than clicking Google ads, which are routinely fake-reseller listings.
  • If you have already paid a fake site, call your credit card fraud line immediately and request a chargeback while booking official tickets in parallel.
Scam #2
Alhambra Unofficial 'Guided Tour' Resellers
⚠️ High
📍 Online — Feel the City Tours, Walker Tours, Viator listings from unofficial operators, some hotel-partner 'exclusive deals' outside the Alhambra walls

Sometimes the Alhambra official site really is sold out for your dates.

This is where a different scam family preys on desperate travelers: unofficial 'guided tour' operators who sell Alhambra 'packages' that either never materialise or redirect you to a low-quality group tour at triple the cost. flags 'Feel the City Tours' specifically: 'I don't know for sure if this site is a scam. But there is a big industry around buying and reselling limited-access tickets.' is a named-anchor community thread with its own warning sticky: 'R/scams D0NT USE THEWALKERTOURS.COM ITS A SCAM.'

The even more insidious variant targets travelers who DID book via legitimate platforms but get bumped. both, and then a week before the visit receives an email saying the Alhambra portion is 'no longer available' — with a strongly-worded push to accept a 'substitute' at higher cost. Users in the comments split on whether Viator itself is complicit or whether individual operators misrepresent their ticket allocation, but the practical damage is the same: the traveler arrives in Granada with no Alhambra entry and a booking company that has pocketed the full payment.

For older travelers arriving by rail from Madrid (AVE Madrid–Granada, 3h 15m) or on a coastal cruise excursion, the single defensive move is to book the Alhambra General Visit directly at tickets.alhambra-patronato.es the day your itinerary is confirmed. If you insist on a guided tour, book only through GetYourGuide, Tiqets, or the Patronato's own authorized guides listed on the official site.

Red Flags

  • Tour operator is not on the Patronato de la Alhambra's own authorized-guide list at alhambra-patronato.es
  • Prices are €70+ per person for a standard group tour when the General Visit ticket alone is €19.09
  • Booking platform sends a 'last-minute availability change' email days before the visit pushing an 'upgrade'
  • Operator website has little history, no local phone number, or recent Google reviews mentioning cancellations
  • Company name incorporates 'Walker Tours,' 'Feel the City Tours,' or other names flagged on community forums

How to Avoid

  • Book the Alhambra General Visit ticket directly at tickets.alhambra-patronato.es first — always.
  • If you want a guide, use only GetYourGuide, Viator (with caution), Tiqets, or a Patronato-authorized guide.
  • Never book via operators whose first Google result matches the Patronato's official site — these are clones.
  • If you receive a 'ticket no longer available' email, immediately dispute the charge with your credit card.
  • Read the last 30 days of reviews on community forums and community forums before paying any reseller — 2025 scam operators are flagged quickly.
Scam #3
Granada Station & Alhambra Entrance Pickpockets
⚠️ High
📍 Granada Train Station (Estación de Granada), Alhambra entrance queue, Albaicín narrow streets, Plaza Nueva, Mirador de San Nicolás, Sacromonte paths

Granada is calmer than Barcelona or Madrid, but tourists let their guard down here and that is exactly where organized pickpocket crews work. is the canonical 2025 case: 'Today at around 6–6:30 PM, my $6500 camera was pickpocketed from my backpack at Granada Station.' The victim tracked the camera via AirTag through several outer-ring Granada neighborhoods, but Policía Nacional reportedly declined to pursue the location data. is a named-anchor community warning specifically about the Alhambra approach: 'we definitely were targets of a pickpocket — she followed us' — the ticket queue and the narrow paths through the Generalife gardens are documented target zones.

flags Granada as part of the Andalusia pickpocket corridor despite the city's overall-safer reputation. The patterns match other Spanish cities but concentrate in three Granada-specific spots: (1) the Granada train and bus stations on arrival, when tourists are juggling luggage and consulting phones; (2) the ticket-collection queue at the Alhambra's Puerta de las Granadas, where distraction is easy amid large multi-national groups; (3) the steep alleyways of the Albaicín where crowds converge on the Mirador de San Nicolás viewpoint at sunset.

For older travelers, the practical risk is highest at Granada Station on arrival from Madrid (AVE) or Seville (regional): juggle one bag at a time, keep one hand on your crossbody, and do not put your phone into any outer pocket of a backpack from the moment you board the train.

Red Flags

  • Crowds converge at the Alhambra ticket queue (Puerta de las Granadas) and you feel light brushing against your outer pockets
  • Someone asks for directions or photo help while their companion approaches from your blind side
  • At Granada Station, a person 'falls' into you or creates a commotion immediately after you disembark the AVE
  • At the Mirador de San Nicolás, someone steps unusually close as the sunset crowd presses toward the viewpoint
  • In the Albaicín's narrow streets, two or three people walk abreast behind you for more than a block

How to Avoid

  • Wear a zipped crossbody bag in front of your chest at Granada Station, Alhambra queue, and Albaicín crowds.
  • Never store phones or wallets in backpack outer pockets — the primary access point for lifts.
  • Keep one hand on your bag at all times in any ticket queue, and check pockets every time a stranger approaches.
  • Report thefts to Policía Nacional at Jefatura Superior (Plaza de los Campos, +34 958 808 000) with the denuncia required for travel insurance within 48 hours.
  • At Granada Station, book an official taxi via FreeNow, Cabify, or Uber rather than walking with luggage through the arrival hall.

Scam #4
Rosemary / Palm-Reading 'Bruja' Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Plaza Nueva, Sacromonte paths, Albaicín alleys, near the Alhambra ticket office, Mirador de San Nicolás

A woman in traditional Romani dress steps from a doorway at Plaza Nueva as you walk toward the Alhambra.

She smiles warmly, folds your palm over a sprig of rosemary, and begins reading your fortune before you have time to say no. Her predictions are unusually specific — she mentions a recent loss, someone close to you named Mary, or a decision you are weighing. The 'reading' takes two minutes. Then she asks for €10, €20, sometimes more. If you refuse, she may loudly call to Romani companions, pull at your sleeve, or accuse you of disrespecting her spirits. Most tourists hand over the money just to end the scene.

This is one of Granada's oldest documented scams. gives the historical framing — Sacromonte was the Romani settlement when the community arrived in the 15th century, and the 'bruja' (witch) palm-reading is a centuries-old local variant of the rosemary scam found across Spain. is a named-anchor 2025 first-person account: 'Something strange happened to me today. While in Granada, a gypsy wrote to me with a rosemary leaf, telling me that I would marry a woman I' — the thread debates whether the predictions were genuinely cold-read or whether the interaction crossed into spiritual harassment, but no one disputes that money was demanded at the end. gives the flat traveler rule: 'Avoid like the plague anyone trying to hand you flowers, bunches of rosemary or hell-bent on reading your palm. Don't even acknowledge their' approach.'

For older travelers walking uphill from the Granada town center to the Alhambra, the signature is someone approaching you within a few steps of Plaza Nueva with a smile and an outstretched hand holding a green sprig. Keep hands in pockets, make no eye contact, and say 'No, gracias' clearly without breaking stride.

Red Flags

  • A woman in traditional Romani dress approaches with an outstretched sprig of rosemary or flower
  • She reaches for your palm or hand before you have indicated any interest
  • The reading begins in Spanish or broken English regardless of whether you respond
  • Romani companions are visible nearby watching the interaction
  • Payment demands begin only after the reading is complete, often accompanied by spiritual pressure or guilt

How to Avoid

  • Keep both hands in your pockets when walking between Plaza Nueva, the Albaicín, and Sacromonte.
  • Say 'No, gracias' firmly and do not break stride — engagement itself is the hook.
  • If rosemary lands in your hand, drop it immediately and keep walking without turning back.
  • Do not feel guilty: this is a professional commercial operation, not an elderly stranger giving gifts.
  • If the encounter escalates, step into the nearest café or shop — the operators rarely follow indoors.
Scam #5
'Free Tapas' Tourist-Trap Restaurant Bait-and-Switch
🔶 Medium
📍 Restaurants near the Cathedral and Capilla Real, Calle Elvira tourist strip, Plaza Nueva perimeter, any restaurant with touts outside

Granada is famous for its one genuinely-free tradition: order a drink in most local bars and a tapa arrives free with it. This is real — it is the reason Granada has one of Spain's densest bar cultures, and the reason young locals move from bar to bar rather than sitting down for a full meal. The scam exploits that reputation. Tourist-trap restaurants near the Cathedral and on Calle Elvira promote 'Free Tapas!' in English on their signs, then execute one of several bill-padding tactics: drinks are priced at €8 instead of €3, the tapa that arrives is smaller than the local norm, or 'free' tapas only accompany one specific overpriced cocktail list.

is the named-anchor warning about one specific venue: 'The place looks great inside and all the produce genuinely looks wonderful, unfortunately the guys are scam artists, be mindful if you decide' — the thread comments describe inflated bills, missing items replaced with cheaper substitutes, and pressure to order more rounds. gives the honest local context: 'Granada is a big college town with significant competition for the nightlife population. The restaurants compete by offering the best tapas' — meaning genuine free-tapa culture is competitive and generous in the right venues. reinforces that 'In Granada - most restaurants give you free tapas with whatever' drink you order, so venues advertising this in English to tourists are typically the ones that have rigged the drink prices.' lists tourist-restaurant patterns that repeat across Andalusia.

For older travelers, the practical rule: any venue whose sign or tout promotes 'free tapas' in English is likely playing the drink-price trick. Walk two streets further up into the Albaicín residential grid where locals actually eat — the Calle Calderería Vieja and the streets off Plaza Larga have posted Spanish menus with honest prices.

Red Flags

  • Signs promoting 'Free Tapas!' in English rather than 'tapa con consumición' in Spanish
  • Touts positioned outside actively recruiting passing tourists from the Cathedral-area streets
  • Drinks menu lists beers and glasses of wine at €8+ when the local rate is €2–€3
  • The tapa served is visibly smaller than what other patrons received
  • Bill adds 'cover charge,' 'service,' or 'terrace supplement' not mentioned before ordering

How to Avoid

  • Walk at least two streets uphill from the Cathedral or Plaza Nueva before choosing a bar — try Calle Calderería Vieja or Plaza Larga in the Albaicín.
  • Order drinks in Spanish ('una caña, por favor') at the bar rather than at a table; tapa arrives complimentary automatically in authentic venues.
  • Check the drink price list before ordering — €2–€3 per caña/vino is the local rate, above €5 signals tourist pricing.
  • Skip any venue with a tout outside actively recruiting or promoting 'free tapas' in English.
  • If a bill shows unexpected charges, politely request an itemised receipt and dispute in Spanish: 'No pedí esto.'
Scam #6
Granada Airport (GRX) & Old Town Taxi Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Federico García Lorca Granada Airport (GRX), Granada Train Station taxi rank, Alhambra taxi queue, hotel-arranged pickups

a handful of daily flights from Madrid, Barcelona, and a few European capitals.

a handful of daily flights from Madrid, Barcelona, and a few European capitals. The official licensed taxi fare from GRX to the city center is approximately €29–€35 depending on time of day, with a small supplement for luggage. Unofficial operators at the taxi rank, particularly on holiday weekends, quote 'fixed prices' of €40–€60 that have nothing to do with the legal meter rate. One traveler wrote: 'Do NOT take Taxis here - charging me 40€ over the Easter weekend as a fixed price from the airport to the city!' captures the structural problem: 'Granada is a tiny airport with very few flights, and even fewer taxis. We waited a good 30 minutes as taxis trickled in and picked up one' at a time.

In the city center, documents the parallel 'listo driver' pattern: 'when the odd listo driver tries to scam you taking the long way you can easily contest it on the app' — meaning app-hailed Cabify or Uber rides create a GPS record you can dispute, while street-hailed taxis leave you defenceless.

For older travelers arriving with luggage, the practical recommendation is: (1) if taking a taxi, confirm in writing the approximate €29–€35 fare before boarding; (2) if using Cabify or Uber, screenshot the fare estimate and pickup address; (3) for maximum safety, use the Airport Bus to Palacio de Congresos then a short hotel taxi.

Red Flags

  • Driver refuses to quote approximate fare before boarding
  • Driver claims the meter is 'broken' or adds a 'fixed price' significantly above €35 to the city center
  • App pickup shows your starting location moving further from the airport to inflate the metered distance
  • Driver takes routes that bypass the direct A-92 or GR-30 highway into central Granada
  • No official taxi-rank receipt offered at arrival

How to Avoid

  • Use Cabify, Uber, or FreeNow for GRX airport pickup — screenshot the fare estimate on booking.
  • If using a licensed taxi, confirm the approximate €29–€35 fare before boarding and request a receipt.
  • Take the Airport Bus (€3, every 30–90 minutes) to Palacio de Congresos as a pickpocket-free alternative.
  • For Alhambra taxi transfers from the city center, use app-hailed rides to avoid 'queue jumping' from unofficial operators.
  • Report overcharges to Policía Municipal Granada with the driver's taxi number photographed from the rear windscreen.
Scam #7
Sacromonte Flamenco Cave & Street-Tout Tour Hustle
🔶 Medium
📍 Cathedral area touts, Sacromonte cave venues (Cueva de la Rocío, La Chumbera, and copycat venues), Albaicín viewpoint paths

the Romani community performs zambra flamenco in whitewashed hillside caves with centuries of lineage.

the Romani community performs zambra flamenco in whitewashed hillside caves with centuries of lineage. The problem is that 'authentic' is exactly the marketing hook tourist-trap copies use, and the streets near the Cathedral are crowded with touts pushing 'cave flamenco' packages that deliver a watered-down tourist show at 3x the price. flags the named scam directly: 'main scam to watch is fake flamenco ticket sellers near the cathedral, always buy from official venues. pickpockets around sacromonte too so' — both the tout ticket and the venue-walk itself are risk points.

The commercial packages typically include a bus from a pickup point, a 'traditional' dinner (often mediocre), and a flamenco show at a generic cave with B-list performers. The cost can be €70–€110 per person. A genuine zambra at Cueva de la Rocío or La Chumbera — Sacromonte's two community-respected venues — runs €28–€45 for the show alone, booked directly from the venue's website. reinforces the cross-Andalusia pattern: 'See Flamenco in Seville or Granada (in Sacromonte)' — always at the real venues, never through a street tout.

For older travelers on a Madrid-based day trip or a one-night Granada visit, the practical rule is: book your flamenco show directly at a community-respected venue (Cueva de la Rocío, La Chumbera, Venta el Gallo) from the venue's website. If a concierge, hotel, or street tout pushes 'exclusive' cave flamenco, politely decline and walk on.

Red Flags

  • A tout near the Cathedral offers 'authentic' cave flamenco at a fixed €70–€110 price with transport and dinner
  • The package includes pickup at a location that is not the venue itself
  • The venue name is unfamiliar and does not appear in community forums recent threads
  • Hotel concierge pushes a partner cave venue with 'exclusive' pricing
  • Bundled Alhambra + flamenco + tapas tour at an unreasonably low price (typical bait for the cancellation-upsell scam)

How to Avoid

  • Book flamenco directly at community-respected venues: Cueva de la Rocío (cuevalarocio.es), La Chumbera, Venta el Gallo.
  • Never buy flamenco tickets from a street tout or accept a 'package' including transport, dinner, and show.
  • If you want a full flamenco evening experience, book your own taxi to Sacromonte and a show-only ticket from the venue.
  • Verify the venue on community forums and Google Maps reviews before paying anything.
  • For older travelers, the early 8 PM or 9 PM show is safer than the 11 PM — Sacromonte paths are steep and dark after hours.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Policía Nacional or Guardia Civil station. Call 091 (Policía Nacional) or 112 (emergency). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at policia.es.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy is at Calle de Serrano, 75, 28006 Madrid. For emergencies: +34 91 587-2200.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
