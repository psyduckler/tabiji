8 Tourist Scams in Palermo

Key Takeaways

  • Via Maqueda is Palermo's primary scippo (scooter bag-snatch) corridor — a September 2022 Giornale di Sicilia arrest of a 29-year-old who stripped €800 and a smartphone from a Serbian tourist, a December 2024 GDS case of a boy-gang near the Quattro Canti who snatched an American tourist's iPhone and fled toward Ballarò, and a March 2025 Blog Sicilia arrest of a 17-year-old Palermitan on an electric scooter all document the same corridor and MO in the last three years
  • 4 of 8 scams are rated high risk — Palermo's scam profile is thinner than Rome's or Venice's on theatrical tourist-menu stings but heavier on mobility-based theft (scooter-scippo, Bus 101 pickpocketing) and on car-targeted scams (specchietto, parcheggiatori abusivi) that most European city guides don't cover
  • The official Falcone-Borsellino airport taxi fare is regulated at €39 to the Viale Michelangelo/Viale Lazio zone, €44 to the historic centre (Corso Calatafimi to Corso Vittorio Emanuele), and €50 to the furthest city zone — anything above is a scam, and the Prestia e Comandè shuttle (€6.30, every 30 minutes, 5am–12:30am) makes the question moot
  • The truffa dello specchietto — a scooter driver throwing an object at your rental car and demanding €100–€300 cash for a fake mirror strike — is documented by a March 2026 GDS arrest of a 20-year-old from Catania on Viale Regione Siciliana, and flagged on r/ItalyTravel as the Palermo scam "most people are too lazy to report"

⚡ Quick Safety Tips

  • On Via Maqueda and Via Roma, walk on the inside of the sidewalk with your bag crossbody on the side away from the road — scooters need curb access for a snatch, so denying it is the single best defence
  • Take the Prestia e Comandè shuttle (€6.30) from Punta Raisi airport rather than a taxi; if you must take a taxi, confirm the fixed fare (€39/€44/€50 depending on zone) before closing the door and check the meter reads €0 at the start
  • Never pay cash roadside to anyone claiming you damaged their scooter mirror — drive to the nearest police station and call 112; the specchietto scam depends entirely on victims wanting to avoid paperwork
  • At markets (Ballarò, Vucciria, Il Capo) and Quattro Canti restaurants, ask "quanto costa?" and confirm euros before ordering any seafood or fresh fish — hidden al etto pricing is the dominant overcharge mechanism
  • Use paid garages (Parking Massimo, Parcheggio Giotto, Parcheggio Politeama) at €10–€15/day rather than street-parking anywhere near Teatro Massimo or La Cala — parcheggiatori abusivi have a documented history of keying cars that refuse to pay

The 8 Scams

Scam #1
The Via Maqueda Scooter Scippo (Bag- and Phone-Snatch)
⚠️ High
📍 Via Maqueda (especially the stretch from Quattro Canti south to Ballarò), Via Roma, Piazza Indipendenza, Corso Vittorio Emanuele

A scooter — often electric to stay quiet — comes up behind you on Via Maqueda, rips your bag or phone from the hand, and is 100 metres away before you turn around. The Quattro Canti and the Maqueda-to-Ballarò corridor account for most of the documented Palermo scippi.

A September 2, 2022 Giornale di Sicilia arrest documents the textbook version: a 29-year-old Palermitan without a fixed address pulled alongside a Serbian tourist on Via Maqueda on a motorscooter, reversed direction, and ripped her bag containing €800 cash, a smartphone, and documents — arrested after the victim identified him in surveillance photos. A December 1, 2024 Giornale di Sicilia case describes a group of teenagers surrounding an American tourist at the Quattro Canti, snatching an iPhone, and fleeing toward Ballarò market. A March 30, 2025 Blog Sicilia arrest documents a 17-year-old Palermitan who on an electric scooter knocked a passerby to the ground on Via Maqueda and took his phone — and whom Chinese tourists separately identified as the perpetrator of an earlier phone theft from them the same day; the same operation ("Alto Impatto") controlled 313 people in the Vucciria that weekend. An r/sicily "Stopped a pickpocket in Palermo" post from 2025 describes the parallel bar-and-restaurant variant, where a friend's phone was lifted while she was seated. The corridor's geometry — a pedestrianised stretch that scooters use as a chute despite the signs — concentrates the problem.

Red Flags

  • You hear an electric scooter whine behind you as you walk near the Quattro Canti, Teatro Massimo, or Via Maqueda
  • A group of teenage boys is clustered at a street corner in central Palermo with no apparent reason to linger
  • Your bag or phone is in your curb-side hand, within reach of a passing scooter
  • A stranger makes eye contact and seems to measure you from across the street
  • You are walking and looking at your phone — "screen-walking" is the single biggest attractor

How to Avoid

  • Carry your bag crossbody on the side away from the road, with the zipper against your body and your hand on the strap
  • Walk on the inside of the sidewalk — force a scooter to cut across your body to reach a bag, which they almost never do
  • Put your phone away between shots; never walk-and-scroll on Via Maqueda or Via Roma
  • If something is snatched, shout "al ladro!" and note the scooter plate — Palermo police make arrests from CCTV and license-plate matches regularly
  • At night stay on the busy Quattro Canti-to-Teatro Massimo stretch and avoid isolated Ballarò side streets
Scam #2
Palermo Centrale & Bus 101 Pickpockets
⚠️ High
📍 Palermo Centrale station (platforms, forecourt, Via Lincoln), Bus 101 route from Piazza Giulio Cesare up Via Roma and Via della Libertà

You arrive on a regionale from Trapani or Catania at Palermo Centrale. On the forecourt or on the crowded Bus 101 northbound, a team of two is already tagging your back pocket. Via Lincoln — the street fronting the station — is where robberies of tourists have reached the point of armed assault.

A Palermo Today arrest report documents a 68-year-old Hungarian tourist who had his wallet stripped at Palermo Centrale and didn't notice until confronted by officers; another Palermo Today case documents two pickpockets denounced on Bus 101 — one sliding the wallet out of a tourist's pocket, the other emptying it in a coordinated hand-off. Palermo Today's Via Lincoln arrest report documents the more violent end of the spectrum: a Vietnamese diplomat was assaulted and robbed on the street directly fronting the station. A Palermo Today "Vucciria, ladro di collane scippa e trascina a terra turista svizzera" report shows the same MO one street over — a Swiss tourist had a gold necklace torn off and was dragged to the ground, with an off-duty policeman from the Oreto-stazione commissariat intervening to stop the attack. Bus 101 is the main tourist artery from the station to the Mondello beach suburbs and carries the highest target density in the city; locals on r/sicily explicitly name it as the route most-worked by pickpocket teams. The "Alto Impatto" operation weekends centre precisely on this station-to-Vucciria axis.

Red Flags

  • A stranger shoves to board Bus 101 at Piazza Giulio Cesare or at the Teatro Politeama stop, creating a doorway crush
  • Someone asks for the time or for directions on the Palermo Centrale forecourt while their partner hovers behind you
  • You are wearing a backpack or have a wallet in a back pocket at the station or on a crowded bus
  • You feel a deliberate push, trip, or bag-bump as the train doors open
  • You are on Via Lincoln after dark, especially near the corners fronting the station

How to Avoid

  • On Bus 101 and other tourist routes, keep your bag in front of you and your wallet in a zipped front pocket — never a back pocket, never a backpack outer pouch
  • Exit Palermo Centrale toward Piazza Giulio Cesare, not the dim Via Lincoln corners after dark — take the signed taxi rank only
  • Don't hold your phone out while boarding the bus or buying a ticket at a self-service machine — pre-load tickets via the AMAT app
  • If a stranger creates a fuss at the door (asking the time, shoving to board, "falling"), step back a pace and place a hand on your bag — this is the distraction cue
  • Report to 113 on the spot if pickpocketed — Palermo state police patrol the station actively and have a track record of on-platform arrests
Scam #3
The Punta Raisi Airport Taxi Overcharge
⚠️ High
📍 Falcone-Borsellino (Punta Raisi) airport taxi rank, 35 km northwest of Palermo

The Palermo Falcone-Borsellino airport taxi fare is regulated: €39 to the Viale Michelangelo/Viale Lazio zone, €44 to the historic centre between Corso Calatafimi and Corso Vittorio Emanuele, and €50 maximum to Brancaccio or the station. Some drivers pre-load the meter at €10, quote €60–€80 "flat", or use abusivi cars that aren't bound by the tariff at all.

A TripAdvisor Taxi Palermo review from August 22, 2023, documents the pre-loaded-meter scam in detail: a Darlington, UK tourist paid €15 for a 900-metre ride after the meter started already at €10; when the same driver tried the trick a second time the same day, the reviewer caught it and the driver blamed confusion ("I was coming from the station") and reset the meter. Palermo Today's "Il taxi dall'aeroporto costa massimo 50 euro" report documents the broader pattern — a Palermitan professional was charged above the fixed fare from Punta Raisi to the university, prompting the proposal to require visible fare stickers on all airport taxis. A Facebook post on Palermo Today documents: "Un professionista palermitano: 'Non è stata rispettata la tariffa fissa prevista…'" — not an isolated incident but the norm enough to prompt municipal intervention. The Prestia e Comandè shuttle (€6.30 single, every 30 minutes 5am–12:30am, direct to Piazza Ruggero Settimo and Stazione Centrale) is the professional traveler's answer and runs for most flight arrivals; the Punta Raisi–Palermo Centrale train (€5.80, 45–60 minutes via a free shuttle from terminal to Punta Raisi station) is even cheaper. Abusivi — unlicensed drivers who approach you inside arrivals with "taxi? city?" — are not bound by the regulated tariff and charge whatever they want.

Red Flags

  • The meter starts above €0, or the driver refuses to show you the starting fare before leaving the airport
  • A driver approaches you inside the arrivals hall offering "taxi? city?" — these are abusivi not bound by the regulated tariff
  • The quoted "flat fare" is above €50 for the airport-to-city trip (the maximum regulated fare)
  • The credit card reader is suddenly "broken" and you are steered to an ATM — a universal taxi-scam tell
  • The vehicle has no visible TAXI roof sign, no license number on the door, and the driver has no visible ID badge

How to Avoid

  • Take the Prestia e Comandè shuttle (€6.30 one-way, every 30 minutes, 5am–12:30am) to Piazza Ruggero Settimo or Stazione Centrale — the booth is clearly signed at arrivals
  • For taxis, only use the signed rank outside arrivals, and confirm the fixed fare (€39/€44/€50 depending on zone) before closing the door
  • Check the meter reads €0 when you start; if it reads above €0 or "fuori servizio", get out and take the next cab
  • Pay by card where possible so the transaction is traceable; always demand a ricevuta with taxi number, time, and route
  • Refuse any driver approaching you inside the terminal — those are unlicensed NCC operators and every tourist overcharge report traces back to them
Scam #4
Truffa dello Specchietto (Side-Mirror Crash Scam)
⚠️ High
📍 Viale Regione Siciliana, Palermo city ring road, highway approaches, rental-car pickup routes, toll booths

You are driving a rental on Viale Regione Siciliana. A scooter pulls alongside, throws a small object at your door, and the driver insists at the next light that you clipped his mirror — he will take €200 cash right now to avoid "insurance headaches." The mirror was never hit.

A March 31, 2026 Giornale di Sicilia arrest report documents the specchietto playbook with forensic clarity: a 20-year-old from Catania — a repeat offender already denunced for the same fraud — threw an object at a woman's car on Viale Regione Siciliana in Palermo to simulate a collision, then manoeuvred to force her to stop. She stayed calm and kept driving toward a safer area; when Carabinieri in Carini located the scammer after her complaint, they caught him attempting the same scam again on another driver, and he was placed under obbligo di dimora. The r/ItalyTravel thread "Is all of Italy as bad as Palermo for scammers?" flags specchietto as the Palermo scam "most people are too lazy to call the police about" — which is the precise reason it keeps running. Variants include a confederate on a second scooter who "witnesses" the impact, a driver who produces a "broken" mirror from under his seat as proof, and the pressure phrase "paghiamo in contanti, evitiamo l'assicurazione" (let's pay cash and avoid insurance) — always the tell. The scam is most prevalent on Palermo's ring road (Viale Regione Siciliana) and autostrada approaches where rental cars with non-Sicilian plates cluster.

Red Flags

  • You hear an unusual thump on your car followed by a scooter immediately flashing its lights or honking
  • The other driver demands you stop right now on a slip road, light, or hard shoulder — never a safe public place
  • The damage is a perfectly clean broken side mirror with no paint transfer from your car
  • The driver pushes "paghiamo in contanti, evitiamo assicurazione" — the classic specchietto phrase
  • A second scooter arrives as a "witness" — these are confederates, not bystanders

How to Avoid

  • If you hear a thump, do not stop where the other driver dictates — drive calmly to the nearest well-lit petrol station, police station, or Carabinieri post, then call 112
  • Never pay cash roadside for any claimed damage — insurance and the carta verde exist precisely for this, and there is no legitimate scenario that requires cash on Viale Regione Siciliana
  • If you stop, immediately photograph the other vehicle, plate, driver, and the alleged damage before any discussion
  • Call 112 the moment a stranger demands money for a traffic incident — the police take specchietto calls seriously and have a track record of in-flagrante arrests
  • Consider not renting a car if you are staying only in Palermo — most tourists never need one, and taking it off the table removes the scam's target entirely
Scam #5
Ballarò, Vucciria & Capo Market Tourist Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Mercato di Ballarò (Piazza del Carmine), Mercato della Vucciria (Piazza Caracciolo), Mercato del Capo (Via Beati Paoli / Via Sant'Agostino)

You follow Instagram into Ballarò for pane ca' meusa. The stallholder waves you to a plastic chair, piles fritto misto on a paper plate, and hands you a bill for €32 for six frozen shrimp and a cold grilled squid. There is no posted price, there is no receipt, and the stall is gone when you come back.

An October 2022 TripAdvisor review from a native Italian (127-review reviewer "240carmelad") documents the textbook Ballarò overcharge: €32 for "6 frozen shrimp and a cold grilled squid" — "un furto legalizzato" (a legalised theft). Palermo Today's own dossier "Capo, Vucciria e Ballarò: ecco perché i mercati storici di Palermo rischiano di scomparire" makes the structural diagnosis in its own voice: "non sono più mercati ma street food dove l'igiene latita e i prezzi sono fatti in funzione del turista" — no longer markets but tourist-priced street food with missing hygiene. r/palermo_city's Markets thread is blunter: "Markets are a scam, avoid them at all cost especially if you're a tourist that doesn't speak italian. Restaurants offer better food for cheaper." r/sicily's "Why are so many small supermarkets trying to scam tourists?" thread extends the pattern to the retail shops lining Via Maqueda. The overcharge mechanisms cluster in three patterns: fresh seafood priced "al etto" (per 100g) that arrives as a 400g portion billed at five times expectation; Aperol Spritz or house wine at €10–€15 in Vucciria nightlife; and "your menu is different from the Italian menu" two-tier pricing. The historic markets have become more street-food-court than traditional market — some legitimate stalls remain, but they do not cluster on the tourist-obvious pathways.

Red Flags

  • No price visible on any stall sign, chalkboard, or counter card
  • A barker calls to you in English ("my friend! ciao amici!") from outside the stall
  • Fish, seafood, or meat priced "al etto" without a menu portion size
  • The queue is made up almost entirely of visible tourists rather than locals doing their daily shop
  • The Aperol or cocktail price is not posted — at night in Vucciria this is the dominant overcharge

How to Avoid

  • Ask "quanto costa?" before ordering anything and repeat the price back in euros — a stallholder who refuses to give a number is the signal to leave
  • Prefer known chains with posted menus — Nni Franco u' Vastiddaru, Antica Focacceria San Francesco, I Cuochini — where prices are public and non-negotiable
  • Pay in cash with small notes (€5, €10) so you cannot be given "the wrong change" as a cover for overcharge
  • Go at midday when locals shop rather than 8pm when the stall has shifted to the tourist-cocktail crowd
  • In Vucciria at night, go to places with visible menu cards and credit-card readers, not the folding-chair improvised bars on Piazza Caracciolo
Scam #6
The Parcheggiatori Abusivi Parking Extortion
🔶 Medium
📍 Teatro Massimo, La Cala port, Piazza Marina, Piazza Borsa, Via Volturno near Palermo Centrale, San Giovanni degli Eremiti, Piazza San Francesco di Paola

You pull into a free curb space near Teatro Massimo. A man in a high-viz vest that isn't actually official walks over and tells you parking here is €5 — in cash, to him. You can refuse. If you do, your rental may come back to you with a long key-scratch down the door.

An April 2017 Carabinieri "Alto Impatto" blitz between La Cala port and Teatro Massimo fined 36 parcheggiatori abusivi — all Palermitan men aged 40–50, operating at Piazza Marina, Piazza Borsa, Via Volturno near Palermo Centrale, and around Policlinico "Paolo Giaccone" — €772 each, for a total of €27,792 in fines. The mechanism is documented: approach drivers arriving at their car and demand "one or two euros" for a secure parking spot. Palermo Today's "Piazza San Francesco di Paola, non paga il posteggiatore abusivo" report documents the consequence of refusing: a woman who declined to pay came back to find her car's side "completamente rigata" (completely keyed), with a 28-year-old later charged. A TripAdvisor Sicily forum entry from a visitor reports being asked for €10 "daily parking" at San Giovanni degli Eremiti, which the abusivo bargained to €5. A 2023 r/ItalyTravel "Driving in Palermo is a nightmare please help" thread documents the same pattern at a parking meter: the attendant "just yells at me over and over. I pay him his 'car watch' fee" — a local comment confirming the same thing happens to residents, not just tourists. r/palermo_city residents note on Facebook that "ormai comandano loro" (they run the show) in the Castello/centro zone.

Red Flags

  • A man in an unofficial high-viz vest waves you into a parking spot you already planned to take
  • He demands cash up-front (€1–€10) and cannot produce any city-issued ID or receipt
  • He reduces the price sharply when questioned (€10 → €5) — a legitimate fee doesn't bargain
  • You are parked near Teatro Massimo, La Cala, Quattro Canti, San Giovanni degli Eremiti, or a hospital
  • No pay-and-display parcometro is nearby — the abusivo has chosen a zone where he's the only option

How to Avoid

  • Use paid ZTL-compliant garages (Parking Massimo, Parcheggio Giotto, Parcheggio Politeama) at €10–€15/day — cheaper than a keyed car
  • If approached in a legitimate street spot, refuse politely ("no grazie") and walk away; paying normalises extortion but the direct risk is the keying retaliation, not assault
  • Never leave valuables, luggage, or rental documents visible in the car — a scratched car is a warning, a broken window is the sequel
  • If your car is damaged after refusing, file a denuncia at the nearest Polizia Municipale — 36 fines in a single 2017 blitz prove the city prosecutes these cases
  • Consider not renting a car for your Palermo stay at all — the historic centre is walkable, the airport has a €6.30 shuttle, and day-trips use Trenitalia or organised tours
Scam #7
The Mondello Beach Lido-Access & Umbrella Theft
🔶 Medium
📍 Mondello beach, Viale Regina Elena, Lido Mondello lots and adjoining spiaggia libera; Viale Principe di Scalea car parks

Mondello is a crescent of white sand 12 km north of Palermo that Mondello Italo Belga has operated since 1909 — and for decades has partitioned with turnstiles and fences. You think you're paying for entry to the beach. You're actually paying for an umbrella-and-lounger "lido" that exists inside what is legally a public beach. And while you swim, the minor behind you is opening your bag.

An August 1, 2025 Giornale di Sicilia report documents the Mondello access dispute: the Coast Guard and Guardia di Finanza opened investigations into the Mondello Italo Belga turnstiles after the regional assessor Giusi Savarino ruled that "it is not possible to deny access to the sea through turnstiles and fences." Regional circulars in 2025 ordered removal; some turnstiles came down (per an August 21, 2025 citizen Facebook post celebrating "the death of the turnstiles after 20 years"), but the gray zone between "free beach" and paid lido remains, and informal concessionaires still charge entry where they shouldn't. A Palermo Today "Mondello, più furti in spiaggia 'Gli autori sono spesso minori'" report documents the parallel problem: a reader writes that "4 family members and friends were hit" by borseggi and estorsioni on the sand, with thieves — often minors — lifting phones, wallets, and cash from towels while swimmers are in the water. The Linkiesta 2025 investigation "Scandalo al sole" notes Sicily's coast has gone from 15% to 22.4% concessioned in five years — and counting closures for erosion and pollution, 76.5% of the shoreline has compromised free access. A 2024 r/sicily "A beach city + Taormina" thread has r/sicily locals actively second-guessing a Mondello recommendation "after reading some reviews."

Red Flags

  • Someone demands a beach-entry "fee" at a turnstile or fence — regional rulings say this is illegal for simple sea access
  • You cannot see clearly where the paid lido ends and the spiaggia libera (free beach) begins
  • A minor or teenager lingers near your towel while you enter the water — classic on-beach theft MO
  • No receipt is offered for an umbrella-and-lounger payment
  • You are being directed to pay cash to someone wearing a t-shirt rather than a uniform with a lido logo

How to Avoid

  • Know in advance where the spiaggia libera is — look for green-and-white public-access signs and use them rather than paying for "entry"
  • Legitimate lido umbrella-and-lounger rental is €15–€30/day and comes with a receipt; anyone asking more or refusing a receipt is not a licensed concessionaire
  • Take only what you need to the beach — phone in a waterproof pouch around your neck, credit cards left in the hotel safe
  • Never leave a bag on a towel while swimming; designate one person to stay with belongings or use a lido with a locker
  • Take the Bus 806 from Teatro Politeama rather than driving — Mondello parking lots are a documented secondary target for car break-ins
Scam #8
The Quattro Canti Tourist-Menu Trap
🔶 Medium
📍 Quattro Canti (Via Maqueda × Corso Vittorio Emanuele), Piazza Pretoria, Piazza Bellini, Piazza San Domenico

You sit down at a restaurant one block from the Quattro Canti and order pesce spada. The menu has four languages and glossy photos. The bill arrives with €4/head coperto, a 15% servizio on your foreign card, a €6 "pane e coperto" you didn't order, and the swordfish billed "al etto" at €9 per 100g for a 550g fillet.

r/ItalyTravel's comment thread on Palermo scams is flat: "Palermo was full of hidden fees and little scammers" — a structural diagnosis of restaurant and small-supermarket practice in the Quattro Canti axis. r/ItalyTravel commenters name the mechanisms repeatedly across similar Italian city traps — unmarked coperto over €3, automatic 10–15% servizio applied to foreign cards where it is never applied to Italian ones, unrequested pane/acqua/antipasti arriving "for free" and then billed, and fresh fish priced "al etto" with no portion-size clarity. Italian law — the Ministry of Economic Development's transparency rules — requires every restaurant to post its menu with prices in public view outside the door; if the menu you saw inside doesn't match the outside menu, or if there is no outside menu at all, the restaurant is non-compliant and almost always a trap. r/palermo_city's "Markets" thread notes tartly that the same over-pricing practice extends from the market stalls to the sit-down restaurants in the same tourist axis. The cluster around Piazza Pretoria (the "Fontana della Vergogna") and Piazza Bellini — both prime photo stops — concentrates the problem because tourists are looking for anywhere to sit with a view and the barkers fill that demand.

Red Flags

  • A tout stands outside calling "ciao amici!" or "my friend, best Sicilian food!" to passing tourists
  • The posted outside menu has no prices for fish or steak, or no menu at all (Italian law requires prices visible outside)
  • The inside menu is four or more languages with glossy photos — a universal tourist-trap tell
  • Pane, acqua, antipasti, or olives arrive without you ordering them — each will be billed
  • The coperto line on the menu is above €3 or buried in fine print

How to Avoid

  • Walk 5–10 minutes outside the Quattro Canti axis — r/palermo_city locals recommend Borgo Vecchio, Via del Genio, or Ballarò daytime trattorias
  • Photograph the full outside menu before sitting; confirm at sit-down: "mi conferma il coperto e il servizio?"
  • Order fish and seafood in whole portions, not "al etto", and confirm the total euro price before the order goes in
  • Pay cash — foreign-card "servizio" surcharges mysteriously vanish when cards are not involved
  • Trust Italian-language Google reviews sorted by Most Recent over English reviews: a venue at 4.5 in Italian and 3.8 in English is authentic; the reverse is a trap

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Carabinieri / Polizia di Stato station. Call 112 (Carabinieri) or 113 (Polizia). The Palermo Questura and Commissariato Centro handle tourist reports on Via Maqueda and Quattro Canti incidents; the Commissariato Oreto-Stazione covers Palermo Centrale and Vucciria. You can also report online at poliziadistato.it.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately and use in-app blocks. On-beach Mondello and Via Maqueda snatch-thieves often attempt an ATM cashout within 10 minutes — freeze cards in under two minutes.

🛂 Lost Passport?

The nearest U.S. Consulate General is in Naples — Piazza della Repubblica, 80122 Naples, +39 081-583-8111. The U.S. Embassy in Rome (Via Vittorio Veneto 121, 00187 Rome; +39 06-4674-1) handles emergencies for US citizens in Sicily. UK citizens use the British Consulate in Naples.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen from a scooter-scippo, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device immediately. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police (113) instead; Palermo CCTV and license-plate matching make recoveries possible when reported fast.
