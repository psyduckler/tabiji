The 2 Scams

Scam #1
The Limoncello Factory Tour Upsell
🟢 Low
📍 Shops along Corso Italia

Retail shops along Sorrento's Corso Italia lure tourists with a "free limoncello factory tour" — a five-minute walk to a back room with lemons — then use multiple rounds of free tastings to soften resistance to a gift-shop pitch where €25 bottles sell for what supermarkets charge €8.

Sorrento's lemon-country reputation makes limoncello a natural purchase, and shops along Corso Italia lean on that by posting "Free factory tour — authentic family recipe" signs at passing visitors. The tour itself is cheerfully informal: a guide walks you through a small production room where actual lemons sit in casks, making it feel like a genuine producer visit.

The real purpose becomes clear at the tasting table, where three to four successively pricier varieties are poured in quick succession. By the time the last sample arrives, social lubrication and sunk-cost feeling combine to make a polite refusal genuinely hard. The "artisanal" limoncello presented at €25–30 a bottle is the same mass-market product that supermarkets nearby stock for €7–10.

Treat the free tasting as a pleasant stop but make any limoncello purchase at a supermarket or enoteca, where authentic options cost €7–10 and you can compare labels without sales pressure. If you want a genuine producer experience, Limonoro — a real Sorrento family operation — offers tours with honest retail prices. Enjoying the samples without buying is perfectly acceptable and requires only a polite "grazie, no."

Red Flags

  • 'Free' tasting or tour from a retail shop
  • Generous alcohol samples before the sales pitch
  • Claims of 'artisanal' or 'family recipe' without verification
  • Price 2-3x what supermarkets charge

How to Avoid

  • Enjoy the free samples — but buy limoncello at a supermarket or enoteca.
  • Authentic limoncello is €6-10 at grocery stores, not €25.
  • If you want a genuine factory tour, visit a real producer like Limonoro.
  • The tasting is nice — just don't feel obligated to buy.
Scam #2
The Unlicensed Capri Boat Tour
🔶 Medium
📍 Marina Grande, Sorrento port

Unlicensed touts at Sorrento's Marina Grande sell bundled Capri day-trips that appear cheaper than official packages, but conceal a mandatory €15 Blue Grotto entry fee, deliver "lunch" as a single panino, and strand passengers on an overcrowded boat waiting hours for the return crossing.

Capri is Sorrento's most popular day-trip, and the official hydrofoil alone costs €30 round trip — so a waterfront tout offering a "boat tour + Blue Grotto + lunch" all-in bundle at an appealing price looks like a genuine find. These operators work Marina Grande's edges, often positioning themselves away from the official terminal and handing over no printed receipt or company name.

Once the boat leaves, the gap between promise and reality opens quickly. Blue Grotto entry costs €15 per person and arrives as an on-the-spot demand the tout never mentioned. The "included lunch" turns out to be a panino distributed onboard. The boat carries far more passengers than the pitch implied, and the return crossing depends on the operator's schedule — routinely adding two or three hours of waiting at Capri's busy port.

Book any Capri day-trip through Viator, GetYourGuide, or your accommodation so you have a verified operator, a written itinerary, and a clear refund path if the trip doesn't match what was sold. Budget €80–120 per person for a legitimate full-day tour; a quote substantially below that range signals hidden costs or cut corners. Always ask explicitly whether the Blue Grotto entry fee is included — a licensed operator will answer without hesitation.

Red Flags

  • All-inclusive price that seems too good for what's promised
  • No company name on the ticket or receipt
  • Meeting point is away from the official port terminal
  • Lunch described vaguely without specifics

How to Avoid

  • Book Capri tours through Viator, GetYourGuide, or your hotel for verified operators.
  • Budget €80-120 per person for a legitimate full-day Capri tour.
  • The Blue Grotto has a separate €15 entry — any operator should disclose this.
  • Check if the boat has a visible registration number.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Carabinieri / Polizia di Stato station. Call 112 (Carabinieri) or 113 (Polizia). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at poliziadistato.it.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Rome is at Via Vittorio Veneto 121, 00187 Rome. For emergencies: +39 06-4674-1.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
