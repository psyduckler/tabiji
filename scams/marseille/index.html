The 13 Scams

Scam #1
Friendship Bracelet/String Scam
🟢 Low
📍 Old Port (Vieux-Port), La Canebière, Tourist areas

Someone approaches you in a friendly manner and loops a colored string or bracelet around your ...

Someone approaches you in a friendly manner and loops a colored string or bracelet around your wrist before you can refuse. Once it's tied on, they claim it's a 'gift' and then aggressively demand cash payment, typically 5-20 euros. They may physically grab your wrist to tie the bracelet, making it difficult to refuse. The scammer creates social pressure by making a scene if you try to walk away without paying.

Red Flags

  • Stranger approaching with string or yarn
  • Attempt to touch your wrist or hand
  • Overly friendly approach with immediate physical contact
  • Working in pairs or groups

How to Avoid

  • Keep your hands in your pockets when walking through tourist areas
  • Say 'Non, merci' firmly and keep walking
  • Do not stop to engage in conversation
  • If they grab your wrist, firmly remove their hand and walk away
Scam #2
Gold Ring Scam
🟢 Low
📍 Old Port (Vieux-Port), La Canebière, Near train stations, Tourist walkways

A passer-by 'finds' a gold ring on the ground near you and picks it up, showing it to you and ...

A passer-by 'finds' a gold ring on the ground near you and picks it up, showing it to you and claiming it's yours or that you must have dropped it. When you say it's not yours, they offer to let you have it as it 'must be your lucky day.' They then ask for a reward or 'finder's fee' for their 'honesty.' The ring is actually cheap brass or gold-plated and worthless, worth perhaps a few cents.

Red Flags

  • Someone conveniently 'finding' jewelry right next to you
  • The ring looks too good to be true
  • Insistence that you should have it
  • Request for money in exchange

How to Avoid

  • Ignore anyone who approaches claiming to have found something valuable
  • Say 'Non, ce n'est pas à moi' (No, it's not mine) and walk away
  • Never accept items from strangers
  • Do not engage in conversation about found items
Scam #3
Pickpocketing
⚠️ High
📍 Vieux-Port (Old Port), Gare Saint-Charles train station, Metro Line 1, La Canebière, Crowded markets, Beach shuttles to Calanques, Old Port tourist trains

Marseille has significant pickpocketing issues, especially in tourist areas.

Thieves often work in groups using distraction techniques. Common methods include: children swarming tourists near the train station while accomplices steal valuables; someone asking for directions while another person lifts your wallet; creating a commotion or 'accidentally' bumping into you; standing unusually close on the metro, especially when doors open and close (thieves grab items and step off quickly). The area around Gare Saint-Charles has a particularly heavy concentration of pickpockets.

Red Flags

  • Someone standing too close in uncrowded areas
  • Children approaching in groups
  • People asking for directions while others crowd around
  • Sudden jostling or commotion
  • Someone spilling something on you

How to Avoid

  • Wear a cross-body bag in front, not behind
  • Keep phones out of back pockets
  • Never sling a bag over a chair back at outdoor tables
  • Use a money belt for large amounts of cash and cards
  • Be extra vigilant when metro doors open and close
  • Avoid displaying expensive watches, cameras, or jewelry
  • Keep only what you need for the day; leave valuables in hotel safe

Scam #4
Fake Charity Petition Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Vieux-Port, Near tourist attractions, Gare Saint-Charles, La Canebière

A person approaches with a clipboard, often claiming to be deaf-mute, and asks if you speak English.

They then request you sign a 'petition' for a charity, supposedly supporting deaf or disabled people. The petition is in English (a red flag in France) and looks official. After signing, they demand a donation, pointing to text on the paper claiming you agreed to donate when you signed. While you're distracted signing, an accomplice may be going through your pockets or bag. Even if there's no accomplice, you may feel pressured into giving money for a fake cause.

Red Flags

  • Petition written in English rather than French
  • Person claiming to be deaf-mute
  • Aggressive approach in tourist areas
  • Request for immediate cash donation after signing
  • Multiple people with clipboards working the same area

How to Avoid

  • Never stop for clipboard petitioners
  • Say 'Non' firmly and keep walking
  • Never sign anything on the street
  • Keep hands in pockets and maintain distance
  • If approached, check that your belongings are secure immediately
Scam #5
Three Card Monte / Shell Game
🔶 Medium
📍 Busy tourist areas, Near the Old Port, Public squares, Near train stations

You encounter a group gathered around someone playing a simple card game (find the queen) or shell ...

You encounter a group gathered around someone playing a simple card game (find the queen) or shell game (find the ball under the cup). The game looks easy to win, and you watch people (who are actually accomplices called 'shills') winning money easily. When you try to play, you lose every time because the operator uses sleight of hand to cheat. The game depends on a lookout watching for police, a 'roper' to bring in players, and shills who pretend to win. The moment you put down money, it's gone. Some operations may become aggressive if you try to leave without paying.

Red Flags

  • Crowd gathered around a street game
  • People appearing to win money easily
  • Pressure to place bets
  • The game looks 'too easy' to win
  • Lookouts watching for police

How to Avoid

  • Never play street gambling games - they are always rigged
  • Do not stop to watch
  • Remember that bystanders are likely accomplices
  • The shell game is fraud in France
Scam #6
Taxi Overcharging / Unlicensed Taxi Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Marseille Provence Airport, Gare Saint-Charles train station, Outside nightlife areas late at night

Unlicensed taxi drivers or unscrupulous licensed drivers target tourists arriving at the airport or train station.

They may refuse to use the meter, quote a fixed price that's significantly inflated, take unnecessarily long routes, or claim the meter is broken. Some unlicensed operators solicit fares inside terminals. At night, prices can be quoted at 2-3x the normal rate. Official airport-to-city fare should be approximately 50 euros daytime and 60 euros at night.

Red Flags

  • Driver refusing to use meter
  • No official taxi markings or license displayed
  • Driver soliciting inside the terminal
  • Quoted price significantly above standard rates
  • Driver claiming meter is broken

How to Avoid

  • Use only official taxis from designated ranks (outside Arrivals Hall at airport)
  • Confirm the fare before getting in
  • Insist on the meter being used
  • Know the approximate fare in advance (50€ day/60€ night airport to center)
  • Use ride-sharing apps like Uber for transparent pricing
  • Note driver ID and license plate if overcharged - report to police
Scam #7
Restaurant Overcharging
🔶 Medium
📍 Restaurants around Vieux-Port, Heavily touristed areas, Restaurants with picture menus outside

Some restaurants in tourist areas use various tactics to overcharge visitors.

Common schemes include: showing a menu with reasonable prices but charging more on the bill; offering 'specials' verbally that turn out to be extremely expensive; adding items to the bill that weren't ordered; automatically serving expensive bottled water instead of free tap water when you ask for water; insisting tourists must tip when service is already included in France; offering only medium or large size drinks at inflated prices; giving tourists a different (higher priced) menu than locals. One documented case showed a Marseille restaurant advertising a 28€ menu where 9 of 13 dishes required an additional 5-10€ each.

Red Flags

  • No prices on menu
  • Menu only in English
  • Waiter not writing down orders
  • Pressure to order quickly
  • Automatic bottled water without asking
  • Different prices on bill than menu
  • Being told you must tip

How to Avoid

  • Ask for the French menu and compare prices
  • Request 'une carafe d'eau' (pitcher of tap water) - it's free
  • Verify prices for specials before ordering
  • Review the bill carefully ('l'addition') against what you ordered
  • Tipping is not required in France - service is included
  • Choose restaurants away from main tourist thoroughfares
  • Read recent online reviews
Scam #8
ATM Skimming / Card Trap Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Standalone ATMs in tourist areas, ATMs in dimly lit areas, Non-bank ATMs

Criminals install skimming devices on ATMs that capture your card data and PIN.

This can include: an overlay placed over the card slot that records magnetic stripe data; a fake keypad overlay or hidden camera recording your PIN entry; or a device that traps your card inside the machine. In the card trap version, your card gets stuck and a 'helpful' stranger appears offering advice, watching as you enter your PIN multiple times. Once you leave to seek help, they retrieve your card and use it with the PIN they observed.

Red Flags

  • ATM parts that appear loose, misaligned, or different in color/material
  • Unusual additions to the card slot or keypad
  • Scratches or adhesive residue on the machine
  • ATMs in isolated or poorly lit locations
  • A stranger offering help when your card gets stuck

How to Avoid

  • Use ATMs inside banks during business hours
  • Cover the keypad with your hand when entering PIN
  • Check for loose or unusual parts before inserting card
  • If card gets stuck, do not leave - call your bank immediately
  • Never accept help from strangers at ATMs
  • Enable transaction alerts on your bank account
  • Avoid standalone ATMs in tourist areas
Scam #9
Fake Police Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Streets near tourist areas, Public transportation, Near bars at night

Two people in official-looking uniforms or plain clothes approach you, flash fake badges quickly, ...

Two people in official-looking uniforms or plain clothes approach you, flash fake badges quickly, and claim to be 'Tourist Police' or plainclothes officers. They claim they need to check your wallet for counterfeit bills, 'drug money,' or verify your identity. While examining your wallet, they discreetly remove bills. They may also ask to see your passport and credit cards. By the time you realize money is missing, they've disappeared. Some variations involve accusing you of a minor infraction and demanding an immediate 'fine' paid in cash.

Red Flags

  • Officers approaching you on the street to check wallet
  • Quick flash of badge without clear examination
  • Request to see wallet or money
  • Demand for immediate cash payment for supposed violations
  • No official vehicle present

How to Avoid

  • Real French police never check tourists' wallets on the street
  • Ask to see ID clearly and note badge number
  • Insist on going to the nearest police station before handing anything over
  • Never give your wallet to anyone on the street regardless of uniform
  • Call 17 (police) if suspicious
Scam #10
Bump and Spill Distraction Theft
🔶 Medium
📍 Metro stations, Crowded tourist areas, Outside cafes and restaurants

A well-dressed person 'accidentally' spills something (coffee, ice cream, sauce) on your clothing.

They apologize profusely and begin helping to wipe off the stain, using the commotion to distract you while an accomplice steals your wallet, phone, or bag. Alternatively, a jogger may bump into you hard, knocking you off balance. While helping you recover, they or a partner steal your belongings. Another variation involves someone dropping coins or papers near you - as you helpfully bend down to assist, an accomplice grabs your bag from behind.

Red Flags

  • Someone spilling something on you in a tourist area
  • Overly apologetic stranger insisting on helping clean you
  • Physical contact from a stranger
  • Someone dropping items near your feet

How to Avoid

  • Immediately check your belongings if anyone spills on or bumps you
  • Do not let strangers touch your clothing
  • Step back from anyone getting too close
  • Keep bags secured and in front of you
  • Be suspicious of any unusual contact in crowded areas
Scam #11
Rental Car Break-in / Smash and Grab
⚠️ High
📍 Calanques parking areas, Beach parking lots, Roadside stops, Scenic viewpoints

Marseille and the surrounding Provence region are notorious for car break-ins targeting tourists, ...

Marseille and the surrounding Provence region are notorious for car break-ins targeting tourists, especially at beaches and the Calanques. Thieves identify rental cars by license plates, sterile interiors, and window stickers. They smash windows and steal bags in seconds, even if you're only away for a few minutes. Some thieves target cars in traffic - approaching at red lights or in slow traffic to grab items through open windows. One traveler reported their car was completely stripped of belongings after parking near a beach for just 10 minutes.

Red Flags

  • Parking in isolated or poorly surveilled areas
  • Leaving any items visible in the car
  • Rental car identifying features

How to Avoid

  • Never leave anything visible in the car - even empty bags look promising to thieves
  • Remove all rental car identifying stickers if possible
  • Park in attended lots when available
  • Never park near the Calanques trailheads with valuables in car
  • Take all valuables with you
  • Keep windows closed and doors locked even in traffic
  • Use the trunk for storage, but load it before arriving at destination
Scam #12
Vacation Rental / Airbnb Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Online - before arrival

Scammers create fake vacation rental listings on platforms like Airbnb or independent websites, ...

Scammers create fake vacation rental listings on platforms like Airbnb or independent websites, using stolen photos from legitimate properties. The listing shows an attractive apartment at below-market prices. Victims pay deposits or full rental fees, then arrive to find the property doesn't exist, belongs to someone else, or is drastically different from the listing. In the 'bait and switch' version, the host cancels just before check-in, claiming the original property is unavailable, and offers an inferior substitute. France, including Paris and coastal areas near Marseille, has seen an epidemic of such scams.

Red Flags

  • Prices significantly below market rate
  • Host pushing to communicate/pay outside the platform
  • Limited or stock-like photos
  • New account with no reviews
  • Pressure to book quickly
  • Request for wire transfer or cash payment

How to Avoid

  • Book only through established platforms with buyer protection
  • Never pay outside the platform
  • Read reviews carefully and check host history
  • Reverse image search listing photos
  • If price seems too good to be true, it probably is
  • Verify the address exists on Google Maps/Street View
  • Contact host with specific questions about the property
Scam #13
Beach Theft at Prado Beaches
🔶 Medium
📍 Prado beaches, Urban beaches, Calanques swimming areas

While tourists swim or relax with their attention elsewhere, thieves steal unattended belongings ...

While tourists swim or relax with their attention elsewhere, thieves steal unattended belongings from beach towels and bags. The Prado beaches are pleasant during the day but have many shaded areas that provide cover for thieves. At the Calanques, visitors often leave belongings at the beach while swimming, making easy targets. Some thieves work in pairs - one distracts with conversation while the other takes items from nearby spots.

Red Flags

  • Strangers lingering near your spot without swimming
  • Someone trying to engage you in extended conversation
  • People walking through the beach area repeatedly

How to Avoid

  • Never leave valuables unattended on the beach
  • Use a waterproof pouch for phone and money while swimming
  • Take turns watching belongings with travel companions
  • Leave valuables at your accommodation
  • Avoid beaches late at night, especially alone
  • Use beach lockers where available

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Police Nationale / SAMU station. Call 17 (Police) or 15 (SAMU medical). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at pre-plainte-en-ligne.interieur.gouv.fr.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Paris is at 2 Avenue Gabriel, 75008 Paris. For emergencies: +33 1 43-12-22-22.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
