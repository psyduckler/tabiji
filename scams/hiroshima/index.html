5 Tourist Scams in Hiroshima

Documented from Chugoku Shimbun, Hiroshima Prefectural Police, traveler reviews, and Miyajima safety reports.

Key Takeaways

  • Nagarekawa-Shintenchi touting is the #1 scam — a 2023 Nagarekawa cabaret overcharged one customer ¥830,000, and in 2019 Hiroshima Central Police disbanded the 20-person "Yomonabi" tout network that delivered 281 customers in a single night
  • Miyajima deer have turned aggressive since the 2010s shika-senbei sales ban — residents and tourists document being gored, kicked, and bitten, with a record 4.96 million visitors in 2025 raising confrontation rates
  • A November 2024 TripAdvisor report documents a Miyajima restaurant triple-charging a tourist's card by falsely claiming two transactions "failed" — dispute with your card issuer immediately, not the merchant
  • Hiroshima is a sightseeing city, not a nightlife hub — the scam profile is smaller than Tokyo or Fukuoka, but the Nagarekawa entertainment district and Miyajima tourist corridor concentrate nearly all the risk

⚡ Quick Safety Tips

  • Never follow any tout into a bar in Nagarekawa, Yakkenbori, or Shintenchi — the Hiroshima Prefecture Nuisance Prevention Ordinance makes street touting itself a crime
  • At okonomiyaki shops, photograph the wall-posted menu before ordering and keep a running tally — demand an itemized receipt (明細書) before paying
  • Never feed the Miyajima deer — commercial shika-senbei sales are banned, and food visible in your hands or a plastic bag triggers headbutts and bites
  • At Miyajima restaurants, watch every card swipe — if told a transaction "failed", check your banking app in real time before agreeing to retry
  • At Hiroshima Station and Fukuoka Airport taxi stands, use only licensed taxis with green license plates — skip any unofficial "flat-rate" driver

The 5 Scams

Scam #1
The Nagarekawa-Shintenchi Tout Shakedown
⚠️ High
📍 Nagarekawa (流川) and Shintenchi (新天地) entertainment district, Naka-ku, Hiroshima

You walk through Nagarekawa after dinner — Hiroshima's biggest nightlife district and the largest entertainment quarter in the Chugoku-Shikoku region. A young man in a suit steps in front of you: "Kyabakura? Girls bar? Karaoke?" If you let one walk you anywhere, the menu won't have prices.

Chugoku Shimbun documented the structural reality in July 2019: Hiroshima Central Police arrested the 30-something leader of "Yomonabi" (よもなび), a tout network of roughly 20 people operating out of a free information center in Nagarekawa-Yakkenbori. On one recorded day, 13 of its touts delivered 281 customers to partner venues, generating ¥770,000 in commissions — top touts earned ~¥60,000 in a single night. The group dissolved, but Hiroshima Central Police have continued arresting touts — a 23-year-old sex-industry employee was arrested in January 2025 after soliciting an undercover officer at 10:50 PM in Shintenchi. A July 2023 Yomiuri report documents a 50-something man charged ¥830,000 in a Nagarekawa cabaret run by an unlicensed operation, with the ringleader and four co-conspirators arrested. Hiroshima Prefecture's Bottakuri Prevention Ordinance (ぼったくり防止条例, in force since 2006) criminalizes false price advertising, aggressive collection, and threats to contact a customer's family or employer — penalties run up to ¥1,000,000 or 1 year imprisonment, and police can shut down offending venues for up to 8 months.

Red Flags

  • Male tout in a suit approaches you on Nagarekawa, Yakkenbori, or Shintenchi streets at night offering "kyabakura/girls bar/karaoke"
  • You're walked to a venue in a multi-tenant building with no street-level sign
  • A "free information center" (無料案内所) sends you specifically to one bar
  • No printed price list is visible inside the venue
  • Staff pour champagne or drinks without asking or quoting prices

How to Avoid

  • Never follow a tout anywhere in Nagarekawa, Yakkenbori, or Shintenchi — Hiroshima Prefecture's Nuisance Prevention Ordinance makes touting itself a crime
  • Skip "free information centers" (無料案内所) — they exist to steer you to commission-paying venues, not to help you
  • Use Google Maps or Tabelog to pick a specific bar before arriving; walk into only venues with 4.3+ ratings and 50+ reviews
  • If a venue has no printed menu with prices, walk out before ordering anything
  • Hiroshima's bottakuri-bōshi-jōrei (ぼったくり防止条例) lets police shut offending venues for up to 8 months — call 110 if pressured
Scam #2
The Okonomiyaki Tourist Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Central Hiroshima okonomiyaki venues — Otemachi, Okonomimura, and Shintenchi

You come to Hiroshima for one reason: the okonomiyaki. You pick a place that looked good on Instagram — maybe a Taiwanese YouTuber recommended it. No one shows you a price sheet in English. The bill comes to ¥4,000, ¥4,300, ¥5,600 for dishes that should have been ¥3,200.

On 2024-12-21, a Taiwanese tourist named Jiang noticed his receipt didn't match the posted prices at a Hiroshima okonomiyaki shop an influencer had recommended — he'd been charged ¥4,000 for a ¥3,300 order. The shop claimed the menu was outdated; Jiang spotted the receipt had been altered, called police, and refused the offered refund because "the issue was bigger than the money." Record China, TVBS, Mintshi News, and CTWANT all covered it. Google Maps 1-star reviews of Okonomiyaki Teppanyaki Kohinata (こひなた, Otemachi 3-3-6, rated 3.0 across 296 reviews) explicitly describe the pattern — a 2022 Japanese-language review accuses staff of inflating the bill and giving "nonsense excuses" about prices having just changed. Okonomimura carries a parallel pattern: a 2023 Italian tourist reported being charged 300 yen extra "because I was a tourist"; a German-speaking party paid ¥4,300 for okonomiyaki pictured as ¥1,800 + ¥1,400 with no drinks or toppings added. A 2020 TripAdvisor review calls Kohinata a "bottakuri store aimed at tourists and business travelers" with oyster teppanyaki priced at roughly double the surrounding competitors.

Red Flags

  • No English menu with clearly-listed yen prices; wall-posted menu only in Japanese
  • Menu item sizes or configurations not explained when you order
  • A one-drink-minimum charge is applied only after you sit down, not posted up front
  • Receipt numbers look altered or hand-written over
  • Staff get hostile if you ask why the bill is higher than the menu

How to Avoid

  • Photograph the posted menu before ordering and keep a running tally on your phone
  • If no prices are visible from your seat, ask for an itemized price list before ordering — this is your legal right in Japan
  • Check Google Maps carefully — any okonomiyaki place under 3.8 stars with multiple 1-star reviews mentioning "bill", "charge", or "観光客" (tourist) is a red flag
  • Demand an itemized receipt (明細書) before paying; you are legally entitled to one
  • Don't follow influencer recommendations blindly — cross-check specific shops against Google Maps and Tabelog reviews first
Scam #3
The Miyajima Deer Food-Grab Attack
🔶 Medium
📍 Miyajima (Itsukushima), Hatsukaichi — ferry landing, Omotesando shopping street, Itsukushima Shrine approach

Miyajima's deer are not Nara's. They used to be fed shika-senbei, but the city banned commercial sales in the 2010s. The deer didn't adapt — they got bolder. Step off the ferry with a konbini bag, and within a minute you're being shoulder-checked by a 60kg buck.

The Miyajima Hakataya local info site explicitly documents resident complaints of "角で突かれた、蹴られた、噛みつかれた" — gored by horns, kicked, bitten. TripAdvisor reviewer reports include one who was bitten on the mouth while eating a soft-serve ice cream and then chased across the street. Sankei reported in June 2023 that Miyajima deer now ransack trash bins because reduced tourism and banned feeding have made them chronically hungry. 4.96 million visitors passed through Miyajima in 2025 — confrontations are not rare. Bag contents are particularly high-risk: a deer will punch its head through a plastic shopping bag, shred maps, eat entire pamphlets, and has been documented biting tourists who tried to pull food back. A 2007-onward feeding-restriction regime (following a 2006 Environment Ministry ecological survey) is the underlying cause — deer that can't find senbei approach tourist food aggressively instead. A government inquiry submission in the Shugiin describes deer "forcefully demanding food from tourists" after senbei sales stopped.

Red Flags

  • You are carrying a konbini bag, food wrapper, or open food after disembarking the ferry
  • A deer has been following you for more than 20 metres without being fed
  • The deer stops chewing and fixes its eyes on your bag — that's the pre-charge stance
  • Young children in your group are holding food at deer head-height
  • Your paper map, brochure, or receipt is sticking out of an open bag pocket

How to Avoid

  • Eat nothing outside on Miyajima — finish any konbini food before boarding the ferry, or eat only inside sit-down restaurants
  • Carry food items inside an opaque backpack or zipped bag, never in a plastic convenience-store bag
  • Do not feed the deer: shika-senbei sales are banned and unauthorized feeding is prohibited by Hiroshima Prefecture
  • Do not touch or pet the deer, even if they appear calm — most injury reports involve people who approached for a photo first
  • If a deer charges, drop the bag item and walk away briskly — do not run (it triggers pursuit) and do not push (it provokes goring)
Scam #4
The Miyajima Restaurant Card-Retry Triple Charge
🔶 Medium
📍 Miyajima restaurants along the Omotesando approach and Omiyamae area

You finish lunch, hand over your card, and the waiter says the transaction failed. You try again. Fails again. You try a third time and it "goes through." Two weeks later your statement shows three charges. Every single one went through.

A November 2024 TripAdvisor reviewer named Lotte V reported this at Niwa Cafe Miyama (庭カフェ 御山, Miyajimacho 527-2) in October 2024. The waiter claimed the payment didn't pass and forced her to try two more times — all three went through. She contacted the restaurant via Instagram and Google; no response. Her hotel phoned for her; the restaurant admitted an error and promised a refund. Two months later no refund had arrived. The restaurant is otherwise well-rated (4.8 overall), but the pattern — staff-induced multiple swipes plus refund stonewalling — is the classic card-retry scam documented globally in tourist areas. Outside Miyajima proper, Google Maps low-rated reviews of tourist-area eateries repeatedly cite language-barrier-exploited pricing and refusal to provide itemized receipts; card-retry is the recent cross-border variant as Japan transitions from cash-only to tap-to-pay. Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency explicitly treats multiple-swipe disputes as a recognized consumer-protection category.

Red Flags

  • Server says "payment failed" without showing you the terminal
  • Server takes the card away from the terminal before showing you the failed-transaction receipt
  • You are asked to re-swipe or re-tap more than once at the same terminal
  • The server does not print a declined-transaction receipt for the "failed" attempts
  • You are not shown the final approved transaction amount in writing before leaving

How to Avoid

  • Ask to see every receipt — both declined (失敗) and approved (承認) — before leaving; this is routine in Japan
  • If a transaction "fails", check your banking app in real time before agreeing to retry
  • Pay cash where possible on Miyajima — most shops accept only cash anyway
  • If told a card failed, photograph the terminal screen as evidence
  • Dispute immediately with your issuing bank (chargeback) rather than relying on the restaurant to refund — documented cases show merchants may simply stop replying
Scam #5
The Miyajima Omotesando Souvenir Markup
🟢 Low
📍 Miyajima Omotesando shopping street and ferry-terminal shops, Hatsukaichi

You step off the ferry and walk Miyajima's Omotesando approach to Itsukushima Shrine. Wooden rice paddles (shamoji), momiji manju, and oyster crackers line every shopfront. Most are legitimate — but the markup between the ferry terminal and the shrine is steep, and identical items in Hiroshima Station often cost significantly less.

Miyajima hit a record 4.96 million visitors in 2025, and Hatsukaichi City has introduced a ¥100 per-visit "Miyajima Visitor Tax" (宮島訪問税) on ferry tickets to fund crowd management. Within this overtourism context, Google Maps reviews of Miyajima souvenir shops repeatedly flag price discrepancies and packaging that implies artisanal provenance for items that are industrially produced. Unlike the harder crimes above, this is "soft" overcharging rather than outright fraud — the standard tourist-premium pattern that globally isn't criminally prosecuted but still hits your wallet. Local r/hiroshima commenters put it plainly: "Miyajima as it exists now is a tourist trap." Mid-rated souvenir shops at 3.7-3.8 stars — such as Ryugu-do and Kyushu-ya — cluster review complaints around pricing and quality inconsistency. Travel bloggers consistently note that the same momiji manju are available much cheaper a short walk or train ride away at Hiroshima Station shops like Nishiki-do and Fujii-ya.

Red Flags

  • Identical-looking momiji manju boxes priced ~30-50% higher at the ferry terminal than at central Hiroshima stations
  • Shops labelling factory-produced items as "宮島特産" or "handmade" without visible on-site production
  • No price tag on the item — you must ask
  • Aggressive "tasting" pressure followed by an expectation that you buy a box
  • Wooden shamoji priced 3-5× what the same item sells for at a Hondori shopping arcade store

How to Avoid

  • Buy momiji manju at Hiroshima Station shops (e.g., Nishiki-do, Fujii-ya) before leaving — same items, lower prices, no rush
  • Walk past the first 200 metres of Omotesando before buying — shops deeper in the approach generally have similar prices to the station
  • Compare prices at 2-3 shops before buying anything over ¥1,000
  • Prefer shops with visible on-site production (a baker steaming manju in the window) over packaged-goods stalls at the ferry terminal
  • Check Google Maps — Miyajima souvenir shops under 4.0 stars with multiple mentions of price gouging are common and worth skipping

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Japanese Police (Keisatsu) station or koban. Call 110. Hiroshima Central Police actively prosecute Nagarekawa bottakuri and kyakuhiki cases under the prefecture's Bottakuri Prevention Ordinance. Get an official crime report — you'll need it for insurance and chargebacks.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block suspicious transactions before they use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

There is no dedicated US Consulate in Hiroshima. Contact US Consulate General Osaka-Kobe, 2-11-5 Nishitenma, Kita-ku, Osaka 530-8543. For emergencies: +81 6-6315-5900.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
