The 4 Scams

Scam #1
Hotel-rank petit-taxi overcharge & per-person ambush
⚠️ High
📍 Royal Atlas hotel rank Boulevard du 20 Août, Sofitel Agadir Royal Bay taxi pool, Iberostar Founty Beach curb, Al Massira Airport (AGA) arrivals lane, Souk El Had taxi cluster

Agadir's hotel ranks line Boulevard du 20 Août and the resort strip from Founty Beach to Marina Agadir.

The petit-taxi (small orange) and grand-taxi (white) drivers parked there run the most-cited 2025 to 2026 tourist scam in the city. Petit-taxis are meter-required by Moroccan law; legitimate grand-taxis quote a flat per-vehicle fare. Hotel-rank drivers regularly refuse the meter and quote three to four times the InDrive baseline.

The approach is fast and rehearsed. A driver waves you over and accepts a quoted fare like 30 dirhams to your destination. Only after you arrive does he flip the script: 'thirty per person, that's 120 dirhams for four of you.' A 2025 community forums post with 57 upvotes documents the tactic ending in a slap to the face while nearby Agadir police did not intervene. The thread is the canonical community anchor for the per-person twist.

Variants escalate. A June 2025 Daily Mail viral-video story documented an Agadir cabbie who hid a 200 dirham note under his car after two British tourists paid the agreed fare. Moroccan authorities arrested him on fraud and breach-of-trust charges. A separate community threads captures a third variant where a hotel-rank crew directs you into a specific taxi, then denies recognition when you return for a forgotten bag.

Hotel-rank crews also block InDrive pickups physically. A 2026 community forums post with 16 upvotes shows two taxis boxing in an InDrive 'comfort' car after the InDrive quoted 92 dirhams for an airport run that the orange taxi wanted 350 dirhams for. Daily wages plus driver-cartel coordination make the per-person and meter-refusal plays a stable business at the major hotel ranks.

**The defensive move is to register InDrive and Careem with face-ID verification BEFORE you leave the hotel lobby.** Photograph the license plate and the driver's permit number before any luggage goes into the trunk. Insist on the meter inside a petit-taxi; walk to the next rank if the driver refuses. Agree the full grand-taxi fare out loud before the door closes. Brigade Touristique Agadir is 0528-823557; police is 19; emergency is 15; US Consulate Casablanca is +212 522-64-2099.

Red Flags

  • driver at a hotel rank refuses the meter or claims it is broken on a petit-taxi
  • fare quoted in 'per person' terms after the door closes on a four-person grand-taxi run
  • two or three nearby drivers physically block an arriving InDrive or Careem pickup
  • driver hides a banknote under the seat or under the car and accuses you of underpaying
  • hotel-rank crew steers you into a specific car, then denies recognition if you return

How to Avoid

  • REGISTER InDrive and Careem with face-ID verification before you leave your home country.
  • PHOTOGRAPH the license plate and permit number before any luggage goes into the trunk.
  • INSIST on the meter inside a petit-taxi; walk to the next rank if the driver refuses.
  • AGREE the full per-vehicle grand-taxi fare out loud before the door closes — AGA to resort strip is 150 to 200 dirhams.
  • CALL Brigade Touristique Agadir at 0528-823557, or 19 for police, if a driver becomes aggressive.
Scam #2
Corniche gift-and-guilt approach & child-pickpocket pairing
🟢 Low
📍 Agadir Corniche promenade, Marina Agadir entrance, Place Al Amal, Founty Beach access ramps, Souk El Had north entrance

The Agadir Corniche is the long beachfront promenade between Marina Agadir and the Sofitel cluster.

It is the city's main evening tourist strip and the stage for a textbook gift-and-guilt confidence approach. A friendly stranger strikes up casual English conversation, asks where you are from, and within two or three minutes presses a small souvenir into your hand 'as a gift.' The pivot follows almost on cue.

The demand lands as a sob story. A 2023 community forums post with 29 upvotes documents the canonical Agadir version. The man tells you he has just had a child and asks for help; if you say you have nothing, his tone shifts. He asks you to empty your pockets. The medina-shakedown sister pattern in nearby Marrakech, captured in a 2025 community forums post with 409 upvotes, runs the same script in restricted alleys for 300 dirhams 'or trouble.'

A second arm uses children as runners. A reply on the 2023 Agadir thread describes a girl no older than nine selling flowers on the Corniche while an adult lookout works pickpocket angles on her father's instructions. Hotel-room versions also exist. A 2025 community forums post with 6 upvotes from an Agadir hotel guest documents a cleaner who manufactured a 'lost towel' debt and asked the guest to cover the imaginary loss as he was checking out.

A 2026 community forums post titled around foreign-visitor disappointment treats the soft-shakedown ecosystem as systemic. A 2026 community forums Morocco-as-a-woman thread surfaces the same pattern as a top concern for solo female visitors. The community pattern is consistent across the past three travel seasons.

**The defensive move is to refuse every unsolicited 'gift' on the Corniche or in the medina — once an object is in your hand, the demand follows.** Split cash between a money belt and a decoy wallet with 50 to 100 dirhams of small notes. Walk in pairs after dark and keep moving when children approach with trinkets. Bill any disputed hotel-room charge to the front desk in person. Brigade Touristique Agadir is 0528-823557; police is 19; emergency is 15.

Red Flags

  • friendly English-language stranger pivots within minutes to a 'gift for my new baby' pitch
  • small souvenir pressed into your hand before any price is mentioned
  • self-appointed 'guide' steers you into restricted alleys and quotes 300 dirhams for the exit
  • child selling flowers or trinkets working with an adult lookout nearby
  • hotel cleaner claims a towel or item is 'lost' and asks you personally to cover their salary

How to Avoid

  • REFUSE every unsolicited gift on the Corniche; do not let any object be placed in your hand.
  • SPLIT cash between a money belt and a decoy wallet holding 50 to 100 dirhams of small notes.
  • WALK the Corniche in pairs after dark and keep moving when children approach with trinkets.
  • BILL any disputed hotel-room charge to the front desk in person — never pay a cleaner directly.
  • CONTACT Brigade Touristique Agadir at 0528-823557 if a stranger becomes aggressive after a refusal.
Scam #3
Souk El Had argan oil dilution & cooperative-tour overcharge
🟢 Low
📍 Souk El Had argan stalls, Boulevard Mohammed V boutiques, Agadir–Essaouira coastal cooperatives, Aourir village argan stops, taxi-arranged 'cooperative' tour route

Souk El Had is the walled market east of central Agadir and the largest argan-oil retail point in the Souss region.

It is also the staging ground for the city's most-documented dilution-and-overcharge play. Genuine pure culinary argan oil here runs roughly 400 to 500 dirhams per liter at wholesale stalls inside the souk. Tourist-facing boutiques on Boulevard Mohammed V routinely quote 1,500 to 3,000 dirhams for the same liter.

The second mechanic is adulteration. A 2025 community forums post with 13 upvotes documents bottles that arrived clear, odorless, and watery — the classic tells that the bottle is mostly sunflower or vegetable oil with a few drops of real argan added for scent. Genuine culinary argan is golden, viscous, and unmistakably nutty when poured.

The same dilution problem hit Morocco's olive-oil market in late 2025. A Hespress investigation reported inspectors seized over 6,500 liters of fake or untraceable olive oil across northern provinces, with genuine product selling at around 60 dirhams per liter. The structurally identical fraud applied to a different cooking oil.

The third arm is the taxi-arranged cooperative tour. A driver on the Agadir to Essaouira coastal road insists on stopping at a 'women's cooperative' near Aourir or further north. You watch a five-minute crushing demonstration, accept mint tea, and then face a hard-sell at 800 to 1,500 dirhams for a 250 milliliter bottle. The driver collects a commission. A 2024 community forums Agadir-specific thread with 7 upvotes establishes the 400 to 500 dirhams per liter benchmark from local commenters.

**The defensive move is to buy argan oil only from inside Souk El Had at a stall that posts per-liter prices in dirhams on the bottle.** Refuse every taxi-arranged cooperative detour; the 'cooperative' is a commission shop. Pour a drop on your wrist — viscous and nutty is real, clear and scentless is diluted. Compare three stalls before paying. Brigade Touristique Agadir is 0528-823557; consumer-protection hotline is 5757; ONSSA handles culinary-oil fraud complaints regionally.

Red Flags

  • argan oil quoted at 1,500 to 3,000 dirhams per liter in a tourist-facing boutique
  • bottle is clear, watery, or has no nutty aroma when opened
  • taxi driver insists on a 'women's cooperative' stop you did not request
  • five-minute crushing demonstration followed by free mint tea and a hard-sell
  • no per-liter price tag posted on the bottle or stall shelf

How to Avoid

  • BUY argan oil only inside Souk El Had at a stall that posts per-liter dirham pricing.
  • REFUSE every taxi-arranged 'cooperative' detour on the Agadir–Essaouira coastal road.
  • TEST a drop on your wrist — viscous and nutty is real, clear and scentless is diluted.
  • COMPARE three stalls inside Souk El Had before paying and never accept the first quote.
  • REPORT adulterated culinary oil to ONSSA or to Brigade Touristique at 0528-823557.
Scam #4
Public-beach parasol & parking 'syndicate' shakedown
🟢 Low
📍 Agadir Beach (Plage Tama), Taghazout Beach parking, Aourir Banana Beach lot, Tikida Beach Club approach, Plage Imourane informal lot

Agadir's public beaches are legally free and the city promenade is unticketed.

Every summer a layered shakedown reasserts informal control over both the sand and the parking strips. A 2025 community forums post with 169 upvotes is the canonical local anchor. Men in unofficial yellow vests stake out parasol clusters along the sand and adjacent parking lots, then charge 'fees' to anyone arriving by car or on foot.

The parasol arm is the most visible. The crew lays out commercial parasols and plastic loungers across long stretches of sand. They demand 50 to 150 dirhams per umbrella per day. Refuse and they tell you the spot is 'reserved' or that you cannot lay a towel anywhere on the beach without renting from them. The 169-upvote thread treats the practice as a recurring summer mafia.

A 2024 community forums normalized-scams thread with 53 upvotes ranks the parasol shakedown — 'they act like they own the damn sea' — among the top community-named informal Moroccan tourist abuses. The same thread surfaces the parallel parking arm.

Self-appointed 'gardiens' in fluorescent vests claim ownership of every public parking strip from Marina Agadir south to Founty Beach and north into Taghazout and Aourir. They demand 10 to 30 dirhams 'to watch the car' and threaten vandalism if refused. A 2026 community forums foreign-visitor thread with 28 upvotes and a 2025 community forums safety-concerns thread with 16 upvotes both surface the parking-shakedown anxiety as a top travel-decision factor for solo and family visitors.

**The defensive move is to bring your own beach mat and refuse every parasol or 'reserved spot' pitch — public Moroccan beaches cannot be privatized by a parasol crew.** For parking, use guarded paid lots near the Marina or your hotel rather than informal strip parking. If a vest-wearer threatens vandalism, photograph them and call police on 19. Brigade Touristique Agadir is 0528-823557; consumer-protection hotline is 5757.

Red Flags

  • men in unofficial yellow or orange vests claim 'ownership' of a public-beach parasol zone
  • parasol fee demanded at 50 to 150 dirhams per umbrella with no posted municipal authorization
  • self-appointed 'gardien' demands 20 to 30 dirhams to 'watch' your car on a public street
  • vest-wearer threatens vandalism or scratched paint if you refuse the parking fee
  • you are told you cannot lay your own towel on the sand without renting an umbrella

How to Avoid

  • BRING your own beach mat and refuse every parasol-rental pitch on a public Moroccan beach.
  • PARK in a guarded paid lot near Marina Agadir or your hotel rather than an informal street strip.
  • PHOTOGRAPH any vest-wearer who threatens vandalism and call 19 immediately.
  • OFFER 10 dirhams maximum as a goodwill tip to a street 'gardien' if you choose to pay at all.
  • CALL Brigade Touristique Agadir at 0528-823557 if a parasol crew physically blocks beach access.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Sûreté Nationale (DGSN) station. Call 19 (Police) or 15 (Emergency/SAMU). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at dgsn.ma.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Consulate General in Casablanca is at 8 Boulevard Moulay Youssef, Casablanca. For emergencies: +212 522-64-2099.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
