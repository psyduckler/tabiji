Key Takeaways

  • The "tre campanelle" (three-shell) game is Rome's single most-prosecuted street scam — a March 29, 2026 ANSA-reported polizia locale operation denounced over 20 people across the Pincio, Fontana di Trevi, Piazza di Spagna, Pantheon and Piazza di Pietra, seizing cash in euros, US dollars and Argentine pesos; Corriere Roma pegs three organised bands in the Tridente at roughly €1,000 an hour each
  • Rome's metro is where most tourist pickpocketing happens — in February 2026 Repubblica Roma reported the opening of the Polmetro headquarters at Termini, coordinating 50 dedicated officers and monitoring 5,000 cameras, after a March 2025 Repubblica piece flagged Termini, Colosseo and Barberini as the stations where "i turisti diventano prede" (tourists become prey)
  • 7 of 10 scams are rated high risk — Rome's scam ecosystem is the most organised in Italy, with documented gangs clustering around four chokepoints: the Trevi–Pantheon–Spanish Steps triangle, the Colosseum-to-Fori-Imperiali axis, Termini station and Vatican queues, and Fiumicino airport arrivals
  • Rome's official fixed taxi fare is €50 from Fiumicino (FCO) to anywhere inside the Aurelian Walls and €30 from Ciampino (CIA) — a July 2022 Corriere Roma report documented a driver charging €70 from Fiumicino with the farewell "Benvenuto a Roma," and a July 2025 r/rome thread documented a driver with the suspicious cooperative name "Società Cooperativa Roma" demanding €55 for a 3.7-km ride from FCO with the meter turned off

⚡ Quick Safety Tips

  • Never stop, never bet, never even watch the tre campanelle crowd near Trevi, Piazza Navona, Piazza di Spagna or the Pantheon — accomplices pickpocket the onlookers while the "croupier" keeps the audience focused on the shells
  • On Metro A (Termini–Barberini–Spagna–Ottaviano/Vatican) and Metro B (Termini–Colosseo), ride with your bag zipped and across your chest, one hand on the zipper; ignore any sudden "malore" (fainting) drama at train doors — it is a documented pickpocket distraction
  • At the Colosseum, refuse any armoured "centurion" or "gladiator" who tries to pose with you, put a sword in your hand, or offer a photo — their organised racket has been a Corriere / Il Messaggero political scandal for over a decade
  • From Fiumicino (FCO) take only official white taxis at the signed rank and confirm the €50 fixed fare to central Rome inside the Aurelian Walls before the door closes — or take the Leonardo Express train (€14, 32 min) to Termini
  • At the Vatican Museums, walk directly to the official entrance on Viale Vaticano; ignore anyone in a uniform, lanyard or name tag who tries to separate "ticket" and "no ticket" lines on the street — Vatican staff never approach you outside the building

The 10 Scams

Scam #1
The Trevi Fountain Tre Campanelle Sting
⚠️ High
📍 Fontana di Trevi, Via delle Muratte, Via in Arcione, Piazza di Pietra, Pantheon, Via del Corso, Piazza di Spagna, Trinità dei Monti, Pincio

A "croupier" at a fold-up cardboard table slides a foam ball under three metal shells. Elegantly-dressed "tourists" around him win easy €50 bets. You bet €100. The ball was never under any shell — it was palmed between his fingers the whole time.

An April 11, 2025 ANSA Lazio arrest report documents the textbook version: on the Trinità dei Monti staircase above Piazza di Spagna, Carabinieri of the Roma Centro company arrested a 50-year-old Romanian man and a 34-year-old Romanian woman for aggravated fraud after a tourist wagered €100 "sperando di riceverne il doppio" — hoping to receive double — only to learn the ball was never under any shell. The January 2026 Repubblica Roma piece "Il business delle tre campanelle" calls it an industrial operation: dozens of operations by polizia locale in the historic centre have failed to stop the "finti croupier," with thousands of euros extorted in minutes. A November 29, 2025 Repubblica Roma report details a six-person gang stopped near the Fontana di Trevi with €1,450 in mixed currencies — euros, US dollars and British pounds — lifted off American tourists. On April 11, 2026 Roma Today documented an eight-person band on Via delle Muratte (five Macedonian men, three Romanian/Moldovan women, ages 26 to 56) caught with the €100 a tourist had just lost plus €320 in earlier winnings; Carabinieri in plainclothes had watched the whole sequence. The April 2025 Corriere Roma feature "A caccia di campanellari nel Tridente" estimates three organised bands earning roughly €1,000 per hour each. In April 2026 Roma Today documented the polizia locale's new tactic: officers wearing rubber masks to slip past the lookouts who had learned their faces. The March 29, 2026 ANSA Lazio bulletin counted over 20 denounced across Pincio, Trevi, Spagna, Pantheon and Piazza di Pietra, with cash seized in dollari statunitensi e pesos argentini — evidence of how global the victim pool is.

Red Flags

  • A fold-up cardboard or plastic table on any pedestrian stretch between Trevi, the Pantheon, the Spanish Steps, Piazza Navona or Via del Corso, with three small metal cups or shells on it
  • A crowd that "wins" bets of €50 to €100 fast and loudly — they are the complici (accomplices) faking vincite facili
  • People along the edges who aren't watching the game but are watching the street — those are the vedette (lookouts) scanning for police
  • The "croupier" asks not to be photographed or filmed — legitimate street performers don't
  • You feel a soft touch on your bag or pocket while you watch — accomplices pickpocket the onlookers

How to Avoid

  • Do not stop. Do not watch. Do not bet under any circumstances — r/ItalyTravel's 123-upvote thread "The Three-card monte: why do tourists fall for it?" states bluntly that the game is "always, always rigged" and Italians know it
  • Walk past with your hand on your bag zipper — the crowd itself is the pickpocket zone, and accomplices work the onlookers while the game holds attention
  • If you see the game forming near a landmark, cross to the opposite side of the street and keep moving — do not pause to photograph it either
  • Report the game by calling 113 (Polizia di Stato) or 112 (Carabinieri) with a location; the polizia locale runs active stings and your tip genuinely helps
  • Warn other tourists who are about to stop — the Corriere Roma feature documents that warning signs alone do not work; only peer interruption does
Scam #2
Termini Station Pickpocket Rings
⚠️ High
📍 Roma Termini platforms, underground galleria, Piazza dei Cinquecento forecourt, Metro A/B interchange at Termini; bus 64 and 40 queues to the Vatican

You step off a Frecciarossa at Termini. Coordinated groups — documented by Repubblica as Latino, North African and Roma networks — are already clocking you. Train staff now announce "attenzione, pickpocket!" as trains arrive. One brush, one elegant jacket-on-the-arm screen, and your wallet or phone is three platforms away.

A March 4, 2025 Repubblica Roma piece flatly names Termini, Colosseo and Barberini as the stations "dove i turisti diventano prede" — where tourists become prey. An October 2025 Repubblica Roma investigation uncovered a deposito clandestino in a Termini-area guesthouse stuffed with "decine di valigie, portafogli, vestiti griffati, gioielli" — dozens of suitcases, wallets, designer clothes and jewellery from Termini-area haul. On February 21, 2026 Repubblica Roma covered the opening of the Polmetro headquarters at Termini, coordinating 50 dedicated agents and monitoring 5,000 cameras across the metro. A November 27, 2022 Corriere Roma report documented the "cavallo di ritorno" extortion variant: a tourist robbed of his smartphone at Termini was then contacted by the thief demanding €50 for its return — he called the carabinieri and the thief was arrested. Il Messaggero's "Pickpocket Express" reporting documented the arrest of Marco Casamatta, Simone Di Matteo and Manuel Severa between July and October 2024, when an investigative unit on Via In Selci found stolen property from dozens of Termini-area thefts. An August 2025 Repubblica Roma piece tracks the bus 64 Termini–San Pietro line specifically, where police logged 15 denunce in 48 hours in late July 2025. A recurring MO documented across r/rome and r/ItalyTravel: someone at the self-service ticket machine insists on "helping" you pay, then pockets your change or photographs your card PIN.

Red Flags

  • A stranger offers unsolicited "help" at a Trenitalia or Italo self-service kiosk in the main Termini hall — a documented pattern that costs tourists card PINs or coins
  • A group of three to five clusters at a train door as it opens, brushing past and bumping as you exit with luggage
  • Someone walks behind your rolling suitcase in the Termini underground passages — they are checking outer-pocket zips
  • A well-dressed person with a jacket draped over one forearm brushes closer than expected — the jacket is a documented pickpocket "screen" used to hide the working hand
  • Anyone offers to flag a taxi for you inside the station or on the forecourt — official taxis are boarded only at the signed rank and never negotiated via a broker

How to Avoid

  • Buy train tickets only on the Trenitalia or Italo app, or at machines marked with the operator logo — never let a "helper" touch your card or your coins
  • At Termini's taxi rank, use only the official white taxis; never accept a "taxi?" offer from someone inside the hall or on Piazza dei Cinquecento
  • On Frecciarossa and Italo, use the luggage rack at the end of the carriage that faces your seat rather than the overhead rack; watch luggage during every station stop
  • Keep your phone out of a back pocket and never in a loose jacket pocket on the platform — lift it only when you're ready to step onto a train, not while standing still
  • Download the polizia locale anti-borseggio app Roma Capitale released in 2026 (referenced by Roma Today as "l'app contro i borseggiatori"): 60 real-time reports in its first six days of operation
Scam #3
The Metro A/B "Finto Malore" Distraction Theft
⚠️ High
📍 Metro A (Termini–Repubblica–Barberini–Spagna–Flaminio–Ottaviano/Vatican), Metro B (Termini–Cavour–Colosseo–Circo Massimo), Metro A/B interchange at Termini, bus 64 Termini–San Pietro

A passenger collapses at the metro doors "with a sudden malore" — clutching her stomach, about to vomit. Caring strangers lean in. Her partner's hand is already inside the pocket of the tourist who leaned in to help. By the time the doors reopen, the "sick" one and the wallet have walked.

A Roma Today eyewitness account reconstructs the MO minute by minute: at 7:20am on Metro A, "due persone sembravano viaggiare insieme — a un certo punto, una di loro, che si trovava davanti alle porte del convoglio, si è accasciata improvvisamente, simulando un malore." A young English-speaking male tourist leaned in to help; a second person lifted his wallet from his trouser pocket; at the next stop both perpetrators exited. A guardia giurata on the platform warned the remaining passengers to check their belongings and confirmed the tactic is recurring. Roma Today also documents la "tecnica del velo" — one accomplice distracts with physical contact while a second works the bag under the cover of a draped garment. A March 11, 2026 Roma Today arrest report documents three Romanian citizens on Metro B at Colosseo (two adults plus a 14-year-old minor) encircling a tourist, lifting her wallet, and being caught by Carabinieri of Viale Eritrea station. On March 17, 2025 Roma Today documented three people arrested for encircling a 60-year-old South American woman on a bus, lifting her credit card, and withdrawing money from it before being stopped. A March 3, 2025 Repubblica Roma report documents female pickpockets spraying pepper spray on a Metro A train between Termini and Repubblica in panic when passengers reacted — the incident was filmed. A May 17, 2025 Repubblica Roma piece documents two Peruvian citizens at Termini who tried to intervene against fellow-Peruvian thieves and were attacked — the incident is now benchmark evidence that metro pickpocketing has escalated to routine violence. Il Messaggero documents a "new generation" of borseggiatori wearing designer clothes and in-ear communication pieces to blend in with tourists.

Red Flags

  • Someone collapses or appears suddenly ill at the metro doors as the train is about to depart — don't rush in, back up one step
  • A second person is suspiciously close to you while the "sick" person holds everyone's attention
  • A jacket, scarf or folded newspaper is draped over someone's forearm near your bag or pocket — the classic tecnica del velo screen
  • A child or minor brushes repeatedly against your luggage or bag in the Termini, Colosseo or Spagna platforms
  • People around you suddenly start checking their own pockets as the train pulls in — they know what's happening

How to Avoid

  • Ride metro and bus 64 with your bag zipped and worn across the chest on the side opposite the door, one hand on the zipper at all times
  • Treat any "malore" at metro doors as a pickpocket distraction first — back up, hand on pocket, let the genuine passengers and the guardia giurata handle the medical situation
  • Never carry a phone or wallet in a back pocket, hip pocket, jacket side pocket, or outer backpack pocket on Roman public transport — use an inside zipped pocket or a money belt
  • If you are groped, grabbed or targeted, shout "ladro!" loudly and point — Roma Today documents multiple cases where fellow passengers, guardie giurate and carabinieri reacted and arrests followed
  • Get off at the first station after a suspicious approach, check yourself on the platform, and board a different carriage — do not confront
Scam #4
The Colosseum Fake Gladiator Photo Shakedown
⚠️ High
📍 Piazza del Colosseo, Via dei Fori Imperiali, Arco di Costantino, Piazza Venezia, Altare della Patria steps

A beefy "centurion" in plastic armour drapes a red cape on your shoulders, hands you a foam sword, poses for a photo — and then demands €20, €50 or more per person for the shot. Say no and a second and third "gladiator" materialise.

The finti gladiatori racket around the Colosseum has been a Rome political scandal for over a decade. In November 2010 Corriere Roma's Paolo Graldi editorial "Il caso Centurioni" called for a specific law against the organised shakedown. Senator Fabrizio Cutrufo's 2011 proposal to criminalise "stalking dei centurioni" documented how the supposed photo operators "continuano insistentemente ad offrirsi per una foto a pagamento ai visitatori" — and that the behaviour was already being labelled "stalking." Il Messaggero's centro-storico reporting documents the same group operating as a "banda dei selfie" near the Colosseum, working the density around the Arco di Costantino where bags and backpacks slip off tourists' shoulders during the photo op. On r/rome the 18-upvote moderator-linked thread "Gladiators scam follow-up: if you've been harassed outside the Colosseum, get in touch!" documents that residents have partnered with an Il Messaggero journalist and the AGTAR (Association for Certified Tour Guides in Rome) to collect evidence, noting bluntly that the photo operators "are part of an organised racket." Separately, in November 2024 Open.online reported on Airbnb's "gladiator combat" Colosseum event (16 participants, a €1.5 million pledge to the Parco Archeologico) — an event that drew Rome's culture assessor Massimiliano Smeriglio to state the city cannot "transform one of the most important monuments in the world into a theme park." In February 2026 Repubblica Roma documented three finti Ciceroni from Iran and Brazil fined €400 each for offering illegal guided tours at the Colosseum — the same fake-authority pattern, different script.

Red Flags

  • Anyone in plastic armour, red cape or plumed helmet who steps toward you around the Colosseo metro exit, the Arco di Costantino or along Via dei Fori Imperiali
  • A "free photo" offer that includes putting a prop, cape or sword in your hand or on your shoulders
  • Two or three "centurions" in coordination — they surround you before the price conversation starts
  • Anyone in costume positioning themselves between you and your backpack, or standing behind a partner who is taking the photo
  • A "certified guide" in plain clothes offering skip-the-line Colosseum tours outside the official access gate — the same Vatican-line fake-staff pattern applies here

How to Avoid

  • Book Colosseum, Forum and Palatine tickets in advance on coopculture.it, the Parco Archeologico del Colosseo app, or a verified tour operator — never buy from a person in costume or on the street
  • Keep walking and say "no, grazie" firmly; do not pause, do not shake hands, do not let anyone drape anything on you
  • If you want a gladiator photo, the only licensed street performers are in Piazza Navona and licensed entertainers in the Villa Borghese zone — never around the Colosseo itself
  • If a fake gladiator demands money after you refused the photo, say "chiamo la polizia" and dial 113 or 112 — the racket is actively prosecuted and your complaint matters
  • Wear your backpack on your front when you walk the Fori Imperiali–Colosseo corridor; the "banda dei selfie" works the photo density, not the empty alleys
Scam #5
The Fiumicino Fake Taxi & Fixed-Fare Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Fiumicino Leonardo da Vinci (FCO) arrivals hall and forecourt, Ciampino (CIA) airport taxi rank, Termini rank at Piazza dei Cinquecento, Piazza San Pietro taxi queue

At FCO arrivals, a man in a blazer grabs your suitcase: "Taxi? Follow me." You end up in an unmarked sedan agreeing to €70 in cash for a ride that should cost €50 on the fixed fare — or €14 on the Leonardo Express. A July 2025 r/rome post documented a driver with "Società Cooperativa Roma" livery charging €55 for a 3.7-km FCO hop with the meter off.

A July 27, 2022 Corriere Roma report documented the textbook version: a driver charged a tourist €70 in cash instead of the official €50 airport-to-centre fixed fare, with the farewell "Benvenuto a Roma." A July 2025 r/rome thread "Help identifying taxi company from Fiumicino airport ('Societa Cooperativa Roma')" documents the current-year version — a driver who refused to turn on the meter, claimed a "fixed fare" of €55 for a 3.7-km 8-minute ride, demanded cash, and said card payments required the passenger to cover "all commission fees." An April 11, 2024 Corriere Roma report documents a Rome driver who charged a Vatican-to-restaurant passenger an imposed "tariffa fissa" in cash, with the victim posting the licence plate publicly on TripAdvisor. An August 2, 2024 Corriere Roma report documents a different twist — a driver who kept a passenger's forgotten laptop and demanded €200 for its return. Rome's official taxi fares are set by the Comune di Roma: €50 fixed from FCO to inside the Aurelian Walls, €30 from Ciampino to inside the Aurelian Walls, each covering up to four passengers and standard luggage; the meter does not run on these routes. The "card reader is broken" variant is documented across r/rome and r/ItalyTravel; drivers use it to force victims into ATM withdrawals where the PIN can be observed or a POS-pirata device applied. Unlicensed NCC-style solicitors inside the arrivals hall are a distinct problem: r/rome and r/solotravel threads flag them consistently, and Rome's Aeroporti di Roma signage explicitly warns travellers to use only the signed rank outside the terminal.

Red Flags

  • Anyone inside the FCO or Ciampino arrivals hall who approaches you with "taxi?" — legitimate drivers stay at the signed rank and never solicit inside
  • A driver refuses to turn on the meter on any route that isn't the posted €50 (FCO) or €30 (Ciampino) fixed fare, or quotes a flat cash rate higher than those numbers
  • The taxi roof sign or doors are missing the official Comune di Roma number and white livery — licensed Rome taxis are always white with a visible "TAXI" roof sign and meter
  • The driver claims the card reader is broken, or that paying by card means you must cover "commission fees" — both are scam tells
  • The driver asks you to follow him out of the official queue or to a vehicle parked apart from the rank

How to Avoid

  • From FCO, take the Leonardo Express train to Termini (€14 one-way, every 15 minutes, 32-minute ride) — there is nothing to negotiate and no cash involved
  • If you do take a taxi from FCO, use only the signed official rank outside the arrivals hall, refuse all interior solicitors, and confirm the €50 fixed fare (or €30 from Ciampino) before closing the door
  • Book through the FREE NOW or itTaxi apps — the meter starts on the app and the receipt is digital, removing the "broken reader" angle
  • Always demand a ricevuta (receipt) with the taxi number, date, time and route; without it you cannot file a complaint at the Comune di Roma's Ufficio Taxi
  • Never let a driver take you to an ATM mid-ride; pay the posted fare in cash if necessary, keep the taxi number, and file a complaint at comune.roma.it or directly with the Polizia Locale
Scam #6
The Vatican Museums "Fake Staff" Line-Jumper
⚠️ High
📍 Viale Vaticano entrance to the Vatican Museums, Piazza San Pietro colonnade queue, the approach streets Via di Porta Angelica and Borgo Pio

A man in a uniform, name tag and lanyard stands near the museum entrance sorting tourists into "ticket" and "no-ticket" lines. He pulls you out of the queue and says you need to buy tickets first — at his "partner office." You're now 10 minutes away paying €65 for a €25 ticket, or being shaken down for cash you never get back.

An r/ItalyTravel post titled "Scammers at Vatican Museums line!" (124 upvotes) lays out the MO in detail: the OP arrived around 2pm without a ticket; a "Museum staff man" with "a uniform, name tag and everything, looked legit" was sorting the queue; he told her she had to buy tickets away from the crowd and steered her toward a "partner" office. Italian commenters on the same thread confirm the pattern is endemic and that police stand meters away without intervening — one 80-upvote reply from an Italian laments that it pains him to hear about it because "we're not talking about infiltrating Isis — just some basic policing." An early-morning variant documented on Piazza San Pietro sees "tour" sellers offering skip-the-line access to St. Peter's Basilica that doesn't actually exist as a separate product (basilica entry is free). A February 15, 2026 Repubblica Roma report documents the Carabinieri fining three finti Ciceroni from Iran and Brazil €400 each for unlicensed guiding at the Colosseum — a parallel pattern at parallel sites. The Vatican Museums' own policy: skip-the-line access is sold only through tickets.museivaticani.va or authorised resellers; anyone selling it on the sidewalk is not authorised. r/ItalyTravel's recurring warning is that the only legitimate Vatican Museums queue is the one physically at the entrance on Viale Vaticano, and genuine staff never approach visitors outside the building to sort them into sub-lines.

Red Flags

  • Anyone in a uniform or with a lanyard who sorts the Vatican queue on the street — genuine Musei Vaticani staff operate inside the entrance, not on the sidewalk
  • A "staff member" who tells you to leave the queue to buy tickets at a nearby office
  • Offers of "skip-the-line" or "fast-track" tours made by someone walking the queue — authentic versions are sold on the official website or by licensed tour operators with visible credentials
  • A price noticeably higher than the official €25 standard online entry (€20 on-site) — or quoted in dollars
  • Pressure to pay in cash immediately before going through any official entrance

How to Avoid

  • Buy Vatican Museums tickets only from the official site tickets.museivaticani.va — preferably in advance with a reserved entry time
  • Once on Viale Vaticano, ignore anyone who speaks to you before you are physically inside the entrance arch; walk to the queue at the door and show staff only your mobile or printed ticket
  • If you must buy tickets on the day, buy them inside the museum building, never from anyone outside
  • Likewise at St. Peter's Basilica: entry to the basilica itself is free and there is no paid "skip-the-line" for it; anyone selling one is running a scam
  • Report sidewalk "staff" to the Gendarmeria Vaticana at the entrance or call 112 — Italian commenters on r/ItalyTravel bluntly note the scam persists because tourists don't report it
Scam #7
The Bracelet & Rose Aggression (Spanish Steps, Ponte Sant'Angelo, St. Peter's)
⚠️ High
📍 Spanish Steps (Piazza di Spagna, Trinità dei Monti), Ponte Sant'Angelo, St. Peter's Square, Piazza Navona, Fontana di Trevi perimeter

A friendly stranger — typically a tall African man — asks where you're from, admires your country, and ties a string friendship bracelet on your wrist "as a gift from my tribe." Then he demands €20. When you refuse, he shoves, curses, or follows you. The rose-seller variant at the Spanish Steps works identically.

An r/rome "Elephant bracelet scam?" thread (24 upvotes) documents the textbook opening: "an African guy approached my family and asked where we were from, how long we'd be here, etc. He then gave us a bracelet with a black elephant saying it's from his tribe back in Africa." Multiple r/rome commenters describe the escalation: "They will give it to you 'for free because you're a nice guy / you look like Brad Pitt / you're from a country he loves' and then when you accept it and try to walk away he will guilt trip you or shame you for not helping out a poor soul." An r/rome "Vent: Scam artist had the nerve to TOUCH ME" thread describes the Ponte Sant'Angelo variant — the OP was poked in the arm after declining to engage, and a top commenter describes a year-ago attempt where the scammer shoved her when she tried to stop the bracelet being tied on another tourist, with "fisticuffs" nearly breaking out. A separate r/rome comment describes being verbally abused — called a "bitch" — after declining, with her boyfriend robbed the same day in Trastevere after a bracelet-guy approach turned into a physical grab for his wallet. The r/rome "Does the city ever do a cleanup?" thread names Piazza San Pietro, the Vatican Museum line and St. Peter's Square specifically as zones saturated with "beggars in bare feet," knockoff-scarf sellers and charger-block scammers. Il Messaggero's centro-storico dossier on "scippi ai turisti" counted 54 borseggiatori arrested between Ferragosto (August 15) and the autumn reporting window alone — a measure of how densely these aggressors work the tourist perimeter.

Red Flags

  • A stranger opens with "where are you from?" or compliments your country — this is the universal Rome scam opening
  • Anyone attempts to tie, place or drape anything on your wrist, neck, shoulders or hair — including bracelets, scarves, rosaries, flowers, ribbons
  • A rose-seller insists on handing a flower to your partner "as a gift to the signorina" — then charges you
  • Someone follows you after you've declined, maintains eye contact, or physically touches you to re-engage
  • You're on the Spanish Steps, at the bottom of the Trinità dei Monti stairs, on the Ponte Sant'Angelo, or anywhere in St. Peter's Square in the approach-to-gate zone

How to Avoid

  • Do not make eye contact, do not reply, do not slow your pace — walk past with "no, grazie" once and do not repeat it
  • Keep your hands at your sides or in your pockets when bracelet and rose sellers approach — a hand out to "receive" is the cue they use to escalate
  • If something is placed on you anyway, take it off immediately and drop it; you owe nothing you did not ask for
  • Travel the dense-scammer zones (Spanish Steps, Piazza San Pietro, Ponte Sant'Angelo) with a bag zipped and worn across the chest; these are all also pickpocket zones
  • If you are physically shoved, cursed or followed, dial 113 (Polizia di Stato) or 112 (Carabinieri) — Rome police actively patrol all three of these zones and the 54-arrest Ferragosto Il Messaggero reporting shows they do act when called
Scam #8
The Trevi / Pantheon / Piazza Navona Tourist-Menu Trap
🔶 Medium
📍 Alleys around Fontana di Trevi, Piazza Navona, Pantheon, Campo de' Fiori, Via della Pace, Via del Governo Vecchio, Piazza di Spagna perimeter

You sit at an outdoor table 90 seconds from Trevi. Pane and acqua arrive unrequested. The fish and pasta are priced "al etto." The bill lands at €220 for two people: €3.50 coperto × 2, a 22% "servizio," a €9 bread basket, and branzino billed at €12/hg × 5hg. Three gelatos plus a bottle of water: €42.

An August 2014 Corriere Roma / Cook piece — widely re-shared since — documented US tourists James and Mariann ordering three gelatos and one bottle of water from a Fontana di Trevi-area shop and being charged €42, with the receipt itemised in a way that made police involvement necessary. A 2025 r/RomeTravel thread "Restaurant scam near Trevi Fountain and Colosseum: $2000 for 2 tourists" documents the upper end of the pattern — a bill several multiples above the menu-posted prices after unrequested items, inflated fish/steak al etto pricing, and automatic "servizio" added to a foreign card. The r/travel "tipping in Italy" thread (111 upvotes) documents a specific Rome variant: a server chased two tourists into the street demanding a tip, in a zone where servizio was already on the bill. Italian law requires every restaurant to post a menu with prices outside the premises; tourist-trap establishments around Trevi, the Pantheon and Piazza Navona routinely omit prices for fish or steak sold "al etto" (per 100g) and then bill 400–600g portions of branzino at €10–€14/hg. The r/europe "Italian café defends charging diners $2.20 to cut sandwich in half" thread (662 upvotes) documented the extreme of the coperto culture. A separate 2025 r/ItalyTravel post "Guilt Trap at a Pharmacy (Piazza di Pasquino)" documents a parallel overcharging pattern at a pharmacy near Piazza Navona: €20 for two thermal patches that cost €2.50 each online, with the staff refusing to quote the price until after the item was rung up.

Red Flags

  • A "barker" calls to you from the pavement, or the venue uses a QR-flipbook photo menu in four or more languages — locals call this the clearest tourist-trap tell
  • The menu has no posted prices for fish or steak, or shows only "prezzo al etto" without indicating typical portion size
  • Pane, acqua, antipasti or a "welcome prosecco" arrive at your table without you ordering them
  • The coperto is higher than €3 per head, or a servizio is added automatically to foreign cards
  • The restaurant is within 150 metres of Trevi, the Pantheon, Piazza Navona or the Spanish Steps, with no Italian-language reviews visible on Google Maps

How to Avoid

  • Walk 5–7 minutes off the Trevi / Pantheon / Navona axis — Monti, Pigneto, Testaccio, Trastevere (southern half) and Prati are locally recommended and consistently 4.5+ on Italian-language Google reviews
  • Before sitting down, photograph the posted outdoor menu; Italian law requires prices to be public — if fish or steak is listed only as "al etto" without portion guidance, leave
  • Ask explicitly when seated: "Mi conferma il coperto e se il servizio è incluso?" and "Qual è la pezzatura media del pesce?"
  • Refuse items you did not order — you are not legally required to pay for pane, acqua or antipasti that arrive unrequested; say "non ho ordinato" and send them back
  • Pay by card and check the receipt on the machine before signing; if the servizio was added automatically, query it — if the posted menu did not state a servizio percentage, you can refuse it
Scam #9
The Fake Five-Star Hotel & Off-Platform Rental Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Bookings referencing Corinthia Hotel, Hassler, Cavalieri, Eden and other named Rome five-stars; Airbnb listings in Prati, Trastevere and the centro storico; vacation-rental sites claiming properties near the Spanish Steps

You book a Rome five-star (or a charming Trastevere apartment) through what looks like the property's own site. You pay. You arrive. The booking doesn't exist, the room was never available, or the apartment is a locked door at an address no host has ever occupied.

A December 13, 2022 ANSA and Corriere Roma investigation titled "Maxi truffa ai turisti americani a Roma" documented at least 200 American tourist victims of a fake-hotel ring: scammers created falsi profili simulating Rome's most prominent luxury properties, harvested credit-card numbers, and left guests at the real reception desks to discover their reservations were fiction. The case pulled in US intelligence and the Corinthia Hotel was named in court documents. On March 16, 2025 Corriere Roma reported that 20 people had been rinviati a giudizio (sent to trial) for the continuing iteration of the same scheme, with more than 200 victims across multiple five-star properties that "non esistono" at the addresses given. A parallel November 2023 ANSA report documented card-cloning in hotel settings with €150,000 in unauthorised transactions across 150 operations. An August 2025 ANSA report about a €2,700 casa vacanze inesistente documents the short-let version: a seller advertised a Rome-area vacation rental that did not exist, took payment, and vanished — the same playbook at lower price points. Booking.com's own periodic phishing-campaign problems, documented in Corriere's April 2026 cyber-attack coverage, add to the risk: scammers impersonate Booking hosts and redirect payment. On r/rome and r/ItalyTravel, the recurring MO is an off-platform contact (direct WhatsApp, personal email) with urgency language and a request to wire a deposit or pay a "security fee" in cash on arrival.

Red Flags

  • A "Booking" or "Airbnb" message arriving via personal email or WhatsApp rather than inside the platform inbox
  • Any instruction to wire a deposit, pay a "security fee" in cash on arrival, or redirect payment to a non-platform IBAN
  • A hotel reservation confirmed only by PDF or email, with no charge to your card at booking time and no confirmation in the hotel's own website portal
  • A price noticeably below the same dates at the real five-star property's own site or Google Hotels
  • A listing that uses stock interior photos you can reverse-image-search to properties in other cities

How to Avoid

  • Book hotels only at the property's official website or on major aggregators (Booking, Expedia, Marriott/IHG/Hilton apps); never via a cold email or a search-ad URL that doesn't match the hotel's domain
  • Confirm the reservation by calling the hotel's own published number (not a number in the confirmation email) and reading your confirmation code to reception
  • For apartments, book only through Airbnb, Booking.com or Vrbo — never wire money to a host, even for a "friend's place" recommendation
  • Check the property on Google Street View before arrival; legitimate Rome apartments have visible signage, intercom labels and a clear street number
  • Rome's per-person tourism tax (€7/night at most hotels, €10 at five-star properties) is collected only by registered accommodations — demand a receipt if cash is requested, and refuse any "city fee" outside those amounts
Scam #10
The POS Pirata Contactless Skimmer
✅ Low
📍 Metro A/B carriages, Termini underground galleria, Fontana di Trevi perimeter, Piazza Navona, Piazza di Spagna, dense bus 64 and 40 crowds

A pickpocket doesn't need your wallet anymore. A wireless POS hidden in a bag, passed close to your pocket or backpack, reads contactless cards and your phone wallet directly through the fabric — and takes €50 to €9,000 in a single touch.

An August 1, 2025 Repubblica Roma report — "Col pos pirata svuotava i conti dei turisti: arrestata" — documents the Rome-specific version: a female thief was denounced for indebito utilizzo di strumenti di pagamento after walking tourist zones with a mobile, contactless POS device. A July 25, 2025 Rainews TGR Campania investigation documented the arrest of a 36-year-old Peruvian woman in Sorrento for the same device, noting she had "numerosi precedenti penali a Roma" and that in a past Rome incident she had used a similar device to subtract circa 9mila euro (roughly €9,000) from a single tourist. A July 31, 2025 Corriere della Sera Tecnologia explainer — "Un Pos mobile per rubare soldi da carte e telefoni tenuti in tasca: il borseggio 2.0 è una realtà" — confirms the device reads contactless payment directly from a card or phone in a pocket within a few centimetres, below the €50 / €100 tap thresholds that bypass the PIN or face-ID step. The Rome case involved a €9,000 transaction, meaning merchant-category or limit logic was manipulated to accept a single high-value charge. Because the attack is contactless, it is the cleanest documented "invisible" pickpocket: victims discover the theft only when the bank notification arrives. r/onebag's "RFID holder for cards" thread reflects traveller uncertainty about the threat — commenters split between "I've never noticed a skim" and "it's real, protect tap-to-pay cards." Low severity in our ranking reflects the still-modest absolute number of confirmed incidents versus classical pickpocketing — but the per-incident loss is unusually large, so the risk-adjusted score is real.

Red Flags

  • Someone stands unusually close to your pocket, backpack or bag in a crowded metro carriage, bus 64 or Vatican queue — especially if they hold a bag or jacket at waist height
  • An unexplained tap-to-pay SMS, app notification or push from your bank immediately after a crowded moment
  • A stranger brushes the outside of your backpack or jeans pocket "by accident" but does not apologise or move away
  • Small card charges (€10–€50) you don't recognise — a common first test before a larger single charge
  • Loss of cell signal or network blocking from a nearby device can correlate with jamming during an attack (uncommon but documented)

How to Avoid

  • Carry your contactless cards in an RFID-blocking wallet or sleeve when in Rome's crowded zones — onebag commenters confirm these cost under €15 and work
  • Disable contactless-only (NFC) payments on cards you don't need for daily spending via your bank app; leave only one card contactless-active
  • Lower your daily tap limit in your banking app — most EU banks allow you to set a €25 or €50 cap per contactless transaction and require PIN for anything higher
  • Use your phone's wallet (Apple Pay / Google Pay) for in-store purchases — the device-bound tokenisation makes POS-pirata attacks significantly harder than against physical cards
  • Enable real-time transaction alerts with your bank and review them on the spot; a POS-pirata loss becomes irreversible only if it goes unreported for hours

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Carabinieri / Polizia di Stato station. Call 112 (Carabinieri) or 113 (Polizia). For metro and bus theft, the Polmetro base at Termini (opened February 2026) coordinates 50 agents across 5,000 cameras. Get an official crime report — you'll need it for insurance claims and card chargebacks. Report online at poliziadistato.it.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately and use in-app blocks. Rome's POS-pirata and ATM-cashout variants both depend on a 3–10 minute reaction lag — freeze cards in under two minutes and review the last 24 hours of activity.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Rome is at Via Vittorio Veneto 121, 00187 Rome. For emergencies: +39 06-4674-1.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — the October 2025 Repubblica Roma deposito-clandestino investigation shows stolen goods travel fast through Termini-area guesthouses. Share the location with police instead.
