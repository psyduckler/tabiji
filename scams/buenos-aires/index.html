The 6 Scams

Scam #1
Florida Avenue 'Cambio!' Touts & Counterfeit Peso Exchange
⚠️ High
📍 Calle Florida pedestrian street (Microcentro, from Plaza de Mayo to Plaza San Martín), Lavalle pedestrian area, Av. Corrientes at Florida intersection, Galerías Pacífico perimeter

Calle Florida is Buenos Aires's main pedestrian shopping street and ground zero for Argentina's informal exchange (cambio) touts.

Men (arbolitos, 'little trees') who shout '¡Cambio, cambio! Dollar, euro!' at every passing tourist. This means the classic 'get blue dollar from Florida Ave' advice is now often a net loss before fraud even enters the picture. and Community reports document the economic inversion. The scam variants: (1) counterfeit peso bills passed as change; (2) bait-and-switch exchange rate (quoted rate drops once you commit); (3) distraction pickpocketing during the transaction; (4) fake 'cueva' (informal exchange office) upstairs that turns out to be a robbery setup.

For older travelers in Buenos Aires, the 2025 defensive playbook: (1) refuse all Florida Ave '¡cambio!' approaches — 'No gracias' and keep walking; (2) for USD-to-peso conversion in 2025, Western Union (Plaza San Martín, Retiro branch, and 40+ other city locations) still offers the best rate for cash USD pickups — typically matches or beats blue rate with no counterfeit risk; (3) alternative: download Lemon Cash, Belo, or Ripio apps to receive USDT (stablecoin) and convert to pesos at in-app rates — Legal and competitive; (4) for card payments, most foreign credit cards now automatically apply the MEP/blue-equivalent rate via Argentina's tourist-card scheme (no IVA reimbursement needed) — verify with your card issuer; (5) if you must use an informal cueva, use an established one on the upper floors of Galería Güemes (Florida 165) or similar — never street-level with a stranger; (6) always count bills in daylight and check watermarks + security thread on every note (especially the 1000, 2000, and 10000-peso notes which have the highest counterfeit rate).

Red Flags

  • '¡Cambio, cambio!' shouted on Florida Avenue or Lavalle
  • Rate quoted on the street drops once you commit to exchange
  • 'Cueva' upstairs location with no visible signage or registration
  • Bills counted in poor light or over-quickly before hand-off
  • 2025: quoted 'blue rate' more than 5% worse than Western Union rate

How to Avoid

  • Refuse all '¡cambio!' street approaches — 'No gracias' + walk
  • Use Western Union for USD-to-peso cash pickup (best 2025 rate)
  • Alternative: Lemon Cash / Belo / Ripio USDT apps for legal crypto conversion
  • Foreign card payments in 2025 auto-apply MEP-equivalent rate — verify with issuer
  • Always count bills in daylight; check watermark + security thread
Scam #2
Buenos Aires Taxi 'Tacheros Estafadores' — Cloned Meter, Long Route, Short-Change
⚠️ High
📍 San Telmo, La Boca, Retiro, Microcentro (around Florida Ave), Palermo Soho nightlife zones, airport transfer runs, late-night from bars/restaurants

is the 2025 anchor on Buenos Aires's.

Persistent taxi-driver scam ecosystem, is a companion primer. Three main variants operate in 2025: (1) Cloned Meter — the meter runs 2–3x legitimate rate; a San Telmo-to-Retiro ride that should cost ~ARS 3,500 shows $10,000+ on the meter; drivers target obvious tourists with luggage; (2) Long Route — driver takes a 4–6 km detour ('traffic' excuse) on what should be a direct 2–3 km trip; (3) Counterfeit Return — you hand over a clean 10,000-peso note; driver quickly swaps it for a pre-prepared counterfeit, shows it back to you, claims it was counterfeit, and demands a clean replacement. Variant (4): floating tariff — driver has 'just changed tariff' mid-ride without authorization; refuses printed receipt. Variant (5): credit-card 'broken' — POS terminal 'fails' so you must pay cash, at which point counterfeit-return or rounded-up-cash scam runs.

For older travelers in Buenos Aires, the 2025 defensive playbook: (1) use Uber, Cabify, or DiDi exclusively — all three operate legally in BA and bypass every taxi scam variant; fares are transparent and app-paid (no cash handling); Uber/Cabify from San Telmo to Recoleta runs ~$2,500–$4,000 ARS in 2025; (2) if you must take a street taxi, use only clearly-marked 'Radio Taxi' cars (yellow-and-black with roof light) — never unmarked or 'remis' flagged down on the street; (3) insist the meter starts at the base tariff (ARS 260 in 2025, check sign on dashboard); watch it count up; (4) pay with small bills matched closely to the fare ($100 / $500 / $1,000 denominations) — never hand over a 10,000 or 20,000 peso note; (5) demand the printed receipt (recibo) and the driver's name/número; legitimate drivers always provide both; (6) for airport runs, use Manuel Tienda León shared-shuttle bus (ARS 15,000), Tienda León private taxi ($35,000), or Uber — avoid Terminal-A 'Remis' touts.

Red Flags

  • Unmarked street taxi or 'remis' flagged down outside a licensed stand
  • Meter at twice the rate shown on dashboard tariff sign
  • Driver takes detour with 'traffic' excuse on a direct route
  • Driver refuses to print recibo (receipt)
  • Credit-card POS 'fails' forcing cash payment

How to Avoid

  • Use Uber / Cabify / DiDi exclusively — all legal, transparent fares in BA
  • If street taxi: Radio Taxi only (yellow-and-black + roof light)
  • Pay in small bills ($500/$1,000) matched to the fare — never $10,000+
  • Demand printed recibo + driver's name/número
  • Airport: Manuel Tienda León shuttle ($15k) or Tienda León taxi ($35k)
Scam #3
Bird Poop / Mustard Distraction Pickpocket
⚠️ High
📍 Plaza de Mayo, Puerto Madero riverfront walk, Avenida 9 de Julio crossings, Recoleta Cemetery perimeter, La Boca/Caminito tourist strip, San Telmo Sunday market (Feria de San Pedro Telmo)

is the 2025 anchor on Buenos Aires's.

Most effective distraction-pickpocket operation. The setup runs in 3 phases: (1) a confederate squirts a liquid (mustard, ketchup, chocolate syrup, or actual bird-droppings simulant) onto the victim's back or shoulder from above or behind; (2) a second team member appears with napkins, offering to 'help clean' — they pat the victim down enthusiastically; (3) during the patting, a third team member lifts wallet, phone, or passport from an unzipped bag or back pocket. Peak-activity zones in Buenos Aires: Plaza de Mayo during weekday business hours, Caminito's narrow cobblestone streets, Recoleta Cemetery entrance queue, San Telmo Feria de Pedro crowded aisles, and Puerto Madero dinner-hour foot traffic. The scam often targets 55+ travelers because of slower reaction time and higher-value items (watches, camera gear).

For older travelers in Buenos Aires, the defensive playbook: (1) if you feel anything hit your back/shoulder unexpectedly, do not stop — walk briskly to a shop entrance, cafe, or hotel lobby and check yourself in their bathroom mirror; (2) refuse all 'cleaning help' from strangers, no matter how apologetic or insistent they seem; the 'good Samaritan' is part of the team; (3) keep wallet in a front pocket or money belt under clothing — never in a back pocket or bag top; (4) keep phone + passport in separate locations (e.g. passport in hotel safe + phone in zipped bag + wallet in money belt) so one loss doesn't cascade; (5) in Caminito, Recoleta, and San Telmo peak hours, wear a small crossbody bag in front of your body with one hand on the strap; (6) if you're hit while alone with no nearby business, call '911' (BA emergency) or walk to the nearest cafe and use their bathroom; (7) report theft to the Comisaría Turística (Tourist Police, Av. Corrientes 436, +54 11 4346 5748, 24/7 English service) within 24h — Required for travel insurance claims.

Red Flags

  • Liquid unexpectedly hits your back/shoulder in crowded tourist zone
  • Strangers immediately approach with 'Let me help you clean that!'
  • Multiple 'helpers' patting you down simultaneously
  • Approach happens near your bag or back pocket
  • Distraction starts just after you've taken out your wallet / phone

How to Avoid

  • If something hits your back: walk briskly to a cafe/shop — Do NOT stop on the street
  • Refuse all 'cleaning help' from strangers — the 'helper' is part of the team
  • Wallet in front pocket or money belt; never back pocket or bag top
  • Passport + cash in separate locations (hotel safe vs money belt)
  • Report theft: Comisaría Turística Av. Corrientes 436, +54 11 4346 5748

Scam #4
EZE / AEP Airport 'Remis' Overcharge & Meet-Greet Service Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Ezeiza International Airport (EZE) Terminal A + B arrivals hall, Aeroparque Jorge Newbery (AEP) arrivals curb, Retiro bus terminal pickup zone, 'Meet & Greet' and 'Private Transfer' online booking sites

is the 2025 anchor on Buenos Aires.

Airport-transfer scams, with secondary reference in. EZE (Ezeiza) is 35 km from central Buenos Aires (45–60 min drive); AEP (Aeroparque) is 3 km from central BA (15–20 min). Legitimate 2025 options: (1) Manuel Tienda León shared bus from EZE to Retiro (downtown BA) — ARS 15,000 one-way, departs every 30 min, scam-proof; (2) Manuel Tienda León private taxi at the official counter — $35,000–$45,000 ARS flat to most central addresses; (3) Uber/Cabify via app from EZE designated rideshare zone — typically $25,000–$40,000 ARS depending on surge; (4) from AEP, Uber/Cabify $6,000–$12,000 ARS to central BA. Scam variants: (1) 'Remis' touts in Terminal-A arrivals hall quoting $80,000–$150,000 ARS ('flat rate' 3–4x legitimate); (2) 'Private Meet & Greet Service' websites demanding USD prepayment at $80–$150 USD for what costs ARS 35,000 ($35 USD) via Tienda León; (3) driver claims 'meter broken' then demands cash in USD; (4) driver takes indirect route via Autopista Ricchieri (adds 30% distance); (5) 'shared shuttle' tout charging per-person at ARS 20,000 × 4 passengers when Tienda León bus is $15,000 one-way total.

For older travelers arriving at EZE or AEP in 2025, the clean playbook: (1) walk past all 'Remis' and 'Meet & Greet' touts in the arrivals hall — they're unlicensed and overcharge; (2) proceed to the official Manuel Tienda León counter (clearly marked 'Tienda León' signage inside the terminal) for bus or taxi tickets — $15k/$35k respectively; (3) for Uber/Cabify/DiDi, walk to the designated rideshare pickup zone (EZE: 'Parking de Ride Sharing' signs outside Terminal A) and request via app — use WiFi in the arrivals hall to confirm fare screenshot before walking out; (4) if you prefer pre-booked, use Viator, GetYourGuide, or your hotel concierge (verified operators) — expect to pay $45–$60 USD for EZE-to-central-BA private; (5) refuse pre-booking via unfamiliar 'Meet & Greet' websites demanding USD prepayment; (6) always confirm the driver's license plate matches the app screenshot; photograph the plate before entering the car; (7) pay via app (Uber/Cabify/DiDi) or card at Tienda León — avoid cash payment at the end of the ride.

Red Flags

  • 'Remis' or 'Transfer' tout inside EZE arrivals hall quoting $80k+ ARS
  • 'Meet & Greet' website demanding USD prepayment $80–$150
  • Driver claims 'meter broken' — Demands cash at end of ride
  • Driver takes indirect route via Ricchieri autopista (adds 30%+)
  • 'Shared shuttle' charging per-person rather than per-trip

How to Avoid

  • Walk past all arrivals-hall 'Remis' / 'Meet & Greet' touts
  • Manuel Tienda León bus $15k ARS to Retiro — buy at official counter inside terminal
  • Tienda León private taxi $35k–$45k ARS at same counter
  • Uber/Cabify/DiDi from designated rideshare zone with app fare screenshot
  • Pay via app or card at Tienda León — avoid cash at end of ride
Scam #5
Counterfeit Peso Bills Returned as Change
⚠️ High
📍 Informal 'cueva' exchange houses, Florida Ave cambio touts, street-vendor transactions at San Telmo Sunday market, late-night taxi payments, small shops in tourist zones

community forums 'Me estoy por pasar a Tuenti y me llegó esto.

Es falso?' (2025) and Community reports frame the counterfeit-peso pattern which remains active in Argentina throughout 2025. Argentina's high-denomination bills (1000, 2000, 10000 pesos; 20000 note introduced 2024) have elevated counterfeit rates — particularly the 1000 and 2000 notes. The primary scam vectors: (1) unofficial 'cambio' / 'cueva' exchange returns a stack with 1–3 counterfeit bills mixed in; (2) taxi driver performs bill-switch — you hand over a clean note, driver shows you a counterfeit and demands a real replacement; (3) small-shop clerk gives change with counterfeits after you pay with a large denomination; (4) street-vendor 'Mercado Pago' QR scam where the scan shows a payment that was reversed; (5) ATMs at small independent operators (not Banco Nación, Santander, BBVA, HSBC, or Galicia main branches) occasionally dispense counterfeit.

For older travelers in Buenos Aires in 2025, the defensive playbook: (1) know the security features: genuine 1000-peso note (General Juan Manuel de Rosas) has a 3D-effect security thread, watermark of Rosas's face, and color-shifting '1000' in lower right corner; genuine 2000-peso note (Ramón Carrillo + Cecilia Grierson) has a color-shifting '2000' and tactile relief; (2) always count exchange and change in daylight or strong artificial light — Hold each high-denomination bill up to the light to check watermark; (3) if a bill feels 'flat' or 'papery' rather than having the tactile relief of genuine pesos, it's counterfeit — Real pesos have raised ink you can feel; (4) use cards (Visa/Mastercard) for any transaction over ARS 50,000 — Virtually eliminates counterfeit-return risk; (5) use ATMs only at Banco Nación, Santander Río, BBVA, HSBC, Galicia, or Macro main branches — Not at street-level small ATMs in pharmacies or mini-markets; (6) for taxi payment, match bills as close as possible to the fare — ARS 3,500 ride should be paid with $3,500 or $4,000 in small bills, not a $10,000 bill; (7) if you receive suspected counterfeit, report to the Banco Central (BCRA) hotline at 0800-999-6663 and the Comisaría Turística.

Red Flags

  • Exchange or change returned in very crisp, 'flat' bills
  • Taxi driver shows you a 'counterfeit' bill claiming it was yours
  • Small shop gives you back 3+ high-denomination bills (1000s or 2000s)
  • Bill lacks tactile relief (smooth vs raised ink on genuine pesos)
  • ATM at small independent operator (not a major-bank branch)

How to Avoid

  • Count all exchange + change in daylight — Hold up to light for watermark
  • Feel for tactile relief on genuine pesos (raised ink on portrait + numerals)
  • Use Visa/Mastercard for transactions over ARS 50,000
  • ATMs: Banco Nación / Santander / BBVA / HSBC / Galicia / Macro main branches only
  • Pay taxis with small bills ($500/$1,000) matched to fare — never $10,000+

Buenos Aires Airbnb / Temporary Rental Off-Platform & 'Illegal Cash' Fraud
⚠️ High
📍 Palermo Soho / Hollywood short-term rentals, Recoleta luxury apartment STRs, San Telmo historic-building apartments, off-platform WhatsApp / Facebook Marketplace listings

Argentina's long-term rental market (mes por mes, 'month by month') traditionally priced in USD cash due to peso instability, and Milei-era reforms haven't fully changed the informal-cash culture by 2025. Common scam patterns: (1) Airbnb host requests the stay be 'paid partly via Airbnb, partly in USD cash on arrival' to 'avoid Argentina taxes' — this violates Airbnb TOS and leaves the guest with zero protection on the cash portion; if anything goes wrong, Airbnb support treats the reservation as fraudulent; (2) off-platform WhatsApp 'Buenos Aires long-term rental' listings demanding USD cash on arrival + 'deposit' — property is often fake, host disappears; (3) Facebook Marketplace 'Palermo apartment' at 30% below market rate with Zelle/PayPal/crypto demand; (4) legitimate-seeming 'host' photos stolen from real Airbnb listings; (5) in-person viewing where 'landlord' shows the apartment then disappears after cash hand-off.

For older travelers booking Buenos Aires accommodation, the protective playbook: (1) book short-term stays only through Airbnb, VRBO, or Booking.com platform payment in full — never accept 'partly cash on arrival' requests, regardless of what the host says about Argentina taxes (it's a TOS violation and invalidates your protection); (2) for long-term stays (1+ month), use ZonaProp, ArgenProp, or Remax Argentina (the three legitimate local real-estate portals) — never WhatsApp or Facebook Marketplace listings; (3) for USD-cash-required legitimate rentals, require a signed Spanish-language contract (contrato de alquiler temporario) with the landlord's DNI (national ID) + property escritura (title) information; (4) pay any USD cash via Western Union transfer (generates a paper trail) rather than hand-to-hand; (5) legitimate Buenos Aires hotels and serviced apartments: Alvear Palace Hotel (Recoleta), Palacio Duhau - Park Hyatt, Faena Hotel (Puerto Madero), NH Collection Buenos Aires Jousten, Hotel Madero; (6) avoid Palermo / Recoleta 'luxury apartment' ads at <50% of comparable verified listings — Too-good-to-be-true is the tell; (7) report fraud to the Defensa del Consumidor (consumer protection, 0800-666-1518) and to the Comisaría Turística.

Red Flags

  • Airbnb host requests 'partly via Airbnb, partly cash on arrival'
  • Off-platform WhatsApp / Facebook Marketplace 'BA long-term rental'
  • Host claims 'paying tax' justifies off-platform cash payment
  • Palermo / Recoleta 'luxury apartment' at 30%+ below comparable listings
  • Zelle / PayPal / crypto demanded for Argentina rental deposit

How to Avoid

  • Book STRs only through Airbnb / VRBO / Booking.com platform payment in full
  • Long-term rentals: ZonaProp / ArgenProp / Remax Argentina only — never WhatsApp
  • If USD cash is legally required: signed Spanish contract + landlord DNI + property escritura
  • Pay USD cash via Western Union (paper trail) — never hand-to-hand
  • Report fraud: Defensa del Consumidor 0800-666-1518 + Comisaría Turística

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Policía Federal Argentina station. Call 911 (Police) or 107 (Medical Emergency). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at fiscales.gob.ar.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Buenos Aires is at Avenida Colombia 4300, C1425GMN Buenos Aires. For emergencies: +54 11-5777-4533.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
