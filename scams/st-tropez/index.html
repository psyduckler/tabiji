The 12 Scams

Scam #1
Restaurant Racketeering & Minimum Spend
⚠️ High
📍 Beach clubs, High-end restaurants, Port area restaurants

St-Tropez restaurants have developed sophisticated systems to extract maximum spending from tourists.

Restaurants compile secret databases tracking how much each diner spends and tips. If you don't spend or tip enough, you'll be blacklisted and refused a table on your next visit. Some establishments demand €5,000 minimum spending or €1,500 per head just to secure a reservation. Despite service charges already being included, staff aggressively pressure tourists for additional 'American-style' tips. In one notorious incident, a waiter chased an Italian customer into the parking lot demanding he return inside to add another €500 to his already €500 tip. The mayor of Saint-Tropez has publicly condemned these practices as 'racketeering.'

Red Flags

  • Being told tables aren't available unless you meet a minimum spend
  • Pressure for tips on top of included service charge
  • Being asked about budget when booking

How to Avoid

  • Research restaurant policies and reviews before booking.
  • Ask explicitly about minimum spending requirements upfront.
  • Remember that service charge is already included in France.
  • Choose smaller local establishments over famous beach clubs.
Scam #2
Luxury Watch Theft
⚠️ High
📍 Pampelonne Beach, Beach restaurants, Port area, Yacht clubs, Town center

Organized criminal groups systematically target tourists wearing expensive watches worth €100,000+.

The operation is sophisticated: spotters identify valuable timepieces, then communicate with thieves who steal the watch—subtly or violently—before escaping on mopeds with tampered plates. Three separate incidents in St-Tropez resulted in €400,000 worth of stolen watches in a single weekend. These thefts are becoming increasingly brazen and sometimes violent.

Red Flags

  • Someone showing unusual interest in your watch
  • Being followed by people on scooters
  • Groups of people crowding around you

How to Avoid

  • Leave valuable watches worth over €5,000 at home.
  • If wearing expensive watches, keep them covered.
  • Stay aware of your surroundings, especially near beach areas.
  • Report suspicious behavior to police immediately.
Scam #3
Villa Rental Fraud
⚠️ High
📍 Online booking platforms

Scammers create attractive listings for luxury villas that either don't exist or aren't theirs to rent.

They use professional photos stolen from legitimate listings and below-market prices to lure victims. Travelers book and pay via wire transfer, only to arrive and discover the property doesn't exist or someone else has already rented it. Fake booking forms designed to look like Airbnb confirmations direct money to fraudulent accounts.

Red Flags

  • Prices significantly below market rate
  • Payment requested via wire transfer
  • Listing only available through their own website
  • Pressure to book quickly without proper vetting

How to Avoid

  • Use only established concierge services and rental agencies.
  • Perform reverse image searches on property photos.
  • Never pay via wire transfer—use credit card for protection.
  • Be suspicious of deals that seem too good to be true.

Scam #4
Hotel Room Burglary
⚠️ High
📍 Luxury hotels, Villas with private terraces

Professional burglars target luxury hotel rooms, particularly those with terrace access.

In one case, two British women lost nearly €1 million in valuables—including 11 Hermès Birkin bags, Patek Philippe watches, and Cartier jewelry—after their five-star hotel room was ransacked within hours of check-in. The thieves broke through the private terrace door and tore the safe from the wall. A hotel employee was questioned, suggesting possible inside involvement.

Red Flags

  • Rooms with easy terrace access from exterior
  • Hotels without proper security measures
  • Staff showing unusual interest in your valuables

How to Avoid

  • Don't bring valuables worth more than €5,000.
  • Request upper floor rooms without easy exterior access.
  • Keep valuable items with you when possible.
  • Consider travel insurance that covers theft.
Scam #5
Gas Burglary (Villa Gassing)
⚠️ High
📍 Rental villas, Hotel rooms with external ventilation access

Criminals allegedly pump anesthetic gas through air conditioning vents or windows to sedate sleeping occupants before robbing them. Notable victims include F1 driver Jenson Button and TV presenter Richard Hammond. Police estimate 90% of villa raids go unreported. However, medical experts dispute whether such 'knock-out gas' actually exists as described.

Red Flags

  • Unusual smells near air vents or windows
  • Feeling unexpectedly drowsy
  • Ground floor rooms with accessible windows

How to Avoid

  • Choose upper floor accommodations.
  • Lock all windows and terrace doors.
  • Don't sleep with windows open.
  • Consider portable door alarms.
Scam #6
Beach Club Overcharging
🔶 Medium
📍 Pampelonne Beach, Club 55, Nikki Beach

Beach clubs employ questionable practices.

Sun loungers cost €35-50 each, and famous clubs like Nikki Beach require €1,500 minimum spend on weekends during high season. Despite websites stating prices include service charges, staff express dissatisfaction with tip amounts. To get weekend reservations at exclusive clubs like Club 55, guests may need to 'tip' waiters in advance.

Red Flags

  • No clear pricing displayed
  • Staff pushing expensive items without showing prices
  • Pressure to tip on top of service charge

How to Avoid

  • Research prices and minimum spend requirements before visiting.
  • Ask for prices upfront before ordering.
  • Remember service charge is already included.
  • Consider less famous beach clubs.
Scam #7
Boat/Yacht Rental Hidden Fees
🔶 Medium
📍 Port of St-Tropez, Marina, Online booking platforms

Boat rental companies advertise attractive daily rates but add substantial hidden costs: fuel (St-Tropez has notably higher diesel prices), delivery/redelivery fees, mooring fees, and water sports equipment charges. Captain fees vary widely—standard rate is €350, but some quote €500+ without justification. Additional unexpected charges may appear for 'gourmet lunch' or mandatory insurance add-ons.

Red Flags

  • Unusually low advertised daily rates
  • Vague pricing without itemized costs
  • Captain fees significantly above €350

How to Avoid

  • Get complete all-inclusive pricing in writing before booking.
  • Compare total costs including fuel across companies.
  • Ask specifically about all potential additional charges.
Scam #8
Pickpocketing & Distraction Theft
🔶 Medium
📍 Place des Lices Market, Port area, Crowded restaurants, Beach areas

Professional pickpockets operate in crowded tourist areas during summer.

Organized gangs use distraction techniques—one member causes a commotion while others target your valuables. Some teams use the 'petition/clipboard approach.' Scooter thieves may grab bags or phones from pedestrians. The Tuesday and Saturday markets at Place des Lices are particularly high-risk.

Red Flags

  • Strangers bumping into you or creating commotions
  • Someone asking you to sign a petition
  • Groups surrounding you

How to Avoid

  • Keep bags zipped and in front of you.
  • Don't carry bags on your street-side shoulder.
  • Use money belts or hidden pouches.
  • Be extra vigilant in crowded market areas.
Scam #9
Taxi Overcharging
🔶 Medium
📍 Nice Airport transfers, Town center, Beach areas

Unlicensed and some licensed taxi drivers target tourists with inflated fares.

Common tactics include claiming the meter is broken, taking long routes, or forcing cash payment. Airport transfers from Nice to St-Tropez legitimately cost €240-275 (day) or €265-285 (night), but scammers may quote much higher.

Red Flags

  • Driver claims meter is broken
  • Credit card reader 'not working'
  • Quotes significantly above €275 for Nice airport transfer

How to Avoid

  • Use only official taxis from designated stands.
  • Research standard fares before arrival.
  • Use ride-sharing apps like Uber for tracked journeys.
  • Pre-book airport transfers through hotels.
Scam #10
Jet Ski Rental Damage Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Beaches, Water sports rental areas, Port Grimaud

Some jet ski rental operators make false damage claims after rental.

After you return the jet ski, they claim you damaged it and demand large sums for repairs—often under threats and coercion. If you refuse to pay, you may be threatened with police, where 'administrative charges' inflate the demanded amount further.

Red Flags

  • Operators demanding passport as collateral
  • No pre-rental inspection offered
  • Resistance to documenting existing damage

How to Avoid

  • Photograph/video the jet ski from all angles before rental.
  • Ensure date/time stamps are visible in documentation.
  • Never surrender your passport as collateral.
  • Research operators thoroughly—check online reviews.
Scam #11
Contactless Card Skimming
🔶 Medium
📍 Crowded areas, Markets, Public transport

Thieves use digital skimming devices to steal money from contactless-enabled cards without physical contact.

They carry concealed readers that capture card information when held close to your wallet or purse in crowded areas, allowing fraudulent purchases or card cloning.

Red Flags

  • Strangers standing unusually close in crowds
  • Someone holding a bag or device near your pockets
  • Unexpected small charges on your card statements

How to Avoid

  • Use RFID-blocking wallets or card sleeves.
  • Keep cards in front pockets or secure inner pockets.
  • Regularly monitor card statements for unauthorized charges.
  • Set up transaction alerts on your banking app.
Scam #12
Fake Charity Petitions
🟢 Low
📍 Place des Lices, Port area, Tourist walkways

Scammers approach tourists with clipboards asking them to sign petitions for fake charities.

While you're distracted reading and signing, an accomplice targets your bag or pockets. Even if no theft occurs, you may be pressured to donate money to a non-existent cause.

Red Flags

  • Aggressive approach with clipboard
  • Vague charity names or causes
  • Multiple people surrounding you

How to Avoid

  • Politely refuse and keep walking.
  • Never stop to engage with clipboard wielders.
  • Keep your belongings secure if you do stop.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Police Nationale / SAMU station. Call 17 (Police) or 15 (SAMU medical). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at pre-plainte-en-ligne.interieur.gouv.fr.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Paris is at 2 Avenue Gabriel, 75008 Paris. For emergencies: +33 1 43-12-22-22.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
