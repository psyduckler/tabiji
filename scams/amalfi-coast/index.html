7 Tourist Scams on the Amalfi Coast

Key Takeaways

  • The Amalfi Coast is one town — it's six: Amalfi, Ravello, Praiano, Atrani, Minori, Maiori. Its scam profile is not petty pickpocketing (as in Rome or Bologna) but a concentrated transport-and-restaurant shakedown driven by the SS163 bottleneck, seasonal cruise-ship day-tripping, and a captive private-car market. 5 of 7 scams are rated high risk
  • The SITA Sud public bus covers Sorrento → Positano → Praiano → Amalfi at €2.10 one-way per leg — the 24-hour Unico Costiera day pass is €10, sold only at tabaccherie and official kiosks. Any "bus ticket" or "day pass" above this being sold by a street seller, driver, or clipboard hawker is a markup; any private-car quote above €150 Sorrento → Positano or €200 Sorrento → Amalfi is the taxi-market gouge that the 2024 r/ItalyTravel thread "How are the taxi drivers in Amalfi Coast not millionaires?" documents line-by-line
  • Fanpage's "Tassisti abusivi dal Napoletano ad Amalfi per truffare i turisti" documents a carabinieri sweep that sanctioned 6 abusivi drivers operating Amalfi, Ravello and the coast — unlicensed NCCs driving in from Naples province to target cruise passengers at Amalfi Molo Pennello. Positanonews adds that abusivi "propose a transfer apparently cheaper than a licensed taxi" and then overcharge with no receipt — the exact tell
  • Sea-view restaurants on Amalfi's lungomare, Atrani's piazza, Ravello's belvederes and Positano's Spiaggia Grande layer four overcharges — coperto at €4–6 per person, automatic 15% servizio on foreign cards, pane/acqua arriving uninvited, and whole fish priced "al etto" arriving as 400–700g — that together can triple a bill. Italian law requires menus with prices posted outside: no posted menu is the single clearest trap signal

⚡ Quick Safety Tips

  • Take the SITA Sud bus (€2.10 single at any tabaccheria, €10 Unico Costiera day pass) or the Alilauro / Travelmar ferry (€20–25) end-to-end across the coast — refuse any €150+ private-transfer quote that has no written itinerary, licence number, or receipt
  • At Amalfi Molo Pennello, Marina Grande and Marina di Praia, book boat charters online in advance with named operators (Cobalto, Restart, Lucibello); walk past clipboard hawkers who pitch "the same tour, €100 cheaper" with cash-only
  • Before sitting at any Amalfi, Atrani, or Ravello sea-view restaurant, photograph the posted menu from outside — Italian law requires prices, and the absence of per-item pricing on fish or steak is the trap signal; ask the waiter to weigh-and-quote the specific fish before cooking
  • Park only at official Piazza Flavio Gioia or Luna Rossa lots in Amalfi, signed lots on the SS163 above Positano, or Piazza del Duomo in Ravello — refuse any man in a vest who flags your moving car into a "reserved" spot
  • Book Amalfi Coast villas and apartments only on Airbnb, Booking.com or Vrbo; never wire money off-platform or pay cryptocurrency for a "discount" — the €180/night villa-with-pool that's cheaper than every other listing is the scam

The 7 Scams

Scam #1
The SS163 Private-Transfer & Abusivo-Taxi Gouge
⚠️ High
📍 The SS163 Amalfi Drive end-to-end — Sorrento station rank, Positano Chiesa Nuova, Praiano Vettica Maggiore, Amalfi Molo Pennello and Piazza Flavio Gioia, Ravello taxi rank, and every cruise-ship drop-off point

You want a ride from Sorrento to Positano, or Praiano to Ravello, or Amalfi to your hotel in Minori. The driver at the rank — or your hotel concierge — quotes €120, €150, €200. The SITA bus covers the same route for €2.10 at any tabaccheria. The Amalfi taxi market is one of the most egregiously gouged in the Mediterranean, and on top of it sits a thin but active layer of unlicensed drivers driving in from Naples province to work the cruise-ship piers.

A widely-read 2024 r/ItalyTravel thread titled "How are the taxi drivers in Amalfi Coast not millionaires?" lays out the going rates from firsthand trips: Sorrento → Praiano (23km) quoted at €120 "cheap compared to the going rate of around €150–180," and Praiano → Ravello (14km) at €120 "cheap compared to the €180 our hotel offered." A top comment distils the situation — "Now you know why the taxi drivers are against Uber and similar ride sharing apps." Another commenter admits that "locals take the train. Which we got robbed on. And then the bus was so nauseating we took a private car back" — confirming the structural scarcity that sets these prices. A separate 2025 r/ItalyTravel "Amalfi Coast Trip Report" from a solo traveller in Minori documents Positano → Amalfi quoted at €200 and the 5km Amalfi → Minori hop at €50. Layered on top of this legal-but-gouged market is outright fraud: Fanpage's "Tassisti abusivi dal Napoletano ad Amalfi per truffare i turisti" reports that carabinieri sanctioned six abusivi drivers working Amalfi, Ravello, and the coast — NCC operators without proper taxi licence intercepting cruise-ship passengers at Amalfi Molo Pennello with inflated "special prices." Positanonews' "Salerno. Taxi abusivi, affare lucroso senza rischi e tasse" quotes Orsa Taxi: the abusivo "proposes a transfer that looks cheaper than a licensed taxi," then charges above the legal rate, leaves no receipt, and carries no commercial insurance — so if anything goes wrong on the famously narrow SS163, the passenger has no recourse. Il Mattino's Naples investigation "Napoli, truffe e disservizi taxi" estimates "at least 700 abusivi drivers" operating in the greater Naples area, many of whom migrate to the coast during the April–October cruise season.

Red Flags

  • A driver at the Sorrento station forecourt or Amalfi Molo Pennello approaches you saying "Positano? Amalfi? Ravello? Special price for you"
  • The quote is delivered verbally — no written itinerary, no fixed return time, no invoice, no named stops
  • Rates above €150 Sorrento → Positano one-way, above €200 Sorrento → Amalfi, or above €250 Sorrento → Ravello (licensed NCC benchmarks)
  • The vehicle has no "NCC" or "TAXI" roof sign and no visible civic licence number — legitimate licensed vehicles display both
  • The driver insists on cash-only, refuses to issue a ricevuta, or steers you to a bancomat at the drop-off

How to Avoid

  • Take the SITA Sud bus — €2.10 single or €10 Unico Costiera 24-hour day pass — at any tabaccheria for Sorrento → Positano → Amalfi and beyond; slow and crowded, but the local reality and roughly 60x cheaper
  • Or take the Alilauro or Travelmar ferry (€20–25 one-way) Sorrento → Positano → Amalfi — scenic, reliable, skips the SS163 entirely, and cancellations depend on sea state not schedule
  • If you genuinely want a private driver, book a licenced NCC through your hotel with a written quote in Euros — itinerary, all stops, and return time fixed before you leave — budget €350–500 for a full-day Positano / Amalfi / Ravello loop
  • Refuse any driver at Amalfi Molo Pennello who approaches cruise passengers with "special price" — Fanpage's 2024 carabinieri sweep specifically targeted this handoff point
  • Demand a ricevuta with driver's name, licence plate, route, and total — without it you cannot file a complaint with the Comune di Amalfi vigili urbani
Scam #2
The Marina Grande Boat-Charter & "Private Tour" Overcharge
⚠️ High
📍 Marina Grande Amalfi, Molo Pennello Amalfi (cruise tender dock), Marina di Praia in Praiano, Spiaggia Grande and Spiaggia del Fornillo in Positano

You want a day on the water — a boat tour to Capri's Blue Grotto, or a sunset cruise of the coast. A man with a clipboard at Marina Grande pitches "private charter, €1,200 all included." The same licensed gozzo, booked online direct from a legitimate operator, runs €400–600. The unlicensed boats look the same, don't carry commercial insurance, and the "all included" turns out to exclude fuel, lunch, and the Grotta Azzurra ticket.

The Amalfi Coast boat-charter market is split into three tiers: named reputable operators who publish rates online (Cobalto Boat Escapes, Restart Boats, Lucibello, Mare di Praia), unregulated resellers who buy a block of Viator/GetYourGuide vouchers and resell them at the pier with 2–3x markup, and outright unlicensed captains without a commercial patente nautica who operate when demand spikes during cruise days. A 2025 r/VirginVoyages Amalfi Coast excursion thread names Cobalto Boat Escapes and Restart Boats as reputable operators — the going rate for a full-day private tender for 4–6 people sits around €400–700 with fuel, skipper and snorkelling gear included. But the lived experience at Marina Grande and Amalfi Molo Pennello during high season is a crowd of clipboard-wielding "guides" intercepting tourists with pitches like "last tender today, €1,200 for the day, Blue Grotto included." What arrives is a smaller boat, fuel billed separately at €80–150, the Blue Grotto entry (€18 per person on top of the rowboat queue) never counted in the quote, a lunch stop at a partner restaurant where the "included" is a cheap pasta and the bottle of wine is €60. Unlicensed captains are the dangerous layer — with no commercial licence, they carry no passenger insurance, and the SS163 coastline has stretches where mobile coverage drops. The Capri equivalent has been documented repeatedly: an r/ItalyTravel thread explicitly warns "whatever you do, don't buy a ticket from a guide on the street. You'll get stuck on their tour for $20/pp" — the same pattern scales up to four-figure boat charters at Marina Grande.

Red Flags

  • A man with a clipboard at Marina Grande or Molo Pennello who is not inside an operator's branded kiosk — this is the textbook reseller or abusivo tell
  • "All-inclusive" pitched verbally with no itemised list of what's included (fuel, captain, lunch, drinks, Blue Grotto entry, snorkelling gear)
  • Cash-only or crypto-only demand; legitimate operators accept card and issue a PDF contract
  • The captain cannot produce a commercial patente nautica ('patente nautica commerciale') on request — private-use licences are not valid for paying passengers
  • The quote is above €1,000/day for a group of 4–6 — reputable operators list rates around €400–700 on their own websites

How to Avoid

  • Book before you arrive — Cobalto, Restart, Lucibello, Mare di Praia publish rates online, issue a PDF contract, and meet you at a branded kiosk
  • Confirm in writing exactly what's included: fuel, captain, lunch, drinks, Blue Grotto entry (€18 pp extra if not confirmed), snorkelling gear, towel
  • Pay through the operator's website or a credit card — refuse cash-only demands at the pier
  • Verify the captain's commercial licence — a legitimate professional will display it without hesitation
  • On a cruise-ship day, use the ship's shore-excursion desk rather than a clipboard hawker at Molo Pennello — shore excursions are vetted and the ship legally must wait if a chartered excursion returns late
Scam #3
The Sea-View Coperto & Pesce "al Etto" Trap
⚠️ High
📍 Amalfi lungomare and Piazza Duomo, Atrani Piazza Umberto I perimeter, Positano Spiaggia Grande beachfront, Praiano Vettica terraces, Ravello Piazza Duomo and Villa Cimbrone belvedere cafes, Maiori and Minori lungomare

You take a sunset table on an Amalfi terrace. The menu is beautiful, the view breathtaking, and the waiter recommends "the fresh catch today." The bill arrives at €280 for two: €12 coperto (not €3), a 15% servizio you didn't see, pane and acqua frizzante you didn't order, and a "whole sea bream" listed on the menu "al etto" (€9/100g) that arrived at 680g. Italian law requires a menu with prices outside the door — this is where the trap starts.

The Amalfi Coast sea-view restaurant trap works the way r/ItalyTravel's pinned "Some advice from an Italian living in Italy" warns: four layers of overcharge that each look individually small and together triple the bill. A 2025 r/viagens post titled "Tomei um golpe num restaurante na Itália" ("I got scammed in a restaurant in Italy") documents the register-versus-menu pattern — "the price on the menu is one thing, at the register it's another," with waiters surrounding diners at the bill to prevent them walking out: "é literalmente um golpe. Eles te servem comida congelada e depois te cobram o dobro do valor que está no cardápio." Positanonews' 2019 cronaca "Vietri sul Mare, truffa ex Pergola: condanna per i protagonisti del raggiro" records a decade-long Italian-court case against the operators of a costiera restaurant convicted for systematic tourist fraud — evidence that regional prosecutors see these overcharges as fraud, not a cultural quirk. The four layers in practice: (1) coperto of €4–6 per person on the Amalfi lungomare, versus €2–3 inland; (2) automatic 15% "servizio" applied to foreign-card bills without mention on the menu; (3) pane, acqua frizzante, or an antipasto platter arriving at the table without being ordered and billed at €4–8 each; (4) the headline trap — whole fish priced "al etto" (per 100g) with the actual fish weighing 400–700g, multiplying the €6–10/etto figure into a €40–70 single plate. Italian law (Codice del Consumo and regional commercio ordinances) requires every restaurant to post a price-bearing menu outside the door in public view; the absence of one, or the use of "prezzo al etto" without specifying the minimum piece size for a particular fish, is the clearest flag.

Red Flags

  • No posted menu outside the door, or a menu in four+ languages with glossy photos but no per-item prices for the fish or steak
  • The coperto line is in small print at the menu bottom and above €3.50 per head
  • Pane, acqua frizzante, or antipasti arrive at the table without you ordering them
  • The waiter pushes "the fresh catch" or "the house fish" without quoting an exact price when you ask
  • A "barker" outside the restaurant waves passing tourists in — a locals-don't-eat-here tell

How to Avoid

  • Photograph the posted menu from outside before you sit; if the fish or steak has no per-item price and is only "al etto," ask the waiter to weigh and quote the exact fish before cooking — in writing if you can
  • Ask at seating: "mi conferma il coperto, il servizio, e il prezzo del pesce che ho ordinato?" — a legitimate restaurant answers clearly; a trap mumbles
  • Send back any pane, acqua, or antipasti you didn't order — Italian law lets you refuse without charge
  • Walk one street uphill from the lungomare: in Amalfi try Via Capuano or Via delle Cartiere; in Ravello skip the Piazza Duomo perimeter; in Positano, walk past Spiaggia Grande and up toward Via Pasitea
  • Trust Italian-language Google reviews sorted by "Più recenti" — a venue rated 4.5+ by Italians and 3.8 by English speakers is authentic; the reverse is usually a trap
Scam #4
The Amalfi & Ravello Parcheggiatori Abusivi
⚠️ High
📍 Amalfi Piazza Flavio Gioia and Via Lungomare dei Cavalieri, Atrani Spiaggia parking area, Ravello Viale Richard Wagner approach, Positano Chiesa Nuova lots, Praiano SS163 turnouts

You drive down the SS163 looking for a spot in Amalfi. A man in a fluorescent vest waves you into a space on Via Lungomare dei Cavalieri and holds out a hand for €25 "for the day." He doesn't own the space, he issues no receipt, and if municipal police happen by you may be fined anyway because he had no authority to "assign" it to you. This is the parcheggiatori abusivi scam — and it works on every approach to every Amalfi Coast town.

Parcheggiatori abusivi are unlicensed "attendants" in fluorescent vests who stake out high-demand parking approaches — Amalfi's Piazza Flavio Gioia seafront, Atrani's single parking strip, Ravello's Viale Richard Wagner, the Positano Chiesa Nuova overflow — and extract cash from arriving drivers in exchange for "holding" a space they do not legally control. Fanpage's 2024 Guardia di Finanza and carabinieri blitz coverage on the Penisola Sorrentina and costiera identifies parcheggiatori abusivi as one of the two priority enforcement targets alongside abusivi taxi drivers — meaning police agree the pattern is systematic, not isolated. The typical demand is €15–30 for a day, against the legitimate municipal rate at Amalfi's Piazza Flavio Gioia pay-and-display of approximately €5/hour with a posted sign. The compounding trap is two-sided: cars parked in spaces "assigned" by the unofficial attendant are frequently ticketed anyway because the space is actually a residents-only or ZTL zone — the tourist pays the parcheggiatore and then a €55–200 comune fine that arrives via the rental company months later. A 2024 r/ItalyTravel thread on a Cefalù parking-fine scam documents this exact ZTL sister pattern: a legitimate receipt, an illegitimate fine, a rental-company charge months after the trip. In Amalfi specifically, because town-centre spaces are scarce during April–October and the SS163 has few pullouts, drivers often accept the vest-waved space out of desperation — which is what keeps the scam profitable enough to survive every municipal crackdown.

Red Flags

  • A man in a fluorescent vest approaches your slowing car with a "come this way" gesture toward an unmarked space — legitimate municipal attendants wear comune-issued uniforms with ID
  • The space has no pay-and-display machine in sight, or the machine is taped off and the "attendant" points to his hand instead
  • The "fee" is demanded in cash at €15–30 and no printed receipt is offered
  • You are being directed into a space near residents-only (gialle/giallo) or ZTL (Zona a Traffico Limitato) signage
  • The "attendant" follows your car or won't leave until you pay — a soft-intimidation cue

How to Avoid

  • Park only at official municipal lots: Luna Rossa and Piazza Flavio Gioia in Amalfi (pay-and-display machine), signed Positano lots on the SS163 above town, Piazza del Duomo and Auditorium Niemeyer in Ravello
  • Refuse any vest-wearing man who flags your moving car; drive on 50 metres and look for a signed municipal machine or posted ZTL boundary
  • If cornered, pay €1–2 "mancia" (a tip) rather than €15–30; most parcheggiatori accept a small tip rather than escalate and lose future tourists
  • Photograph the "attendant," the vehicle, and the surrounding signage before paying; file a report at the local comune's vigili urbani office if threatened
  • Consider skipping the rental car entirely — the SITA bus and Alilauro / Travelmar ferries cover every town on the coast, and parking hassles disappear
Scam #5
The "Antica" Limoncello Shop & Factory-Tour Upsell
🔶 Medium
📍 Amalfi Via Lorenzo d'Amalfi and Piazza Duomo, Atrani small shops, Positano Via dei Mulini and Via Colombo, Ravello Via Roma, Praiano SS163 retail, driver-commission "factory stops" en route

You walk into an "antica" limoncello shop on Via Lorenzo d'Amalfi, take the free tasting, and walk out with a 500ml bottle for €28. The same limoncello — under the same IGP Costa d'Amalfi consortium seal — sells at any inland alimentari or supermarket for €6–10. A worse variant: a "private factory tour" your NCC driver pitches at €60/person, which turns out to be a 15-minute showroom walk where the driver collects a commission on your purchase.

Amalfi's limoncello economy is real: Costa d'Amalfi lemons (the Sfusato variety, grown on the terraced hillsides above Amalfi and Minori) carry a protected IGP designation, and consortium-certified producers like Valle dei Mulini, Villa Massa, and Pallini make genuinely distinctive product. The tourist shop layer on top is where the markup lives. Shops along Amalfi's Via Lorenzo d'Amalfi and Piazza Duomo charge €18–35 for 500ml bottles branded "Antica" or "Dal 1898" with vague IGP claims; the same bottle — often the identical producer with a different sticker — sells at any Coop, Conad, or inland alimentari for €6–10. An r/amalficoast "Limoncello tips" community thread names La Valle dei Mulini (amalfilemon.it), reachable free on foot past the Amalfi basilica and paper factory up the Valle delle Ferriere path, as the legitimate producer to visit: their on-site shop publishes prices online. The "private factory tour" variant compounds the markup: a driver or tour guide pitches a €30–60 per-person escorted visit, drives you to a showroom attached to a tourist-oriented retail operation, and takes a 15–25% commission on any bottle you buy. A sister pattern flagged across r/ItalyTravel and r/amalficoast: bottles branded "Amalfi limoncello" produced not in the consortium but in Salerno province with unregulated lemons — no consortium seal, tourist price, and often a harsher product. The IGP "Limoncello Costa d'Amalfi" mark is the only guarantee that what you're paying for is what the label claims.

Red Flags

  • The bottle is branded "Antica" or "Dal 1800s" but carries no "Limoncello Costa d'Amalfi IGP" consortium seal on the label
  • A taxi or tour driver insists on a "factory stop" on the route — this is a commission-driven detour, not a free add-on
  • The price for 500ml is above €20 and the producer is not named (Valle dei Mulini, Villa Massa, Pallini, etc.)
  • The "free tasting" pressure escalates as you try to leave without buying
  • The shop is clustered on Via Lorenzo d'Amalfi or Via dei Mulini with identical bottles at three different price points

How to Avoid

  • Check any bottle for the "Limoncello Costa d'Amalfi IGP" consortium seal before paying €20+ — this is the only authentic-product guarantee
  • Compare prices at a random inland alimentari, Coop or Conad supermarket in Amalfi town first — €6–10 for 500ml of a perfectly good brand is the baseline
  • Walk to Valle dei Mulini (free, on foot up past Amalfi basilica and the paper factory) rather than paying €30+ for a driver-escorted "factory tour"
  • Refuse "commission tours" sold by taxi or NCC drivers en route — legitimate producer visits are bookable direct from the producer's own website at published rates
  • Pick one nice gift bottle with the IGP seal and a named producer — avoid generic "Antica" shelf-fill with no consortium mark
Scam #6
The SITA Bus Ticket & Board-Without-Ticket Confusion
🔶 Medium
📍 SITA Sud stops end-to-end — Sorrento, Positano Chiesa Nuova and Sponda, Praiano, Conca dei Marini, Amalfi Piazza Flavio Gioia terminus, Atrani, Minori, Maiori, Cetara, Vietri sul Mare, Salerno Piazza della Concordia

SITA Sud is the €2.10 bus line that covers the entire Amalfi Coast — but tickets must be bought before boarding from a tabaccheria and validated on the bus. Tourists who board without a pre-bought ticket are told (in rapid Italian) that tickets aren't sold on board, then a controllore appears and issues a €55–200 fine. Meanwhile, street sellers at crowded stops offer "day passes" at €10–20 that are either invalid or just the €2.10 single with a markup.

The SITA Sud model is straightforward once you know it and disorienting if you don't. A single ride across any section of the coast is €2.10, a 24-hour "Unico Costiera" day pass is €10, and both are sold only at tabaccherie, official SITA kiosks, and a few partner bar-tabacchi — never on the bus itself, never by a street seller. The ticket must be stamped in the orange validation machine as you board; an unvalidated ticket is treated as no ticket. A 2024 r/ItalyTravel "Got bus fine even though we tapped to pay our ticket" thread documents the coercion pattern (Florence-based but the model is national): tourists are blocked from exiting by a controllore and pressed to pay the fine on a handheld card terminal immediately, with no clear path to contest. In Amalfi's high-season peak, a second layer appears: SITA buses at Positano Chiesa Nuova and Amalfi Piazza Flavio Gioia often fill before tourists at the queue can board — an r/ItalyTravel Amalfi trip report describes buses "not stopping when full" and tourists waiting 2–3 hours. Street sellers at those same stops move in with "day passes, €15 each" pitches — sometimes a real €2.10 ticket at a markup, sometimes an invalid slip that draws a controllore-issued fine anyway. The legitimate alternatives: Mobility Amalfi Coast (mobilityamalficoast.com) sells reservable seats on smaller buses at a modest markup; ferries cover Sorrento, Positano, and Amalfi on days when the wind is under Beaufort 7.

Red Flags

  • Someone at the bus stop selling "day passes" or "tickets" from a handbag or clipboard, not from inside a tabaccheria or official kiosk
  • A "day pass" quoted above €10 (the real Unico Costiera 24-hour is €10) or a "single" quoted above €2.10
  • Boarding without validating your ticket in the orange on-bus machine — an unvalidated ticket is treated as no ticket
  • A controllore demanding immediate cash payment of a fine on a handheld terminal with no written citation
  • A driver refusing to let you buy on board but providing no clear directions to the nearest tabaccheria

How to Avoid

  • Buy SITA tickets at any tabaccheria before boarding — €2.10 single or €10 Unico Costiera 24-hour day pass; ignore street sellers completely
  • Validate the ticket in the orange on-bus machine the moment you step aboard; keep the validated stub until you exit
  • If the bus is full and the driver refuses boarding, walk 200m along the SS163 and flag the next bus from a mid-route curve — many slots sit between bends
  • For peak reliability, use Mobility Amalfi Coast (mobilityamalficoast.com) — reservable seats on smaller buses at a modest markup
  • If a controllore issues a fine you dispute, ask for a written citation with the company name and dispute number — pay by card not cash and contest in writing within 30 days via the bus company's customer-service portal
Scam #7
The Fake Villa Rental & Off-Platform Wire
⚠️ High
📍 Listings across Amalfi, Atrani, Praiano, Minori, Maiori, Ravello, Furore and hillside "villas with pool" on the SS163 — especially standalone websites with generic regional names

You spot a dream listing — "Villa with pool, sea view, Amalfi Coast, €180/night" — on Airbnb, Vrbo, or a standalone booking site. The host emails you off-platform offering a "discount" if you wire the deposit directly. You send the money. On arrival day, the address is a vacant lot, the website is gone, and your bank confirms the wire is irreversible.

Amalfi Coast's extreme supply-and-demand imbalance is the structural soil this scam grows in: peak-season studios in Positano start at €500/night, villas with a view cost four figures, and a "villa with pool for €180/night" advert is cheaper than any real listing should be. An r/ItalyTravel 2024 thread titled "Has anyone rented from livingAmalfi.com before??" captures the anxious pattern — travellers second-guessing standalone booking sites with plausible-sounding generic names, no platform-protection recourse, and no way to verify the property until arrival. (In that particular case, the site turned out to be legitimate; in many others it doesn't.) The scam pattern that repeats: a listing appears on Airbnb or Booking but the host then contacts the guest off-platform (WhatsApp, email, direct message) offering a "discount for paying directly" — the switch to a bank wire or cryptocurrency removes the platform's protection, and on arrival day the host vanishes. TripAdvisor's long-running rental-scam forum threads document this across Italy; Bologna's Fiera-district scam pattern (see our Bologna guide) uses the same off-platform handoff, with named fraudsters like "Bjarne" recurring across forums. A compounding Amalfi-specific layer: Campania's 2025 Meta tourism-tax increase (up to €4/night — Metropolis, December 2025) is now sometimes used as cover for a fake "city fee in cash on arrival" surcharge by questionable hosts; the real imposta di soggiorno varies by comune but is capped and disclosed on booking.

Red Flags

  • The host contacts you outside the booking platform (email, WhatsApp, personal mobile) and asks to move payment off-platform
  • You are told a bank wire or cryptocurrency will get you a "discount" or "avoid platform fees"
  • The price is dramatically below comparable peak-season Amalfi Coast listings — a villa with pool under €200/night in June/July/August
  • The address cannot be verified on Google Street View, or the photo set is reverse-image-findable on other Italian (or Greek, or Croatian) properties
  • Urgency push: "two other parties are interested for those dates, we need the wire today"

How to Avoid

  • Book only on Airbnb, Booking.com, or Vrbo — never wire money or send cryptocurrency to a private bank account for an Amalfi Coast rental, no matter how compelling the discount pitch
  • Reverse-image-search every interior photo before booking; scam listings reuse legitimate photos from other Italian, Greek and Croatian coastal rentals
  • Check the property on Google Street View — a legitimate Amalfi Coast villa has a visible gate and at minimum a plausible address
  • Require the host to message you inside the platform's chat with a photo of the entrance and key-handoff procedure; scammers refuse because it breaks anonymity
  • Budget for the real imposta di soggiorno (€2–4/night in 2025–26, varies by comune) — any host adding a surprise "city fee in cash" above that is pocketing it; refuse any credit-card surcharge (EU PSD2 bars them on most consumer cards)

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Carabinieri / Polizia di Stato station — Amalfi has a carabinieri post on Via Casamare, Ravello on Via della Marra. Call 112 (Carabinieri) or 113 (Polizia). Get an official crime report for insurance and card chargebacks. You can also report online at poliziadistato.it.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately and use in-app blocks. For taxi or boat-charter cash overcharges, dispute the card payment as a service not rendered / misrepresented; screenshots of the agreed written quote are critical.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest consulate. The US Consulate General in Naples is at Piazza della Repubblica, 80122 Napoli — emergencies: +39 081-583-8111. The US Embassy in Rome is at Via Vittorio Veneto 121 (+39 06-4674-1).

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
