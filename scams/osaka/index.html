The 8 Scams

Scam #1
Kuromon Market Tourist-Tier Pricing (¥5,000 Crab Legs, ¥3,500 Single Shrimp)
🔶 Medium
📍 Kuromon Ichiba Market, Nippombashi, Chuo-ku, Osaka (between Nipponbashi and Nihombashi subway stations)

You walk into Kuromon Market — "Osaka's kitchen" — expecting working-class Showa-era food stalls. Instead you find aggressively marketed uni, tuna, and "Kobe beef" skewers with prices missing, shown only in English, or hidden behind a second sign. Your bill: ¥14,950 for six small pieces, or ¥48,100 solo.

A 2024 Shueisha investigation documented ¥30,000 for four taraba crab legs and ¥3,500 for a single shrimp at Kuromon stalls; a follow-up in December 2024 showed prices had come down to ¥15,000-¥19,000 for four crab legs after the market association distributed flyers pleading with shopkeepers to "price fairly" — but a 2025 Asahi piece still found ¥2,500 seafood okonomiyaki and ¥5,000+ single taraba legs. Toyo Keizai's February 2025 column on "Niseko-ization" flagged ¥5,000 Kobe beef skewers. PRESIDENT Online quoted Japanese shopkeepers frustrated at being lumped in with the overchargers; Merkmal reports that of roughly 150 stalls, about 25 carry the bottakuri reputation and about 10 are foreign-owned newcomers who aren't in the traditional merchants' association. News on Japan notes locals now make up only ~10% of shoppers. On Google Maps, a 1★ review of Furusato no Kaori reads: "Huge Rip-Off — I ordered just a few grilled seafood items... the bill was an unbelievable 14,950 yen. Before ordering, I asked how much it would cost, but the staff refused to answer clearly and kept dodging the question."

Red Flags

  • No prices visible on the food in the display case, or prices obscured by another sign
  • Staff dodges or refuses to answer "how much?" before you order (repeatedly noted in Google reviews)
  • "Kobe beef" or "A5 wagyu" skewer priced at ¥2,000-¥5,000 for a single piece
  • Shop is not a member of the Kuromon Ichiba Shotengai association (members display the association's fair-price flyer)
  • You're pulled in with an English-language tout gesture and a Michelin Guide sticker at the entrance

How to Avoid

  • Ask the exact total price — in Japanese if possible ("ikura desu ka?") — for every item before the staff starts grilling; walk away if they won't commit
  • Eat at Tenjinbashisuji Shopping Street (Japan's longest covered arcade) or Kizu Market instead — both recommended by Osaka locals on r/JapanTravelTips
  • For seafood, sit down at a standalone sushi restaurant listed on Tabelog — same quality for a third of the Kuromon markup
  • Photograph every price sign before ordering so you have evidence if the bill doesn't match
  • Shinsekai and the Namba Yasaka Shrine area offer legitimate street food at normal Osaka prices
Scam #2
The Dotonbori "Wagyu Hamburger" Portable-Card-Reader Bait & Switch
⚠️ High
📍 Dotonbori and side streets off Shinsaibashi-suji, Chuo-ku, Osaka

A staff member outside a Dotonbori restaurant waves a laminated menu: "A5 wagyu ribeye — Osaka special." Once seated, the waitress says "card only" — no cash register, just a portable handheld reader. The "A5 wagyu" arrives cold, chewy, and flavorless. The ¥29,568 charge is already processed.

An October 2025 r/JapanTravelTips post documenting this exact pattern drew 800+ upvotes and 570+ comments. The original poster wrote: "From the start, something felt off. The waitress said they only accept cards, and there was no real cash register — just a portable card reader." Veteran posters confirmed it was "definitely not A5 Wagyu." A parallel r/JapanTravel thread reported the same restaurant, same portable-card-reader pattern, with bills around ¥29,568-¥30,316 per person. Earlier 2024 r/JapanTravel posts — "I just got scammed in Osaka (Dotonbori)" — describe the same street-tout-plus-undisclosed-surcharge pattern. Japanese-language forum Yahoo! 知恵袋 documents a tourist lured from their intended Daruma kushikatsu restaurant by a Dotonbori tout into a nearby bar with ¥5,000+ cover / otoshi charges ("お通し代もばかにならず、ぼったくりでした"). Osaka City's Minami anti-tout ordinance makes this illegal, but enforcement is sporadic and the shops rotate names.

Red Flags

  • A staff member is actively pulling tourists off the street with a laminated English menu
  • "A5 Wagyu" or "Kobe beef" set meal priced around ¥25,000-¥30,000 (authentic A5 in a Tabelog-listed restaurant is usually ¥8,000-¥15,000 for lunch)
  • No cash register — payment is taken on a handheld portable card reader (Airpay / Square-style device)
  • "We only take cards" — combined with a street tout, this signals there's no refund mechanism
  • No Google Maps listing, no English/Japanese reviews, or a very recently created listing

How to Avoid

  • Do not follow any street tout in Dotonbori, ever — Osaka Prefecture and Osaka City both have anti-tout ordinances that make kyakuhiki illegal in Minami
  • Pick your wagyu restaurant on Tabelog or Google Maps before arriving — 4.0+ stars with 100+ Japanese reviews is the safe bar
  • Real Osaka wagyu places: Yakiniku Gyuemon, Hajime, Steakland Kobe — publicly listed prices, regular POS terminals, dozens of Japanese reviews
  • If you realise you're in a tourist trap before ordering, stand up and leave — staff cannot legally stop you (no otoshi / cover charge if you haven't ordered)
  • If the waitress insists on a portable card reader as the only payment option, refuse the charge and call 110 (police) citing Osaka Prefecture's bottakuri ordinance
Scam #3
The Minami / Kitashinchi Girls-Bar & Hostess-Bar Bottakuri (Hangure-Run)
⚠️ High
📍 Minami (Dotonbori, Shinsaibashi, Namba) and Kitashinchi (Umeda), Osaka

You're walking between Shinsaibashi and Namba at night when a tout offers "girls bar, 3,000 yen one hour, all-you-can-drink." Three hours later the bill is ¥300,000-¥950,000. Door staff block the exit; an ATM run is "suggested." This pattern has been documented by Osaka police for more than a decade.

A January 2019 Sankei investigation ("大阪・ミナミ半グレぼったくりバー") traced a Minami girls-bar to five teenage tout-enforcers who lured a male customer with "1時間飲み放題で3千円" then confined and beat him, breaking his ribs and causing a month-long hospitalization after a bill dispute. A 2013 Umeda case saw a girls-bar operator arrested after two customers were beaten severely over a bill, per a Zakzak retrospective. In Suita in January 2025, two men racked up ¥950,000 at a girls bar after being pushed to order two ¥200,000 bottles of champagne — both customers were later arrested when they refused to pay. In January 2026, Sankei reported the arrest of Minami dart-bar operators for unlicensed "entertainment" (women seated with male guests), with the bar implicated in earlier bottakuri complaints. Bakusai threads name specific active venues in Kitashinchi: one poster reported "右目損傷出血全身打撲" (right eye injury, bleeding, full-body bruising) after a bill dispute. r/OsakaTravel's verdict: "Girls bars, lounges and those kind of places with sexy señoritas catching customers tend to be always a scam. If they talk to you in English in Dotonbori, 100% scam."

Red Flags

  • Tout outside a tenant-building bar offers "girls bar, 3000 yen/hour" or "飲み放題 3000円" — these baseline rates virtually always come with undisclosed table charges and set-time bottles
  • No printed menu with prices visible in the seating area
  • The hostess keeps ordering "shampan call" or champagne without quoting prices — ¥200,000+ bottles are standard in Kitashinchi
  • You're seated on an upper floor of a multi-tenant building with no street-level signage
  • Door staff or "bouncer-types" near the exit once you question the bill — police have documented kansaku (confinement) and assault cases

How to Avoid

  • Never enter any bar, lounge, or girls bar in Minami or Kitashinchi that was recruited from the street — Osaka Prefecture's bottakuri ordinance exists because this is endemic
  • If you want the cabaret-bar experience, book a Tabelog-listed Kitashinchi snack or lounge with published prices and at least 20 Japanese reviews
  • If seated without a price list, stand up and leave before accepting any food or drink — otoshi (compulsory appetizer) is the legal hook
  • If challenged, say "警察を呼んでください" (please call the police) and dial 110 yourself — Osaka Police have a Minami-specialised unit
  • The US Embassy explicitly warns citizens that ATM-withdrawal coercion has been reported in Japanese nightlife venues — never let staff accompany you to an ATM
Scam #4
The Midosuji Line "Stolen Wallet / Missed Flight" Panhandle
🟢 Low
📍 Osaka Metro Midosuji Line (especially Namba↔Daikokucho), Shin-Osaka Shinkansen arrival concourse, and Osaka/Umeda station exits

A young man sits next to you on the Midosuji Line. His wallet was stolen, his hotel denied him his room, he missed his flight, and "the police wouldn't lend him money." Could you spare ¥5,000-¥10,000 for a cab to the airport? You pay. Minutes later he's working the next car.

A July 2025 r/Osaka post ("Scam Warning: Midosuji Line", 105 upvotes, 39 comments) described the encounter in detail: "Told me his wallet was stolen, hotel denied him his room, missed his flight that day, and police wouldn't lend him money." A nearby Japanese passenger confirmed to the poster it was a scam. A separate February 2025 r/JapanTravelTips post ("Beware of scammer in Osaka", 462 upvotes) documents two female tourists approached outside Shin-Osaka Station by a young Japanese man with the same "bus fare" story — the tell was when he offered "card is okay" after being told there was no cash. An r/japanlife "people asking for money at train stations? scam?" thread confirms that genuine Japanese homeless do not approach strangers, and that koban (police boxes) at every major station can legally issue emergency fare to anyone genuinely stranded — so anyone bypassing the koban to ask strangers is scamming. A variant where the approach starts as "can I practice English with you?" and escalates to a soft money ask is also documented near Osaka Castle and Shinsaibashi. A 2025 NTV English News report featured a 41-year-old thief rearrested after CCTV caught him stealing tourists' wallets in central Osaka; he told police "Japanese people are easy to target."

Red Flags

  • Someone (often young, well-dressed, not visibly homeless) actively approaches you on a train or at a station exit
  • A specific emotional narrative: stolen wallet, missed flight, denied hotel, stranded
  • Asks for cash ¥3,000-¥10,000, "I'll pay you back, give me your address" — no one ever pays back
  • Offers to take card payment via a QR code — an obvious escalation turning a small scam into card fraud
  • If you decline, they escalate urgency or switch languages (Japanese → English → gestures)

How to Avoid

  • Know that genuine Japanese homeless people never approach strangers for money — the active approach is the scam signal
  • Keep moving: a polite "sumimasen" (excuse me) while walking past is the standard Japanese response and is not rude
  • Never give cash; never tap a QR code a stranger presents to you
  • If they follow you or become aggressive, walk into any koban (交番) — there is one at every major Osaka Metro station and every shinkansen exit
  • Report persistent operators to station staff — Osaka Metro security does respond
Scam #5
Kansai Airport / Itami Airport "Unofficial Taxi" Kyakuhiki
🔶 Medium
📍 Kansai International Airport (KIX) arrivals, Itami (ITM) airport arrivals, and Shin-Osaka taxi overflow

You clear customs at KIX and a friendly man in a suit approaches: "Taxi? Osaka hotel 8,000 yen, no wait." He's bypassing the official taxi queue. The "flat rate" turns into ¥15,000-¥32,000 with surprise fees for "tolls" and "night service."

Legitimate Japanese taxis have green (緑) license plates and wait at official taxi stands; unauthorized drivers (shiro-taku / 白タク) approach you in arrivals and their plates are white. A 2024 r/OsakaTravel thread "Did we just get scammed by a taxi?" documents a standard-looking taxi charging ¥11,640 on a route that should have been ¥3,000, with the OP noting "We flag down taxis a lot but never had this happen before." A TripAdvisor Osaka MK Taxi review: "Be aware of this taxi driver. He over charged me and my family because we are foreigners. He charged us extra 500yen because he said we used his trunk." OhaJapan's 2025 travel-safety guide lists KIX alongside Narita T2 and Haneda as confirmed kyakuhiki hotspots, documenting one case of ¥32,000 charged against a legal fixed rate of ¥20,000-¥22,000, and noting that even some green-plate drivers have been caught running surprise fees. Shiro-taku operation is a violation of Japan's Road Transportation Act (道路運送法) — drivers can be arrested — and the Kansai Transport Bureau (近畿運輸局) accepts complaints in English.

Red Flags

  • Anyone approaches you in the arrivals hall offering a taxi — real Japanese taxi drivers wait at the official stand
  • White license plates (unauthorized) instead of green (regulated taxi)
  • A "flat rate" quoted verbally with no meter — legal Japan taxis must use the meter except on officially regulated KIX-to-city fixed fares
  • Driver demands cash only, says the card reader "is broken"
  • Surprise fees appear at the end: tolls you didn't approve, luggage-in-trunk charge, night-rate "special"

How to Avoid

  • Walk directly to the official taxi rank outside KIX Terminal 1 (exit 1F gate D) or the Itami rank — ignore everyone who approaches you
  • Better yet, take the Nankai Rapi:t to Namba (¥1,450, 34 min) or JR Kansai-Airport Rapid to Osaka Station (¥1,210, 65 min) — trains are cheaper and honest
  • If you must take a taxi, use the fixed-rate official KIX-Osaka service (publicly posted rates: ~¥17,500 to Umeda)
  • Check the license plate is GREEN (緑ナンバー) — this is the legal marker of a licensed taxi in Japan
  • If charged unexpectedly, photograph the receipt, the license plate, and the driver ID card inside the cab — legal taxis have the ID visible — and file a complaint with the Kansai Transport Bureau
Scam #6
Counterfeit Brand Goods in Shinsaibashi / Amemura / "Tsuruhashi Fake Goods Street"
🔶 Medium
📍 Shinsaibashi-suji, Amerikamura (Amemura), and the Tsuruhashi district in Ikuno-ku — ~3km east of central Minami

A Louis Vuitton wallet at ¥8,000 or a "Moncler" puffer at ¥15,000. The goods look convincing. They're counterfeit — and buying them can mean Japanese Customs confiscating them on your way home, plus fines in your destination country for importing fakes.

Mainichi Shimbun's 2024 investigation "Going behind the scenes in Osaka's fake goods street" documented the Tsuruhashi area nicknamed "鶴橋偽ブランド通り" (Tsuruhashi fake-brand street), where some shops openly display counterfeit luxury goods and sellers ask reporters "You're not the police, right?" before revealing their stock. In early 2024, Osaka Prefectural Police raided multiple Tsuruhashi-area shops and seized over ¥8 million in counterfeit goods, arresting two suspects. Later in 2024, police arrested one shop manager after seizing 360 counterfeit items including fake Moncler jackets from a single business. Osaka Customs separately reported ~9,600 counterfeits blocked in FY2024, ~90% from China, including fake Osaka Expo 2025 Myaku-Myaku mascot merchandise — meaning even novelty Expo souvenirs from unofficial stalls may be counterfeit. r/TheHermesGame notes that "counterfeit laws in Japan are strictly enforced. It also comes with a penalty of 10 years in prison and a $73,000 USD fine" — meaning sellers take on real risk, and so do buyers who try to export the goods.

Red Flags

  • Louis Vuitton / Gucci / Hermès / Moncler at 10-20% of normal retail price
  • Goods sold from a small back-street shop, garage-style stall, or an unmarked upper-floor showroom (rather than an official boutique or major department store)
  • Seller is evasive about the product's provenance — won't show you import papers or authenticity cards
  • Seller asks "you're not the police, right?" before revealing stock (documented Tsuruhashi pattern)
  • Pop-up "Osaka Expo" Myaku-Myaku mascot merchandise from stalls outside the official Expo store — thousands of fakes have been seized at Kansai Airport

How to Avoid

  • If the price of a luxury item is "too good," it's counterfeit — this holds true even in Japan
  • Buy name-brand goods only from official boutiques or major department stores: Hankyu, Daimaru, Takashimaya, Mitsukoshi
  • For secondhand luxury, use established authenticators with 50+ Japanese reviews: Komehyo, RagTag, 2nd Street, Daikokuya — all have Osaka branches with returns and authentication guarantees
  • Do not buy Expo 2025 Myaku-Myaku mascot goods from any stall that isn't the official Expo merchandise store
  • Bringing counterfeits back into the EU, UK, US, Australia, or South Korea is a customs offense — goods will be seized and you may be fined
Scam #7
Osaka Expo 2025 Fake-Ticket & Fake-Website Fraud (Extends Into 2026)
🔶 Medium
📍 Online, with in-person pickup/ticket-swap attempts reported near Yumeshima Expo site and Osaka Station

You search Google for "Osaka Expo 2025 tickets" and click a top result that looks official — Myaku-Myaku mascot, Japanese government logos, polished design. You pay ¥7,500; your card is charged, and the QR code never works at the gate.

A March 2025 Trend Micro report (covered by Yomiuri, NHK, and essential-japan.com) warned of "multiple fake 2025 Osaka-Kansai Expo websites" designed to harvest card details and sell counterfeit tickets. NHK and the Expo Association issued repeat warnings through 2025 about scam sites using Expo-themed domains and social-media redirects. Even after the Expo closed in October 2025, a post-Expo YouTube report documented scam sites pivoting to selling counterfeit Myaku-Myaku merchandise to capitalize on nostalgia. A TripAdvisor review "Borderline scam" of the real Expo describes how the on-site reservation structure left many ticket-holders frustrated — a separate complaint category, but one that reinforced the fraud climate. The Consumer Affairs Agency (消費者庁) hotline 188 accepts fraud reports from tourists still in Japan; if you paid by card, file a chargeback immediately.

Red Flags

  • Search result or social media ad offering Expo 2025 / post-Expo Myaku-Myaku tickets — official Expo ticket sales ended October 2025
  • URL is not the official Expo site (expo2025.or.jp); beware of lookalikes like "expo2025-ticket.xyz" or social-media redirects
  • Price significantly lower than the official ¥7,500 day pass
  • Seller asks for payment via bank transfer or crypto instead of a regulated payment processor
  • In-person ticket handover at Osaka Station or near the Yumeshima Expo gate — fake at this point since Expo has closed

How to Avoid

  • Expo 2025 has concluded (Apr-Oct 2025) — any site still selling "Expo tickets" in 2026 is a scam by definition
  • For post-Expo Myaku-Myaku merchandise, buy only from official licensees: Osaka Tourism Bureau shops, major department stores
  • Do not click Expo-themed ads on Facebook, X, Instagram, or TikTok — Trend Micro specifically named social media as the primary vector
  • If you were scammed during Expo 2025, file a chargeback with your card issuer and report to the Consumer Affairs Agency (消費者庁) hotline 188
  • Use Japanese government tourism resources (JNTO, Osaka Convention & Tourism Bureau) for all event-related purchases
Scam #8
Dotonbori Pickpocketing & "Phone-Hold" Distraction Theft
⚠️ High
📍 Dotonbori crowds, Namba Walk, Shin-Osaka Shinkansen platforms, Osaka Metro Midosuji Line (rush hour), Nara Park day-trippers

You're packed into the Ebisu Bridge Glico-sign photo crowd with 500 other tourists. Your phone is in your back pocket. When you reach the other side it's gone. Or: a stranger asks you to "hold my phone and take a photo" — while you're distracted, a second person rifles your bag.

A 2024 r/JapanTravelTips post "Pickpocket in Osaka" warned: "Watch out for pickpockets in Dotonbori (and just anywhere you travel). The guy (white male with European features)..." A 2025 r/OsakaTravel post "My wallet has been stolen in Osaka/Dotonbori" was met with a notable reply from a 10-year Osaka resident: "Japan absolutely has a small but very real pickpocketing scene. I've had my wallet stolen in Osaka before and I've lived there for 10 years." An NTV English News report featured a 41-year-old thief rearrested in 2025 after CCTV caught him stealing a ¥35,000 wallet from a backpack in central Osaka; he told police "Japanese people are easy targets" and was seen working Osaka Expo crowds in July 2025. Visitor-related thefts represented roughly 68% of visitor-related crimes nationally in 2024, and visitor arrests rose 31.4% year over year according to Japan National Police Agency statistics — Osaka ranked among the top hot zones along with Tokyo, with Chuo-ku (Dotonbori/Shinsaibashi) and Kita-ku (Umeda) consistently the top two pickpocketing districts in the prefecture.

Red Flags

  • Extreme crowd density — Dotonbori's Ebisu Bridge evening photo spot is the single highest-risk pickpocketing zone in Osaka
  • A stranger asks you to hold something, take their photo, or "help" them carry a bag
  • Someone bumps into you and immediately apologizes while making sustained physical contact
  • A second person "accidentally" stands extremely close on your other side during the apology — the classic team lift
  • On the Midosuji Line during rush-hour crush, a backpack worn behind you is exposed to the person behind you

How to Avoid

  • Wear a cross-body bag with the zipper forward or a money belt under clothes — never put valuables in back pockets or open jacket pockets in Dotonbori
  • When photographing the Glico sign, stand with your back to a wall or railing, not in the center of the Ebisu Bridge crush
  • Don't hand your phone to a stranger to "take a photo" — ask a uniformed shop staff instead
  • On the Midosuji Line, wear your backpack on your front during rush-hour crush (all Japanese commuters do this)
  • If anything is stolen, report to the nearest koban (交番) immediately — you need the police report for travel insurance

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Japanese Police (Keisatsu) station or koban. Call 110. Osaka Prefectural Police operate a Minami-specialised unit that actively prosecutes bottakuri and kyakuhiki cases. Get an official crime report — you'll need it for insurance and chargebacks.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block suspicious transactions before they use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Consulate General Osaka-Kobe is at 2-11-5 Nishitenma, Kita-ku, Osaka 530-8543. For emergencies: +81 6-6315-5900.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
