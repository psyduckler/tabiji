The 6 Scams

Scam #1
Ubud Sacred Monkey Forest Theft & Aggressive Monkeys
⚠️ High
📍 Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary (Mandala Suci Wenara Wana), Ubud center; surrounding Jalan Monkey Forest Road

Ubud's Monkey Forest is a documented pickpocket-by-primate ecosystem with a secondary human-scam layer.

One traveler wrote: the trained long-tailed macaques (trained by human handlers) target tourists with visible valuables — sunglasses, hats, phones, and specifically bags with passports. Once a monkey takes an item, a 'guide' appears offering to 'negotiate' retrieval for a 100–500K IDR 'fee' and a bag of peanuts (which the monkey trades).

Traveler reports document the official-sanctuary perspective. The scam economy: some monkeys are systematically trained by local handlers to target tourists, steal specific items, and return them via the handler for a fee. The sanctuary officially denies this but traveler reports are consistent. The specific patterns: (1) monkey grabs sunglasses or hat — 'guide' offers retrieval for 50–100K; (2) monkey takes phone or wallet — 'guide' quotes 300–500K; (3) monkey bites or scratches — rabies risk, required 5-dose post-exposure shots costing $500–$2000; (4) aggressive monkey clamps onto tourist's leg or arm (common with small children); (5) passport theft — the most severe scenario, requiring embassy replacement at $150+ plus replacement-visa time.

For older travelers considering the Monkey Forest, the defensive playbook: (1) remove ALL valuables before entering — no sunglasses (worn or hair-clipped), no hats, no visible jewelry, no loose phone in pockets; (2) keep bags zipped and worn across the chest with one hand covering; (3) never feed monkeys (illegal AND dangerous); (4) never make direct eye contact (primate challenge); (5) never try to retrieve a stolen item yourself — back away and let the item go; (6) if bitten or scratched, go IMMEDIATELY to BIMC Hospital Ubud (+62 361 2300 150) for rabies post-exposure series; (7) official entrance fee is 80,000 IDR adults, 60,000 IDR children (2025 rate) — pay ONLY at the official ticket booth.

Red Flags

  • Monkey suddenly approaches holding eye contact
  • Monkey follows you specifically (trained to target tourists)
  • 'Guide' appears immediately after a monkey takes an item
  • Sunglasses, hat, hair clip, or visible phone on body
  • Small child brought into the forest (primary target)

How to Avoid

  • Remove ALL valuables before entering (no sunglasses, hats, jewelry, loose phones).
  • Keep bags zipped across chest with one hand covering.
  • Don't feed monkeys; Don't make direct eye contact.
  • If an item is stolen, back away — do not try to retrieve.
  • If bitten/scratched, go IMMEDIATELY to BIMC Hospital Ubud (+62 361 2300 150) for rabies series.
Scam #2
Kopi Luwak 'Coffee Plantation' Animal-Cruelty Tour & Fake Product
🔶 Medium
📍 Tegallalang, Kintamani, Ubud-area 'coffee plantation' tourist stops; tour-package 'coffee tasting' kickback venues

Ubud-area 'Kopi Luwak coffee plantation' tours are a documented animal-cruelty-plus-tourist-trap ecosystem.

Traveler reports document the core pattern: 'The Luwak might be 'authentic' but they keep the cats in small cages and force feed them coffee bean' s — the 'free-range wild luwak' marketing is false; the animals are caged and abused. One traveler wrote: 'Most are fake, and those selling legit luwak coffee that has been pooped out by a civet cat, obtaine' d it through battery-farming cruelty, not wild foraging.

Traveler reports confirms the broader tourist-trap ecosystem: 'The crap icing on the cake is the sad token luwak to promote said specialty coffee. Avoid these plac' es. The scam mechanics: (1) tour package includes 'free coffee plantation visit' with kickback to the operator; (2) 'demonstration' shows one caged luwak while claiming 'wild-foraged' product; (3) 'tasting' of 8–12 coffees ends with pressure to buy 200–500K IDR bags of kopi luwak (legitimate genuine wild kopi luwak is $50–$100 USD for 50g at licensed premium producers like Kopi Jawa Dua); (4) 'luwak coffee' sold at tourist-trap plantations is typically regular robusta with artificial flavouring; (5) 'coconut coffee' or 'chocolate coffee' upsells at 150K+ IDR per bag.

For older travelers, the practical playbook: (1) skip ALL 'free coffee plantation' stops on tour packages — they are pure sales funnels with caged animals; (2) for GENUINE ethically-sourced Indonesian coffee, visit Seniman Coffee Studio (Jalan Sriwedari, Ubud — award-winning specialty roaster), Kopi Kultur (multiple Ubud locations), or Anomali Coffee (Ubud + Seminyak); (3) expect legitimate specialty coffee at 35K–80K IDR per cup or 150K–250K IDR per bag of beans (non-kopi-luwak); (4) genuine wild-foraged kopi luwak with certification is a premium product rarely found in tourist plantations — buy at licensed specialty roasters only if at all; (5) decline 'coffee tasting' sessions at plantation stops — they always end in sales pressure.

Red Flags

  • Tour package includes 'free coffee plantation visit' in Tegallalang or Kintamani area
  • Caged luwak animal displayed as 'free range' or 'wild forager'
  • 'Coffee tasting' of 8–12 varieties with escalating sales pressure
  • Kopi Luwak bag priced 200–500K IDR (legitimate is $50–$100 USD for 50g at licensed producers)
  • 'Coconut coffee' or 'chocolate coffee' upsells

How to Avoid

  • SKIP all 'free coffee plantation' stops on tour packages — pure sales funnels with caged animals.
  • For genuine Indonesian specialty coffee: Seniman Coffee Studio, Kopi Kultur, Anomali Coffee (Ubud).
  • Expected specialty coffee price: 35–80K IDR cup, 150–250K bag of beans.
  • Genuine wild kopi luwak is premium ($50–$100/50g at licensed producers only).
  • DECLINE 'coffee tasting' sessions at plantation stops.
Scam #3
Fake-Guru Yoga Retreat & 'Spiritual Healing' Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 Ubud yoga studios (Yoga Barn area), 'spiritual retreat' villas, independent 'healer' practitioners advertising through hotel concierges and Instagram

Ubud has a documented ecosystem of fake-guru / fake-healer scams targeting spiritually-oriented tourists.

One traveler wrote: 'I gave 100,000 IDR to ask honest questions and was told the Yoga Barn has become one of the biggest' commercial operations with complex quality issues. It's illegal for foreigners to' operate as spiritual healers but the industry operates outside regulation.

The specific scam patterns: (1) 'spiritual healer' charges $200–$1000 per session for 'energy clearing' or 'past-life regression'; (2) 'ayahuasca ceremony' retreats at $500–$3000 per weekend with unlicensed facilitators; (3) '7-day transformation retreat' at $2000–$5000 with escalating upsells for 'private sessions'; (4) 'Australian-expat-guru' scenarios where foreigners operate illegally as healers with no medical or psychological training; (5) victims (often mental-health-vulnerable) are targeted for additional sessions via emotional dependency. gives the legitimate side: established yoga studios like Yoga Barn itself are genuine businesses; the scam is in the satellite 'private healer' and 'retreat' marketplace around them. Traveler reports confirm the 2025 community view that Ubud's core yoga and wellness industry is worthwhile but has a scam periphery.

For older travelers visiting Ubud for wellness, the practical playbook: (1) book classes ONLY at established studios with clear commercial licensing: Yoga Barn (theyogabarn.com), Radiantly Alive, Intuitive Flow, Ubud Yoga House; (2) typical class rate: 150–200K IDR drop-in (~$10–$13 USD); weekly passes 800K–1.5M; (3) Avoid 'private healer' sessions booked through hotel concierges or Instagram 'spiritual coaches' without verifiable credentials; (4) Don't participate in 'ayahuasca' or 'plant medicine' ceremonies in Bali — unlicensed, unsafe, and illegal; (5) Avoid transformation-retreat packages at $2000+ — most are high-pressure upsell operations; (6) for genuine spa/wellness, use named hotel spas (Four Seasons Sayan, COMO Shambhala, Hanging Gardens Ubud) with posted prices.

Red Flags

  • 'Private healer' booked through hotel concierge without verifiable credentials
  • 'Spiritual healer' charging $200–$1000 per session
  • 'Ayahuasca' or 'plant medicine' ceremony offering (illegal in Indonesia)
  • '7-day transformation retreat' at $2000+ with escalating session upsells
  • Foreign (non-Indonesian) operator claiming to be a 'healer' or 'guru'

How to Avoid

  • Book ONLY at established studios: Yoga Barn, Radiantly Alive, Intuitive Flow, Ubud Yoga House.
  • Typical rate: 150–200K IDR drop-in class; 800K–1.5M weekly.
  • Avoid 'private healer' sessions booked through concierges or Instagram.
  • Don't participate in 'ayahuasca' or 'plant medicine' ceremonies (illegal).
  • For spa/wellness, use named hotel spas (Four Seasons Sayan, COMO Shambhala, Hanging Gardens Ubud).

Scam #4
Ubud Taxi Mafia & Anti-Grab Blockade
⚠️ High
📍 Ubud center (Jalan Raya Ubud, Jalan Monkey Forest), Campuhan Ridge Walk entrance, Sacred Monkey Forest exit, tourist-hotel taxi ranks

Ubud has one of Indonesia's most-documented 'taxi mafia' situations where local private drivers physically block Grab and Gojek drivers from picking up passengers in tourist zones. One traveler wrote: 'Taxi mafia has always been around, even before online taxis. It's just local private drivers protect' ing their livelihood — but the enforcement is often aggressive with physical intimidation of app drivers.

The specific Ubud patterns: (1) Grab/Gojek drivers won't accept pickups in Ubud center (the app shows 'no drivers available'); (2) taxi-mafia drivers at hotel ranks quote 'fixed prices' 3–5x the Grab rate; (3) if a Grab driver IS nearby, local taxi drivers physically block them from picking up passengers, sometimes attacking the driver; (4) tourist forced to walk 500m–1km to a 'safe zone' (Ubud Starbucks, Warung Biah Biah area) where Grab pickups are tolerated; (5) hotel-arranged transfers at 2–3x Grab rate.

For older travelers, the practical playbook: (1) pre-book Grab/Gojek OUTSIDE Ubud center — walk to designated safe pickup zones (Starbucks Ubud, Warung Biah Biah, Ubud Market car park); (2) for hotel-to-Ubud-center rides, ask concierge for 'Grab-safe drop-off' destinations; (3) for day trips (Tegallalang, Tanah Lot, Uluwatu), pre-book a private driver for the full day at 600K–800K IDR (~$40–$55 USD) through a licensed operator rather than trying to use app rides; (4) Blue Bird Bali (the national licensed metered taxi) operates in Ubud — the dark-blue vehicles with '690 9000' phone number are safe and metered; (5) walk where possible — Ubud center is compact and pedestrian-friendly; (6) for Ubud-DPS airport transfers, book through hotel at fixed 300–400K IDR rather than trying Grab from Ubud center.

Red Flags

  • Grab/Gojek app shows 'no drivers available' in Ubud center (forced blockade)
  • Taxi-mafia driver at hotel rank quoting 300K+ IDR for a 2–5 km trip
  • Driver physically blocking a Grab driver from picking you up
  • Hotel concierge pushing 'partner' transfer at 2–3x Grab rate
  • Local driver following your phone-booked Grab driver aggressively

How to Avoid

  • Pre-book Grab/Gojek from outside Ubud center — designated safe zones: Starbucks Ubud, Warung Biah Biah, Ubud Market car park.
  • For day trips, pre-book a private driver for full day at 600–800K IDR (~$40–$55 USD) via licensed operator.
  • Use Blue Bird Bali metered taxi (dark blue, phone 690 9000) — safe, metered, licensed.
  • Walk where possible — Ubud center is compact and walkable.
  • For Ubud-DPS airport, book through hotel at fixed 300–400K IDR.
Scam #5
Ubud Pushy Massage & 'Extended Service' Upcharge Pressure
🔶 Medium
📍 Ubud center massage shops (Jalan Hanoman, Jalan Monkey Forest), hotel-doorstep massage solicitation, tourist-strip spa storefronts

Ubud's massage economy has a documented 2025 upsell and pressure-sales pattern.

One traveler wrote: 'They usually argue and concede to 1 girl, the massage is never worth it afterwards. It's more of a p' ressure environment than a relaxation experience. The pattern: advertised '60-minute massage for 100K IDR' pivots mid-session to upsells for 'oil,' 'extended time,' 'herbal compress,' or 'special treatment' at 200–500K additional, with social pressure to accept.

Traveler reports document the related card-skim-at-spa variant: 'I get Fraud alert on my cards. I paid cash. My cards were in my wallet within reach while I was get' ting a massage — some spa operations clone cards remotely via RFID while the tourist is in the treatment. The specific patterns: (1) street-side massage shops on Jalan Hanoman and Jalan Monkey Forest advertising '100K IDR/hour' with pressure escalation during session; (2) 'oil upgrade' offered mid-session at 150K; (3) 'extended time' offered at 200–300K; (4) 'herbal compress' or 'cupping' add-on at 200–500K; (5) tip pressure at end with staff expecting 100–200K minimum or refusing to unlock treatment room door; (6) card-skimming risk when cards are in bags left in treatment-room lockers or chairs.

For older travelers, the practical playbook: (1) book ONLY at named reputable spas with posted prices: Karsa Spa, Bodyworks, Kush Ayurvedic Spa, Spa at Four Seasons Sayan (premium); (2) expected cost: 200–400K IDR for 60-minute massage at reputable Ubud spa, 800K–1.5M for premium hotel spa; (3) Refuse all mid-session upsells firmly — say 'non, terima kasih' and maintain position; (4) Don't leave cards or cash in treatment-room lockers — take only what you need for payment; (5) use RFID-blocking wallet or leave cards in hotel safe; (6) reputable tip is 20–50K IDR after 60-minute massage — never accept tip pressure above this; (7) walk out of any venue that blocks your exit until payment is resolved.

Red Flags

  • Street-side massage shop advertising '100K/hour' with no posted upgrade menu
  • 'Oil upgrade' or 'extended time' offered mid-session at 150–300K
  • Tip pressure of 100–200K+ at end with staff blocking exit
  • Card-skim risk at spa with cards in treatment-room locker or chair
  • Hotel-doorstep massage solicitation (often unlicensed, inflated pricing)

How to Avoid

  • Book at named reputable spas: Karsa Spa, Bodyworks, Kush Ayurvedic Spa, Four Seasons Sayan.
  • Expected: 200–400K IDR 60-min massage at reputable spa; 800K–1.5M premium hotel spa.
  • Refuse mid-session upsells firmly ('non, terima kasih').
  • Don't leave cards/cash in treatment-room lockers — take only payment amount.
  • Reasonable tip: 20–50K IDR after 60-min massage.
Scam #6
Tegallalang Rice Terrace 'Mandatory Guide' & Photo-Spot Fees
🔶 Medium
📍 Tegallalang Rice Terrace (30 min north of Ubud), Jatiluwih Rice Terrace (1 hr northwest), photo-op swing operators at both

Ubud's iconic Tegallalang Rice Terrace has a documented tourist-fee ecosystem that layers multiple charges on what should be a 20,000 IDR entry...

The scam patterns: (1) 'parking attendant' demands 20–50K IDR at multiple points along the terrace road; (2) 'entrance fee collectors' at various access points charge 10–30K IDR (the official single entry is 15K IDR); (3) 'mandatory local guide' demanded at 100–200K IDR for a walk tourists can do themselves; (4) 'scenic viewpoint fee' at photo-op spots (the famous Instagram bamboo swings) at 100–500K IDR per photo; (5) 'Bali Swing' operators charging $35–$50 for a 30-second photo ride. traveler reports specifically flags 'mandatory guides' as part of the 2025 tourist-trap pattern. The official 2025 Tegallalang rates: 15,000 IDR per person entrance (at the main parking ticket booth), 5,000 IDR parking fee. Anything above these rates at multiple checkpoints is scam-tier charging.

For older travelers, the practical playbook: (1) pay entrance ONLY at the main official ticket booth (near the large Tegallalang signboard) — 15K IDR; (2) park at the main designated car park — 5K IDR; (3) Refuse 'mandatory guide' demands — the terrace is walkable without a guide; (4) SKIP the 'Bali Swing' photo-op operators — they're expensive tourist traps; take your own photos at free viewpoints; (5) for a less-scammed alternative, visit Jatiluwih Rice Terrace (1 hour northwest of Ubud, UNESCO World Heritage, less commercial) at 40K IDR official entrance; (6) avoid visiting during peak 10 AM–2 PM when scammer density is highest; early morning (7–9 AM) is quieter and more authentic.

Red Flags

  • 'Parking attendant' demanding 20–50K IDR at multiple road points
  • Non-official 'entrance collector' charging 10–30K IDR at secondary access points
  • 'Mandatory guide' demanded at 100–200K IDR
  • 'Scenic viewpoint fee' 100–500K IDR for photo-op spots
  • 'Bali Swing' operators charging $35–$50 per 30-second ride

How to Avoid

  • Pay entrance ONLY at main official ticket booth: 15K IDR + 5K parking.
  • Refuse 'mandatory guide' demands — terrace is walkable without guide.
  • SKIP 'Bali Swing' photo-ops — expensive tourist traps; take free viewpoint photos.
  • Alternative: Jatiluwih Rice Terrace (UNESCO, less commercial) at 40K IDR.
  • Visit 7–9 AM (quieter) rather than 10 AM–2 PM peak.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Indonesian National Police (Polri) station. Call 110 (Police) or 112 (Emergency). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at polri.go.id.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Jakarta is at Jl. Merdeka Selatan No. 3-5, Jakarta 10110. For emergencies: +62 21-5083-1000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
