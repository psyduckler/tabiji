Scam #1
Windsor Castle Admission Third-Party Reseller Inflation
🔶 Medium
📍 Windsor Castle main entrance (Castle Hill, Windsor), third-party reseller websites — targeting Windsor Castle's £33.50 admission

Windsor Castle admission is £33.50 adult / £20 senior 60+ direct at rct.uk (Royal Collection Trust, audio guide included free) — but third-party resellers (windsor-castle-tickets.com and SEO variants) buy Google ads above rct.uk and charge £49–£62 with bogus £4–£8 'booking fees,' £2–£5 'priority entry,' and £10–£15 'audio guide upgrades'; 'VIP Experience' tickets at £129–£199 claim non-existent behind-the-scenes access, and combo bundles at £149–£229 vs £97.80 DIY (Windsor £33.50 + Hampton Court £29.50 + Tower £34.80) are pure markup.

Windsor Castle is the world's oldest and largest inhabited castle (still an official royal residence) and Windsor's #1 tourist attraction. Official admission at rct.uk (the Royal Collection Trust site, the only legitimate source) is £33.50 adult, £20 concessions for age 60+, and £18.50 children — card-only at the gate with timed-entry slots in peak season (Easter–September). The audio guide is included free with admission, and St George's Chapel (the burial place of Queen Elizabeth II) is included but closed on Sundays. The Royal Collection Trust membership at £55/year individual or £85 joint gives unlimited free entry to Windsor, Buckingham Palace, Hampton Court, Kensington Palace, and Holyroodhouse — excellent value for travellers planning multiple royal sites.

The trap menu has eight recurring mechanics. Third-party reseller sites (windsor-castle-tickets.com, windsor-royal-castle.co.uk, and SEO-gamed variants) buy Google ads above rct.uk and display £33.50 or £35 prominently, then add £4–£8 'booking fee' plus £2–£5 'priority entry' plus £10–£15 'audio guide' (which is included free at official admission) at checkout — final price £49–£62 for the same £33.50 ticket. 'Windsor + Changing of the Guard premium view' tickets at £75–£99 fabricate access since the Changing of the Guard is free to watch from the public pavement outside the castle. 'Windsor VIP Experience' tickets at £129–£199 claim 'behind the scenes access' that doesn't exist — Windsor Castle doesn't offer retail behind-the-scenes tours. Combo bundles 'Windsor + Hampton Court + Tower of London' at £149–£229 per person via third parties when direct booking totals £97.80 (Windsor £33.50 + Hampton Court £29.50 + Tower £34.80). The senior concession at £20 for age 60+ saves £13.50 per ticket against the £33.50 adult rate, but resellers don't apply this discount automatically. Royal Collection Trust membership at £55/year covers unlimited entry across the 5 royal sites and pays off on 2+ visits during a UK trip.

For older travellers visiting Windsor Castle, the defense is to book at rct.uk and claim the senior concession. Book Windsor Castle tickets only at rct.uk (the Royal Collection Trust official site, verified by manual URL entry) at £33.50 adult or £20 senior for age 60+ (saves £13.50 per ticket) — the audio guide is included free, so refuse any reseller 'audio guide upgrade' at £10–£15 — and refuse every 'Windsor VIP Experience' at £129–£199 (no retail behind-the-scenes tours exist), every 'Changing of the Guard premium view' at £75–£99 (the ceremony is free from the public pavement), and every 'Windsor + Hampton Court + Tower of London' combo at £149–£229 against the £97.80 DIY total (£66.50 with senior concessions). For peak season (Easter–September), book timed entry 1–3 weeks ahead; off-season allows walk-up. Consider Royal Collection Trust Membership at £55/year individual or £85 joint for unlimited entry across 5 royal sites — pays off on 2+ visits during a UK trip. St George's Chapel (where Queen Elizabeth II is buried) is included with admission but closed on Sundays. UK Senior Railcard at £30/year for age 60+ drops GWR fares 34% on the London Paddington to Slough to Windsor & Eton Central route.

Red Flags

  • Google search result above rct.uk for 'Windsor Castle tickets' (lookalike reseller domain)
  • Reseller price £33.50 + £4-£8 booking fee + £2-£5 'priority' + £10-£15 'audio guide' (audio is INCLUDED free)
  • 'Windsor VIP Experience' at £129-£199 (no retail behind-the-scenes tour exists)
  • 'Windsor + Hampton Court + Tower' combo at £149-£229 (direct is £97.80 adult / £66.50 senior)
  • Reseller not applying Senior concession (£20 vs £33.50 for age 60+) automatically

How to Avoid

  • Book tickets ONLY at rct.uk (Royal Collection Trust official site) — check URL manually.
  • Claim Senior concession (£20 for age 60+) — saves £13.50 per ticket.
  • Audio guide is INCLUDED free — refuse 'audio guide upgrade' at £10-£15.
  • For combo sites, book each directly: Windsor + Hampton Court + Tower = £97.80 adult / £66.50 senior.
  • Consider Royal Collection Trust Membership at £55/year for unlimited entry to 5 royal sites.
Scam #2
Windsor Changing of the Guard 'Premium View' Tour Upsell
🟢 Low
📍 Windsor Castle Upper Ward, Castle Hill pavement, State Entrance — targeting the Changing of the Guard free public ceremony

Windsor Castle Changing of the Guard runs alternating days at 11:00 AM (typically Tue/Thu/Sat April–July, Mon–Sat August, limited winter, weather permitting) — the ceremony is free to watch from the Castle Hill pavement, but third-party operators sell 'Premium View' tours at £39–£99 and 'VIP Guard Ceremony Access' at £99–£249 that either deliver free pavement viewing or Windsor Castle admission at markup; check royal.uk for the current schedule.

Windsor Castle's Changing of the Guard ceremony runs at 11:00 AM on alternating days (typically Tuesday/Thursday/Saturday April–July, Monday–Saturday in August, with a limited winter schedule) when weather permits. The ceremony itself takes place at the castle's Upper Ward, which requires £33.50 paid admission to enter — but the guards' procession to and from the castle via Castle Hill and High Street is fully visible from the public pavement for free with no ticket required. Long-time community consensus is direct: 'just stand on Castle Hill for free.' Royal.uk publishes the official schedule for both Windsor and the larger Buckingham Palace ceremony, with weather-dependent cancellations announced day-of.

The trap menu has six recurring mechanics. Third-party 'Premium View + Changing of the Guard' tours at £39–£99 per person that deliver either public-pavement viewing (free) or Windsor Castle admission at markup. 'VIP Guard Ceremony Access' at £99–£249 claiming 'behind the ropes' access that doesn't exist — all public viewing is either from the free pavement or the general-admission Upper Ward with the £33.50 ticket. Schedule-variability hook: operators sell 'guaranteed access' on dates when the ceremony isn't running (weather-dependent, alternate-days pattern). 'Changing of the Guard + Castle Admission + Long Walk photo package' bundles at £89–£149 — the Long Walk is a free public park stretching from the castle to the Copper Horse statue. Tourists confusing the Windsor ceremony with the larger Buckingham Palace ceremony, with operators exploiting the confusion. The most aggressive variant sells access to the ceremony on cancelled days without refunding when weather cancels the procession.

For older travellers wanting to see Changing of the Guard at Windsor, the defense is to stand on the free pavement and check the schedule at royal.uk. Arrive at Windsor Castle Hill by 10:30 AM for the 11:00 AM ceremony on scheduled days (verify the schedule and weather at royal.uk the night before) and stand on the public pavement at Castle Hill or High Street where the guards' procession is fully visible for free — for ceremony views inside the Upper Ward, buy Windsor Castle admission at £33.50 or £20 senior at rct.uk for full ceremony plus castle tour; refuse every 'Changing of the Guard Premium View' tour at £39–£99 (paying for pavement access that's free), every 'VIP Guard Ceremony Access' at £99–£249 (no behind-the-ropes access exists), and every 'Changing of the Guard + Long Walk photo package' bundle at £89–£149 since the Long Walk is a free public park. Arrive at 10:00 AM for a good pavement spot. After the ceremony, walk the free Long Walk from the castle to the Copper Horse statue (1.5 miles, castle views). If you're in London, the Buckingham Palace ceremony is often more accessible with a more regular schedule and larger viewing areas. For older travellers with mobility concerns, Castle Hill has limited seating — arrive early or bring a folding stool.

Red Flags

  • 'Windsor Changing of the Guard Premium View' tour at £39-£99 per person (viewing from pavement is free)
  • 'VIP Guard Ceremony Access' at £99-£249 claiming 'behind the ropes' (no such access exists)
  • 'Guaranteed Changing of the Guard access' for specific dates without checking royal.uk schedule
  • 'Changing of the Guard + Long Walk photo package' at £89-£149 (Long Walk is free public park)
  • Operator confusion between Windsor Changing of the Guard and Buckingham Palace version (separate schedules)

How to Avoid

  • Stand on the public Castle Hill pavement — the guards PROCESSION is free to view.
  • For ceremony INSIDE Upper Ward, buy Windsor Castle admission at rct.uk (£33.50 / £20 senior).
  • Refuse ALL 'Premium View' tours at £39-£99 — pavement viewing is already free.
  • Verify ceremony IS running at royal.uk the night before (weather/alternate-days pattern).
  • Long Walk after ceremony is a free public park — 1.5-mile walk with castle views.
Scam #3
Windsor Train-Station Confusion & Wrong-Station Upsell
🟢 Low
📍 Windsor & Eton Central station, Windsor & Eton Riverside station, Slough junction — targeting tourists unfamiliar with Windsor's two-station setup

Windsor has two stations 400m apart with different operators: Windsor & Eton Central (GWR from London Paddington via Slough, contactless works) and Windsor & Eton Riverside (SWR direct from London Waterloo at £11–£15 off-peak, contactless NOT yet available) — this routing complexity drives 'Windsor Rail + Castle' aggregator bundles at £65–£89 per person against £44.50–£48.50 DIY direct, plus 'Windsor & Eton Riverside transfer service' at £25–£45 by unlicensed operators when the actual walk between stations is 3–4 minutes.

Windsor has two railway stations served by different train operators at different pricing. Windsor & Eton Central terminates GWR trains from London Paddington via a Slough change. Windsor & Eton Riverside terminates SWR trains from London Waterloo direct. The two stations are 400 metres apart in central Windsor, but the operator and routing differences create exactly the kind of confusion that third-party aggregators monetise. The community-flagged detail older tourists miss: contactless tap-and-go works on the GWR Paddington-Slough-Central route but is not yet available on the SWR Waterloo-Riverside route, so travellers expecting to use Oyster or contactless cards on the direct Riverside route get caught at the Waterloo gate.

The trap menu has three recurring mechanics. Third-party 'Windsor Rail + Castle' packages at £65–£89 per person bundle a £11–£15 train fare with a £33.50 castle ticket for what's a £44.50–£48.50 DIY total, with the £20–£40 spread going to the aggregator as commission. 'Windsor & Eton Riverside transfer service' at £25–£45 per person sold by unlicensed operators at Central station — the actual walk between Central and Riverside is 3–4 minutes through the town centre, no taxi needed. 'London-Windsor-Hampton Court' combo rail tickets at £79–£119 that cover standard GWR journeys legitimately bookable at gwr.com for £35–£55. UK Senior Railcard at £30/year for age 60+ drops both GWR and SWR fares 34% (£11–£15 becomes £7.50–£10) and is the cleanest single defense for travellers planning multiple UK rail trips. The BritRail Pass at £39–£69 per day is cheaper only if you're making 3+ UK rail trips on the same day.

For older travellers travelling London to Windsor, the defense is to book direct on GWR or SWR and apply the Senior Railcard. If holding an Oyster or contactless card, use the GWR route via London Paddington → Slough → Windsor & Eton Central (contactless tap-and-go works, £9–£14 off-peak) — if pre-booking tickets, the SWR direct from London Waterloo → Windsor & Eton Riverside is 55 minutes direct at £11–£15 off-peak (note contactless does NOT yet work on this route) — book tickets only at gwr.com, swrailway.com, or trainline.com, apply the UK Senior Railcard at £30/year for age 60+ (34% discount on both routes), and refuse every 'Windsor Rail + Castle' aggregator bundle at £65–£89 against the £44.50–£48.50 DIY total, every 'Windsor & Eton Riverside transfer service' at £25–£45 (the walk between stations is 3–4 minutes), and every 'London-Windsor-Hampton Court' combo at £79–£119. Both Windsor stations are in the town centre — Central is next to Windsor Castle, Riverside is 400 metres away by the river. For Windsor plus Hampton Court same-day, the route is London to Windsor in the morning then Clapham Junction to Hampton Court in the afternoon. For Windsor plus Legoland same-day, the direct 702 bus runs from Windsor town centre to Legoland at £4 single. Both stations have step-free access; Central is slightly easier for older travellers with mobility needs.

Red Flags

  • Third-party 'Windsor Rail + Castle' package at £65-£89 per person (direct booking: £44.50-£48.50 with senior concession)
  • 'Windsor & Eton Riverside transfer service' at £25-£45 per person (stations are 400m apart, 4-min walk)
  • Tourist using Oyster/contactless on SWR Waterloo-Riverside route (contactless NOT supported)
  • 'London-Windsor-Hampton Court' combo rail at £79-£119 (direct gwr.com fare is £35-£55)
  • Reseller not applying UK Senior Railcard 34% discount (for age 60+)

How to Avoid

  • For contactless tap-and-go: GWR London Paddington → Slough → Windsor & Eton Central.
  • For direct-route: SWR London Waterloo → Windsor & Eton Riverside (NO contactless, pre-book).
  • Book tickets ONLY at gwr.com, swrailway.com, or trainline.com.
  • Apply UK Senior Railcard (£30/year, age 60+) for 34% discount — both routes.
  • Refuse 'Windsor Rail + Castle' combos at £65-£89 — £11-£15 train + £33.50 Castle = £44.50 direct.

Scam #4
Legoland Windsor Ticket Reseller & Parking 'Car Park Exit Fiasco'
🔶 Medium
📍 Legoland Windsor Resort (Winkfield Road, Windsor), Legoland Windsor car park — targeting family travelers and tourists combining Windsor + Legoland same-day

Legoland Windsor (Merlin Entertainments) admission is £49–£79 adult direct at legoland.co.uk plus £8 per car ANPR-enforced parking — but third-party resellers (legoland-tickets.com, windsor-legoland.co.uk) charge £55–£91 with bogus 'booking fee' plus 'seat selection,' some sell 'sold out' dates with vouchers that fail at the gate, and the 'car park exit fiasco' issues £100+ Parking Charge Notices via ANPR cameras 2–4 weeks after visit when machines malfunction or barriers lift for the wrong car.

Legoland Windsor (operated by Merlin Entertainments) is one of the UK's largest theme parks and Windsor's #2 tourist destination after Windsor Castle, drawing a steady flow of grandchild-tourism with grandparents who don't always know UK theme-park ticketing or ANPR parking enforcement. Official admission at legoland.co.uk is £49–£79 adult with dynamic pricing — peak weekend rates higher, advance booking typically cheaper. Parking is £8 per car, pre-bookable online and ANPR-enforced (automatic number-plate recognition cameras at the entry and exit). The Merlin Annual Pass at £149–£229 per year covers Legoland, Alton Towers, Thorpe Park, LEGO Discovery, SEA LIFE, and Madame Tussauds — excellent value for families visiting 2+ parks during a UK trip. Over 2024–2025 the community has documented a specific reseller-fraud pattern and a 'car park exit fiasco' where ANPR-enforced private parking operators issue £100+ fines for vaguely-defined violations weeks after the actual visit.

The trap menu has seven recurring mechanics. Third-party reseller sites (legoland-tickets.com, windsor-legoland.co.uk, and SEO variants) sell tickets at the same £49–£79 face value but add £4–£8 'booking fee' plus £2–£5 'seat selection' at checkout, bringing the final price to £55–£91 per adult. 'Sold out' dates that aren't actually sold out get sold by resellers who take payment then deliver voucher tickets that fail at the gate. 'Legoland + Windsor Castle combo' bundles at £79–£139 via third parties rarely beat direct booking (Legoland £49–£79 + Windsor Castle £33.50 = £82.50–£112.50). The car-park exit fiasco runs in three sub-variants: tourists exit without paying the £8 fee at the pay machine because the machines malfunction on busy days, the pre-paid booking doesn't register because the licence plate was entered wrong, or tourists drive through exit barriers lifted for previous cars triggering fines. The £100+ Parking Charge Notice arrives 2–4 weeks later from a private enforcement company. Older travellers are particularly affected because they may not realise the ANPR system is active and pay the PCN unnecessarily — the fine is technically an invoice (civil, not criminal) and is appealable via parkingappeals.co.uk with evidence of pre-booked parking.

For older travellers visiting Legoland Windsor (typically with grandchildren), the defense is direct booking, careful licence-plate entry, and bus 702 if avoiding the car park. Book Legoland tickets only at legoland.co.uk direct (verify URL manually) and book parking at £8/car via the same site (ensure the confirmation email shows your licence plate registered correctly) — for 2+ UK theme-park visits during a trip, the Merlin Annual Pass at £149–£229 covers Legoland plus Alton Towers, Thorpe Park, and 11 more attractions; refuse every third-party 'Legoland ticket' booking site, every 'Legoland + Windsor Castle' combo at £79–£139, every 'sold out' reseller voucher, and don't immediately pay any 'Parking Charge Notice' arriving 2–4 weeks after visit since the fine is appealable via parkingappeals.co.uk with your pre-booked parking confirmation as evidence. On arrival, confirm you entered your licence plate number correctly at the pay machine. Exit via the main exit lane and wait for the barrier to lift for your specific car — don't tailgate the car in front. For transport without any parking risk, bus 702 from Windsor town centre to Legoland is £4 single or £7 day return — eliminates the ANPR fiasco entirely. Legoland provides free wheelchair and buggy loans for older travellers with walking difficulty — specify at ticket booking.

Red Flags

  • Third-party 'Legoland tickets' at £49-£79 + £4-£8 booking fee + £2-£5 seat selection at checkout
  • 'Sold out' date on reseller site that isn't sold out on legoland.co.uk (potential fake ticket fraud)
  • 'Legoland + Windsor Castle combo' at £79-£139 per person (direct is £82.50-£112.50)
  • Parking Charge Notice (PCN) £100+ arriving 2-4 weeks after visit (ANPR 'car park exit fiasco')
  • Pay machine malfunction or misread license plate at Legoland car park

How to Avoid

  • Book tickets ONLY at legoland.co.uk — verify URL manually.
  • For 2+ theme-park visits, Merlin Annual Pass at £149-£229 covers Legoland + Alton Towers + Thorpe Park + 11 more.
  • Book parking at £8/car via legoland.co.uk with correct license plate.
  • If you get a PCN 2-4 weeks later, appeal via parkingappeals.co.uk — don't immediately pay.
  • Bus 702 from Windsor town center to Legoland at £4 single avoids the car park entirely.
Scam #5
Windsor + Stonehenge + Bath Coach-Park Combo Day-Trip Fraud
⚠️ High
📍 London Victoria Coach Station, London Paddington pickup — targeting the Windsor/Stonehenge/Bath/Hampton-Court combination day-trip market

London 'Windsor + Stonehenge + Bath' 3-city coach-tour combos at £99–£145 per person cram Windsor into 30–45 minutes (insufficient for any meaningful exploration), often include Windsor only as 'external viewing' from the Long Walk without castle admission (£33.50 separate), claim 'exclusive Guard view' that's the free public pavement, and run 14-hour door-to-door days with non-refundable 48-hour cancellation; pick Windsor alone via direct train at £11–£15.

Windsor is frequently marketed as 'one leg' of a multi-city London day-trip combo alongside Stonehenge, Bath, and sometimes Hampton Court Palace. These combo products are the most-booked UK day-tour category by tourist volume and the most-criticised product category by traveller community consensus. The pattern is closely related to the Bath and Stonehenge coach-tour scams flagged elsewhere, but Windsor is the destination where the individual-destination choice most often collapses into a rushed combo because Windsor sits closest to London and gets allocated the smallest time slot.

The trap menu has nine Windsor-specific mechanics. The 'Windsor external only' pattern: many combo tours include Windsor as 'external viewing' from the Long Walk without interior State Apartments access. Windsor Castle admission at £33.50 is not included in the coach-tour price unless explicitly listed — tourists discover at the castle that interior access requires a separate £33.50 per person. 'Windsor + Changing of the Guard' combos claim 'exclusive Guard view' that's actually the free public pavement. Hampton Court + Windsor same-day is possible but tight — each site needs 3+ hours plus 1 hour transit, but combo tours typically give 1.5 hours each. The 'Windsor + Stonehenge + Bath' 3-city combo crams Windsor into 30–45 minutes, insufficient for any meaningful exploration. Coach-tour pickup at London Victoria is typically 7:30–8:00 AM — older travellers on US jet-lag struggle with this start and miss the first pickup. Return to London is 20:00–22:00 — a 14-hour door-to-door day. Cancellation policies are often 'non-refundable within 48 hours,' catching tourists who need to change plans. The 'Windsor coach park drop' is typically at Windsor Coach Park 400 metres from the castle — the Long Walk is 1.5 miles from the coach park, requiring bus or taxi add-on not in the itinerary.

For older travellers considering Windsor as a London day-trip, the defense is to pick Windsor alone or combine with Hampton Court only. Pick Windsor alone for a proper day-trip — train London Waterloo to Windsor & Eton Riverside (55 min direct, £11–£15) or London Paddington to Windsor & Eton Central (via Slough, £9–£14) — spending a full day at Castle, Long Walk, and St George's Chapel; if combining Windsor with another site, Windsor + Hampton Court is the most feasible same-day combo (both on the Thames within 30 minutes by train, each site 2–3 hours); refuse every 'Windsor + Stonehenge + Bath' 3-city combo at £99–£145 per person (Windsor allocation is too short for meaningful visit), every 'Windsor external only' coach tour where castle admission isn't included, and every 'non-refundable within 48 hours' cancellation policy that contradicts the standard 24-hour Viator/TripAdvisor norm. If you want a coach combo with Windsor properly included, Rabbie's Tours or Evan Evans 'Windsor + Stonehenge' 2-city at £129–£159 is better value than 3-city combos. UK Senior Railcard at £30/year for age 60+ drops direct train fares 34%. For older travellers with mobility concerns, a single-destination Windsor day is vastly less physically taxing than a 3-city coach marathon. If booking a coach tour, confirm 24-hour refund window and check what 'Windsor included' actually means before paying.

Red Flags

  • 'Windsor + Stonehenge + Bath' 3-city combo day-tour at £99-£145 per person from London Victoria
  • Itinerary lists 'Windsor external only' (means NO State Apartments admission — requires separate £33.50)
  • 'Windsor + Changing of the Guard exclusive view' (the ceremony is free from the public pavement)
  • 'Non-refundable within 48 hours' cancellation policy (Viator/TripAdvisor standard is 24-hr refund)
  • Coach-tour 'Windsor stop' of 30-45 min (insufficient for meaningful visit)

How to Avoid

  • Pick Windsor OR Stonehenge — don't combine three sites in one London day-trip.
  • For Windsor alone: train Waterloo → Windsor & Eton Riverside (55 min, £11-15) + Castle £33.50.
  • If combo, Windsor + Hampton Court (both on Thames, 30 min apart) is the most-feasible same-day.
  • If coach tour, use Rabbie's or Evan Evans 2-city Windsor + Stonehenge (not 3-city combos).
  • UK Senior Railcard (£30/year, age 60+) = 34% off all direct train fares.
Scam #6
Windsor Castle Hill Tourist-Trap Cafes & Thames Pier Boat Upsell
🟢 Low
📍 Castle Hill + Windsor High Street restaurants (adjacent to Windsor Castle), Windsor Promenade Thames boat pier — targeting post-castle-visit tourists

Windsor's Castle Hill and High Street restaurants 200m from the castle exit charge £22–£35 for pub-standard food (legitimate Windsor pub pricing is £14–£22), with £3–£5 'castle-view' terrace premiums and tour-guide kickback restaurants — and Thames Promenade pier touts sell 'Windsor Luxury Thames Cruise' at £160 per person for what's a £18–£28 French Brothers Ltd boat (frenchbrothers.co.uk, the main licensed operator) plus £15 of champagne.

Windsor's Castle Hill and High Street — the streets running from Windsor Castle main entrance to the Thames Promenade — host a concentrated tourist-trap restaurant and cafe corridor calibrated for one-time post-castle-visit meals. The local verdict is consistent: 'Windsor has good pubs, tourist-trap shops, and a castle.' The Thames Promenade pier-side operators add a separate scam layer where licensed French Brothers Ltd boat tours at frenchbrothers.co.uk (the main Windsor operator at £18–£28 for a 2-hour cruise) get marked up to £160 per person via aggregator-sold 'Windsor Luxury Thames Cruise' bundles that wrap a £25–£35 boat trip with £15 of champagne for a £110–£120 markup.

The trap menu has six recurring mechanics. Castle Hill and High Street restaurants positioned within 200 metres of the castle exit charge £22–£35 for pub-standard food (legitimate Windsor pub pricing is £14–£22). 'Windsor castle-view' restaurants add £3–£5 premium for outdoor seating with castle views — rarely worth the uplift. The Crooked House Tea Room and 'King's Cafe' branded establishments sometimes appear on coach-tour-recommended lists with 10–15% kickback to tour guides. Thames Promenade boat-tour touts at the pier sell 'Windsor-Maidenhead Thames cruise' at £25–£45 per person for 60–90 minute trips — decent value if it's the licensed French Brothers Ltd boat tour, but marked up if not. 'Windsor Luxury Thames Cruise' at £160 per person via third parties bundles a 60-minute boat + 'champagne' + 'castle photo opportunity' for what's a £25–£35 boat plus £15 of champagne — overpay of £110–£120. 'Thames River Cruise + Windsor Castle + Wine Tasting' combos at £189–£299 via aggregators bundle at 2–3× individual costs. 🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Metropolitan Police station. Call 999 (emergency) or 101 (non-emergency). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at met.police.uk.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy is at 33 Nine Elms Lane, London SW11 7US. For emergencies: +44 20 7499 9000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
