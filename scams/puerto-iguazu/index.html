The 6 Scams

Scam #1
IGR Airport Transfer & Puerto Iguazú Taxi Overcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Cataratas del Iguazú International Airport (IGR), Puerto Iguazú downtown taxi ranks (Av. Victoria Aguirre, Terminal de Ómnibus), hotel-concierge transfer desks, Sheraton Iguazú / Gran Meliá Iguazú in-park hotel booking

IGR airport sits 17 km southeast of Puerto Iguazú and 25 km from the Iguazú Falls National Park entrance.

Legitimate 2025 options: (1) pre-booked remise (private car service with posted flat rates) at ARS25,000–30,000 (~$25 USD) one-way to Puerto Iguazú town per traveler reports (2025); (2) VES-style shuttle vans at ARS6,000–10,000 per person; (3) hotel shuttle if staying at Loi Suites / Hotel Saint George / Awasi Iguazu (verify booking confirmation). The scam patterns: arrivals-hall 'private transfer' touts quote ARS60,000–100,000 one-way (2.5–4x legitimate), claiming 'fixed flat rate' and 'airport-approved'; hotel-concierge 'VIP transfer' upsells at $80–$150 USD one-way for the same 20-min run; Belmond / premium-hotel desks quote what OP called 'absurd' rates.

For older travelers in Puerto Iguazú, the clean playbook: (1) pre-book remise with your hotel before arrival — verify ARS price in writing via WhatsApp, and confirm driver's name + license plate + arrival time; (2) alternative: official IGR airport-taxi counter inside arrivals with posted flat-rate chart (ARS25,000–30,000 to town), and pay via card if accepted; (3) for IGR → Foz do Iguaçu (Brazilian side, requires border crossing), book a cross-border remise at ARS55,000 round-trip ($55 USD) — verify the driver stops at both migraciones posts (Argentine exit + Brazilian entry) to get legal entry/exit stamps; (4) within Puerto Iguazú town for short hops, use remise (taxi with radio dispatch) and confirm price before starting trip — most trips within town are ARS3,000–8,000.

For the border-crossing leg specifically: unlicensed taxi drivers frequently skip migraciones stamps to save 15 minutes, which creates overstay exit-fine exposure (~$100 USD) when you leave Brazil or Argentina later. Always insist on both stamps, photograph both stamps, and never accept a driver's 'it's faster without' reasoning. Save Comisaría Seccional 1ra Puerto Iguazú (Av. Victoria Aguirre, +54 3757 619784, 24/7) and 911.

Red Flags

  • Arrivals-hall IGR 'private transfer' tout quoting ARS60,000–100,000 or $50–$150 USD one-way — legitimate remise is ARS25,000–30,000
  • Hotel-concierge 'VIP airport transfer' at $80–$150 USD one-way for a 20-minute run — Same service for ARS25k via hotel-booked remise
  • Belmond / Gran Meliá Iguazú / premium-hotel desks quoting cross-border transfer rates dramatically higher than $55 USD round-trip street-rate
  • Cross-border driver offering to 'skip migraciones' to save time — this creates overstay exit-fine exposure at later border
  • Ciudad del Este taxi drivers at triple-frontier attempting to handle your bags or push you to their vehicle outside terminal

How to Avoid

  • Pre-book IGR airport remise via your hotel before arrival — verify ARS price in writing via WhatsApp, confirm driver's name + license plate + arrival time, and pay via card at drop-off not cash.
  • Alternative: use the official IGR airport-taxi counter inside arrivals with posted flat-rate chart (ARS25,000–30,000 to Puerto Iguazú town) — pay via card if accepted, get printed recibo.
  • For IGR → Foz do Iguaçu (Brazilian side) book a cross-border remise at ARS55,000 round-trip (~$55 USD) — insist driver stops at both migraciones posts and photograph both entry/exit stamps on your passport.
  • Refuse all arrivals-hall 'private transfer' / 'flat rate' / 'airport-approved' touts quoting above ARS30,000 — walk past them to the official taxi counter or meet your pre-booked remise.
  • Save Comisaría Seccional 1ra Puerto Iguazú (Av. Victoria Aguirre, +54 3757 619784, 24/7) + ITUREM complaint line (+54 3757 554545) in phone before arrival.
Scam #2
Iguazú National Park Tour Package & Gran Aventura Upsell
🟢 Low
📍 Iguazú Falls National Park main entrance (Centro de Visitantes), Argentine-side park concessionaire Iguazú Jungle (Gran Aventura / Macuco Safari / Paseo Ecológico), hotel-concierge tour desks, Puerto Iguazú downtown travel agency strips (Av. Victoria Aguirre, Av. Brasil)

Iguazú Falls National Park (Argentine side) is Argentina's crown-jewel UNESCO site with ~1.5 million annual visitors.

2025 foreigner entrance fee: ARS45,000 (~$32 USD, effective 2025-01-06) per argentina.gob.ar/parquesnacionales, paid at the park gate — Includes all walkway circuits (Upper, Lower, Garganta del Diablo) and the Tren Ecológico de la Selva (reopened 2024-07-13 after the November 2023 flood closure). The park entrance does not include boat tours, jeep rides, or guided tours — these are separate operator products sold in-park by Iguazú Jungle, the sole official concessionaire. Iguazú Jungle 2025 product rates at tickets.iguazujungle.com: Gran Aventura (4x4 jungle + zodiac-boat under-falls) ARS70,000 (~$50 USD); Macuco Safari ARS45,000; Paseo Ecológico (quiet-boat upper-river) ARS35,000.

The scam ecosystem targets tourists at two points. First: Puerto Iguazú downtown travel agencies (Av. Victoria Aguirre + Av. Brasil strips) and hotel concierges sell 'VIP Iguazú Day' packages at $150–$300 USD/person that bundle the $32 park entrance + $50 Gran Aventura + $20 transfer into a $150+ product with a 2–3x markup. Second: Viator / GetYourGuide / third-party booking sites sell 'Iguazú Falls Skip-the-Line' listings at $100–$180 USD claiming priority access — There is NO skip-the-line product at Iguazú; you still queue at the gate for the same ticket. Per, the Brazilian-side Macuco Safari boat is separately criticized as 'borderline a scam' when water levels are low (advertised under-falls run becomes a distant wave), but Argentine-side Gran Aventura is legitimate and recommended.

The defensive playbook for older travelers: (1) buy park entrance direct at the gate or via iguazuargentina.com official site — ARS45,000 foreigner; (2) book Gran Aventura direct at tickets.iguazujungle.com or at the in-park Iguazú Jungle booth (located near the Cataratas Station of the Tren Ecológico) — ARS70,000 adult; do not prebook via Viator/GetYourGuide at $120+ USD markup; (3) for Garganta del Diablo circuit (the main viewing platform over the main cataract): the last Tren Ecológico train departs Garganta at 17:30, plan 3–4 hours to do all three circuits; (4) avoid 'Iguazú Falls Skip-the-Line' and 'Private Guide Priority Access' third-party products — these do not exist; (5) budget one full day for Argentine side + half-day for Brazilian side (Parque Nacional do Iguaçu, separate entrance BRL131 foreigner, requires border crossing).

Red Flags

  • Puerto Iguazú downtown travel agency 'VIP Iguazú Day' package at $150–$300 USD/person — bundles $32 park + $50 Gran Aventura at 2–3x markup
  • Viator / GetYourGuide 'Iguazú Falls Skip-the-Line' listing at $100–$180 USD — No skip-the-line product exists at Iguazú; everyone queues at the gate
  • Hotel concierge 'exclusive guided tour' at $200–$400 USD with vague itinerary — the real product is $32 park entrance + $50 Gran Aventura direct
  • Brazilian-side Macuco Safari boat marketed as 'under-the-falls' when water levels are low per traveler reports (2023) — the Argentine-side Gran Aventura is the legitimate equivalent
  • Travel agency claiming Tren Ecológico de la Selva is 'out of service' or 'Garganta del Diablo closed' — Reopened 2024-07-13; verify at iguazuargentina.com before accepting reroute

How to Avoid

  • Buy park entrance direct at the gate or via iguazuargentina.com — ARS45,000 foreigner (2025), includes all walkway circuits + Tren Ecológico.
  • Book Gran Aventura direct at tickets.iguazujungle.com or at the in-park Iguazú Jungle booth (near Cataratas Station) — ARS70,000 adult; not via third-party resellers.
  • For Garganta del Diablo, board Tren Ecológico at Cataratas Station before 16:00 — Last train departs Garganta at 17:30; plan 3–4 hours total for all three circuits (Upper, Lower, Garganta).
  • For Brazilian side (Foz do Iguaçu) book independent cross-border remise to Parque Nacional do Iguaçu (BRL131 foreigner entrance at the gate) — avoid 'both sides one-day' bundles from Puerto Iguazú agencies quoting $250+ USD.
  • Verify tour operator has current Iguazú Jungle authorization (official concessionaire badge) before paying — only Iguazú Jungle can sell in-park boat/jeep products.
Scam #3
Puerto Iguazú Hotel & In-Park Lodge Booking Fraud
🔶 Medium
📍 Puerto Iguazú downtown hotels + STRs (Av. Victoria Aguirre, Av. Brasil, Tres Fronteras), in-park Gran Meliá Iguazú (formerly Sheraton Iguazú Resort — the only hotel inside Iguazú National Park), Awasi Iguazu / Loi Suites Iguazú / Hotel Saint George premium-segment off-platform fraud targets

Puerto Iguazú's accommodation market has two tiers with very different scam profiles.

Premium in-park: Gran Meliá Iguazú (formerly Sheraton Iguazú Resort, rebranded 2024) is the only hotel inside the national park with direct walkway access to the Cataratas — ~$640 USD/night on Expedia 2026 dates, rates $246–$1 USD,350 depending on view/season. Premium town-side: Loi Suites Iguazú, Awasi Iguazu (ultra-premium lodge), Hotel Saint George. Mid-range town: Hotel Saint George, Jasy Hotel, Orquideas Palace. Budget: multiple hostels + Airbnbs near Tres Fronteras.

Common scam variants: (1) 'corporate rate' emails claiming to be from Gran Meliá Iguazú / Loi Suites offering 40–60% discount via intermediary booking agent — all premium Iguazú properties book only via their official websites or direct phone reservations, never via third-party 'booking agent' emails; (2) Airbnb fake listings near Tres Fronteras demanding 30–50% USD cash deposit 'off-platform' via Western Union or USDT — these listings are often photo-stolen from legitimate properties; (3) WhatsApp / Instagram / Facebook Marketplace 'Puerto Iguazú direct rental' offers from sellers claiming to own cabañas on Av. Tres Fronteras — verify via Google Street View of the address and reverse-image-search the photos; (4) 'Sheraton Iguazú' typo-squat domain emails still circulating months after the Gran Meliá rebrand, directing deposits to fraudulent accounts.

The safer path for older travelers is (1) book all accommodation via Booking.com / Hotels.com / Airbnb platform payment with full platform protection — never off-platform wire or crypto deposits; (2) for premium in-park stay, book Gran Meliá Iguazú direct at melia.com or marriott.com legacy Sheraton page (which auto-redirects to Meliá as of 2024 rebrand) — verify URL matches TripAdvisor/Booking.com listing link; (3) for Awasi Iguazu ultra-premium book direct at awasi.com — verify booking confirmation includes full itinerary + meals + guide; (4) mid-range: Loi Suites / Hotel Saint George via Booking.com platform payment; (5) verify every Airbnb has 50+ reviews + 'verified host' badge + photos that pass Google reverse-image-search; (6) refuse all 'discount' / 'corporate rate' / 'direct wire' offers from anyone claiming to be a 'booking agent' for a premium Iguazú property. Save Comisaría Seccional 1ra Puerto Iguazú (+54 3757 619784).

Red Flags

  • 'Corporate rate' email from 'Gran Meliá Iguazú booking agent' offering 40–60% discount via wire transfer — all premium Iguazú properties book only via official sites
  • Airbnb listing near Tres Fronteras demanding 30–50% USD cash deposit off-platform via Western Union or USDT — Platform fraud-protection voided the moment you pay off-platform
  • WhatsApp / Facebook Marketplace 'Puerto Iguazú cabaña direct' seller requesting wire deposit — Photo-stolen from legitimate property is the #1 Puerto Iguazú STR scam
  • 'Sheraton Iguazú Resort' email address using a typo-squat domain (sheratoniguazu[.net/org/co] instead of marriott.com) — the property rebranded to Gran Meliá Iguazú in 2024
  • Booking confirmation with incomplete address, vague check-in instructions, or no phone number for the property — legitimate Iguazú hotels all publish phone + full address

How to Avoid

  • Book all Puerto Iguazú accommodation via Booking.com / Hotels.com / Airbnb platform payment in full — never off-platform wire or crypto deposits, even for 'verified' listings.
  • For in-park Gran Meliá Iguazú book direct at melia.com — verify URL matches TripAdvisor/Booking.com listing link, and confirm the 'Sheraton' legacy auto-redirects to Meliá as of 2024 rebrand.
  • For Awasi Iguazu ultra-premium lodge book direct at awasi.com with full meal + guide + transfer inclusive booking confirmation — never via 'travel agent' intermediary.
  • Verify every Airbnb has 50+ reviews + 'verified host' / 'Superhost' badge + photos that pass Google reverse-image-search — Tres Fronteras photo-stolen listings are the #1 Puerto Iguazú STR scam.
  • Refuse all 'discount' / 'corporate rate' / 'direct wire' offers from anyone claiming to be a 'booking agent' — Gran Meliá / Loi Suites / Awasi / Saint George sell only direct or via platform.

Scam #4
Triple Frontier (Tres Fronteras) Border-Crossing Scams
⚠️ High
📍 Hito Tres Fronteras viewpoint (Puerto Iguazú), Tancredo Neves International Bridge (Argentina–Brazil), Puente de la Amistad (Argentina–Paraguay via Brazil), Ciudad del Este border-market tour operators, Puerto Iguazú downtown 'triple-frontier day trip' travel agency sellers

The Triple Frontier (Tres Fronteras) where Argentina, Brazil, and Paraguay meet is a signature Puerto Iguazú experience.

The Argentine monument viewpoint is a 5-min taxi ride from downtown and free to visit. But the 'Triple Frontier Day Trip' that crosses all three borders to visit Ciudad del Este's electronics markets is one of Argentina's riskiest tourist experiences per traveler reports (2023). The scam patterns to watch include: (1) Puerto Iguazú travel agencies selling 'Ciudad del Este Shopping Day' at $80–$150 USD/person with drivers who deliberately skip migraciones stamps to save time — this creates $100+ USD overstay exit-fines at your eventual Argentina or Brazil departure when the missing entry stamps are flagged; (2) Ciudad del Este taxi drivers at the border attempting bag grabs during the Puente de la Amistad crossing chaos — traveler reports OP documented a driver who attempted to steal their bags during transfer; (3) 'Triple frontier photo spot' scams where unofficial 'guides' demand $10–$30 USD for access to the Hito monument viewpoint (entrance is free — it's a public municipal park); (4) Paraguayan street money-changers at the border offering 'better rate' that always involves counterfeit Paraguayan Guaraní or short-changed USD.

The safer path for older travelers: (1) visit the Argentine Tres Fronteras monument (Hito Tres Fronteras) for the photo — it's a free municipal viewpoint at the end of Av. Tres Fronteras in Puerto Iguazú, 5-min taxi from downtown, no tour needed; (2) if you want to see Brazilian-side Foz do Iguaçu attractions (Itaipu Dam, Bird Park), book a cross-border remise at ARS55,000 round-trip ($55 USD) with insistence on both migraciones stamps and photograph each stamp; (3) skip Ciudad del Este entirely — per traveler reports (2023) the electronics markets there are high-crime, counterfeit-heavy, and known for bag theft / overcharge / counterfeit currency; the 10-20% discount on electronics vs. Buenos Aires is not worth the security risk for tourists; (4) never exchange money at border street-stands — use Western Union in Puerto Iguazú (Av. Victoria Aguirre) for USD-to-peso or USD-to-real at bank rates; (5) carry photocopies of passport with entry/exit stamps, not the physical passport, across any border casual crossing.

The Ciudad del Este security environment is a serious consideration for older travelers. Always carry your passport in a zipped internal pocket, never visible; travel with a small day-bag only; and do not accept transport offers from anyone approaching you inside the Ciudad del Este side of the bridge. Save Comisaría Seccional 1ra Puerto Iguazú (+54 3757 619784) and the Argentine embassy in Asunción (+595 21 210-683) before crossing.

Red Flags

  • Puerto Iguazú travel agency 'Ciudad del Este Shopping Day' at $80–$150 USD offering 'fast border crossing' — driver skips migraciones stamps, creating $100+ USD overstay exit-fine exposure
  • Ciudad del Este taxi driver approaching you inside terminal or at Puente de la Amistad attempting to handle your bags without asking per traveler reports (2023)
  • 'Triple Frontier photo spot guide' at Hito Tres Fronteras demanding $10–$30 USD for access — entrance is free, it's a public municipal viewpoint
  • Paraguayan street money-changer offering 'better rate' in Guaraní or USD at border — Counterfeit Guaraní / short-changed USD is the default outcome
  • Tour promising to cross 'all three borders in 4 hours' — legitimate triple-border visit requires 6 migraciones checkpoints and at least 6 hours

How to Avoid

  • Visit Argentine Hito Tres Fronteras monument (end of Av. Tres Fronteras, 5-min taxi from downtown Puerto Iguazú) — it's free, well-maintained, and has the classic three-flags photo; no tour required.
  • For Brazilian-side Foz do Iguaçu attractions, book a cross-border remise at ARS55,000 round-trip ($55 USD) — insist driver stops at both Argentine migraciones (exit) and Brazilian migraciones (entry), photograph both stamps.
  • Skip Ciudad del Este entirely — per traveler reports (2023) the electronics markets there are high-crime, counterfeit-heavy, with bag-theft documented; the 10-20% electronics savings vs. Buenos Aires is not worth the security risk.
  • Never exchange money at border street-stands — use Western Union in Puerto Iguazú (Av. Victoria Aguirre) for USD-to-peso or USD-to-real conversion at posted bank rates with zero counterfeit risk.
  • If you must cross into Ciudad del Este, travel with passport in zipped internal pocket (not visible), small day-bag only, and photocopies of stamped passport pages — never accept transport from anyone approaching you inside the bridge.
Scam #5
Puerto Iguazú Downtown Tour-Agency Overcharge & 'Both Sides One Day' Bundles
🟢 Low
📍 Puerto Iguazú downtown travel agency strips (Av. Victoria Aguirre, Av. Brasil, Calle Hipólito Yrigoyen), hotel-concierge tour desks at Jasy Hotel / Hotel Saint George / Orquideas Palace, Viator / GetYourGuide / Civitatis third-party booking listings

Puerto Iguazú downtown has 40+ travel agencies along Av.

Victoria Aguirre and Av. Brasil selling packaged day-trip products to Iguazú Falls (both sides), Itaipu Dam, Bird Park (Parque das Aves Foz do Iguaçu), and Triple Frontier. These agencies operate legitimately but universally mark up direct-book products by 30–100%. The 'VIP Iguazú Full Experience' packages run $250–$500 USD/person for a combination that costs $100–$150 USD when booked individually: (1) ARS45,000 Argentine park entrance = $32 USD; (2) ARS70,000 Iguazú Jungle Gran Aventura = $50 USD; (3) Cross-border remise to Foz do Iguaçu = $15 USD; (4) BRL131 Brazilian park entrance = $27 USD; (5) Optional BRL90 Bird Park entrance = $18 USD. Total DIY: ~$142 USD — Vs. Agency bundle at $350+.

A specific 'Both Sides One Day' bundle is particularly popular and particularly inefficient. The pattern: agency promises 'See Argentine + Brazilian falls + Triple Frontier in 10 hours' at $180–$280 USD/person. In practice: you get 2.5 hours at Argentine side (not enough for Garganta + Upper + Lower circuits with Tren Ecológico), 1.5 hours at Brazilian side (barely enough for the walkway), 30 minutes rushed at Triple Frontier monument, and 2+ hours lost to border-crossing queues + transfers. The Argentine side alone deserves a full 6–8 hour day to do all three circuits; both strongly advise spending one full day on each side.

The safer approach for older travelers: (1) allocate minimum 2 days to Iguazú — Day 1 Argentine side (6–8 hours, all three circuits + optional Gran Aventura), Day 2 Brazilian side (4–5 hours, walkway + Macuco Safari if water is high + optional Itaipu/Bird Park); (2) book park entrances direct at iguazuargentina.com (ARS45,000) and at the Brazilian park gate (BRL131); (3) book Gran Aventura direct at tickets.iguazujungle.com (ARS70,000); (4) arrange cross-border remise via your hotel at ARS55,000 round-trip ($55 USD) with migraciones stops; (5) skip all 'Both Sides One Day' / 'VIP Iguazú Full Experience' agency bundles — you pay 2–3x for a worse experience; (6) if you need a walking guide for the park (recommended for first-time visitors), hire a private Spanish-English guide at the park Centro de Visitantes for ARS40,000–80,000 ($30–$60 USD) for 3 hours — Versus agency 'VIP Private Guide' at $150–$300 for identical service.

Red Flags

  • Av. Victoria Aguirre / Av. Brasil agency 'VIP Iguazú Full Experience' at $250–$500 USD per person — Bundle of $142 USD worth of direct-book products at 2–3x markup
  • 'Both Sides One Day' bundle promising Argentine + Brazilian + Triple Frontier in 10 hours — legitimately requires 2 full days to see properly; rushed version is $180–$280 for a worse experience
  • Hotel-concierge 'VIP Private Guide' at $150–$300 USD — equivalent Spanish-English guide hired at park Centro de Visitantes is ARS40,000–80,000 ($30–$60)
  • Travel agency pushing 'exclusive boat access' or 'private jeep ride' — Iguazú Jungle Gran Aventura IS the official jeep+boat product at ARS70,000 direct, no exclusive alternative exists
  • Tour vendor quoting Argentine park entrance at $60+ USD or Brazilian park entrance at $50+ USD — 2025 official rates are ARS45,000 (~$32) and BRL131 (~$27)

How to Avoid

  • Allocate minimum 2 days — Day 1 Argentine side (6–8 hours for all three circuits + Gran Aventura), Day 2 Brazilian side (4–5 hours for walkway + optional Itaipu/Bird Park).
  • Book park entrances direct at iguazuargentina.com (ARS45,000 foreigner, 2025) and at the Brazilian park gate (BRL131 foreigner) — skip all third-party 'entrance included' bundles.
  • Book Iguazú Jungle Gran Aventura direct at tickets.iguazujungle.com (ARS70,000, 2025) — the only official boat+jeep concessionaire; not via Viator/GetYourGuide at $120+ markup.
  • Arrange cross-border remise via your hotel at ARS55,000 round-trip (~$55 USD) with confirmed migraciones stops at both borders — Not via 'Both Sides Tour' bundle.
  • If you want a walking guide, hire a private Spanish-English guide at the park Centro de Visitantes for ARS40,000–80,000 ($30–$60) for 3 hours — Versus agency 'VIP Private Guide' at $150–$300 for identical service.
Scam #6
Puerto Iguazú Restaurant Bill-Padding & Tourist-Menu Overcharge
🟢 Low
📍 Av. Victoria Aguirre tourist restaurant strip (Puerto Iguazú), Av. Brasil waterfront restaurants, Calle Córdoba pedestrian tourist dining zone, hotel restaurant at Gran Meliá Iguazú / Loi Suites / Hotel Saint George, Selva misiones jungle-lodge restaurants

Puerto Iguazú tourist restaurants use the standard Argentine cubierto and propina sugerida system.

The Av. Victoria Aguirre and Av. Brasil strips aggressively pad bills with unlisted cubiertos, 'bottled water included' surprises, and surprise 'servicio especial' charges beyond the posted menu.

The specific Puerto Iguazú restaurant-scam patterns: (1) unlisted cubierto at $5,000+ ARS/person added to the bill (cubierto must be disclosed on the printed menu before ordering under Argentine consumer-protection law — an unlisted cubierto can be refused); (2) surprise 'aperitivo' or 'panera especial' or 'bottled water included' items appearing on bill for items you didn't order; (3) tourist-menu vs. Locals-menu dual pricing where the tourist-facing menu shows items at 40–60% markup over the Spanish-only menu staff carry separately; (4) 'servicio incluido' (service included) or 'propina obligatoria' (mandatory tip) language added to bills without menu disclosure — these are not legal under Argentine law; (5) wine-list fabrication where the bottle price is invented on the spot rather than from a printed list — Common in Selva misiones jungle-lodge restaurants where 'local bottle' runs $80+ USD.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Policía Federal Argentina station. Call 911 (Police) or 107 (Medical Emergency). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at fiscales.gob.ar.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Buenos Aires is at Avenida Colombia 4300, C1425GMN Buenos Aires. For emergencies: +54 11-5777-4533.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
