The 8 Scams

Scam #1
Scopolamine (Burundanga) Drugging
⚠️ High
📍 Bars and clubs in El Poblado, especially around Parque Lleras

You're having a great night at a bar near Parque Lleras when an attractive local —

often a woman — begins chatting with you and offers to buy you a drink. The drink has been spiked with scopolamine, a drug derived from a local plant that causes total compliance and memory loss. You wake up hours later having handed over all your cash, PIN numbers, and valuables while fully conscious but unable to resist.

Red Flags

  • Overly forward strangers in nightlife areas who immediately offer drinks
  • Drinks brought to you rather than ordered at the bar directly
  • Feeling unusually intoxicated from a small amount of alcohol

How to Avoid

  • Never accept drinks from strangers you just met in Medellín nightlife
  • Order your own drinks at the bar and watch them poured
  • Go out in groups and look out for each other — scopolamine victims appear functional but aren't
Scam #2
Express Kidnapping by Fake Taxi
⚠️ High
📍 Street-hailed taxis throughout Medellín

You hail a taxi on the street late at night and get in without thinking.

The driver takes an unexpected route and suddenly you're locked in the car with additional men who take your phone and wallet. They drive you to ATMs and force withdrawals until your accounts are empty, then leave you somewhere unfamiliar. This 'paseo millonario' (millionaire's walk) is a documented Medellín crime pattern.

Red Flags

  • Taxi hailed from the street rather than called or app-ordered
  • Unexpected detour or locked doors after entering the vehicle
  • Driver seems to be communicating with someone else covertly

How to Avoid

  • Use Uber or InDriver exclusively — never hail a street taxi in Medellín under any circumstances
  • Have the Uber app open before you need it so you're not tempted to wave down a car
  • Ask your hotel to call a trusted taxi company if apps aren't available
Scam #3
Phone Snatching in Public
⚠️ High
📍 Streets, parks, and the Metro system

You're walking through Laureles or El Centro with your phone in your hand looking at Google Maps ...

You're walking through Laureles or El Centro with your phone in your hand looking at Google Maps when someone on a motorbike drives onto the sidewalk, snatches your phone without slowing down much, and is gone before you process what happened. Phone theft by motorbike is extremely common throughout Medellín and locals have had their phones stolen this way multiple times.

Red Flags

  • Walking with phone visibly in hand on busy streets
  • Motorbike riding close to pedestrians on a sidewalk
  • Distraction from one person while another approaches from behind

How to Avoid

  • Keep your phone in your pocket, bag, or pocket when walking on streets
  • Use Google Maps offline so you don't need to look at your phone constantly
  • If you need to use your phone, step inside a café or shop to do so

Scam #4
Fake Drug Deal & Police Setup
⚠️ High
📍 Tourist areas and markets

A man approaches you offering drugs at a friendly price.

Shortly after you interact with him, men claiming to be police officers arrive and threaten to arrest you. They offer to make the whole thing go away for a 'fine' paid directly to them in cash — often $200-500. Both the seller and the 'police' are working together.

Red Flags

  • Drug offer in a tourist area from someone who specifically targets foreigners
  • Police appear almost immediately after any interaction with a drug seller
  • Plain-clothes 'police' who won't show official credentials or go to a station

How to Avoid

  • Do not engage with anyone offering drugs on the street — walk away immediately
  • Real Colombian police wear uniforms — plain-clothes officers showing cash fines are almost always scammers
  • If detained by someone claiming to be police, ask loudly for their badge number and say you'll go to the station
Scam #5
Tinder/App Robbery Setup
⚠️ High
📍 Apartments and unfamiliar locations in the city

You match with an attractive person on Tinder or similar apps who quickly suggests meeting at their apartment.

You arrive, things seem to be going well, and then associates emerge and rob you at knifepoint — or you're given a scopolamine-spiked drink and wake up to find everything gone. This is one of the most reported crime methods against male tourists in Medellín.

Red Flags

  • Match moves very fast to invite you to a private location
  • Location is in an unfamiliar or outer neighborhood
  • Profile photos look too perfect or professional

How to Avoid

  • Always meet Tinder matches in busy public places first
  • Verify the person is real through a video call before meeting
  • Never go to a private apartment for a first meeting with someone you met online in Medellín
Scam #6
Overpriced Tour to Pablo Escobar Sites
🟢 Low
📍 Tourist agencies around El Poblado

You want to visit the sites associated with Pablo Escobar — a major draw for many visitors.

A tout near your hostel offers a tour for $80 per person. You later discover the same tour runs for $20-30 through established operators, and that some unofficial 'guides' have no actual knowledge and are simply charging premium prices for access to locations that are public anyway.

Red Flags

  • Tour price significantly higher than online listings without explaining why
  • Guide can't answer basic questions about Escobar's history
  • Tour booked via a street tout rather than a registered operator

How to Avoid

  • Book Pablo Escobar tours through registered agencies like Paisa Road or Real City Tours
  • Compare prices on GetYourGuide or Viator before booking anything locally
  • Be aware that some Escobar family members run 'authentic' tours — research the ethics before booking
Scam #7
The Gringo Tax and Restaurant Overcharge
🟢 Low
📍 El Poblado restaurants and bars, Parque Lleras area, Comuna 13 tour operators

You sit down at a restaurant in El Poblado and order arepas and a beer.

The bill comes to 85,000 pesos -- roughly three times what a local would pay at the same establishment. Some restaurants in the tourist-heavy Poblado zone operate with unofficial dual pricing, quoting higher prices to foreign visitors. The phenomenon is known locally as the 'gringo tax' and has grown significantly as more foreigners visit Medellin. It extends to taxis, street food, and tour operators, particularly around Parque Lleras and Comuna 13. While not a scam in the traditional sense, the practice is widespread and openly discussed on r/colombia.

Red Flags

  • Menu prices seem significantly higher than what apps or guides suggest
  • Taxi driver quotes a fare without using the meter
  • Tour price for the same activity varies widely between operators
  • Vendor quotes prices in dollars rather than Colombian pesos
  • No menu or price list is visible and prices are given verbally only

How to Avoid

  • Learn basic Spanish phrases for ordering and negotiating -- it reduces the perceived tourist premium
  • Check Google Maps reviews for restaurant prices before sitting down
  • Always insist on the taximeter being used -- legal fares in Medellin are metered
  • Book tours through established platforms like Viator or GetYourGuide for transparent pricing
  • Eat where locals eat -- walk a few blocks from Parque Lleras for dramatically better value
Scam #8
The Stranger Emergency Cash Scam
🔶 Medium
📍 El Poblado parks, Parque Lleras area, hostels in Manila area

You are chatting with someone at a cafe in El Poblado who seems friendly and genuine.

After a few conversations over a couple of days, they tell you about a family emergency — a sick parent, a hospital bill, an eviction — and ask you to withdraw money for them because their own bank account is frozen. A Reddit user on r/travel confirmed this as a well-known Medellín pattern: 'When you barely know someone in Colombia and after a few days they ask you for money, it is a 100 percent scam.' Some victims have become homeless in Medellín after being gradually drained of their savings by people they believed were friends or romantic partners.

Red Flags

  • A new acquaintance asks for money within the first few days of knowing you
  • The emergency is always urgent and specific — hospital bills, rent, or a family crisis
  • They ask you to withdraw cash from an ATM rather than transferring digitally
  • The requested amounts escalate over time — starting small and growing
  • They become emotional or manipulative when you hesitate

How to Avoid

  • Never give money to anyone you have known for less than a very long time
  • If someone tells you about a family emergency, offer sympathy but not cash
  • Be especially cautious with romantic interests who discuss financial problems early
  • Connect with the expat community through verified groups for safety advice
  • Remember that Colombian banks and emergency services exist for genuine emergencies

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Colombian National Police (Policía Nacional) station. Call 123 (Emergency) or 112. Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at policia.gov.co.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Bogotá is at Calle 24 Bis No. 48-50, Bogotá. For emergencies: +57 1-275-2000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
