The 6 Scams

Scam #1
Hua Lamphong Fake-SRT Minibus Routing
⚠️ High
📍 Bangkok Hua Lamphong (Krung Thep Aphiwat Central) ticket-hall approach, Bang Sue Grand Station concourse, Khao San Road kerbside taxi ranks, Chatuchak BTS exits to bus terminals

Tourists trying to reach Ayutthaya by the official ฿20 SRT 3rd-class train from Bangkok are intercepted before the ticket window and rerouted onto private minibuses that bill ฿800 to ฿1,500 for the same trip. The setup begins as soon as you walk toward the platform at Hua Lamphong or the new Krung Thep Aphiwat (Bang Sue Grand) terminus. Four or five men in matching polo shirts, lanyards, and clip-on ID cards step into your path. They carry printed SRT-style timetables and a clipboard, and they speak fluent enough English to deflect the first round of questions.

The pivot lands at the diagram. The 'agent' points to a number well above your fare, says the morning train is full or cancelled, and offers a 'VIP minibus package' to Ayutthaya that conveniently includes 'temple admission' for ฿800 to ฿1,500 per person. The real SRT 3rd-class fare to Ayutthaya is ฿20, the rapid is ฿35, and the express runs ฿65 to ฿245 depending on class. Refusing draws a second man in uniform who insists the official counter is 'closed for renovations' and walks you to a side desk that takes only cash.

The trick exploits two gaps: most foreign tourists cannot tell a real SRT lanyard from a printed one, and the 2023 closure of long-haul Hua Lamphong service left many travelers confused about which station handles which trains. Reddit threads document the routing playing daily, with travelers noting four or five fake officials working in shifts. **Walk past anyone in a lanyard and buy your ticket only at the glass-fronted SRT counter inside the main hall, ignoring 'agents' who approach you on the platform side.**

Red Flags

  • Person in polo shirt and lanyard intercepts you before you reach the ticket window
  • Printed timetable on a clipboard rather than the digital screens above the counter
  • Claim that the 3rd-class train is full, cancelled, or 'no longer running' to Ayutthaya
  • Quote of ฿800 to ฿1,500 per person for transport that should cost ฿20 by SRT 3rd class
  • Cash-only side desk pitched as the 'VIP' or 'official' alternative to the counter

How to Avoid

  • BUY your Ayutthaya ticket only at the glass-fronted SRT counter inside the main station hall.
  • VERIFY the SRT 3rd-class fare to Ayutthaya is ฿20 and the rapid is ฿35 before paying any 'agent'.
  • IGNORE anyone in a polo shirt with a clip-on lanyard who approaches you on the platform side.
  • REFUSE any 'package' that bundles transport with temple admission or hotel transfers.
  • CALL the Tourist Police on 1155 from the station if a 'uniformed' agent will not let you reach the counter.
Scam #2
Ayutthaya Station Mafia Tuk-Tuk Per-Person Trap
⚠️ High
📍 Ayutthaya Railway Station kerbside, Wat Mahathat parking apron, Phra Nakhon Si Ayutthaya bus terminal, river-pier drop-offs near Hua Raw Market

A line of tuk-tuks waits across the road from Ayutthaya Railway Station, and the drivers there quote ฿800 per person for a half-day temple loop that should cost ฿200 to ฿300 per hour for the entire vehicle. The setup looks like organized transport. Six or seven painted tuk-tuks queue under a sign reading 'Ayutthaya Tour'. A lead driver greets every train, hands out laminated maps, and shepherds groups toward the front of the line so no one shops the price. The maps list the same five temples in the same order at every stand.

The pivot is the per-person quote. The driver writes ฿800 on the laminated map and gestures to two seats, implying ฿1,600 for a couple. The fair Ayutthaya rate, posted by the city's own tourism office and quoted on every Reddit thread for the route, is ฿200 to ฿300 per hour for the whole tuk-tuk regardless of passenger count. Four hours visiting Wat Mahathat, Wat Phra Si Sanphet, Wat Chaiwatthanaram, Wat Ratchaburana and Wat Yai Chai Mongkhon should land at ฿800 to ฿1,200 total for a couple or a family of four.

The trick is the queue. By design, the front driver hands you to the next driver in rotation and there is no competing quote within sight of the platform. Refusing draws shrugs, a wave at the queue behind, and a comment that 'all prices are the same'. They are not. **Walk five minutes south to the main road, flag a circulating tuk-tuk that has not been pre-staged for arrivals, and confirm a per-vehicle hourly rate before you climb in.**

Red Flags

  • Tuk-tuk queue marked 'Ayutthaya Tour' or 'Temple Tour' with a lead driver greeting every train
  • Laminated price card showing per-person quotes rather than per-vehicle hourly rates
  • Quote of ฿800 per person for a half-day loop instead of ฿200 to ฿300 per hour for the whole vehicle
  • Driver insists on starting the loop at a 'jewelry center' or 'silk weaving demonstration'
  • Refusal to write the agreed price, time, and temple list on a piece of paper before departure

How to Avoid

  • WALK past the rank tuk-tuks at Ayutthaya Railway Station and flag one on the main road five minutes south.
  • AGREE a per-vehicle hourly rate of ฿200 to ฿300 in writing before you climb into any tuk-tuk.
  • CONFIRM the temple list — Wat Mahathat, Wat Phra Si Sanphet, Wat Chaiwatthanaram — on paper with the driver.
  • RENT a bicycle for ฿50 a day at any guesthouse near U-Thong Road if you prefer to skip tuk-tuks entirely.
  • REPORT a refusing driver to the Tourist Police on 1155 with the tuk-tuk's license-plate photographed.
Scam #3
Temple Closed Detour to Commission Shop
⚠️ High
📍 Wat Mahathat entrance gate, Wat Phra Si Sanphet path, Wat Chaiwatthanaram river-side approach, Wat Yai Chai Mongkhon car park, Ayutthaya Historical Park access roads

A friendly local at the gate of Wat Mahathat tells you the temple is closed until 14:00 for a Buddhist ceremony, then offers a tuk-tuk to 'a beautiful temple on the way' that ends at a silk shop or gem store with a 30-minute pressure pitch. The setup is borrowed wholesale from the Bangkok Grand Palace closure scam and grafted onto Ayutthaya's ruin gates. The man wears a polo shirt, holds a printed laminated card, and speaks unusually careful English. A tuk-tuk driver with engine running waits two metres behind him.

The pivot is the redirect. The temple is not closed. Wat Mahathat opens 08:00 to 18:30 daily, charges a ฿50 admission, and only closes for posted national holidays. The tuk-tuk diversion drops you at a 'royal weaving center', a 'lapidary' or a 'tailor cooperative' along the route, where staff lock the door at the back of the showroom and lean on the sale. Drivers earn 30 to 40 percent commission on every garment, gemstone or silk runner sold; the ฿200 'temple tour' is loss-leader bait.

The trick exploits two reflexes: tourists trust strangers at official-looking sites, and a closed gate seems like a routine fact. It is not. The official Ayutthaya Historical Park hours are posted at every main entrance and on fad.go.th, and the only legitimate temporary closures are listed there. **Walk past anyone telling you a temple is closed, pay your ฿50 directly at the gate kiosk, and decline any tuk-tuk that materialises with the 'helpful' bystander.**

Red Flags

  • Stranger at the gate insisting a major temple is closed for a 'ceremony' or 'restoration' mid-morning
  • Tuk-tuk waiting two metres away with engine running before you say yes
  • Itinerary detour that lands at a 'silk weaving center', 'lapidary' or 'tailor cooperative'
  • Polo-shirted intermediary with a laminated map and unusually fluent English
  • Sales staff at the detour shop lock the showroom door once you are inside

How to Avoid

  • VERIFY temple opening hours on fad.go.th or at the official Tourist Information sign before believing anyone at the gate.
  • PAY the ฿50 admission directly at the temple kiosk and walk past anyone offering a 'better' temple.
  • REFUSE any side-stop at a silk center, lapidary, gem shop or tailor offered as part of a temple tour.
  • WRITE the temple list and total time on paper with your driver so unscheduled stops are visibly off-plan.
  • CALL the Tourist Police on 1155 if you are pressured at a commission shop and ask the driver to leave.

Scam #4
Floating Market Combo Van Bait-and-Switch
🟢 Low
📍 Khao San Road tour-booth strip, Sukhumvit Soi 4 hostel pickups, Hua Lamphong forecourt vans, Chatuchak Park rendezvous, Damnoen Saduak floating-market drop-off lot

A Khao San Road booth sells a ฿1,500 'Ayutthaya plus floating market plus Maeklong railway market' combo day-tour, and every leg is shorter and more commercial than the leaflet suggests.

The setup is the brochure. Glossy photos show a busy 06:30 floating market at Damnoen Saduak, then four temples in Ayutthaya, then a sunset at Maeklong. The booth agent prints an itinerary on a thermal receipt and takes payment in cash, and a 12-seat van picks you up the next morning 40 minutes late.

The pivot lands when the van pulls into an orchid farm, a cobra-show car park and a silk demonstration before reaching Damnoen Saduak around 10:30. By then the local fishermen are gone and only the staged tourist stalls remain, with noodles at ฿150 a bowl that cost ฿40 in central Bangkok. Ayutthaya gets a 90-minute window across two temples, with Wat Chaiwatthanaram skipped because 'the road is busy'. Maeklong is replaced with a souvenir shop. Refunds are refused on the basis of 'flexible itinerary' wording on the receipt.

The trick is geography. Damnoen Saduak is two hours west of Bangkok and Ayutthaya is 90 minutes north, so the operator schedules commission stops to cover the loss-leader price and trims the headline destinations to whatever time remains. **Book Ayutthaya as a single dedicated day via the SRT train, and book any floating market separately with a verified Klook or GetYourGuide operator that publishes named stops with arrival times.**

Red Flags

  • Combo brochure that bundles Ayutthaya, Damnoen Saduak and Maeklong into one day
  • Booth pickup time printed in handwriting on a thermal receipt with no operator license number
  • Van schedule includes orchid farm, cobra show or silk demonstration as 'cultural stops'
  • Damnoen Saduak arrival listed at 10:00 or later when the local fishermen finish at 09:00
  • Ayutthaya leg lists 'temples' rather than naming Wat Mahathat or Wat Chaiwatthanaram by name

How to Avoid

  • BOOK Ayutthaya as a dedicated day via the ฿20 SRT 3rd-class train from Hua Lamphong.
  • BOOK Damnoen Saduak separately on Klook or GetYourGuide with a 06:30 arrival written into the confirmation.
  • DECLINE any combo tour that lists 'orchid farm', 'cobra show' or 'silk demonstration' on the itinerary.
  • VERIFY the operator's TAT license number on tourismthailand.org before paying any Khao San booth.
  • REFUSE to pay cash up front — pay by card so a chargeback is possible if the itinerary is gutted.
Scam #5
Elephant Ride First-Price Upsell at Ayutthaya Camps
🟢 Low
📍 Ayutthaya Elephant Palace and Royal Kraal off Pa Thon Road, Wat Phra Si Sanphet kerbside elephant stops, U-Thong Road photo-elephant pull-in, ruin-area mahout staging points

An Ayutthaya mahout offers a 15-minute elephant ride for ฿400, and the price doubles or triples once the elephant has walked you out of sight of the booth.

The setup is the price board at the camp gate. Painted figures show ฿400 for a short loop, ฿800 for a longer route past the ruins, and ฿1,000 for an 'all-day ride'. A handler in a sash leads the elephant into a low platform, helps you climb the howdah and waves the booth staff off as the elephant ambles toward Wat Phra Si Sanphet.

The pivot lands at the halfway point. The mahout stops the elephant under a tree, claims the loop is longer than the booth said, and asks for an extra ฿600 'for the time'. Climbing down means standing in the mud while the elephant blocks the path back. A second handler arrives on a moped to escort the conversation. The booth refuses to honour the original quote on the basis that the route was 'extended at the customer's request', a phrase that appears on the printed receipt only after payment.

The trick has two layers. The first is the upsell, which is purely financial. The second is the welfare problem flagged by every major Thai-tourism subreddit through 2025: the bullhook training, the chained overnight enclosures, and the 'sanctuary' wording used by ride camps that are not sanctuaries at all. **Skip elephant interactions in Ayutthaya entirely, rent a ฿50-a-day bicycle, and save genuine elephant-welfare visits for accredited northern operators such as Elephant Nature Park.**

Red Flags

  • Painted price board offering ฿400, ฿800 and ฿1,000 ride tiers with no printed terms
  • Mahout asking for 'extra time' money once the ride is past the booth's line of sight
  • 'Sanctuary' or 'conservation' wording used at a camp that sells paid rides on howdahs
  • Elephant kept on a short chain at night or visible bullhook in the mahout's belt
  • Receipt that surfaces a 'route extension' clause only after the rider has dismounted

How to Avoid

  • DECLINE every elephant ride offer in Ayutthaya — the welfare and overcharge risks are universally flagged.
  • RENT a bicycle for ฿50 a day at a guesthouse on U-Thong Road and tour the ruins on two wheels.
  • BOOK ethical elephant visits only at accredited northern sanctuaries such as Elephant Nature Park near Chiang Mai.
  • REFUSE any verbal 'extension' offer mid-ride and dismount at the next safe pull-in if pressed.
  • REPORT a coercive mahout to the Tourist Police on 1155 with the camp name and a photograph of the price board.
Scam #6
Fake Tourist Information Centre Package Sale
🟢 Low
📍 Khao San Road shopfronts, Hua Lamphong forecourt approach, Silom Soi 4 walkup desks, Sukhumvit BTS exits near Asoke, Ayutthaya railway-station car-park kiosks

Storefronts branded 'Tourist Information Center' or 'TIC Bangkok' on Khao San Road and near Hua Lamphong sell ฿3,500 Ayutthaya 'packages' for trips that should cost ฿200 to ฿300 by SRT and tuk-tuk.

The setup is the signage. The shopfront uses the colors and font of Thailand's Tourism Authority (TAT), the wall behind the desk holds a printed map of Ayutthaya, and the man inside wears a polo and a name badge. A second 'consultant' on Khao San Road steers walk-ups to the door with a clipboard.

The pivot is the package. The consultant prints a glossy itinerary that bundles transfers, temple admission and a 'tour leader' for ฿3,500 per person, then asks for cash before the wifi-printed receipt has cooled. The real Tourism Authority of Thailand sells nothing — TAT offices answer questions and hand out brochures, full stop. Khao San storefronts and station-car-park desks are private commission shops that wear the colors of the agency but operate on travel-agent margins of 30 to 50 percent.

The trick exploits brand confusion. Most arriving travelers cannot tell a real TAT branch from a private storefront, and the polo-and-badge cosplay closes the gap. The genuine TAT call-center is 1672, and the verified Ayutthaya provincial office is at 108/22 Moo 4, Tambon Pratu Chai. **Treat any 'Tourist Information' shopfront on Khao San Road or in a railway forecourt as a private travel agent, and book Ayutthaya transport directly at the SRT counter for ฿20.**

Red Flags

  • Storefront branded 'Tourist Information Center' on Khao San Road or beside a station forecourt
  • Consultant in polo shirt and badge selling fixed-price packages rather than handing out free brochures
  • Itinerary priced at ฿2,500 to ฿3,500 for transport and admission that should cost ฿200 to ฿300
  • Cash-only payment pressure with a 'today only' or 'last seat' line on the pitch
  • Package bundles a 'silk demonstration' or 'gem cooperative' alongside the temple stops

How to Avoid

  • CALL the genuine TAT call center on 1672 to verify any 'Tourist Information' shopfront before paying.
  • BOOK Ayutthaya as DIY: ฿20 SRT 3rd-class train plus ฿200 to ฿300 per hour for a tuk-tuk on arrival.
  • DECLINE any package whose itinerary lists silk centers, gem cooperatives or tailor visits.
  • PAY by card always — chargeback leverage is the only practical refund mechanism for a fake-TIC sale.
  • VISIT the verified Ayutthaya TAT office at 108/22 Moo 4, Tambon Pratu Chai for actual neutral advice.

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Tourist Police station. Call 1155 (Tourist Police) or 191 (General Police). Get an official crime report — you'll need this for insurance claims. You can also report online at touristpolice.go.th.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block any suspicious transactions before the thieves use your details.

🛂 Lost Passport?

Contact your nearest embassy or consulate. The US Embassy in Bangkok is at 95 Wireless Road, Pathumwan, Bangkok 10330. For emergencies: +66 2-205-4000.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
