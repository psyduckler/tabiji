6 Tourist Scams in Yokohama

Documented from Kanagawa Police, Asahi Shimbun, Noge Tourism Association warnings, and extensive traveler reviews.

Key Takeaways

  • The Fukutomicho drug-and-charge scam is the most dangerous — Kanagawa Prefectural Police documented ¥147.39M stolen from 249 victims in just the first 11 months of 2023, with one victim hit for ¥2.7M in a single night
  • Yokohama Chinatown's all-you-can-eat trap hides a 6-item order limit and a leftover surcharge (¥15,000 in one documented case) that are announced only after you sit down — never on the street signage
  • The Noge Tourism Association maintains its own public blacklist of rip-off girls bars and snacks on noge-kankou.org and do-gp.net — check it before visiting Komachi-dori or Isezaki-cho adjacent streets
  • Kanagawa's updated Nuisance Prevention Ordinance (effective May 1, 2025) makes touting arrestable regardless of gender, expands coverage to izakaya and karaoke, and empowers the Public Safety Commission to suspend offending venues

⚡ Quick Safety Tips

  • Never accept drinks from strangers in Fukutomicho — Asahi Shimbun documented shops serving alcohol over 90 proof (45%+ ABV) plus drink-spiking to accelerate blackouts
  • Before sitting at any Chinatown all-you-can-eat, ask explicitly about order-limits and the leftover surcharge — Japan's 景表法 price-display law requires written rules
  • Check noge-kankou.org for the Noge Tourism Association's own blacklisted venues before entering any upstairs girls bar or snack
  • Avoid Yokohama Station West Exit touts around Minami-Saiwai Bridge and Don Quijote — never hand over a ¥500–1,000 "deposit" (前金) before seeing the venue
  • At Haneda Airport, use only the licensed taxi queue (green license plates) — or skip taxis entirely and take the Keikyu Airport Line (¥500) to Yokohama Station

The 6 Scams

Scam #1
The Fukutomicho Drug-and-Charge Blackout Scam
⚠️ High
📍 Fukutomicho / Isezakicho, Naka-ku, Yokohama

A tout approaches you near Kannai or Isezakicho at night: "Onii-san, where you wanna go? Girls bar, very cheap." You follow him into a snack bar in Fukutomicho. The first drink tastes strong. You wake up hours later with no memory and ¥600,000+ in card charges.

This is not theoretical: Kanagawa Prefectural Police documented 249 victims reporting exactly this pattern to Isezaki Police in the first 11 months of 2023, with total losses of ¥147.39 million — nearly double 2022's 148 victims / ¥75 million. One victim was charged ¥2.7 million in a single night. A December 2023 Asahi Shimbun investigation reported some shops using alcohol above 90 proof (over 45% ABV) to accelerate blackouts, with staff members then "helping" victims withdraw cash at convenience-store ATMs. A Yokohama lawyer has published a case log of two Fukutomicho venues — identified only as "Pl●mer●a" and "e●" — where a single drugged client had ¥283,800 in card charges across both venues plus another ¥160,000 in cash withdrawn; the firm recovered a 40% cash settlement from one and a full chargeback from the other. Tokyo Shimbun documented a Kyushu-based man in his 40s losing ¥120,000 + ¥300,000 ATM + ¥270,000 across a single blackout incident. Yomiuri's 2022 tally showed 353 cumulative complaints and over ¥100M in losses since 2019 — and figures have rebounded sharply as post-COVID tourism recovers.

Red Flags

  • Tout approaches you near Kannai or Isezakicho with "where you wanna go" (お兄さん、どこ行きたいか)
  • Bar is in Fukutomicho, Wakaba-cho, or a Fukutomicho-adjacent side street — upstairs, no street-facing menu
  • First drink tastes unusually strong or bitter for what was ordered
  • A staff member insists on accompanying you to a convenience-store ATM
  • You wake up outside or at your hotel with no memory of signing for anything
  • Next morning you find multiple card charges from venue names you don't recognize

How to Avoid

  • Treat Fukutomicho at night as a no-go zone — a long-time Yokohama resident on r/Yokohama calls it "the most dangerous area of Yokohama after dark"
  • Never follow a tout (kyakuhiki) — it's illegal under the revised Kanagawa Nuisance Prevention Ordinance (effective May 1, 2025), and any bar using them is already breaking the law
  • Don't accept any drink you didn't watch poured; refuse over-the-top hospitality from strangers
  • Stay on main Noge streets where venues are open to the road — police-recommended safe geometry
  • If you black out and find unauthorized charges, contact Isezaki Police Station (伊勢佐木署) — their dedicated team has this pattern catalogued
  • Set a low daily ATM withdrawal limit and a low per-transaction card cap before you travel
Scam #2
The Chinatown All-You-Can-Eat 6-Order Limit & Leftover Surcharge
🔶 Medium
📍 Yokohama Chinatown (Yamashita-cho, Naka-ku)

A woman in a cheongsam waves you in — ¥2,500 for 90 minutes all-you-can-eat. You sit down and the rules appear: only 6 items at a time, the next 6 come after the first are finished, and uneaten food triggers a ¥15,000 leftover surcharge never printed anywhere.

A September 2025 Yahoo! 知恵袋 post documents exactly this pattern: "Leftover surcharge existed but was written nowhere." Google Maps 1-star reviews of specific shops show the same hidden-rule pattern repeatedly — 横浜酒家 ("one-dish-per-person rule, not written anywhere"), 龍海飯店 ("premium all-you-can-eat — everything cold and tasteless, family lost a lot of money"), and 品珍閣 ("male staff told me what I ordered was not unlimited and rushed me out"). A viral TripAdvisor review of Kafuku Hanten (嘉福飯店) titled "ALL YOU CAN EAT - SCAM!!!!" described a family of three wasting three hours being told they could only order six items at a time. At 老北京 (ROUPEKIN), reviewers documented crab advertised as all-you-can-eat that "halfway through they said wasn't all-you-can-eat" plus a ¥500 solo-diner surcharge and "freeze-thaw-repeated jerky-state" crab. Local investigation site Hamarepo documents this as the business model of 50 of Chinatown's 200+ restaurants — and specifically warns: "Never enter a shop with plainclothes touts soliciting outside" since Yokohama Chinatown Development Association members are prohibited from soliciting beyond their property line.

Red Flags

  • Tout outside in street clothes (not uniform) shouting "all you can eat ¥1,980!" — Association members may not solicit outside their property line
  • No posted six-item-limit rule or leftover surcharge on the menu or door
  • Photos on the street display show tapas-size plates but you get full single servings
  • Staff rush you to leave ("頼んだのは無制限ではない" — "what you ordered isn't unlimited") even with seats available
  • Peking duck is only skin, crab is "freeze-thaw repeated until it's jerky", or a ¥500 solo-diner surcharge appears at bill time

How to Avoid

  • Only eat at shops with the 横浜中華街発展会協同組合 certified plaque — enforcement has been active since the early 2020s
  • Stick to 4.3+ rated shops with 500+ Japanese-language reviews; complaint clusters sit consistently at 3.6–3.8 stars
  • If you see a tout in civilian clothes, walk past — member shops do not recruit this way
  • Read the leftover-surcharge rule before sitting; if it isn't written, ask to see it in writing — no written rule, no surcharge
  • Photograph the menu and posted rules at the door before ordering; Japan's 景表法 law requires clear price display
  • For buffet-style, look for 食べ残し無料 (no leftover surcharge) or shops that serve family-style rather than order-style
Scam #3
The Yokohama Station West Exit Tout-Izakaya Trap
🔶 Medium
📍 Yokohama Station West Exit (Minami-Saiwai-bashi, Nishi-ku) — especially around Bibre and Don Quijote

You exit Yokohama Station's West Exit. A young man with a clipboard steps into your path: "Cheap place! ¥1,300 all-you-can-drink, 2 hours!" You hand over a ¥1,000 "deposit," and discover at the end that surcharges and mandatory minimums double the advertised price.

A Hamarepo undercover investigation walked through this scam end to end at an unnamed West Exit izakaya: a ¥1,000 upfront "deposit," a 2-hour ¥1,300 nomihoudai stacked with a mandatory 2-dish-per-person minimum, ¥1 worth of bean sprouts served as the otoshi, a 10% mystery service charge, filthy bathrooms, and all-frozen mains. Kanaloco reported a major sweep of West Exit touts with a 25-year-old part-time tout among those arrested by the Kanagawa Prefectural Police Life Safety Division / Tobe Police. Sankei described the industry as "free-tout" operators registered as subcontractors to a shell company, specifically structured to evade enforcement. A November 2025 tvk News report documented the arrest of a 43-year-old man for Yokohama West Exit street touting with ongoing bottakuri complaints in the area; Jisin.jp previously reported a single West-Exit-area tout earning ¥1 million per month. The Kanagawa Nuisance Prevention Ordinance was specifically broadened in May 2025 to cover izakaya touting around Yokohama Station, with the Public Safety Commission empowered to issue business-suspension orders.

Red Flags

  • Tout with clipboard/menu stops you between Minami-Saiwai Bridge, Don Quijote, and the West Exit station plaza
  • Quotes a sub-¥1,500 all-you-can-drink price that sounds too cheap for Yokohama
  • Asks for an upfront "deposit" (前金) of ¥500–1,000 before even walking you to the venue
  • Venue is a generic izakaya several minutes away, tucked in an older building, not on Tabelog
  • Menu adds an unadvertised 10% service charge, a ¥500/person otoshi, or a 2-dish-per-person minimum only after you're seated

How to Avoid

  • Pre-book on Tabelog or Google Maps before leaving your hotel — tout venues are almost never in the top results
  • Exit Yokohama Station via the East Exit or the Minatomirai walkway if possible — touts concentrate around Minami-Saiwai Bridge
  • Never hand cash to a tout (前金) even if they swear it's a "deposit"
  • Read the menu and explicitly ask about otoshi (お通し), service charge, and minimum-order rules before sitting
  • If pressured, call 110 and cite the Kanagawa Nuisance Prevention Ordinance (神奈川県迷惑行為防止条例) — touting is now arrestable for both the tout and the employing venue
Scam #4
The Noge Girls Bar / Komachi-dori Hostess Overcharge
⚠️ High
📍 Noge yokocho (Sakuragicho side, Naka-ku) — especially Komachi-dori (小町通り)

Noge is the hip, narrow-alley yokocho everyone says to visit — and mostly it's safe. But a handful of small girls bars on and around Komachi-dori run bottakuri: young women hired to stand on the street, dim upstairs bars, hostess charges never quoted, and bills of ¥100,000+ for 90 minutes.

The Noge Tourism Association (野毛観光振興協会) has so much trouble with this that it keeps a live warning page on its own website — a June 2018 entry names one specific girls bar on Komachi-dori using aggressive street-girl solicitation as "what we believe is bottakuri." Its broader map notes that Isezaki-cho's Fukutomi-cho-adjacent stretch holds "several" known bottakuri girls bars and snacks. The resident-run tourism site noge-kankou.org explicitly states: "As of March 2016, several bottakuri venues operate near Isezaki-cho shoten-gai (Fukutomi-cho) — girls bars and snacks that are actually small cabaret clubs." Bakusai forum threads name individual venues and hostesses ("hostess Saaya at Pink Panther in Noge — beyond bottakuri, a dangerous store"). Kanaloco reported a dedicated Noge awareness campaign by Kanagawa Police and Yokohama City on April 23, 2025, ahead of the May 1 ordinance expansion. NTV reported police swept Yokohama, Kawasaki, and Atsugi entertainment districts, arresting 8 girls-bar staff for illegal touting of plainclothes officers. Both the UK FCDO and US State Department warn about this broader pattern, noting British travelers have been arrested over excessive-bill disputes and U.S. citizens have been threatened with weapons at such venues.

Red Flags

  • Young woman standing outside a narrow alley venue on Komachi-dori (野毛小町通り) waving a flier — post–May 2025 this is illegal
  • Bar on an upper floor, no street-level window, no printed menu at the entrance
  • Hostess "sits with you" without explaining the per-hour charge
  • Champagne or "special" drinks are offered but unlisted on any price sheet
  • Bill is presented verbally, not itemized; staff refuse to accept card at the listed total
  • Venue is not on the Noge Tourism Association's do-gp.net member map

How to Avoid

  • Stick to Noge izakaya with 4.5+ Google ratings and 500+ reviews — bottakuri venues cluster at 3.6–3.8 stars with suspiciously few recent reviews
  • Only enter bars with printed menus visible from the street
  • Check the Noge Tourism Association map (野毛くいだおれマップ, noge-kankou.org) — the association itself flags problem streets
  • Refuse any hostess joining your table unless the per-hour charge is printed
  • Ask for the bill itemized in writing before paying
  • If pressured, call 110 — under the post-May-2025 ordinance, both the tout and the employing venue are committing an offense
Scam #5
The Chinatown Sweet Chestnut Sample-Then-Force-Sell
🟢 Low
📍 Yokohama Chinatown main street (中華街大通り) and side alleys, Naka-ku

You're walking down Chinatown's main street when a woman thrusts a free sample of amaguri (sweet chestnuts) at you. You take one — she instantly starts bagging up a kilogram. When you try to leave, the price changes. Refusing now feels socially impossible, which is the entire design.

A 2014 Yahoo! 知恵袋 thread documented the classic tactic: "She called out 千円の200円でいいよ — the ¥1,000 bag for ¥200" to hook you, but once you accept the sample she packs the full ¥1,000 bag. A viral Togetter collection of Yokohama locals' warnings says: "If you take the sample, she'll immediately say オマケしとくからね (I'll throw in a bonus) and start scooping more in." The City of Yokohama and Kanagawa Prefectural Police have run joint patrols against this since at least 2014 (Sankei, Nikkei, Kanaloco coverage). A 2024 Sirabee investigation documented the problem has sharply declined — but complaints still arrive, dishonest vendors' stock is often over 50% insect-damaged, and sample chestnuts are handled with bare hands. The Hamarepo historical record includes aggressive sales tactics "blocking wheelchairs, grabbing arms, pushing goods on children and elderly." The Yokohama Chinatown Development Association now runs a recommended-vendor certification system specifically to counter this pattern.

Red Flags

  • Woman waves a free chestnut sample at you on the main street — Association-member stalls don't recruit this way
  • Quoted bait price (e.g. "¥200" or "50% off today") shifts once you've accepted the sample
  • Stall has no visible certification plaque from the Yokohama Chinatown Development Association
  • Vendor handles chestnuts with bare hands, no gloves (a documented 2024 complaint pattern)
  • Chestnuts turn out to be over 50% pest-damaged when opened at home — a documented Sirabee figure

How to Avoid

  • Do not accept the sample. Once you take it, the script begins — refusal afterward feels socially impossible, which is the entire design
  • Only buy from stalls displaying the Yokohama Chinatown Development Association certification
  • Confirm the per-bag price out loud in Japanese before the seller bags anything ("いくらですか？")
  • If you're overcharged, photograph the stall, keep the receipt, and report to the Yokohama City Consumer Affairs Center or Kagacho Police (加賀町警察署)
  • Prefer packaged chestnuts from established confectioneries (e.g. 聘珍樓) rather than street stalls
Scam #6
The Yokohama Station / Haneda Airport Taxi Meter Games
🟢 Low
📍 Yokohama Station West Exit taxi rank; Haneda Airport arrivals to Yokohama

A cabbie at the Yokohama Station West Exit quotes "¥1,000 off" — then adds mystery surcharges that push the meter ¥2,000 over the route fare. At Haneda, independent taxis toggle highway-mode repeatedly on the Wangan Expressway, turning a ¥7,000 route into ¥8,000+.

A long-running Yahoo! 知恵袋 case documents a Haneda→Yokohama Yamashita Park trip charged ¥8,000 when the reasonable meter fare was around ¥7,000 (~22 km), with the driver "constantly fiddling with the meter." A professional driver's reply confirmed the behavior was unprofessional and typical of some independent taxi operators. A Toyo Keizai feature titled "「ぼったくりタクシー天国」に今起きている変化" (the Yokohama "bottakuri taxi paradise" is finally changing) documented that Yokohama cabbies averaging over ¥10 million/year revenue is a historical sign that detour-and-route-inflation was profitable. Another Yahoo! 知恵袋 case records a Yokohama Station rider whose driver "offered ¥1,000 off" and then charged well above a reasonable fare. Post-pandemic and post-Uber the problem is smaller but still reported — especially against jet-lagged tourists arriving at Haneda's Yokohama routes. The Yokohama Individual Taxi Cooperative publishes an official rate card (initial ¥4,940 for standard car, ¥4,860 for large) — anything far above that should be reported.

Red Flags

  • Driver at Yokohama West Exit offers a "discount" and then adds unexplained charges
  • Driver toggles meter mode repeatedly during the ride so that the fare keeps reclimbing
  • Driver insists on cash only — legitimate Yokohama cabs take card via JPN TAXI and onboard terminals
  • Taxi lacks the green license plate (営業ナンバー) required for legal passenger service in Japan
  • Route taken is not what Google Maps shows — detours add minutes and meter clicks

How to Avoid

  • Use the official taxi queue only; refuse drivers who approach you personally
  • Pre-check the fare on NAVITIME or the GO app before entering the cab so you know the expected meter
  • Ride with the GO or Uber apps where the fare is computed upfront — much harder to manipulate
  • Request an itemized receipt (領収書); Japan law requires drivers to issue one on demand
  • Haneda-to-Yokohama: take the Keikyu Airport Line (¥500) or Limousine Bus (¥650) instead — taxi is ¥7,000–8,000 plus tolls even fairly driven
  • If overcharged, photograph the plate and call the Kanagawa Transport Bureau (神奈川運輸局) or 110

🆘 What to Do If You Get Scammed

📋 File a Police Report

Go to the nearest Japanese Police (Keisatsu) station or koban. Call 110. Kanagawa Prefectural Police's Isezaki Station has a dedicated team cataloguing Fukutomicho bottakuri, and Kagacho Police (加賀町警察署) covers Chinatown. Get an official crime report — you'll need it for insurance and chargebacks.

💳 Cancel Your Cards

Call your bank immediately. Most have 24/7 numbers on the back of the card (keep a photo saved separately). Block suspicious transactions before they use your details — especially urgent after a suspected drink-spiking incident in Fukutomicho.

🛂 Lost Passport?

The nearest US consular services for Yokohama are at the US Embassy in Tokyo, 1-10-5 Akasaka, Minato-ku, Tokyo. For emergencies: +81 3-3224-5000. UK, Australian, and Canadian embassies are also in central Tokyo.

📱 Track Your Device

If your phone was stolen, use Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) from another device. Don't confront thieves yourself — share the location with police instead.
