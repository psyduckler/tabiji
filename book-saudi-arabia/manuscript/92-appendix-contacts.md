# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

The short emergency numbers in this appendix (999, 997, 998, 911, 1900) are national, toll-free, and stable. The longer institutional numbers — embassies, consulates, hospitals — were checked against official sources at the time of publication, but phone numbers do change. Where a number could not be verified against an official source, this appendix deliberately does **not** invent one; instead it tells you the exact search to run or the official website to check before you travel. If any number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus *Saudi Arabia* and you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results.

**Before you travel**, double-check your own embassy or consulate's current number directly on its official website (the US State Department's travel.state.gov, the UK's gov.uk, Canada's travel.gc.ca, Australia's smartraveller.gov.au, and the equivalents for other countries). Save both the main switchboard and the after-hours emergency line on your phone before departure. Even a verified number can become a wrong number between editions.

## National emergency numbers (anywhere in Saudi Arabia)

- **Police: 999.** The general police emergency line, free from any phone with or without a SIM. This is your number for theft, intimidation, a refused passport, a driver who will not stop, or any crime in progress.
- **Unified emergency (Mecca & Riyadh regions): 911.** In the Mecca region (which includes Mecca, Jeddah, and Taif) and the Riyadh region, **911** is the unified emergency number and reaches police, ambulance, and fire through a single dispatch. Outside those two regions, use the dedicated numbers below.
- **Ambulance / Saudi Red Crescent: 997.** Free from any phone. State your location as a landmark or hotel name rather than a street address.
- **Civil Defense / Fire: 998.** Fire, rescue, and major-incident response.
- **Traffic accidents (Najm / road incidents): 993.** For road-traffic accidents specifically; for a car-rental "damage" dispute that is a fraud rather than an accident, use the consumer line (1900) and the police (999).
- **Consumer protection & commercial fraud (Ministry of Commerce): 1900.** The national line for overcharging, counterfeit or mislabeled goods, refused receipts, and rental-car or booking disputes. **This is the single most important number for the commercial scams in this book.** Pair it with the Ministry's "Balagh Tijari" (commercial-report) channel in its app and website.

A note on the apps. Two official Saudi apps are worth installing before you travel: **Absher** (أبشر), the government-services hub that many visitors already use for visa and entry services, and **Kollona Amn** (كلنا أمن, "We Are All Security"), the Ministry of Interior's reporting app for reaching police services from your phone. Together with the Ministry of Commerce app (for "Balagh Tijari" complaints), they cover most of the reporting you would ever need to do.

## How to report a scam

For a **crime** — theft, a held passport, intimidation, confinement — call **999** (or **911** in the Mecca and Riyadh regions), or use the **Kollona Amn** app. Ask for the report's reference number.

For a **commercial rip-off** — an inflated bill, counterfeit or adulterated goods, a refused receipt, a fabricated rental-car charge, a fake booking — call **1900** or file a **"Balagh Tijari"** complaint through the Ministry of Commerce app. Give the business's full name, a Google Maps pin, the exact amount, the date and time, and your photos.

For a **card** that was used fraudulently, file the police or commercial report, then call your card issuer (numbers below); the issuer's fraud team will ask for the reference when they reverse the charge. Saudi Arabia's most common card scam is the rental-car "damage" charge run on a deposit hold — fight it with time-stamped pickup photos plus a chargeback.

## Card issuer emergency lines (callable from Saudi Arabia)

When you call, have your card number (or the last four digits), the approximate transaction amount, and the time of the charge ready.

- **Visa Global Customer Assistance:** +1-303-967-1096 (call collect from Saudi Arabia). 24/7, English.
- **Mastercard Global Service:** +1-636-722-7111. 24/7.
- **American Express Global Assist:** +1-336-393-1111. 24/7.
- **Discover** (US-issued cards): +1-801-902-3100. 24/7, collect.

If your card was issued by a bank rather than directly by Visa / Mastercard / Amex / Discover, the fastest route is the number printed on the back of your card — that line reaches your own issuer's fraud desk and can freeze the card in under a minute. The international lines above route through to the same fraud teams if you cannot reach the back-of-card number.

## Embassies and consulates

The numbers below were checked against official sources at the time of publication. Most missions also run a separate after-hours emergency line for citizens in distress; those are not always public-facing, so look yours up on your own government's website before you travel. **Where a switchboard number could not be confirmed against an official source, this appendix says so and points you to the official site rather than printing a number that might be wrong — do not guess a number for an embassy.**

### United States

- **US Embassy Riyadh:** Diplomatic Quarter (DQ), Riyadh · **+966-11-488-3800**. Handles US-citizen services for the central region.
- **US Consulate General Jeddah:** Al Hamra District, Jeddah. For the western region (Jeddah, Mecca, Medina, Taif). Confirm the current direct number on the consulate's page at **sa.usembassy.gov** before you travel.
- **US Consulate General Dhahran:** Dhahran, Eastern Province. For the eastern region (Dammam, Al Khobar, Dhahran). Confirm the current direct number at **sa.usembassy.gov** before you travel.
- **After-hours emergency line for US citizens (callable from anywhere):** +1-202-501-4444. 24/7. This State Department line connects you to the duty officer for Saudi Arabia outside business hours.

### United Kingdom

- **British Embassy Riyadh:** Diplomatic Quarter, Riyadh. The FCDO directs most enquiries through its online contact forms; confirm the current switchboard and the consular contact route at **gov.uk** ("British Embassy Riyadh"). The British Consulate General in Jeddah serves the western region.
- **FCDO 24/7 consular assistance:** for an emergency involving a British national anywhere, the FCDO global consular line is reachable via the contact details on **gov.uk** — save it on your phone before you travel.

### Canada

- **Embassy of Canada to Saudi Arabia, Riyadh:** Diplomatic Quarter, Riyadh. Confirm the current switchboard at **travel.gc.ca** (or international.gc.ca).
- For Canadians needing after-hours help, the Emergency Watch and Response Centre in Ottawa can be reached collect at **+1-613-996-8885** (24/7).

### Australia

- **Australian Embassy Riyadh:** Diplomatic Quarter, Riyadh. Confirm the current switchboard at **saudiarabia.embassy.gov.au**.
- For after-hours emergencies, the Consular Emergency Centre in Canberra is reachable at **+61-2-6261-3305** (24/7, callable collect from Saudi Arabia).

### Other nationalities

Most countries maintain an embassy in Riyadh's Diplomatic Quarter, with several also keeping a consulate general in Jeddah for the western region and the Hajj/Umrah corridor. Search *"[your country] embassy Saudi Arabia"* before you travel, and save both the Riyadh switchboard and the after-hours emergency line on your phone.

## City-specific notes

- **Mecca and Medina.** Security and police are present in force in and around the Masjid al-Haram and the Prophet's Mosque, and they are used to assisting foreign pilgrims. For theft in the prayer crowds, walk to the nearest uniformed officer rather than chasing anyone. Note that non-Muslims cannot enter the city of Mecca and certain central areas of Medina; plan accordingly, and use official Hajj/Umrah operators only.
- **AlUla.** The only official seller of Hegra and heritage-site tickets is the Royal Commission for AlUla's platform, **experiencealula.com** (booking via tickets.experiencealula.com). Report a lookalike reseller through that platform and the Ministry of Commerce (1900). Mobile coverage and app-taxi availability can be thinner here than in the big cities; arrange transport through your hotel or the official site shuttles.
- **Tabuk / NEOM.** Several scams in this region are recruitment and investment frauds (fake NEOM job offers, "NEOM crypto" pitches) that begin online. Treat any up-front "fee" to secure a NEOM job, tender, or investment as fraud; the legitimate first move is a police report (999) and your bank's fraud team.
- **Abha and Taif.** App-taxi coverage is thinner and street-taxi overcharging more common in these mountain cities; the cable cars are a known "per head vs. whole cabin" trap. Confirm the price in writing before boarding, and dial 1900 for an overcharge dispute.
- **Dammam, Al Khobar, and the Eastern Province.** Covered by the US Consulate General in Dhahran; the King Fahd Causeway insurance-booth markup documented in this book is a commercial dispute (1900), not a road-accident matter.

## A note on calling 999 / 997 / 911 with limited Arabic

If you dial an emergency number and cannot explain the situation in Arabic, say *"English, please"* clearly and wait. Operators in the major cities and the holy-site regions are equipped to handle English or to bring an English speaker onto the line. State your location as a landmark or a hotel name — *"the Clock Tower, Mecca,"* *"the Kingdom Centre, Riyadh,"* *"the Corniche, Al Khobar,"* *"Hegra, AlUla"* — rather than a street address that is hard to pronounce. If you are calling from a mobile, the dispatcher can sometimes locate you automatically, so even a rough *"I'm near the Masjid an-Nabawi"* is enough to get help moving.

For the commercial scams that make up most of this book — an inflated bill, counterfeit gold, a fabricated rental-car charge, a refused receipt — the line you want is **1900** (Ministry of Commerce), and the magic words are the business's name and a Google Maps pin. That combination is what turns a bad afternoon into an official record, and an official record is what gets your money back and keeps the next traveler from walking into the same shop.
