Al Khobar is the Eastern Province's seafront and the Saudi end of the bridge to Bahrain, and its seven documented scams — all tourist-trap category, two rated high-severity — cluster around the airport road, the causeway, the gold souqs, and the Corniche. The arrival pull is the Dammam Airport "Broken Meter" Taxi to Khobar: the roughly 50-kilometer run from King Fahd International (DMM) to the seafront should be about SAR 90-150, but drivers who legally must run a meter "almost never" do, naming a flat SAR 250-350 instead and pricing by how tired you look. The two high-severity scams are the Car-Rental Office Without License or Insurance — the Eastern Province has a documented rogue-office problem, with a Public Transport Authority sweep of Riyadh, Dammam, Al-Khobar, and Jeddah catching more than 2,000 violations and over SAR 3 million in fines for unlicensed operation and renting uninsured cars — and the Fake "Plainclothes Officer" ID Shakedown, where a man in local dress flashes a card, claims to be undercover police, and hints at a "fine" you can settle on the spot, an impersonation Arab News has tracked to fake checkpoints in Taif and Jeddah.

The risk concentrates on three corridors. The King Fahd Causeway crossing, departing south of Al-Khobar at Aziziyah, runs the Insurance-Booth Markup: the physical Passport Island booths sell only a one-month Bahrain policy for around SAR 160, so a three- or five-day crosser is forced to overpay for coverage they won't use. Souk Al-Khobar on Prince Turki Street, the Mall of Dhahran jewelry shops, and Eastern Province dealers carry the Underweight & Mixed Gold in the Souqs scam, after a Precious Metals Committee member went viral over dealers manipulating weight and the World Gold Council earlier suspended activities in the Kingdom over glass mixed with gold. The Corniche, Sea Front, and Half Moon Bay strip runs the low-severity Corniche Seafood "Sea View" Overcharge, where seafront restaurants run SAR 250-500 a head and the most theatrical climb to SAR 400-700, with fish priced by weight off the ice. Online, the Fake Booking & WhatsApp "Confirm Your Hotel" Fraud quotes your real reservation reference — obtained via a Booking.com partner breach — and pushes a 24-hour "verify your card or lose the room" deadline to a fake payment page.

A safe default for first-timers: order a Careem or Uber that shows the full DMM-to-Khobar fare before you confirm, knowing the real rate is about SAR 90-150, and rent only from a licensed company with a real office, paying the deposit by credit card and filming every existing scratch at pickup. Buy your Bahrain insurance for the exact days online through JESR, Absher, or UIC before you reach the causeway rather than at the booth. Never verify a hotel booking or pay a deposit from a message link — confirm directly with the property's official phone number and treat any "or lose your room" deadline as the red flag it is. And if anyone flashes an ID demanding an on-the-spot cash fine, refuse it and insist on moving to a real police station.
