Jeddah is the volume's anchor city and the Kingdom's main pilgrim gateway, so its scam profile is the broadest in this book: seven documented scams, every one of them a tourist-trap variant, and most of them keyed to the same crowds that pass through Al Balad and the Gold Souq on the way to or from Mecca. The Airport Taxi Fare Inflation is the one you meet first — a 500 SAR quote for a ride to Al Balad that should run 100 to 120, justified with a "broken meter" the moment your bags are loaded. The Hajj and Umrah Package Fraud is the costliest, the only high-severity scam here: an agency takes your wired payment, then the hotel does not exist and the visa was never processed. Around the Gold Souq, two scams share the same victim — Fake Gold and Jewelry at the Gold Souq sells gold-plated brass at an 800 SAR "21-karat" price, while Al Balad Souq Pickpocketing works the same crowded lanes with bump-and-lift teams. Rounding out the set are the Camera Drop Scam Near Landmarks (a pre-cracked lens, a 1,000 SAR demand, a fake "mediator"), the Rental Car Damage and Deposit Hold (a 57 SAR seat-stain charge and a 2,000 SAR deposit held for weeks), and the low-grade Overpriced Corniche and Waterfront Vendors.

The risk is concentrated in three zones. The first is King Abdulaziz International Airport arrivals, where the fare-inflation and unlicensed-taxi pitches start, alongside the airport rental counters. The second is the Al Balad historical district — a UNESCO World Heritage site — and specifically the Gold Souq around the Sharbathili Building, where pickpocket teams, fake-gold street vendors, and unlicensed package-tour touts all converge on visitors carrying cash for jewelry. The third is the waterfront: the Corniche promenade, King Fahd's Fountain viewpoints, and the floating Al-Rahma Mosque, where the camera-drop setup and the price-after-you-consume vendors operate. The Al Hamra district adds the local rental-agency and tour-operator approaches. All of it intensifies during Hajj and Umrah seasons, when the city's population swells and the pickpocket and counterfeit-gold trades have the largest possible target pool.

A safe default for first-timers: order Careem or Uber from the terminal Wi-Fi before you leave arrivals — both run reliably at the airport with fixed upfront pricing, and Careem is the most widely used ride-hailing app in the Kingdom, so a glance at its estimate also tells you the fair price for any taxi. Book Hajj and Umrah packages only through agencies verified on the official Nusuk platform, pay by credit card rather than wire transfer, and confirm every hotel reservation directly with the property. The two habits that defeat most of the rest are simple: never load your bags or hand over money before a price is agreed, and buy gold only from licensed shops like Malabar Gold & Diamonds with a detailed invoice — never from a street vendor, however good the karat claim.
