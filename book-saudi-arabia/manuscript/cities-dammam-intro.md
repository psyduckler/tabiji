Dammam runs as an Eastern Province business gateway, and its scams reflect that: six documented scams, all tourist-trap category, none rated high but several built to catch a tired arrival or a distracted shopper. The front door is King Fahd International Airport (DMM), where two pulls stack. The Broken Meter Taxi loads your bags, pulls away, and only then mentions the meter is "broken today" — demanding 150 SAR for an airport-to-Al-Shatea run that should be 40-60 by meter, with your luggage in the trunk as leverage. The Fake Airport Official is the colder version: a man asks to "verify your entry," examines your passport, invents a paperwork problem, and steers you toward a waiting unlicensed car instead of the official rank — though real immigration officials never approach you outside passport control. Beyond the airport, the Gold Souk Switch sells gold-plated pieces as solid 21-karat gold to buyers who can't read karat markings, turning a 2,000-SAR set into a fraction of that.

The risk concentrates at the airport arrivals hall, the central gold souks, and the waterfront promenades. The Al Khobar Corniche and the Dammam waterfront carry the low-severity Bird Dropping Distraction, where a fake "dropping" squirted by one accomplice becomes a shoulder-cleaning fuss while a partner works your pockets. The rest of Dammam's exposure is digital and runs to your phone. The Haraj Phantom Car Deposit advertises a Camry a few thousand below market on Haraj, OpenSooq, or Facebook Marketplace, with a "departing expat" in Al-Faisaliyah asking for a SAR 3,000 deposit by STC Pay to "hold" it before vanishing. The Customs-Fee Delivery Text impersonates Saudi Post, Aramex, and other couriers with an SMS demanding a tiny SAR 17 "customs clearance" — a fee small enough to tap through, on a page that mirrors the courier's branding, before a far larger charge hits your card.

A safe default for first-timers: use Uber or Careem instead of any street taxi — both are widely used in Dammam and show upfront pricing — and keep your luggage in the back seat with you rather than the trunk. Never hand your passport to anyone outside the official immigration counters, and walk straight to the marked ride-share pickup or taxi stand, ignoring solicitors in the arrivals hall. Treat every texted "fee" or "deposit" link as fraud: never pay a courier or customs charge from an SMS link, never wire a deposit to hold a too-cheap car, and inspect any vehicle in person with the Istimara in hand before money moves. For gold, buy only from established souk shops that display Saudi commerce ministry certificates and give a receipt listing weight, karat, and registration number.
