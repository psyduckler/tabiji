MiniMax Music 2.0 vs 2.5 vs 2.5+: Everything We Learned About AI Music for Travel Reels

Published March 11, 2026 · By the tabiji.ai team · See also: AI Image Generation Compared

We produce dozens of Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts every week at tabiji.ai — AI-generated travel videos that need background music. Licensing real tracks doesn't scale. Royalty-free libraries get repetitive. So we went all-in on AI music generation.

After months of real-world music production use — generating hundreds of tracks across three MiniMax Music API models, testing native video audio from Veo 3 and Z.AI, downloading royalty-free Pixabay soundtracks, and building a trending Instagram audio pipeline — here's everything we've learned about music creation with AI for short-form travel video content.

Every audio sample on this page was generated fresh by the API on the same day with identical prompts. Press play and hear for yourself. We also cover how AI music fits into the broader LLM and generative AI landscape — from OpenAI's GPT models to DeepSeek, Claude Opus, and beyond.

Why AI Music for Travel Reels?

Short-form video needs background music. Every Reel, every Short, every TikTok. When you're producing 4–10 videos per day through automated workflows, your options are:

  • Licensed music — expensive at scale, rights management nightmare, can't automate
  • Royalty-free libraries — free/cheap but limited variety. Your audience hears the same 20 tracks on repeat.
  • AI-generated music — unique per video, ~$0.01/track, fully automatable via API, infinite variety

We chose option three. The MiniMax Music API became the backbone of our audio pipeline — it's the only service we found that combines decent audio quality, true API access, instrumental mode, and pricing that rounds to zero. If you're looking to optimize your content pipeline for scale, this is the intro you need.

Music 2.0 — The Previous Generation (Vocals Required)

The first MiniMax music model we used — and now the previous generation compared to 2.5+. It always requires a lyrics field — you cannot generate without one. It doesn't support is_instrumental: true at all. Pass the parameter and it gets ignored; the track will still have vocals.

To get something close to instrumental, we pass placeholder lyrics like "la la la". The model interprets this as a wordless vocal melody — humming, "la la" singing, or vocalizations layered on top. The vocal performances are decent — there's natural vibrato and surprisingly expressive timbre in the singing. For some use cases (retro vibes, dreamy atmospheric) this sounds pleasant. For clean background music behind text overlays, it's a problem.

Music 2.5 — The Middle Child (Skip It)

Music 2.5 improves on 2.0 slightly — the instrumentation is more refined and the mix is better. But is_instrumental: true doesn't work on this model. Sending it returns a 2013: invalid params, lyrics is required error. You still need to pass lyrics, which means you still get vocals.

If you want instrumental, use 2.5+. If you want vocals, use 2.0. There's no reason to use 2.5.

Music 2.5+ — The Production Choice

This is the one. Music 2.5+ (sometimes referenced alongside MiniMax-M2.5 in the broader model family) is the only MiniMax model where is_instrumental: true actually works. Set it, and you get pure instrumental music — no vocals, no humming, no "la la." Just clean music. It excels at producing high fidelity output that holds up in published content.

Quality is noticeably better: richer instrumentation, better stereo separation, more dynamic range. Tracks are also longer (60–192s vs 20–65s), giving you more material to trim from. At ~$0.01 per generation, the pricing is essentially free. In our benchmark of all three models across 50+ prompts, Music 2.5+ scored highest for instrumental travel background music — it's the SOTA (state-of-the-art) for API-accessible music generation.

Native Video Audio: Veo 3, Z.AI CogVideoX, MiniMax Hailuo

Before settling on a separate music generation step, we tested whether the video generation models themselves could produce usable audio.

Google Veo 3 — Decent SFX, Not Music

Veo 3 has a generate_audio parameter that produces synchronized audio for generated video. The sound effects are surprisingly good — ambient street noise, wind, water, footsteps. For establishing shots (a busy market, a temple garden), it adds atmosphere.

But it's not music. You can't prompt Veo 3 for "acoustic guitar background" and get anything usable. The audio generation is tuned for environmental ambience, not composed tracks. We disable it for most Reels and add music separately via MiniMax.

Z.AI CogVideoX-3 — Synthetic and Unusable

CogVideoX-3 claims "AI SFX audio" in its docs. In practice, the quality is mediocre — it sounds synthetic, thin, and obviously AI-generated. We tested it on several clips and never produced audio we'd want in a published video. Never used in production.

MiniMax Hailuo I2V — No Audio At All

MiniMax's video model (Hailuo) generates video only — no audio output whatsoever. This is actually why we built the music pipeline: we needed something to pair with Hailuo's otherwise excellent video generation. Music 2.5+ is the natural companion.

Alternatives We Explored (But Didn't Use)

Suno AI & Udio — Amazing Music, No API

Let's address the elephant in the room. Suno AI and Udio produce genuinely incredible AI music — full songs with complex arrangements, rich vocal performances, natural vibrato, convincing duets, and emotional dynamics that rival human music production. The audio quality is high fidelity enough for Spotify. In a blind test, they'd probably beat MiniMax on raw quality.

The problem: neither has a public API (as of March 2026). Both are web-only interfaces. You can't script them, can't integrate them into automated workflows, can't generate 50 tracks programmatically overnight. For one-off music creation projects and standalone soundtracks they're fantastic. For automated Reel production at scale, they're useless.

When (if) they launch APIs, we'll re-evaluate immediately. Until then, MiniMax excels as the only viable option for programmatic music generation in production workflows.

Pixabay Royalty-Free Library — Our Fallback

We downloaded 21 royalty-free tracks from Pixabay, organized by mood:

  • adventure — upbeat, driving rhythm, travel montage energy
  • dreamy — ambient pads, soft piano, golden hour
  • tropical — ukulele, steel drums, island vibes
  • cinematic — orchestral swells, dramatic builds
  • cultural — region-specific instruments (Japanese, Middle Eastern, etc.)

Pros: Zero latency (no API call), zero cost, predictable quality. When MiniMax's API has issues, we fall back to these. Our one-thing-video skill uses them by default.

Cons: Only 21 tracks. After a week of 4 Reels/day, your audience has heard each one multiple times. No variety.

Trending Instagram Audio Pipeline

We also built a completely different system: download trending Instagram audio via YouTube search, then categorize it by mood using Gemini Flash audio analysis.

The pipeline: identify trending audio on Instagram → search YouTube for the track → extract audio with yt-dlp → analyze mood with Gemini 2.5 Flash → rename and categorize. We downloaded 19 tracks this way at ~$0.007 per track (Gemini audio analysis cost).

This is for licensed/trending audio, not AI-generated — a different use case entirely. When a Reel needs to ride a trending sound for algorithmic reach, this pipeline delivers. For original background music at scale, MiniMax is better.

AI Music in the Broader AI Model Landscape

It's worth stepping back to understand where AI music generation sits within the broader generative AI ecosystem in 2026. While LLM models like GPT-5 from OpenAI, Claude Opus from Anthropic, Gemini 2.5 Pro from Google, DeepSeek V3, GLM-5 from Zhipu AI, and Kimi from Moonshot AI are dominating leaderboard rankings on text and coding benchmark tasks (like SWE-Bench), music generation models operate in a completely different paradigm.

Text LLMs are measured in input tokens and output tokens, competing on reasoning, code generation, and instruction-following. Many use MoE (Mixture of Experts) architectures to balance quality and efficiency. Music models don't process tokens at all — they generate continuous audio waveforms. The evaluation criteria are entirely different: audio quality, timbre accuracy, vocal performances, rhythmic coherence, and prompt adherence.

Some open-source efforts have emerged (Meta's MusicGen, Stability's Stable Audio), but they trail the proprietary APIs significantly. The music creation space has no equivalent of HuggingFace's leaderboard for LLMs — there's no standardized benchmark for comparing AI music quality at scale. Our testing here is the closest thing to a real-world production benchmark you'll find.

What's interesting is how AI music models mirror the LLM pricing race. Just as DeepSeek disrupted LLM pricing with dramatically cheaper input tokens and output tokens, MiniMax Music's ~$0.01/track pricing makes music production essentially free at scale. The cost barrier has shifted from generation to curation — it's cheaper to generate 10 tracks and pick the best one than to spend 5 minutes searching a royalty-free library.

Key Learnings & Gotchas

⚠️ Music 2.0 requires the lyrics field

You cannot generate with Music 2.0 without lyrics. Pass placeholder like "la la la" for instrumental-ish output. The model will still vocalize it.

⚠️ Music 2.5 is_instrumental is broken

Sending is_instrumental: true to Music 2.5 returns error 2013: invalid params, lyrics is required. Only Music 2.5+ supports true instrumental mode.

⚠️ MiniMax Music is a MUSIC model, not a foley model

Even when prompted for "ambient coffee shop sounds" or "ocean waves," the model produces melodic musical compositions inspired by the prompt — not sound effects. If you need foley/ambience, use a different tool.

⚠️ Generated tracks are 60–192s but you only need 8–15s

Always trim with FFmpeg. The first 8–15 seconds are usually the best part. Don't use the whole track — it wasn't composed for short-form structure.

💡 FFmpeg muxing command we use in production

Trim music to fit video, add 2-second fade-out:

ffmpeg -i video.mp4 -i music.mp3 \
  -filter_complex "[1:a]atrim=0:12,afade=t=out:st=10:d=2[a]" \
  -map 0:v -map "[a]" \
  -c:v copy -c:a aac -shortest output.mp4

This trims the music to 12 seconds, applies a 2-second fade-out starting at the 10-second mark, copies the video stream untouched, and encodes audio as AAC. The -shortest flag ensures the output matches the shorter of the two streams.

How We Use It (Code)

Our CLI wrapper for the MiniMax Music API:

# Generate instrumental background music (Music 2.5+)
python3 scripts/minimax-video.py music \
  "warm cinematic travel, acoustic guitar, golden hour" \
  -m music-2.5+ --instrumental \
  -o /tmp/my-track.mp3

# Generate with vocals (Music 2.0, requires lyrics)
python3 scripts/minimax-video.py music \
  "dreamy lo-fi travel vibes, soft and nostalgic" \
  -m music-2.0 -l "la la la, la la la" \
  -o /tmp/vocal-track.mp3

The underlying API call is straightforward:

curl -X POST "https://api.minimax.io/v1/music_generation" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $MINIMAX_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "model": "music-2.5+",
    "prompt": "warm cinematic travel background music",
    "is_instrumental": true
  }'

The response includes a task ID. Poll for completion, then download the mp3 from the returned URL. Our CLI handles the polling automatically.

Full pipeline in a shell script (what our cron jobs actually run):

#!/bin/bash
# Generate music → trim → mux with video → publish

# 1. Generate instrumental track
python3 scripts/minimax-video.py music \
  "$MOOD_PROMPT" -m music-2.5+ --instrumental \
  -o /tmp/reel-music.mp3

# 2. Trim to 12s with fade-out
ffmpeg -y -i /tmp/reel-music.mp3 \
  -af "atrim=0:12,afade=t=out:st=10:d=2" \
  /tmp/reel-music-trimmed.mp3

# 3. Mux with video
ffmpeg -y -i /tmp/reel-video.mp4 -i /tmp/reel-music-trimmed.mp3 \
  -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v copy -c:a aac -shortest \
  /tmp/reel-final.mp4

# 4. Publish
bash ~/tabiji/functions/publish-reel.sh \
  /tmp/reel-final.mp4 "$CAPTION"

The Verdict

🏆 Winner: MiniMax Music 2.5+ (Instrumental Mode)

For automated travel video production, Music 2.5+ is the only real option. It's the only model with working instrumental mode, the only one producing long enough tracks for flexible trimming, and the only one with consistently clean output suitable for background music.

At ~$0.01 per track, cost is literally not a factor. We generate hundreds of tracks per month and the total bill is under $5. The quality isn't Suno-level, but it doesn't need to be — it's background music for 8-second Reels, not a Spotify release.

🥈 Music 2.0: Niche Use for Retro/Vocal Vibes

Music 2.0 still has a role when you want vocals or a lo-fi vocal texture. Our vintage POV Reels sometimes benefit from the dreamy "la la la" quality — it adds a human, nostalgic warmth that pure instrumentals lack. Use it intentionally, not as a workaround.

🥉 Music 2.5: Skip It Entirely

It's slightly better than 2.0 but with broken instrumental mode and no compelling advantage over either sibling. Save yourself the confusion.

What We'd Change

If we could wave a magic wand:

  • Suno or Udio API access — would replace MiniMax overnight for quality-critical soundtracks and vocal performances
  • Shorter generation option — 8–15 second tracks in multiple formats purpose-built for Reels, so we don't need to trim 192 seconds down to 12
  • Mood-to-prompt templates — a standardized mapping from destination + mood → optimal music prompt (we're building this internally to optimize our workflows)
  • Better foley/SFX model — MiniMax Music generates music even when you want ambient sounds. A separate foley model for coffee shop ambience, rain, market chatter would be valuable.
  • Open-source alternatives — open-source music models (MusicGen, Stable Audio) are improving but still trail behind. When they reach SOTA quality, self-hosting would eliminate API dependency entirely.
  • Industry-standard benchmark — AI music needs its own SWE-Bench equivalent: a standardized benchmark and leaderboard for comparing music generation models on audio quality, timbre, prompt adherence, and real-world production suitability.

For now, MiniMax Music 2.5+ with is_instrumental: true is the best tool for the job. It's cheap, reliable, automatable, and good enough. Just as GPT-5 and Claude Opus compete on the LLM leaderboard for text reasoning while Kimi, GLM-5, and DeepSeek push pricing lower via MoE architectures, MiniMax Music has won the AI music creation API race on sheer practicality. In the world of AI-generated short-form video, "good enough at scale" beats "perfect but manual" every time.
