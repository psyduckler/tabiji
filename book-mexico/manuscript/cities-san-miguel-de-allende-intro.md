San Miguel de Allende is the *Pueblo Mágico* in the Bajío highlands that became Mexico's most-Americanised town in the digital-nomad decade — the cobblestone-and-pink-cantera city around the *Jardín* Allende and the Parroquia where the population of full-time US and Canadian residents now passes 12,000 and the cafés on San Francisco Street post their menus in dollars. The volume scams ride that gentrification story. They concentrate in three corridors: the **Jardín Allende** restaurant perimeter and the San Francisco Street tourist strip, the **gallery and boutique-hotel zone** along Hernández Macías and Cuna de Allende, and the **airport-transfer routes** in from Guanajuato (BJX, ninety minutes) and Querétaro (QRO, one hour). *Reforma* and *Mexico News Daily* have both covered the 2024-2025 community backlash against expat pricing, and SECTUR's national tourist-assistance line **078** has logged a steady increase in SMA-origin complaints over the past two years.

The **Jardín-perimeter "expat tourist menu" markup** is the volume risk. Restaurants ringing the central plaza print USD-priced menus at two to three times Mexican-market rates — *enchiladas mineras* listed at US$18 against MX$80-120 at working-class spots three blocks away — then layer on a pre-added 18% gratuity, a 10-15% credit-card "processing" fee, and a "bilingual service charge" of roughly US$10 that is simply illegitimate. The defense is to walk off the *Jardín*. La Parada on Recreo, Lavanda Café on Hernández Macías, Cumpanio on Correo, El Pegaso on Corregidora, and La Posadita on Cuna de Allende all run authentic menus at MX$280-500 per person with transparent pricing and Google ratings above 4.6. Inspect every bill, refuse double-tipping on a pre-added gratuity, and ask for the bill in MX$ rather than dollars.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **BJX/QRO airport-transfer kerb** runs touts inside arrivals quoting US$150-250 for the SMA drive when the legitimate prepaid shuttle (BajioGo, Viajes San Miguel, Querétaro-Express) is US$30-65 per person shared and Uber from BJX is MX$900-1,200. The **Hernández Macías and Cuna de Allende sidewalk-gallery scene** sells mass-produced prints as "famous local artist" originals at US$500-3,000, sometimes with a "mandatory gallery fee US$20" to browse — no legitimate gallery in Fábrica La Aurora charges anyone to walk in. And the **Instagram-DM luxury villa pitch** uses stolen Rosewood and Belmond photos to extract US$1,000 non-refundable deposits via WhatsApp bank transfer for properties that either don't exist or arrive without working plumbing.

A safe default: pre-book the BJX or QRO shuttle through BajioGo or Querétaro-Express, or take Uber direct from either airport. Eat off the *Jardín* at the verified Mexican spots above and refuse any "tourism surcharge 20%" demand — it is unlawful and reportable to PROFECO. Buy art only at Fábrica La Aurora or Instituto Allende with documented artist CVs and provenance paperwork. Book luxury hotels through rosewoodhotels.com, belmond.com, or hotelmatilda.com directly, never through Instagram DMs. For trouble, the SMA Tourist Police direct line is **+52 415 152 0890**, the SECTUR national tourist-assistance line is **078**, the *MP* in Guanajuato state handles a *denuncia*, and **911** is universal.
