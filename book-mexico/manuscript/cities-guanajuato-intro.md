Guanajuato is a UNESCO-listed silver-mining city built into a steep ravine — narrow *callejones*, painted facades, the underground tunnel road — and its scam ecosystem clusters along three corridors: the **Jardín de la Unión** and the Teatro Juárez plaza where the nightly *callejoneadas* form, the **approach to the Museo de las Momias** at Panteón de Santa Paula, and the **León/Bajío Airport (BJX)** thirty kilometres southwest at Silao. *El Universal Bajío*, *Milenio León*, and *Periódico Correo* have covered the *callejoneada* "private *estudiantina*" overcharge and the porter-extortion pattern at hilltop hotels through 2025.

The **callejoneada "private estudiantina" upsell** is the signature evening risk on the Jardín de la Unión. The traditional walking serenade — *estudiantinas* in Renaissance costume, Spanish guitars, a small *sangría* serving, ending at the **Callejón del Beso** — runs MX$150–$250 per person from the official Teatro Juárez ticket booth, with departures at 7 PM, 8 PM, and 9 PM nightly. The 2025 scam layer sells "private estudiantina callejoneada US$150 per person" via hotel concierges, "VIP couples upgrades" at US$200 for two, and "exclusive Callejón del Beso ceremony MX$500" entries to a free public alley anyone can walk at any hour. Buy at the Teatro Juárez booth and refuse every private upsell.

The risk extends in three more corridors. The **Mummy Museum "premium access" reseller fraud** at Panteón de Santa Paula pushes MX$500–$800 "skip-the-line" tickets and "mandatory MX$250 guides" for a museum where the official window charges MX$108 (entry plus audio guide), the queue runs ten to twenty minutes, and no mandatory guide exists; "mummy + Diego Rivera + Alhóndiga US$80" bundles cost under MX$400 if you DIY the three sites. The **BJX airport overcharge** quotes MX$1,200–$2,000 from arrivals-kerb kiosks for the thirty-kilometre run when Uber meters at MX$250–$380 and the official Transporte Terrestre prepaid booth charges MX$450–$600 — the budget option is the shuttle to León Central plus an ADO bus to Guanajuato for MX$140 total. And the **Jardín de la Unión menu-in-dollars trick** prices *enchiladas mineras* at US$18 (real range MX$120–$180), adds an 18% gratuity above the tip line, layers a 10–15% "credit-card processing" surcharge that should be 0–3%, and tags a "mariachi serenade" upcharge onto the bill — eat off the perimeter at Mestizo (Truco 8), Los Campos (Sopeña 11), or Casa Valadez instead.

A safe default: pack light because the *callejones* require a fifty- to two-hundred-meter walk with luggage from any taxi drop-off, and book hotels with short approaches (Hotel Boutique 1850, Hotel Edelmira on Positos, Hotel Abadía Plaza) or call ahead so the property sends staff — fair porter tips are MX$50–$100 per bag, never the MX$300–$500 "luggage porter" extortion; take Uber or the official Transporte Terrestre booth from BJX, or Primera Plus direct from Mexico City's Terminal Norte (not TAPO) at MX$800–$1,000; for Bajío day trips to Dolores Hidalgo, Atotonilco, Cristo Rey, or San Miguel de Allende, DIY by Uber (MX$400–$1,200 each way) rather than buying the US$150–$200 concierge bundle. Call **911** for any emergency, the **Policía Turística Guanajuato** on **+52 473 732 2121**, and file a *denuncia* at the **Fiscalía General del Estado de Guanajuato** *Ministerio Público* within 48 hours.
