Mexico City runs Latin America's largest tourist-scam ecosystem, and almost all of it converges on four corridors: the **Centro Histórico** (Zócalo, Bellas Artes, the Calle Madero pedestrian strip), **Roma Norte and Condesa** after dark, **Polanco**'s ride-hail pickup curbs along Avenida Presidente Masaryk, and **Benito Juárez International Airport (MEX)** Terminals 1 and 2. *Reforma*, *El Universal*, and *Milenio* have covered the *secuestro exprés* pattern and the airport *sitio* overcharge for years, and the *Policía Turística* (INFOTUR) keeps a 24/7 English desk on +52 55 4891 1166.

The **MEX airport "authorized sitio taxi" overcharge** is the volume risk the moment you clear immigration. The legitimate yellow Sitio 300 and Porto Taxi counters inside arrivals quote MX$250–$330 flat-rate prepaid to Polanco, Roma, Condesa, or the Zócalo with a printed receipt; the touts wandering between counters quote MX$700–$1,500 for the same ride, often paired with a "premium private driver" pitch and a request to swipe your card for a "deposit" — which is where the cloning happens. Ignore every approach inside the terminal, walk to the posted Sitio counter, pay cash or in-app, and never hand a physical card to any driver. If you prefer Uber or Didi, book on airport Wi-Fi after luggage and meet at the signposted pickup zone (Terminal 1 level 1; Terminal 2 level 2).

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Zócalo and Calle Madero pickpocket and phone-snatch ring** runs distraction teams around Bellas Artes — one bumps, one lifts — and grabs phones off outdoor café tables on the pedestrian strip; Metro Lines 1, 2, and 3 at Zócalo, Pino Suárez, and Hidalgo stations are picked routinely in the 7–9 AM and 5–8 PM rush. The **fake-police document-check shakedown** in Roma Norte and Condesa after 10 PM targets solo foreigners: a uniformed officer (often Policía Auxiliar) demands your passport and phone, "finds" an infraction, and quotes MX$500–$5,000 cash to make it disappear — real fines are written tickets paid at a *delegación*, never on the spot. And the **off-app Uber pickup** scam at Polanco (Masaryk), Plaza Garibaldi, and Zona Rosa late nights uses drivers who message "app broken, WhatsApp please," cancel, and re-quote 2–3× cash; cancel the ride yourself and rebook.

A safe default: pay Uber or Didi in-app only with a card linked before arrival; verify the plate matches the app; eat off the Zócalo perimeter where Roma Norte and Condesa run posted menus at honest prices; use ATMs only inside HSBC, Banamex, Santander, BBVA, or Citibanamex branches between 9 AM and 4 PM and decline every "home currency conversion" prompt; carry a laminated passport photocopy for any *control* and never surrender the original. Call **911** for any emergency, the *Policía Turística* CDMX desk on +52 55 4891 1166 (English), and file a *denuncia* at any **Procuraduría General de Justicia** branch within 24 hours for chargeback documentation. The Policía Turística headquarters is at **Calle Florencia 20, Colonia Juárez, +52 55 5208 1030** — five minutes from the Ángel de la Independencia.
