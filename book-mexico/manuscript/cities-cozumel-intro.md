Cozumel is the Caribbean reef-diving capital of Mexico and the largest cruise port on the Riviera Maya — more than 3 million cruise passengers a year arriving across three piers (Puerta Maya, Punta Langosta, and the International Pier) on what is, for most visitors, a single 8-hour shore day. The scam ecosystem is shaped by that compressed window. It concentrates on three corridors: the **cruise piers** themselves, the **San Miguel downtown strip** along Avenida Rafael E. Melgar (the malecón), and the eco-park gates at **Chankanaab** and **Punta Sur**. Cozumel has no Uber and no Didi — the taxi union is protected, and SITUR is the only licensed operator, with rate cards posted at every pier. There is no dedicated tourist-police desk; pressure incidents route through **911** and the local Cozumel police at **+52 987 872 0092**, with the regional press covered in *Riviera Maya News*, *The Yucatan Times*, and Spanish-language *Por Esto!* and *Novedades Quintana Roo*.

The **cruise-arrival taxi flat-rate to Punta Sur** and its sibling overcharges across the pier rate card are the volume risk. The posted SITUR fare from Puerta Maya to San Miguel is **MX$200**, to Chankanaab **MX$250**, and to Punta Sur **MX$400** — quoted in pesos on a board you can photograph at the stand. Pier-front "authorized" sellers quote US$40–$80 per leg and bundle "pier + snorkel + beach club transfer" packages at US$150 per person against a real cost of roughly US$25 in taxi plus US$30 at the beach club gate. The defense is to walk to the SITUR rate card before any conversation, photograph it, and insist on a yellow SITUR or COZTAX-marked taxi at the posted fare.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **snorkel-set "cracked-mask" damage-fee at malecón rentals** charges US$25–$50 for damage on returned equipment that arrived already worn, and pier-front operator booths sell "private Palancar reef" boats at US$400 per diver when reputable Google 4.7-plus shops (Scuba Club Cozumel, Deep Blue, Papa Hog's, Aldora Divers, Scuba Dogs) run 2-tank dives at US$90–$130. The **Punta Sur "private guide" overcharge inside the park** packages a US$40-per-person "mandatory guide" onto a US$18 gate fee that requires no guide at all, with the same "eco-conservation donation" demand at the exit that the cenote-road touts run on the mainland. And the **San Miguel main-strip Taxco-silver "antique" markup** along Avenida Melgar sells silver-plated brass at .950-stamped prices when genuine Mexican sterling is .925 — the same shops bundle "authentic Cuban cigar" sales at US$45 per single when the government-licensed **Casa del Tabaco Cubano** runs US$10–$25, and "añejo reposado" at US$80 that is silver tequila tinted with food coloring.

A safe default: photograph the SITUR rate card at your pier the moment you step off the ship; book Chankanaab (US$40–$45) and Punta Sur (US$18) directly at the gate, not through a pier-bundle seller; book dives only with PADI/SSI 5-Star Google 4.7-plus shops; verify any silver piece's .925 hallmark with a magnifying glass and buy tequila only at Chedraui or a CRT-certified liquor store; verify the gas-station pump resets to zero before any attendant fills the tank, state the grade and the peso amount in Spanish, and count change before driving off; and call **911** or **Cozumel Police +52 987 872 0092** the moment a pier-side or downtown approach escalates. The Mr Sancho's "free transfer" hidden minimum and similar beach-club bundles are best avoided by booking direct at **mrsanchos.com** or **playamia.com** for US$30–$49 all-inclusive instead of the US$120-plus pier package.
