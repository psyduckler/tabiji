# Appendix B: Post-Scam Recovery Playbook {-}

If something in this book has happened to you, this appendix is where you start. The actions below are organized by urgency, not by severity. Even if you are not sure yet how serious the scam was, working through the first two timelines will put you in the best possible position to recover your money, your evidence, and your peace of mind.

## In the first fifteen minutes

Leave the venue. If you are physically inside a place where you were scammed — a Cancún time-share sales room, a Tulum beach restaurant with a surprise bill, a Mexico City *sitio*-taxi return desk, a Cabo jet-ski rental return — do not argue, do not try to negotiate, and do not pay with a second form of payment. The single most important thing is to get yourself out of the environment and somewhere quiet.

Do not sign anything, including receipts or credit-card slips. A signed slip is much harder to dispute than one you refused to sign. If staff are blocking your exit, dial **911** — Mexico's universal emergency line, operational nationwide since 2017, with English-capable dispatchers in major tourist cities — or walk into the nearest OXXO, Starbucks, Sanborns, 7-Eleven, or a hotel lobby and ask the staff to call police. For medical emergencies, **911** is also the ambulance line (the Cruz Roja); the older **065** still works for Cruz Roja direct.

Screenshot everything you can, as discreetly as possible: the bill, the sign outside the venue, the street intersection (a Google Maps dropped-pin is ideal), the shop's *aviso de privacidad* and RFC tax-ID (Mexican retail shops are required by law to display these), the card terminal, and the face of the person who served you if you can do it without escalating. The information you capture in these fifteen minutes is the evidence you will use for weeks afterward.

If your bag or wallet was just stolen in the street, shout *"¡auxilio!"* ("help!") or *"¡ladrón!"* ("thief!") — Mexican bystanders and nearby shopkeepers will often intervene. If you are on the Mexico City Metro or Metrobús, the station supervisor's office is the closest authority; station staff can call police and hold CCTV footage.

## Within the first hour

Walk to the nearest **Policía Turística** desk if one is within ten minutes (Mexico City has a desk at Florencia 20, Col. Juárez; Cancún has one at +52 998 884 1107; smaller cities rely on the regular *Policía Municipal* or *Estatal*); otherwise, to the nearest **Ministerio Público** (MP). Say "*Quiero presentar una denuncia*" — "I want to file a complaint." You will walk out with an *acta de denuncia* — a printed, stamped report with a case number. This is the document your credit-card issuer and travel insurer will demand.

The major city Policía Turística and SECTUR Tourist Assistance contacts (full directory in Appendix C):
- **Mexico City:** Policía Turística HQ at Calle Florencia 20, Col. Juárez. +52 55 5208 1030. English-capable.
- **Cancún:** Policía Turística at Av. Tulum, Centro. +52 998 884 1107. English by default.
- **SECTUR national tourist-assistance line:** **078** (free from any Mexican phone). Operates 24/7 in Spanish and English; can dispatch to local authorities and arbitrate disputes with SECTUR-registered operators.
- **PROFECO consumer-protection complaint line:** **800 468 8722** (free) or +52 55 5568 8722 from abroad. For merchant misrepresentation, time-share fraud, overcharge cases, and *factura* disputes.
- **Anonymous tip line (*denuncia anónima*):** **089** (free, nationwide).

**Freeze the card that was used.** International emergency lines for the major card issuers are listed in Appendix C. These numbers work from Mexico and route you to an English-speaking representative. Have your card number (or the last four digits) and the approximate transaction amount ready before you call. Additionally, call your bank's fraud line directly — fraud desks sometimes freeze faster than the Visa/Mastercard emergency lines.

## Within the first twenty-four hours

**File a chargeback with your card issuer.** Use the *acta de denuncia* case number as documentation. Chargebacks initiated within twenty-four hours of the transaction have a significantly higher success rate than those filed a week later. Visa and Mastercard both offer strong chargeback rights for unauthorized or materially misrepresented charges — one reason we recommend credit over debit for every Mexico trip.

**Open a PROFECO complaint** at **profeco.gob.mx** or by calling **800 468 8722**. PROFECO is Mexico's federal consumer-protection agency and has specific authority over time-share contracts, merchant misrepresentation, *factura* fraud, and tourist overcharges. A formal PROFECO complaint sometimes produces restitution that police cannot — particularly for time-share contracts, which under Mexican federal law (Ley Federal de Protección al Consumidor) include a five-day mandatory cooling-off period during which you can rescind any contract for any reason. PROFECO routinely annuls Cancún and Cabo time-share contracts on this basis.

For **transport overcharging** — licensed taxis at the airport, *sitio* operators, ferry-terminal touts — file a parallel complaint with the SECTUR national line at **078** and the local tourism office. With the license-plate number (always photograph it before getting out), authorities can identify the driver, issue fines, and sometimes produce compensation.

If your passport was photographed, held, or taken by anyone involved, notify your embassy or consulate immediately. Passport data tied to a scam can show up in fraud attempts weeks later, and your embassy will advise on whether a replacement is needed. The US embassy in Mexico City and consulates in Cancún, Guadalajara, Mérida, Monterrey, Puerto Vallarta, San Miguel de Allende, and a dozen other cities handle these cases routinely. UK, Canadian, Irish, and Australian missions are listed in Appendix C.

Email your travel-insurance provider with a summary of what happened, timestamps, the *acta de denuncia* case number, and photographs of the bill. Even if you are not sure whether the loss is covered, opening the claim early protects your position.

If the scam involved an off-platform booking (a roadside cenote-tour chalkboard, a beach-tout boat charter, a WhatsApp time-share invitation), open the dispute on the booking platform (Booking.com, Expedia, Airbnb, Viator, GetYourGuide) as well as with your card issuer.

## Within the first week

**File a formal consumer-protection complaint** with **PROFECO** at **profeco.gob.mx/Buzon/PortalDenuncias.aspx** or by calling **800 468 8722** (English-capable for tourist cases). This is where Mexico's most-effective regulatory pressure on tourist scams lives. PROFECO does not always recover individual losses, but its actions against repeat-offender time-share operators, hotel chains, and tour packagers are one of the few effective pressures on the scam economy. Time-share contracts in particular have a high success rate: under federal law, you have a **five-day cooling-off period** during which you can rescind any contract without penalty. PROFECO walks tourists through that rescission routinely.

For **transport overcharging** — licensed taxis at airport ranks, *sitio* operators with regulated rates — file a parallel complaint with the local *Coordinación de Transporte* or *Movilidad* office, depending on the city:
- **Mexico City:** SEMOVI (Secretaría de Movilidad CDMX). semovi.cdmx.gob.mx, +52 55 5208 9999.
- **Cancún / Quintana Roo:** Instituto de Movilidad de Quintana Roo. +52 998 887 4060.
- **Puerto Vallarta / Jalisco:** SEMOV Jalisco. +52 33 3030 1900.
- **Cabo / Baja California Sur:** Dirección General de Transporte BCS. +52 612 124 0100.

For **banking and payment disputes**, escalate to **CONDUSEF** (Comisión Nacional para la Protección y Defensa de los Usuarios de Servicios Financieros) at **condusef.gob.mx** or **800 999 8080**. CONDUSEF handles disputes with Mexican-issued cards, ATMs, and banks, and operates as Mexico's financial-consumer ombudsman.

Submit the full travel-insurance claim with all evidence gathered so far. Include the business's name and address, the exact transaction details, the *acta de denuncia* case number, the PROFECO case number, and the chargeback reference.

Consider leaving a neutral, factual review on Google Maps or TripAdvisor. Describe what happened, the amounts involved, and the date, without emotional language or accusations. Do not name individual staff members; describe the business by its official name and location only. Mexican defamation law has been criminally decriminalized since 2007 in Mexico City and most states, but civil-defamation suits remain — stick to verifiable facts and receipts, and you are protected.

## If your phone or SIM was stolen

Call your carrier immediately to suspend the line. The major Mexican carriers:
- **Telcel:** dial **\*111** (in Mexico) or +52 55 2581 4811 (from abroad).
- **AT&T Mexico:** dial **\*611** or +52 55 4170 8000.
- **Movistar Mexico:** dial **\*611** or +52 55 4188 8000.

If you are on a tourist eSIM (Airalo, Saily, Holafly), the app will let you suspend the line in a few taps; do that before the *acta de denuncia*.

File an *acta de denuncia* at the nearest MP and include your phone's **IMEI** number (the fifteen-digit serial; find it on the original box, in your Apple or Google account, or by dialing \*#06# *before* the theft). The IMEI lets police flag the device and is required for any insurance claim.

Lock and wipe remotely via Apple's Find My or Google's Find My Device. Do this before the police report — a wiped phone cannot leak your email and banking apps while you wait in line.

Change passwords immediately for any account whose app was installed on the phone. Start with email; everything else resets off it.

## If the scam involved drink-spiking or physical harm

Go to a hospital before you go to the police. Toxicology tests have a short window and cannot be run retroactively. A list of 24/7 hospitals with English-speaking staff in each city is in Appendix C. At the hospital, ask for a *examen toxicológico* (toxicology screen); Mexican private hospitals capable of international care (Hospital ABC, Médica Sur, Hospital Español, Hospital Galenia in Cancún, Amerimed in Cabo) run these routinely and will print a report you can use as evidence.

For **drug-planting scams** — where a stranger claims to have "found" a substance in your possession — stay calm, do not resist, and dial **911** before any money changes hands. Real Mexican narcotics cases go through the *fiscalía* with written documentation, photographed evidence, and a stamped *acta* you sign. Demands for cash to "make it go away" are the signature of a scam, not of legitimate enforcement.

**Emergency numbers in Mexico:**
- **911** — universal emergency (police, ambulance, fire) — operational nationwide since 2017
- **065** — Cruz Roja direct (still operational, redundant with 911)
- **078** — SECTUR national tourist-assistance line (Spanish + English)
- **089** — anonymous tip line (*denuncia anónima*)
- **800 468 8722** — PROFECO consumer protection
- **800 999 8080** — CONDUSEF financial-services protection
- **800 903 9200** — INM (Instituto Nacional de Migración) for visa / FMM card issues

Your embassy can help coordinate English-speaking medical care and, if your passport was involved, coordinate a replacement. Consular registration — STEP (US), Smartraveller (Australia), LOCATE (UK), ROCA (Canada), Irish consular registration — is worth doing before you travel, specifically for situations like this.
