Puerto Escondido is the Oaxacan Pacific surf town that turned digital-nomad capital — anchored by **Zicatela Beach** (the "Mexican Pipeline"), Puerto Escondido Airport (PXM), and a coastline that runs from **Playa Principal** west to *Playa Carrizalillo* and south to the **La Punta** surf strip. The scam ecosystem concentrates on three corridors: **PXM arrivals and the Oaxaca-City road transfer**, the **Zicatela** and **La Punta** surf-rental and restaurant strips, and the **Playa Principal** boat-tour launches. Patterns are documented in *El Imparcial de Oaxaca* and *NVI Noticias*. The Oaxacan coast is one of the calmer regions on the Mexican Pacific, but the 2020–2025 surf and digital-nomad boom has outpaced legitimate accommodation supply, and the commercial-pressure ecosystem now resembles Cabo more than the sleepy fishing village it was a decade ago.

The **PXM and Oaxaca-transfer taxi overcharge** is the volume risk. Arrivals-curb kiosks quote MX$600–$1,000 for a Zicatela transfer that runs MX$250–$350 via the official **Transporte Terrestre** prepaid booth inside the terminal, and MX$150–$250 via Uber when supply is available. The Oaxaca-City connection is more dangerous: rental agencies offering "one-way to Puerto Escondido" tack on a MX$3,000–$5,000 "drop fee" against a fair MX$1,200–$2,000, and the 7-hour mountain road is not safe to drive at night — book the **ADO** *primera-clase* night bus at MX$380–$480 instead.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Zicatela surf-school and board-rental "fin damage" deposit-hold** sells "private lessons US$80" to non-certified beach-approach "instructors" and books beginners onto Zicatela's 8-to-25-foot expert-only waves (beginners belong at La Punta or *Playa Carrizalillo*) — book only at Google 4.7+ schools (Puerto Surf School, Oasis Surf, Mexpipe) at MX$500–$800 per two-hour lesson and photograph the board condition before leaving the shop. The **Playa Principal unlicensed-boat dolphin and turtle tour** sells "private dolphin boats" at MX$2,500 when reputable group operators (Lalo Ecotours, Hidden Voyages) run the same trip at MX$400–$700 per person and *Manialtepec* lagoon bioluminescence at MX$500–$800 (August through December only, dark-moon nights); refuse every "guaranteed sighting" upcharge and "swim with wild dolphins" offer (illegal). And the **Zicatela and La Punta restaurant tourist-menu padding** runs English-only menus at 2-to-3x local prices and an 18% pre-added gratuity plus a tip line — eat at *Mercado* Benito Juárez at MX$60–$120, or Almoraduz and La Hostería off the beachfront where the menu prints in pesos.

A safe default: book PXM transfers at the **Transporte Terrestre** prepaid booth; ride **ADO** *primera-clase* between Oaxaca City and Puerto Escondido; book accommodation only through Booking.com, Hotels.com, or Airbnb — never bank-transfer to an Instagram or WhatsApp surf-house DM; buy your own snorkel mask at OXXO for MX$150 instead of renting at *Playa Carrizalillo*; agree per-kilogram price for any seafood in writing; and call **911** for emergencies, the Puerto Escondido *Policía Turística* at **+52 954 582 0175**, or **078** for the federal SECTUR tourist line. The Oaxaca *Fiscalía General* handles formal *Ministerio Público* (**MP**) complaints.
