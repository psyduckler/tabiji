Tulum is the cliffside Mayan-ruins town turned bohemian beach destination — and, since the new Felipe Carrillo Puerto Airport (TQO) opened in December 2023, the fastest-rising scam corridor on the Riviera Maya. The ecosystem concentrates on three corridors: the **cenote road** (the unmarked turn-offs along Highway 307 toward Dos Ojos, Gran Cenote, Calavera, and Casa Cenote), the **Beach Road** (Carretera Tulum-Boca Paila) running south from the ruins past Papaya Playa, Nomade, Be Tulum, and Azulik, and the **Tulum Archaeological Zone** approach itself. Patterns are covered regularly in *Riviera Maya News*, *The Yucatan Times*, and Spanish-language *Por Esto!* and *Novedades Quintana Roo*. Tulum has no dedicated tourist-police desk; pressure incidents route through **911** and the **Quintana Roo state *fiscalía*** at **+52 998 311 5300**, which has issued multiple advisories during 2024–2025 about a recurring "park ranger" entry-fee shakedown on the cenote road.

The **cenote-road "park ranger" entry-fee shakedown** is the volume risk. Touts in approximated uniforms intercept rental cars at unmarked turn-offs demanding "ecological conservation fees" of MX$500 per person on top of legitimate gate prices — Gran Cenote is **MX$500**, Dos Ojos **MX$350**, Calavera **MX$250**, Cristal **MX$250**, Casa Cenote **MX$150** — paid at the official ticket window only. The same template runs at the Tulum ruins themselves: the legitimate INAH entry is **MX$100** with no mandatory guide and no "conservation surcharge," and the official parking lot is **MX$150** with a government-issued receipt. Anything else demanded at the gate is fake.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Beach Road no-price seafood overcharge** runs USD-only menus at Papaya Playa, Nomade, and the Azulik cluster where breakfast is US$18, mains run US$35-plus, an "environmental fee" of MX$150 per person appears on the bill, a 18% service charge stacks under a tip line, and credit-card surcharges of 10–18% are announced at payment — bill switcheroos between the itemized receipt and the card-swipe total are common, in the MX$300–$1,000 range. The **cliffside "Mayan blessing" *chamán* donation** and "mandatory community donation MX$200" approaches operate at the ruins exit and at cenote entries, packaged as cultural respect but extracted in cash with no receipt. And the **Tulum-Pueblo ATV-tour deposit-hold and INAH "Express Line" ticket markup** at the ruins turn what should be a self-guided MX$100 visit into a MX$2,000 "combined access" upsell with commission-stop lunch.

A safe default: drive yourself to cenotes (or hire an Uber day-rate from Playa) and pay only at the gate window; book guided tours only through Google 4.7-plus operators (Jugo Cenote Experiences, Mex-On Adventures, Yuchitur); eat in Downtown Tulum along Avenida Tulum and Avenida Satélite — Charly's Vegan Tacos, Antojitos La Chiapaneca, El Tacoqueto — at roughly a third of Beach Zone prices; pay your **Visitax** at **visitax.gob.mx** before you fly and ignore every "tax agent" at TQO; arrive at the ruins for the 8 AM opening before the tour-bus flood; and if a uniformed approach escalates on the cenote road or Beach Road, ask for a badge with name and officer number, never surrender your passport, and call **911** or the Quintana Roo *fiscalía* at **+52 998 311 5300**. File any *denuncia* at the Tulum **Ministerio Público** (**+52 984 871 2109**) within 48 hours.
