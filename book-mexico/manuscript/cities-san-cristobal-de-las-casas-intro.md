San Cristóbal de las Casas is the Chiapas highland *Pueblo Mágico* — the 2,200-meter colonial town in a valley ringed by *Tzotzil* and *Tzeltal* villages, where the cobbled *Centro* opens onto Real de Guadalupe and the Andador Eclesiástico, and where a half-hour *colectivo* ride brings you to San Juan Chamula's living-Maya church or to Zinacantán's textile-weaving cooperatives. Chiapas is poorer and more remote than Yucatán or Jalisco, and the scams here are less about volume and more about cultural mediation — tours that promise indigenous "experiences" they should not be selling. The risk concentrates in three corridors: the **Real de Guadalupe and Andador Eclesiástico** tourist strip through the *Centro*, the **village-tour day-trips** to San Juan Chamula and Zinacantán, and the long **Tuxtla-to-San Cristóbal** highway from TGZ airport. *Cuarto Poder* and *El Heraldo de Chiapas*, the Tuxtla-based dailies, have both covered the village-tour-operator landscape, and SECTUR's national tourist-assistance line **078** routes Chiapas calls to the Tuxtla-based Tourist Police desk at **+52 961 617 0550**.

The **Real de Guadalupe textile "all hand-woven" markup** is the volume risk. The tourist strip's craft shops sell mass-produced *huipiles*, machine-loomed *rebozos*, and copal-resin "amber" jewellery as authentic Tzotzil cooperative goods at MX$1,500-4,000, where genuine hand-woven pieces from the Sna Jolobil weavers' collective on the Santo Domingo plaza run MX$800-2,500 with documented village-cooperative provenance. Amber requires a UV-light test — real Chiapas amber glows blue under ultraviolet; copal does not. The defense is to walk one block off Real de Guadalupe to Sna Jolobil (Tzotzil cooperative), the Museo del Ámbar de Chiapas (certified amber dealers), and Café Museo Café on Ma. Adelina Flores for direct-from-farmer coffee at MX$180-400 per kilo. Refuse "authentic cooperative" claims that don't carry verifiable Sna Jolobil or Jolom Mayaetik certification.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **San Juan Chamula village-tour pitch** sold from kiosks along Real de Guadalupe runs US$150 per person for a "private tour" that small-group reputable operators (Alex & Raul Jaramillo Tours, Marvinjaime Tours, César Tours) sell for MX$400-800, often layered with "exclusive shaman ceremony US$100" and "Mayan blessing MX$300" pitches that should be refused outright — the rituals at Chamula's church are sacred, not tourist performances, and photography inside the church is a strict cultural taboo, not a fee. The **Palenque day-trip operator scene** runs unlicensed vans on the 200-kilometre, five-hour winding mountain road to the UNESCO ruins, sometimes with no insurance; legitimate operators (Explora Chiapas, Kukulcán Travel, Chiapas Selva Lacandona) charge MX$800-1,500 per person for the full Palenque-Agua-Azul-Misol-Ha day, and the official Palenque entrance is MX$130 total, not the MX$500-800 "mandatory guide" fee touts demand. And the **TGZ airport transfer kerb** quotes MX$1,200-2,000 for the one-hour highway drive into San Cristóbal when the ADO/OCC direct bus is MX$180 and the Transporte Terrestre prepaid booth is MX$450-600.

A safe default: take the ADO/OCC bus from TGZ to San Cristóbal (MX$180, the safest option), or book Transporte Terrestre at the official prepaid booth in arrivals. Eat off Real de Guadalupe at El Caldero, La Lupe, Las Lupes, or El Fogón de Jovel (MX$150-350 per person). Buy textiles at Sna Jolobil and amber only with the UV-blue-glow test or at the Museo del Ámbar's certified vendors. Book village tours only through cultural-sensitivity-credentialed operators with Google ratings above 4.7, and refuse every "shaman ceremony" pitch — they are not authentic and the offer is itself the tell. Use only bank-branch ATMs along Avenida 16 de Septiembre and Avenida Belisario Domínguez, always selecting "Continue in MX Pesos" on the dynamic-currency-conversion prompt; San Cristóbal's bank infrastructure is thin, so withdraw cash at Tuxtla before traveling up. For trouble, **Chiapas Tourist Police** is at **+52 961 617 0550**, the *fiscalía* in Chiapas handles the *denuncia*, the SECTUR line **078** runs in English and Spanish, and **911** is universal.
