Cancún is the Caribbean flagship of Mexican tourism — the highest-volume foreign-arrival city in the country, anchored by Cancún International (CUN) and the 22-kilometer barrier-island strip known as the **Hotel Zone** (Boulevard Kukulcán). The scam ecosystem concentrates on three corridors: the **CUN arrivals halls** (Terminals 2/3/4), the **Hotel Zone** from Punta Cancún south to Playa Delfines, and **Downtown Cancún** around Mercado 28 and Parque Las Palapas. Patterns have been documented for years in *Riviera Maya News*, *The Yucatan Times*, and Spanish-language *Por Esto!* and *Novedades Quintana Roo*, and the city is one of the few in the region to maintain a dedicated **Policía Turística** desk reachable at **+52 998 884 1107**.

The **CUN "authorized taxi" overcharge** is the volume risk. Sign-holders inside the arrivals hall quote US$80–$150 for a Hotel Zone transfer that pre-paid shuttle services (eTransfers, Canada Transfers) will run for US$15–$30, the ADO bus to the Hotel Zone terminal will run for MX$150, and Uber — legal at CUN since 2024 — will meter at MX$350–$500. "VIP airport taxi" booths quote MX$2,500 against a posted official Yellow Taxi rate of MX$900–$1,200. The defense: book the shuttle online before you fly, walk past every sign-holder inside the terminal, and use the Uber app from the curb after collecting your luggage.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Hotel Zone time-share "free breakfast" sign-up** rotates tourists at resort welcome desks (Iberostar, Palace, Grand Fiesta Americana) into 3-to-5-hour high-pressure pitches with credit-card "holds" that PROFECO has annulled hundreds of contracts on under Mexico's 5-business-day cooling-off statute; the right move is to decline at the desk and never authorize a card hold. The **Hotel Zone ATM and front-desk DCC scam** runs on the "Would you like your home currency?" prompt — accept it and you lose 8–15% to dynamic currency conversion, with hotel exchange desks padding the rate to MX$16.5 against an interbank rate near MX$18.5. And the **Mercado 28 "antique silver" markup** sells silver-plated brass at .950-stamped prices when genuine Mexican sterling is .925 — the same template runs at La Isla and Plaza Forum souvenir stalls.

A safe default: pay your **Visitax** (MX$271 per person) at **visitax.gob.mx** before you fly and ignore every "tax agent" at the airport; pre-book your CUN transfer or use Uber; eat in Downtown Cancún on Avenida Yaxchilán or around Parque Las Palapas instead of the Hotel Zone tourist menus; use bank-branch ATMs inside HSBC, Banamex, Santander, or BBVA on Av. Tulum and always select "Continue in MX$"; and call the **Cancún Policía Turística** at **+52 998 884 1107** or **911** the moment a uniformed approach escalates. The US Consulate Cancún direct line is **+52 998 883 0272**.
