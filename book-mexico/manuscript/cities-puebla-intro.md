Puebla is Mexico's gastronomic capital and its *talavera* heartland, a UNESCO-listed colonial grid two hours south of CDMX, and its scam ecosystem clusters along three corridors: the **Centro Histórico** (Zócalo, Avenida 5 de Mayo, the El Parián craft market), the **CAPU bus terminal** on arrival from Mexico City's TAPO, and the **Cholula day-trip route** twenty minutes west, where the world's largest pyramid by volume sits beneath the Iglesia de los Remedios. *El Universal* and *Milenio Puebla* have covered the CAPU *sitio* overcharge and the *talavera* certification fraud through 2025, and PROFECO keeps a Centro desk for bill-padding *denuncias*.

The **CAPU bus-terminal taxi overcharge** is the volume risk the moment a CDMX day-tripper steps off an ADO Primera Clase coach. The terminal's interior *sitio* counters quote MX$400–$800 to Centro Histórico Puebla, fifteen kilometres away, when an Uber from the main street outside meters at MX$100–$180 and a *colectivo* runs MX$60–$80 per person. The same counters happily take a card "for deposit" on the way to cloning it, and the layered upsell is a "combined Mexico City–Puebla–Cholula tour US$200" bundle that costs under US$60 if you buy ADO at the official window and Uber the rest. Walk past the *sitio* line and book the ride yourself.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Cholula "mandatory archaeological guide" pitch** at the Great Pyramid quotes MX$500 per person for the tunnel walk and MX$100 to enter the church on top — both invented; the real INAH ticket is MX$95 and includes the tunnels, the archaeological zone, the museum, and free walk-up to the Iglesia de los Remedios. The **El Parián *talavera* "authentic certified" markup** sells MX$2,000–$6,000 plates without the kiln-embedded **DO4** *Denominación de Origen* stamp — much of it Oaxacan or Portuguese-style majolica — when Uriarte Talavera (4 Norte 911), Talavera de la Reyna, and La Casa del Café (6 Sur 301) carry certified pieces with transparent pricing and free workshop tours. And the **Zócalo-perimeter restaurant tourist menu** runs no posted prices, charges MX$600 for *mole poblano* (real range MX$150–$350) and MX$600 for in-season *chile en nogada* (real range MX$250–$350), and tucks an 18% gratuity into the printed total before the tip line.

A safe default: book ADO or Estrella Roja only at the official counter inside TAPO or on ado.com.mx; Uber from the CAPU main street, not the *sitio* queue; eat at Fonda de Santa Clara (3 Poniente 307), El Mural de los Poblanos (16 de Septiembre 506), or Sacristía de la Compañía (6 Sur 304) where prices are posted in pesos, and request the bill in MX$; buy *talavera* only with the kiln-embedded **DO4** stamp visible (a printed sticker is the tell); withdraw cash only inside HSBC, Banamex, Santander, BBVA, or Banorte branches on Avenida 5 de Mayo between 9 AM and 4 PM. **Popocatépetl** activity is a separate hazard tracked by CENAPRED — check ash-fall advisories before any Cholula day. Call **911** for any emergency, the **Policía Turística Puebla** on **+52 222 237 2211**, and file a *denuncia* at the **Fiscalía General del Estado de Puebla** *Ministerio Público* within 48 hours.
