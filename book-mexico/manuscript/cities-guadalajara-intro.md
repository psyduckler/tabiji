Guadalajara is Mexico's mariachi heart and tequila country — the Jalisco capital where the 60-kilometre drive west to the agave fields around the town of Tequila has been a national pilgrimage since the 1940s, and where the *Plaza de los Mariachis* off Calzada Independencia still books trios for Saturday-night photographs. The scams converge in three corridors: the **Centro Histórico** axis from Plaza de Armas through the Catedral and on to Mercado San Juan de Dios (the largest indoor market in Mexico), the **Tlaquepaque and Tonalá** craft-tourism satellites on the city's southeast edge, and the **GDL airport** ground-transport zone, where the long-running taxi-mafia situation shifted in late 2024 when Uber became formally legal at the kerb. *Mural* and *El Informador* both covered the Uber-legalisation transition, and the US Consulate General in Guadalajara — the largest US consular presence outside Mexico City, at **+52 33 3268 2100** — has issued repeated alerts on Centro Histórico late-night police-document checks targeting foreign visitors.

The **GDL airport "authorized taxi" overcharge** is the volume risk. Inside arrivals, sign-holders and kiosks branded as "official" quote MX$800-1,500 for transfers to Centro Histórico that legitimately run MX$250-350 by Uber or MX$450-600 by the Transporte Terrestre prepaid booth. A handful of drivers still tell arrivals that "Uber doesn't work at GDL" — this stopped being true in late 2024. The defense is to book Uber on the airport Wi-Fi after collecting luggage, walk to the signposted pickup or to the OXXO gas station 400 meters from the terminal where Uber drivers cluster for efficiency, and pay in-app. Verify the licence plate against the app before boarding and never negotiate a cash fare off-app.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Tlaquepaque artisan strip** along Independencia and Juárez marks identical ceramics, glass, and "925 silver" pieces three to five times the price they sell for at the Tonalá Sunday market 8 kilometres away, with "silver certificate" stickers slapped over silver-plated brass — the .925 hallmark must be stamped into the metal itself, verifiable with a magnifying glass. The **Plaza de los Mariachis "free song" approach** is the classic: an unsolicited trio strikes up next to your table at one of the perimeter cantinas, and a MX$400 demand follows the final note. Agree per-song price (MX$100-200) before any music starts. And the **Tequila day-trip commission tour** sold by hotel concierges runs US$250 per person on packages that real Google 4.7+ operators (Casa Sauza Tour, Tequila Experience Tour, Realize Tours) sell for US$60-120 — and the José Cuervo Express train, the genuine experience, is US$200 fixed at josecuervoexpress.com.

A safe default: Uber to and from GDL airport with in-app payment only; carry a money belt or zipped crossbody bag in Centro Histórico and Mercado San Juan de Dios, and never set a phone on a café table along Avenida Chapultepec; agree mariachi prices in writing before the first song; buy tequila at Chedraui (CRT-certified bottles MX$500-1,500) rather than Tlaquepaque gift shops; and use only bank-branch ATMs (HSBC, Banamex, Santander, BBVA, CIBanco) along Avenidas Juárez, Hidalgo, and Américas, always selecting "Continue in MX Pesos" to defeat the dynamic-currency-conversion padding. For trouble, **Tourist Police Guadalajara** is at **+52 33 3668 1600**, the **US Consulate General Guadalajara** at **+52 33 3268 2100**, the *fiscalía* in Jalisco handles the *denuncia* (file within 48 hours), and **911** is universal.
