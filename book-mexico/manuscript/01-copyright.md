## Disclaimer

**This book is for informational purposes only.** It is not legal, financial, medical, or insurance advice. Scam patterns evolve, legal protections vary by country of travel, and venue-specific details may change between editions.

References to streets, plazas, beaches, neighborhoods, and business types describe patterns of scam activity reported by Mexican newspapers, federal and state authorities, and travelers at the time of writing. Mention of a specific area does not imply that all venues or individuals operating there engage in the described behavior. Where this book references named operators or establishments, it does so on the basis of published news coverage or direct consumer reviews; any such reference is intended as consumer information and not as an accusation of wrongdoing against any individual business that was not the subject of a specific cited report.

Information is current as of **2026 Edition**. For newly reported scams and live updates, visit **[tabiji.ai/scams](https://tabiji.ai/scams)**.

If you are victimized, consult local authorities (dial **911** for the universal Mexican emergency line — police, ambulance, and fire — operational nationwide since 2017 with English-capable dispatchers in major tourist cities; **078** reaches the SECTUR national tourist-assistance line; **089** is the anonymous *denuncia* — police-tip — line. For consumer-protection complaints, **PROFECO** (Procuraduría Federal del Consumidor) at **800 468 8722** handles merchant misrepresentation, time-share fraud, and overcharge cases — addresses for filing in person are in the appendix), your embassy or consulate, your credit card issuer, and your travel insurance provider. Do not rely on this book as your sole source of guidance.

A note on place names and accents: Mexican Spanish uses tildes and acute accents (é, í, ó, ú, ñ) that English keyboards omit but that change meaning and pronunciation. We use them throughout — *Mérida*, *Cancún*, *Tulum*, *Yucatán*, *Zócalo*, *Teotihuacán*, *Popocatépetl* — to keep the spellings recognizable to readers who follow these places in Mexican press, on Google Maps, or on official signage.
