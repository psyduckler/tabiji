Isla Mujeres is the small Caribbean day-trip island off the southern endpoint of the Cancún Hotel Zone, reached by 15-minute Ultramar ferry from Puerto Juárez or Playa Tortugas. The island has no cars for tourists — golf carts are the universal transport — and the scam ecosystem clusters in three corridors: the **Puerto Juárez and Gran Puerto ferry approaches** on the mainland, **Avenida Rueda Medina and the Hidalgo Avenue pedestrian strip** in the island Centro, and the **Playa Norte beach-club row** plus the **Punta Sur / Garrafón snorkel-park axis** at the southern tip. The Quintana Roo *fiscalía* and SECTUR's tourist hotline **078** handle the bulk of complaints, and traveler reports through 2025 document the same patterns repeating month after month.

The **golf-cart rental pre-damage and "scratch claim" fraud** is the volume risk and the iconic Isla scam. Legitimate 24-hour rental at Google 4.6+ shops (Cart Cecy, Ciros Golf Cart Rental, Kokonuts Rentals) runs MX$800–$1,200 with a US$100 credit-card pre-authorization deposit. The 2025 scams are unphotographed scratches at pickup billed as "tourist damage" at return for US$200–$800; "spare key" shops that retain a copy, move the cart overnight, and claim "theft" with MX$30,000+ replacement; and ferry-arrival touts pitching MX$1,800 "tourist rate" carts at twice the Google-reviewed price. The defense: photograph and video every scratch, dent, tire and floor panel before driving off, refuse cash deposits, keep the only key yourself, and require the same agent walk around the cart with you at return.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Puerto Juárez and Hotel Zone ferry-tout overcharge** sells "VIP express ferry" tickets at MX$1,500 when Ultramar's real round-trip is MX$590 from Puerto Juárez or MX$780 from Playa Tortugas; WhatsApp resellers offer "discount passes" at MX$400 that are fake or already-used, and "private boats" from Zócalo beach are unlicensed and uninsured. The **Punta Sur and Garrafón "combined entry" pad** quotes MX$1,500 packages when the real Parque Garrafón de Castilla is MX$280 (snorkel and life jacket included) and Punta Sur is MX$60 self-guided — with a "mandatory snorkel guide MX$500" and "Mayan ruins guide MX$400" tacked on for things that need neither. And the **Hidalgo Avenue tourist-menu trick** runs English-only menus in dollars at two to three times Spanish prices, with fish tacos at MX$120 versus MX$35 two blocks inland at Javi's Cantina, El Poc Chuc, or La Lomita, plus "service 18%" pre-added beside a tip line, and "Mexican silver" at MX$2,500–$8,000 that lacks a .925 hallmark.

A safe default: buy ferry tickets only at the official Ultramar windows at Puerto Juárez or Playa Tortugas — printed QR ticket, card or cash, no "VIP" tier exists; rent golf carts only at Google 4.6+ shops with a credit-card pre-auth deposit and a timestamped photo set; treat Playa Norte as the free public beach it is and refuse "VIP bed" upsells at Zama, Na Balam, and Buho's; book MUSA snorkel through Carbyjo, Squalo, or Contoy Adventures with the captain's *Secretaría de Marina* license visibly displayed; and call **911** for emergencies, **078** for the federal SECTUR tourist line, or the Quintana Roo *fiscalía* through SECTUR for written complaints, ideally from inside the Ultramar terminal or a hotel lobby.
