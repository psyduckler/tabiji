Puerto Vallarta is Mexico's longest-established Pacific-coast beach destination, set on the curve of Banderas Bay in Jalisco, and runs the country's most-aggressive timeshare-presentation ecosystem alongside the standard tourist-menu and taxi patterns. The scam ecosystem clusters in three corridors: **Puerto Vallarta International Airport (PVR) arrivals**, where the area immediately past customs has been nicknamed the **"Shark Tank"** by 2025 traveler reports; the **Romántica Zone** (the Old Town strip running through Olas Altas and Basilio Badillo) and the adjacent **Malecón** waterfront promenade; and the **Nuevo Vallarta and Marina Vallarta resort corridor** north of town, where the largest timeshare properties sit. *Vallarta Daily* and *Tribuna de la Bahía* cover the patterns regularly, the Jalisco *fiscalía* handles formal complaints, and Puerto Vallarta has its own US Consular Agency on top of the SECTUR **078** hotline.

The **PVR airport "Shark Tank" timeshare ambush and taxi overcharge** is the volume risk and the first scam most travelers meet. Polo-shirted salespeople posing as "tourist information" or "hotel transfer" agents line the corridor right after customs; the opening line — "Where are you staying? I have a special discount" — leads to "free transport" in exchange for a "90-minute presentation" that runs three to five hours of rotating closers, "sign today or lose the discount" pressure, and US$5,000 deposit-traps. Vidanta Nuevo Vallarta, Garza Blanca, Villa del Palmar, and the Villa Group are the named properties in 2025 community warnings, with one thread titled "Don't go to Garza Blanca - massive fraud schemes and bait." The "authorized taxi" counters inside the terminal quote MX$800–$1,500 to the Romántica Zone when the real Uber fare is MX$250–$400. The defense: walk straight through the Shark Tank without stopping, do not answer "where are you staying," and book Uber on airport Wi-Fi after exiting — the legal pickup point is across the pedestrian bridge at the Sheraton, 100 m from the terminal.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Romántica Zone and Malecón restaurant bill-padding** runs English-only menus at two to three times Spanish prices (fish tacos MX$180 on the Malecón versus MX$50 at locally-oriented Romántica spots), "gratuity 18%" pre-added beside a tip line, "credit-card processing 10–15%" announced at the bill, "seafood market price" verbal quotes at MX$1,200–$2,500 per lobster on Olas Altas, and "complimentary" botanas that arrive at MX$250 each — eat instead at El Arrayán on Güemes 277, La Leche on Marcela Valadez 195, Barcelona Tapas on Matamoros 906, or Joe Jack's Fish Shack. The **Marietas Islands and Malecón "free tequila tasting" tout** ends at a single high-markup tequila shop where bottles are MX$1,800 versus MX$400–$800 at Chedraui, and beach vendors on Playa Los Muertos run "free henna" that becomes MX$400, "friendly photographer" tips of MX$200–$500, and mariachi swarms demanding MX$300–$500 after playing without invitation. And the **Nuevo Vallarta resort timeshare "breakfast presentation"** continues the airport pattern indoors, with The Villa Group asking guests to "write down personal goals" as a manipulation hook and "closing rooms" with financial counsel pushing mortgage-style commitments — Mexican law gives a five-business-day cooling-off period enforceable through PROFECO at *profeco.gob.mx*.

A safe default: book Uber from PVR at the Sheraton bridge for MX$250–$400 to the Romántica Zone, ignore taxi-driver claims that "Uber is banned" (it is legal; only curbside terminal pickup is restricted); refuse every timeshare perk at airport, lobby, and pool deck; eat at named Romántica Zone restaurants and request bills in MX$ rather than padded USD; use bank-branch ATMs (HSBC, Banamex, Santander, BBVA) during business hours and always select "Continue in MX Pesos" at the DCC prompt; treat all Mexican beaches as federally public — no beach club can deny waterline access; and call **911** for emergencies, **078** for the SECTUR tourist line, the Policía Turística at **+52 322 290 1212**, or the US Consular Agency Puerto Vallarta at **+52 322 222 0069** if a presentation escalates into pressure or detention. The Jalisco *fiscalía* handles formal *MP* complaints.
