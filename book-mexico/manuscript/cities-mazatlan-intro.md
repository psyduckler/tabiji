Mazatlán is Sinaloa's Pacific port — a *Pueblo Mágico*-adjacent old town, major cruise stop, and snowbird capital of the Mexican *Pacífico* — anchored by Mazatlán International (MZT) and a tourism corridor that runs from the **Zona Dorada** south along the *Malecón* into **Centro Histórico** and *Plazuela Machado*. The scam ecosystem concentrates on three corridors: **MZT arrivals**, the **cruise pier and *Olas Altas* approach**, and the **Zona Dorada** beachfront restaurants and "tequila museum" gift shops. Patterns are documented in *El Sol de Mazatlán* and *Noroeste*. One important note before you book: the surrounding state of Sinaloa carries an active US Department of State Level 4 advisory tied to cartel activity in the rural interior, while Mazatlán's tourist zones in Centro and the Zona Dorada are statistically separate from that risk; verify current status with your travel insurer before flying, and stay inside the tourist zones.

The **cruise pier *pulmonía* overcharge** is the volume risk. The iconic open-air VW *pulmonía* taxis at the pier quote MX$500–$1,200 for a Centro Histórico transfer that runs MX$200–$300 if you agree the price before boarding, and MX$100–$150 one-way via Uber (which works in Mazatlán). The bundled "*pulmonía* + Golden Zone + Old Town tour" at US$120 per person mostly delivers free things — Cliff Divers at *Cerro del Crestón* perform from a public cliff, the *Malecón* is a free walk, and Stone Island is a MX$70 *launcha* ferry from the south pier. The defense: agree the *pulmonía* price out loud before boarding, or open Uber on the pier Wi-Fi.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **MZT airport flat-rate taxi tout** runs the standard arrivals-curb script — kiosks quoting MX$1,200–$2,000 for a Zona Dorada transfer that runs MX$350–$500 via Uber and MX$500–$850 via the official **Transporte Terrestre** prepaid booth inside the terminal. The **Zona Dorada USD-only menu** at beachfront restaurants on Av. Camarón Sábalo and Av. Playa Gaviotas pads prices 10–15% on the exchange and quotes seafood at "market price" without weight; the move is to ask for the *MX$* menu, agree per-kilogram price for shrimp or lobster in writing, and eat at *Plazuela Machado* spots like Pedro y Lola, El Presidio, or Topolobampo. And the **"snowbird welcome" timeshare desk** at Pueblo Bonito Mazatlán, El Cid El Moro, and Riu Emerald Bay routes retirees into 3-to-4-hour pitches with US$15,000–$50,000 "lifetime memberships" PROFECO will annul under Mexico's 5-business-day cooling-off statute.

A safe default: book MZT transfers at the **Transporte Terrestre** prepaid booth or via Uber; agree every *pulmonía* price before the wheels move; eat at *Plazuela Machado*, not the Zona Dorada beachfront; buy *artesanías* and tequila at Chedraui where bottles are CRT-certified at MX$400–$1,500 instead of "tequila museum" gift shops; decline every welcome-desk "snowbird program"; and call **911** for emergencies, or **078** for the federal SECTUR tourist line. The Sinaloa *Fiscalía General* handles formal *Ministerio Público* (**MP**) complaints, and the US Consulate General Guadalajara — the closest consular post — is reachable at **+52 33 3268 2100**.
