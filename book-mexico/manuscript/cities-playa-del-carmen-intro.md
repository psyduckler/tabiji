Playa del Carmen is the Riviera Maya's beach-town hub and the ferry gateway to Cozumel — a 60-kilometer drive south of Cancún International, with a tourist economy built around two corridors: **Quinta Avenida** (5th Avenue), the 2-kilometer pedestrian strip running parallel to the beach, and the **Cozumel ferry terminal** at Muelle Fiscal. The scam ecosystem concentrates on those two corridors plus **Highway 307** (the Cancún-Tulum road) where rental-car checkpoints are common. There is no dedicated tourist-police desk; pressure incidents route through **911** and the *fiscalía de Quintana Roo*, whose press releases on tourist-targeted fraud are a standing feature in *Riviera Maya News*, *The Yucatan Times*, and Spanish-language *Por Esto!* and *Novedades Quintana Roo*.

The **Cozumel-ferry-terminal "official taxi" flat overcharge** and its companion ticket scam are the volume risk at the pier. Touts impersonating Ultramar agents sell tickets at 2x face value when the actual round-trip rate is **MX$820** at Ultramar or **MX$680** at Winjet, bought at the official window with a printed QR ticket. Hotel-concierge "ferry + snorkel bundle" offers at US$120 per person package items that cost US$50 separately. There is no VIP tier and no upgrade — Ultramar and Winjet are the only licensed operators, ferries run every 60–90 minutes from 6 AM to 11 PM, and no advance booking is needed for the return leg.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Quinta Avenida bracelet-drop and tourist-menu trap** combines sidewalk friendship-bracelet pushes with restaurants whose English-only menus run 2–3x the Spanish-menu prices: a fish taco that costs MX$45 two blocks off the strip becomes MX$180 on 5th Avenue, with a pre-added 18% "service charge" stacked under a tip line and a 10–15% credit-card surcharge announced only when the bill arrives. The **5ta Avenida time-share "free dinner" pitch** runs the same Hotel Zone rotation that PROFECO has documented in Cancún — clipboard-carrying English-speaking promoters offering "free excursion vouchers" and US$200 resort credits in exchange for 3-to-5-hour presentations. And the **Playa-Tulum ADO "express ticket" upsell** sells 2x face value tickets outside the terminal, when the legitimate ADO bus to Tulum is **MX$140** at the official window or **ado.com.mx**, and shared *colectivos* southbound from Calle 2 run **MX$60–$80**.

A safe default: buy ferry tickets only at the Ultramar or Winjet windows at Muelle Fiscal; eat 2–3 blocks off 5th Avenue on Avenida 15 or Calle 2/4 north; use bank-branch ATMs on Av. 10 Norte (HSBC, Banamex, Santander) and refuse every DCC "home currency" prompt; walk past every 5th Avenue clipboard promoter without breaking stride; and on Highway 307 keep documents in the glove box, drive conservatively, and if pulled over ask for a badge with name and officer number, refuse to surrender your passport, and insist that any fine be written and paid at the *delegación*. Call **911** for emergencies and the **US Consulate Playa del Carmen** at **+52 984 873 0303** if a shakedown escalates.
