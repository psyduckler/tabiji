# The Scams: City by City {-}

The chapters that follow document every scam reported in twelve Canadian destinations. Cities are ordered by how most visitors actually travel: starting with Toronto, the Pearson gateway; west to Niagara Falls; north to Ottawa's Parliament Hill; east to Montreal and Quebec City's French-Canadian corridor; across the Maritimes to Halifax; then west again to Calgary, Banff, Jasper, and Whistler through the Canadian Rockies; and finishing on the Pacific coast at Vancouver and Victoria.

If you're hitting only a few of these, skip to the chapter you need. If you're doing a longer loop, skim them all — the same core scams recur with local variations. The airport Uber cancel-and-cash and taxi card-swap script that works Pearson runs identically at YOW Ottawa, YHZ Halifax, YQB Quebec City, YYC Calgary, YVR Vancouver, and YYJ Victoria. The vacation-rental permit-hijacking and off-platform payment-fraud pattern runs in Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, Banff, Whistler, and Halifax. And the "skip-the-line" tout pattern at tourist attractions — where free or easily-booked attractions (Parliament tours, Halifax Citadel, Whistler lift tickets) are resold at a steep markup by a person with a folder and a plausible story — threads through Ottawa, Calgary, Halifax, and Niagara Falls. Learn the pattern in one city, and you'll spot it the instant you arrive in the next.

## The Twelve Cities at a Glance {-}

![Toronto — CN Tower and streetcar at golden hour](assets/cities/toronto.jpg)

**Toronto** — Canada's largest city, the Pearson (YYZ) gateway, and home to Canada's most-documented tourist-scam ecosystem. The Pearson Airport cancel-and-cash Uber scam; the Toronto taxi card-swap fraud documented across Union Station, Yonge-Dundas Square, and the Entertainment District; the fake eTA visa-website scam that overcharges arrivals 40–100 dollars for a 7-dollar real application; pickpocketing at Yonge-Dundas Square, Eaton Centre, and the PATH underground corridor; Airbnb permit-hijacking and fake-rental-listing fraud; the downtown fake-police identity check that asks to "inspect" your wallet for counterfeit bills; the TTC transit-ticket and Presto-card reseller scam at subway exits; and impersonation phone scams targeting hotel guests with "front-desk" calls asking to re-verify credit cards.

![Niagara Falls — Horseshoe Falls and the Maid of the Mist at golden hour](assets/cities/niagara-falls.jpg)

**Niagara Falls** — Canada's most-visited natural attraction, and the tourist-trap capital of Ontario's Clifton Hill strip. The hidden TIF (Tourism Improvement Fee) that adds 10% to restaurant bills without disclosure; the Clifton Hill attraction-bait packages that bundle unwanted activities; the Fallsview dining trap with $80-per-person "minimum window tables"; the unofficial tour-ticket hustler on Clifton Hill selling fake Maid of the Mist and Journey Behind the Falls tickets; and the camera-drop shakedown where a "tourist" asks you to take their photo, drops a cheap camera, and demands replacement.

![Ottawa — Parliament Hill and the Peace Tower at dusk](assets/cities/ottawa.jpg)

**Ottawa** — The national capital, Parliament Hill, and the Byward Market. The fake Parliament Tour Reseller scam — real tours are free through the Parliament of Canada website but resellers sell them for 40 dollars; the Tulip Festival vendor and bus-tour markup fraud in May; the ByWard Market restaurant tourist-trap and Beavertails markup; the YOW airport taxi and Uber cancel-and-cash scam; the Rideau Canal skating "pass" and Winterlude vendor fraud; and Ottawa vacation-rental and government-worker apartment fraud on Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace.

![Montreal — Vieux-Port and Basilique Notre-Dame in early winter](assets/cities/montreal.jpg)

**Montreal** — Old Montreal, the Plateau, and a winter-parking-tow economy that the city itself calls a persistent enforcement problem. The winter parking ticket and tow trap on unmarked snow routes (fines and tows 400–700 CAD); Old Montreal tourist-trap restaurants on Place Jacques-Cartier and Rue Saint-Paul; ATM skimming and card fraud on St-Laurent Boulevard and Crescent Street; festival and event phone theft at Just for Laughs, Jazz Fest, and Osheaga; unlicensed tour guides and calèche (horse-carriage) overcharging in Vieux-Montréal; the street-parking fake attendant scam in reflective vests; the sob-story cash request near the Palais des congrès; and the forced-tipping restaurant POS scam that rolls service into the total then pre-fills the tip screen at 20%.

![Quebec City — Château Frontenac and Dufferin Terrace at golden hour](assets/cities/quebec-city.jpg)

**Quebec City** — UNESCO-listed Old Town, Petit Champlain, and a cruise-ship-day economy with its own tourist-price layer. The Old Town restaurant tourist-trap overcharge along Rue Saint-Louis and La Bûche; the Petit Champlain luxury-goods and "authentic souvenir" markup; the calèche (horse-drawn carriage) tour pricing and route-skimming; cruise-day excursion reseller markups (Saguenay, Île d'Orléans, Montmorency Falls); the YQB airport and Old Town taxi overcharge; and the "authentic" maple syrup and sugar shack tourist-trap pricing at the Rue du Trésor artisan stalls.

![Halifax — Halifax Harbour and the Citadel at golden hour](assets/cities/halifax.jpg)

**Halifax** — Nova Scotia's capital, a major cruise-ship port, and the Maritime gateway. The cruise-day shore excursion markup for Peggy's Cove, Mahone Bay, and Cape Breton that doubles the Halifax Waterfront Tourism price; the YHZ airport taxi and Uber cancel-and-cash scam; the Halifax waterfront restaurant tourist-trap pricing at the Bluenose II and harbour-front seafood docks; the Maritime Museum and Citadel Hill skip-the-line tout pressure; Halifax vacation-rental and long-stay rental fraud on Craigslist and Kijiji; and Lunenburg and South Shore tour bundle reseller markups.

![Calgary — Calgary Tower and the Rockies at sunset](assets/cities/calgary.jpg)

**Calgary** — Alberta's economic capital, the Calgary Stampede (every July), and the gateway to the Canadian Rockies. The Stampede ticket scalper online fraud that has hit families for 500–3,000 CAD per booking; the Stampede festival vendor and counterfeit "VIP wristband" scam; the YYC airport taxi and Uber cancel-and-cash scam; the Calgary-to-Banff hotel-concierge transfer and "private tour" markup (fair Banff transfer: $100–$160 one-way, not $400+); Calgary vacation-rental and Stampede-week apartment fraud on Facebook Marketplace; and the Calgary Tower and Heritage Park skip-the-line tout pressure.

![Banff — Moraine Lake and the Ten Peaks at dawn](assets/cities/banff.jpg)

**Banff** — The flagship Rocky Mountains national park destination, and the single highest-dollar scam environment in Canada. The Pursuit Collection combo-pass American-monopoly pricing that overcharges American tourists 150 USD more than Canadian visitors; the Columbia Icefield Adventure and Glacier Skywalk tour-package markup; the Moraine Lake and Lake Louise shuttle reservation reseller fraud (real reservations are exclusively through Parks Canada); Banff town restaurant and hotel pricing inflation (glass of wine $20, burger $28 — the captive-market premium); Banff vacation-rental and hotel booking fraud; and the YYC-to-Banff shuttle and hotel parking overcharge.

![Jasper — Maligne Lake and Spirit Island at golden hour](assets/cities/jasper.jpg)

**Jasper** — The northern Rockies park, the Icefields Parkway, and Maligne Lake's Spirit Island. The Pursuit Collection cross-park combo-pass pricing (Jasper SkyTram + Maligne Lake Cruise bundled at a confusing USD premium); the Maligne Lake cruise booking and "Spirit Island Premium" upsell; Jasper town hotel parking and restaurant pricing inflation; the YEG Edmonton airport-to-Jasper rental-car one-way drop fees; Jasper wildlife tour and "bear-spotting" operator markups; and Jasper town vacation-rental fraud tied to the 2024 Jasper wildfire recovery period.

![Whistler — Whistler Village with Blackcomb and Whistler peaks at dusk](assets/cities/whistler.jpg)

**Whistler** — British Columbia's flagship ski resort, a short Sea-to-Sky drive from Vancouver, and home to the most CBC-documented scam cluster in Canada. The fraudulent parking QR-code stickers scam (CBC-documented across 2024–2025) — stickers placed over legitimate parking lot signs that redirect QR scans to a fraudulent payment site; Whistler vacation-rental fraud on Craigslist and Mountaincountry.com (losses 2,500–8,000 USD per incident in the 2024–2025 ski season); Whistler restaurant "subtle" bill-padding that adds service charges and then pre-fills 20% tips; Whistler Blackcomb lift-ticket resale and "discount pass" fraud on Facebook Marketplace; the YVR-to-Whistler Sea-to-Sky shuttle and "private transfer" markup; and Whistler hotel parking and resort-fee inflation.

![Vancouver — Coal Harbour and the North Shore mountains at sunset](assets/cities/vancouver.jpg)

**Vancouver** — British Columbia's Pacific gateway, Gastown, Stanley Park, and Canada Place. The Gastown fake-monks donation scam with beaded bracelets; the gold / jewelry blessing scam where a "monk" or "spiritual guide" blesses your watch and then demands a $200+ donation; the fake Uber and rideshare scam at YVR arrivals; the Canada Place street-magic hustle with forced payment after casual watching; the downtown parking-meter extortion (men with fake attendant vests collecting fees on app-based meters); rental listing fraud on Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace; and the phoney-police-officer donation scam on Robson Street with dollar-store badges and clipboards.

![Victoria BC — Inner Harbour and BC Parliament at golden hour](assets/cities/victoria-bc.jpg)

**Victoria BC** — Vancouver Island's capital, the Inner Harbour, and Butchart Gardens. The BC Ferries "Experience Card" and prepaid-resale fraud that oversells non-transferable passes on Craigslist; Inner Harbour whale-watching operator variance and safety concerns (some unlicensed operators running without Transport Canada certification); Butchart Gardens tour-bundle markups on cruise-ship days; Vancouver Island trafficking and stranger-car safety warnings specifically for young female travelers on the ferry-to-Victoria late-night approach; the YYJ Victoria airport and downtown taxi overcharge; and Victoria apartment rental and online romance / donation scams on Kijiji.
