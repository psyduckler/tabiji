Vancouver is British Columbia's Pacific gateway — Gastown, Stanley Park, Canada Place, Kitsilano, and YVR as the cruise-ship embarkation hub for Alaska. The scam ecosystem splits between Gastown's street-scam cluster and the airport-to-downtown transport corridor. The *fake monks donation scam* is the most photographed scam in downtown Vancouver: a person in saffron or orange robes approaches on Robson Street, in Gastown's Water Street corridor, or outside the Vancouver Art Gallery, places a beaded bracelet on the tourist's wrist or hands them a small amulet, then demands a "donation" of 40–200 CAD; the *Vancouver Sun* and the Canadian Buddhist Federation have both publicly noted that real Buddhist monks do not solicit donations on sidewalks and do not place items on passersby's wrists. Return the item, walk away, and do not engage further.

Six further scams structure the Vancouver visit (Vancouver has seven total — one more than most Canadian cities). The *gold / jewelry blessing scam* is the Gastown variant of the fake-monk pattern: a "monk" or "spiritual guide" offers to "bless" the tourist's watch, wedding ring, or necklace for safe travels — the blessing involves handling the item, and a sleight-of-hand swap with a lookalike replacement is executed during the "blessing ritual." Never hand any jewelry to a stranger for any reason. The *fake Uber and rideshare at YVR* follows the standard Canadian airport pattern — touts at the arrivals curb claim to be Uber drivers and quote 80–120 CAD cash for rides Uber prices at 35–55; book in the Uber or Lyft app and meet at the designated rideshare pickup zone (Level 2, marked with Uber/Lyft signage). The *Canada Place street-magic hustle* works the cruise-ship departure zone and the Convention Centre — a "performer" begins a trick for a small crowd, selects a tourist, and demands payment after the tourist has "participated" without agreeing to any fee; decline from the start with "No thank you" and keep walking. The *parking-meter extortion* in downtown Vancouver, Gastown, and Yaletown has men with reflective vests collecting "parking fees" at real PayByPhone meter zones; Vancouver's meters operate via PayByPhone app or Parking Meter Zones only, never cash to a person. The *rental listing fraud* on Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace works Vancouver's chronic housing-shortage market — "furnished Kitsilano apartments" and "downtown condos" that take Interac e-Transfer deposits for nonexistent properties; book only on Booking.com, Airbnb, or VRBO. Finally the *phony-police-officer donation scam* on Robson Street, Gastown, and outside Coal Harbour has men in ill-fitting "RCMP" hoodies or fake "Vancouver Police" vests with dollar-store badges asking for "donations to the police foundation"; real police foundations do not solicit sidewalk donations, and politely declining ends the interaction immediately.
