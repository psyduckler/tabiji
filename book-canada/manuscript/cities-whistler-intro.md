Whistler is British Columbia's flagship ski resort — a 90-minute drive north of Vancouver on the Sea-to-Sky Highway (Highway 99), Whistler Blackcomb's lift system connecting two peaks, and the most CBC-documented scam cluster in Canada. The scam ecosystem concentrates on three venues: the parking / shuttle pipeline, the vacation-rental market (particularly winter), and Whistler Blackcomb's lift-ticket ecosystem. The *fraudulent parking QR-code stickers scam* is the flagship: fraudsters place counterfeit "PAY HERE" stickers over legitimate paid-parking signs in Whistler Village's lots, redirecting QR scans to a fraudulent payment site that captures credit-card data. CBC Vancouver has covered the scam across a sustained 2024–2025 investigation; the Whistler Blackcomb and Resort Municipality of Whistler have issued multiple consumer warnings. The defense is to scan parking QR codes only when the sticker is clearly the original (unpeeled, flush with the sign, no layer beneath) and to pay only through the official PayByPhone app or the posted RMOW parking website — if in doubt, use a Whistler-village-centre paid-parking garage (Day Lot 1–5) where fraudulent stickers are less common.

Five further scams shape the Whistler visit. The *Whistler vacation-rental fraud on Craigslist and Mountaincountry.com* is the highest-dollar scam in this book — CBC Vancouver's 2024–2025 investigation documented winter-season losses of 2,500–8,000 USD per case for "ski-in ski-out chalets" paid by wire transfer or Interac e-Transfer that turned out to be nonexistent properties. The Whistler municipal short-term-rental registry (on *whistler.ca*) is public and searchable; a "chalet" without a valid registry listing is almost certainly a fraud. Book only on Booking.com, Airbnb, or VRBO. The *Whistler restaurant "subtle" bill-padding scam* adds a 3–5% "resort fee" to the bill total and then pre-fills the POS tip screen at 20% — the combination produces 25–28% effective service charges; the resort fee is genuinely mandatory at some hotel-attached restaurants but should be called out on the menu, and the POS tip field is always editable. The *Whistler Blackcomb lift-ticket resale and "discount pass" fraud* on Facebook Marketplace sells "discounted" day passes at 80–120 CAD that scan as "already used" at the chairlift; buy lift tickets only on *whistlerblackcomb.com* or at the Whistler Blackcomb ticket window. The *YVR Vancouver-to-Whistler Sea-to-Sky shuttle and "private transfer" markup* parallels the Calgary-to-Banff pattern — "private transfer" quotes at 400–600 CAD for what the Pacific Coach SkyLynx or Epic Rides shuttle does for 75–110 CAD per person; book the scheduled shuttle in advance. Finally the *Whistler hotel parking and resort-fee inflation* is the captive-market pattern — hotels charge 45–65 CAD per night for parking, a 3–5% resort fee on top of the quoted rate, and "destination marketing fees" of 2–4 CAD per night; budget these into the trip cost.
