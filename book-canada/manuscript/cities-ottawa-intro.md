Ottawa is Canada's national capital — Parliament Hill, the Rideau Canal, the ByWard Market, and the Tulip Festival each May. The scam ecosystem is small and tourist-attraction-adjacent rather than transport-dense. The *fake Parliament tour reseller* is the flagship: men with lanyards and folders approach arrivals on Wellington Street or at the Visitor Welcome Centre and sell "VIP Parliament tours" at 30–80 CAD per person. Real Parliament tours — of Centre Block (currently closed for restoration) and West Block / Senate — are **free** and bookable through the official *ourcommons.ca* and *parl.ca* websites. No intermediary is ever required, and no skip-the-line exists. The defense is to book through the official Parliament of Canada websites in advance and to walk past any lanyard-wearing figure offering tours at the gate.

Five further scams shape the Ottawa visit. The *Tulip Festival vendor and bus-tour markup fraud* runs every May during the Canadian Tulip Festival (2–3 weeks in early May) around Commissioners Park and Major's Hill Park — unofficial vendors sell "festival passes" (the festival is free to attend) and "bus tours" at 60–120 CAD for routes that Ottawa's city buses run at the standard 3.95 CAD fare; use OC Transpo or an on-foot visit. The *ByWard Market restaurant tourist-trap and Beavertails markup* hits two specific blocks — the short strip between ByWard and York — where a handful of restaurants charge 35–45 CAD for lunch mains that run 18–25 in Westboro or the Glebe, and Beavertails pastries that sell at the airport and most vendors for 8 CAD sell at the ByWard Market kiosk for 12–14. The *YOW Ottawa Macdonald-Cartier Airport taxi and Uber cancel-and-cash scam* is the standard Canadian airport pattern — real YOW to downtown is 35–45 CAD on Uber or the metered Coventry Connections taxi; decline any flat-rate $90+ cash quote. The *Rideau Canal skating "pass" and Winterlude festival vendor fraud* runs January–February on the canal's skating season — skating on the Rideau Canal is always free, and Winterlude entry is free; unofficial booths selling "skating passes" and "Winterlude VIP" are frauds. Finally the *Ottawa vacation-rental and government-worker apartment fraud* is the off-season variant — Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace listings for downtown short-term rentals and "furnished government-worker apartments" that take Interac e-Transfer deposits for nonexistent properties; book only through Booking.com, Airbnb, or VRBO.
