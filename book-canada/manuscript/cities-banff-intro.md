Banff is Canada's flagship Rocky Mountains national-park destination — Lake Louise, Moraine Lake, the Banff Gondola, and Johnston Canyon — and it is the single highest-dollar scam environment in the country. The scam ecosystem concentrates on national-park reservation infrastructure and captive-market mountain-town pricing. The *Pursuit Collection combo-pass American-monopoly pricing* is the flagship: Pursuit Collection (a Banff-concentrated hospitality conglomerate) owns the Banff Gondola, the Columbia Icefield Adventure, the Glacier Skywalk, the Maligne Lake Cruise (Jasper), and the Jasper SkyTram, and sells combo passes with confusing US-dollar pricing that charges American tourists 150 USD more than the posted Canadian rate for the same day of activities. The defense is to check the official Pursuit Collection website in Canadian dollars (*banffjaspercollection.com*) and to ignore US-dollar quotes from hotel concierges or pier touts; the Canadian rate is what you should pay regardless of your passport.

Five further scams shape the Banff visit. The *Columbia Icefield Adventure and Glacier Skywalk tour-package markup* is a Calgary/Banff-based bundle that sells 220–280 CAD per person combos for two activities whose separate tickets run 110 + 55 CAD = 165 CAD on the official Pursuit website; book each separately if you want both, or book the combo only through Pursuit's own channel. The *Moraine Lake and Lake Louise shuttle reservation reseller fraud* is the most consequential Banff scam because the real shuttle is the only way to access Moraine Lake (private vehicles banned since 2023) and reservations sell out months in advance — Parks Canada opens the reservation window on *reservation.pc.gc.ca* and sells through a single official portal. Fraudulent reseller websites (with Google-ads top placement) charge 80–150 CAD per ticket for reservations that are either invalid at the gate or re-sold copies of Parks Canada bookings; the real Parks Canada shuttle is 9 CAD per adult, reservation only through *reservation.pc.gc.ca*. The *Banff town restaurant and hotel pricing inflation* is less a scam and more a captive-market premium — a glass of wine is 20 CAD, a burger is 28 CAD, a hotel that would be 180 CAD in Canmore is 420 CAD in Banff town — but the 20-minute drive to Canmore saves 30–50% on food and 40–60% on hotels; if you're on a budget, stay in Canmore and day-trip into Banff. The *Banff vacation-rental and hotel booking fraud* is the captive-market version of the national permit-hijacking pattern, with "luxury Banff townhouses" and "ski-in chalets" sold on Facebook Marketplace that turn out to be nonexistent or occupied; book only on Booking.com, Airbnb, or Expedia. Finally the *YYC Calgary Airport-to-Banff shuttle and hotel parking overcharge* is the Banff-specific cousin of the Calgary-to-Banff scam — captive hotels in Banff charge 45–60 CAD per night for parking on top of room rates, and "airport shuttles" sold by hotel concierges at 250 CAD per person run what Brewster Express sells for 85 CAD.
