Quebec City is the only walled city in North America north of Mexico — a UNESCO World Heritage site, the Château Frontenac, Petit Champlain's cobblestone quarter, and a major cruise-ship destination that adds a specific cruise-day price layer to the whole Old Town economy. The scam ecosystem is entirely tourist-trap and concentrates on the Upper Town (Haute-Ville) Old Town corridor and the cruise-ship-day excursions market. The *Old Town restaurant tourist-trap overcharge* along Rue Saint-Louis, La Bûche, and the restaurants that cluster around the Château Frontenac quotes 35–55 CAD for lunch mains that run 22–30 CAD in Saint-Roch or Limoilou. The *La Bûche* on Rue Saint-Louis has had repeated *Le Soleil* and *Le Journal de Québec* coverage for bill-padding — specifically rolling "service" into the printed total and then pre-filling the POS tip field at 20%, which results in 34–40% effective tips. The defense is to check whether "service compris" or "service inclus" is printed on the menu, and if so, adjust the POS tip field down to zero (or the Canadian customary 15%).

Five further scams shape the Quebec City visit. The *Petit Champlain luxury-goods and "authentic souvenir" markup* targets cruise-day visitors in the short-hop lower-town quarter — "authentic Quebecois" maple-syrup tins, First Nations art, and "hand-crafted" souvenirs sold at 60–150 CAD are frequently mass-produced and sell at SAQ, Simons, or the *Jean Béliveau* gift shop for a third of the price; pick a real Quebecois artisan market (Marché du Vieux-Port, Marché Jean-Talon in Montreal if you're also visiting) rather than Petit Champlain for souvenirs. The *calèche (horse-drawn carriage) tour pricing and route-skimming* has carriages quoting 120–200 CAD for a "full tour" that ends after 15 minutes with a claim that "the return leg is extra"; confirm the route and total price in writing before boarding, and use licensed carriages with numbered badges at the Place d'Armes stand. The *cruise-day excursion reseller markups* for Saguenay, Île d'Orléans, and Montmorency Falls charge 180 CAD per person for day trips that the Quebec City Tourism Office (Centre Infotouriste at 12 Rue Sainte-Anne) sells at 80–100; use the Tourism Office or the cruise line's onboard excursion desk. The *YQB Airport and Old Town taxi overcharge* follows the standard Canadian airport pattern — real YQB-to-Old-Town is 35–45 CAD on the metered Taxis Coop or Uber; decline any flat-rate $90+ cash quote. Finally the *"authentic" maple syrup and sugar shack tourist-trap pricing* at the Rue du Trésor artisan stalls and at "maple experience" stops outside the city quotes 40–80 CAD for tins that sell at 20–30 at the Marché du Vieux-Port or at the Metro or IGA supermarkets in town — the "sugar shack" day trips are also marked up 50–100% over the operator's own website price (Érablière du Cap or the Sucrerie du Nord sites).
