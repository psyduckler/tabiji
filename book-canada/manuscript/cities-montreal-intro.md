Montreal is Canada's second-largest city and its French-Canadian cultural capital — the Plateau, Old Montreal (Vieux-Montréal), Mile End, and a festival calendar (Just for Laughs, Montreal Jazz Festival, Osheaga) that draws millions each summer. The scam ecosystem concentrates on three venues: the Old Montreal tourist corridor, the winter parking-enforcement ecosystem, and festival-crowd phone theft. The *winter parking ticket and tow trap* is the signature Montreal scam and the most financially consequential: between November and April, the city declares snow-removal routes on designated streets with temporary signage that is sometimes obscured by snow, and vehicles parked on these stretches are ticketed ($60–$200) and towed at 400–700 CAD per incident. CBC Montreal has covered the genuinely-coordinated-enforcement nature of this scam cycle across multiple winters. The defense is to **use only parking lots and garages during November–April**, to never park on a street in Old Montreal or the Plateau overnight during winter without reading every sign within 30 meters of your spot, and to download the *Info-Stationnement* app from the City of Montreal for real-time snow-route alerts.

Seven further scams shape the Montreal visit (Montreal has eight total scams — the most of any single Canadian city). The *Old Montreal tourist-trap restaurants* on Place Jacques-Cartier and Rue Saint-Paul East charge 35–55 CAD for lunch mains that run 20–30 three blocks over on Rue Saint-Paul West or in Mile End; pick restaurants using Montreal local review sites (Cult MTL, Eater Montreal) rather than sandwich-board menus. The *ATM skimming and card fraud* circuit on St-Laurent Boulevard and Crescent Street has been active through the 2024–2025 period — use ATMs only inside bank branches (BMO, Royal Bank, TD, Scotiabank, National Bank) during business hours, and cover the keypad on every withdrawal. The *festival and event phone theft* at Just for Laughs, Jazz Fest (Place des Arts), and Osheaga is a crowd-surface-grab pattern — keep phones in a zipped front pocket and use a low-visibility strap if photographing. The *unlicensed tour guide and calèche (horse-carriage) overcharging* in Vieux-Montréal has been addressed by city licensing (since 2018 the city has phased out most calèches), but a small number of unlicensed carriages still operate and quote 120–200 CAD for 30-minute rides; if you want a calèche, use the licensed Locomotion operators at Place Jacques-Cartier at the posted 60 CAD rate. The *street-parking fake attendant* scam on Saint-Denis and in the Plateau has men in reflective vests collecting "parking fees" at real Stationnement de Montréal app-based meter zones; don't pay any person on the street for parking — the city's meters use the PayByPhone app or kiosk only. The *sob-story cash request* near Palais des congrès, Quartier des Spectacles, and downtown metro exits uses a well-rehearsed "I need to get home to my family" script with specific dollar amounts ($40 or $60 for "the bus to Longueuil"); police recommend politely declining and moving on. Finally the *forced-tipping restaurant scam* — the Montreal POS terminal pre-fills a 20% tip screen even after "service included" has already been rolled into the total, resulting in 34–40% tips for customers who sign past without reading. The defense is universal: the POS tip field is always editable down to zero, and if you are a visitor who did not request "service included" pre-billed, you can adjust the tip to the Canadian standard of 15–18%.
