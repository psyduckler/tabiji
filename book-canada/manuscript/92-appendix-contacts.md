# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication, but phone numbers do change, consulates move, and hospitals occasionally renumber their switchboards. If a number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus *Canada* and you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results.

**Before you travel**, we strongly recommend you double-check your own embassy or consulate's current number directly on its official website — the US State Department's travel.state.gov, the UK's gov.uk, Australia's smartraveller.gov.au, Ireland's dfa.ie. Save the main switchboard and the after-hours duty-officer line on your phone before departure, and screenshot this appendix so you can read it on a BC Ferry or in a Banff gondola where your signal cuts out. Even a verified number can become a wrong number between editions.

## The numbers that work everywhere in Canada

These are the lines to dial from any phone — mobile or landline, Canadian SIM or roaming — anywhere in the country. All of them are free.

- **Universal emergency (police, fire, ambulance):** **911**. English and French language options are available at every dispatch center in Canada. This is the *first* number to call for any active-threat situation, any physical altercation, any theft in progress, or any medical emergency.
- **Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (CAFC):** **1-888-495-8501**. The national fraud-reporting line run jointly by the RCMP, the Competition Bureau, and the Ontario Provincial Police. Report non-active-threat scams here: mail scams, phone scams, investment scams, rental-listing frauds, and after-the-fact credit card card-swap or skimming. The CAFC aggregates reports for police investigation; your report number is also useful when speaking with your bank or credit card issuer. English and French via *antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca*.
- **City non-emergency police (311 routing or direct):** **311** reaches most major-city non-emergency municipal services (noise complaints, parking, road issues), not police. For police non-emergency, use each city's direct line (below).
- **Service Canada (lost passports, Service Canada issues):** **1-800-O-Canada** (**1-800-622-6232**). For lost eTA or visa questions, for Service Canada benefits, for passport reissuance information.
- **Passport Canada (emergency passport services abroad):** Contact your home country's embassy or consulate; Canada does not issue emergency passports to non-Canadian citizens.

## Provincial police non-emergency lines

For scams that do not meet the 911 active-threat threshold, call the provincial police non-emergency line or the specific municipal police non-emergency number. These are the direct lines:

- **Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP):** **1-800-387-0020** (all-Canada general RCMP information). For a specific detachment, call the detachment's posted local number.
- **Ontario Provincial Police (OPP):** **1-888-310-1122**. Covers Ontario outside Toronto, Ottawa, and other municipal police jurisdictions.
- **Sûreté du Québec:** **310-4141** (no area code needed in Quebec). Covers Quebec outside Montreal SPVM and Quebec City SPVQ jurisdictions.

## Card issuer emergency lines (callable from Canada)

When you call, have your card number (or the last four digits), the approximate transaction amount, and the time of the fraudulent charge ready.

- **Visa Global Customer Assistance (collect from Canada):** **+1 303 967 1096**. 24/7, English.
- **Mastercard Global Service (collect from Canada):** **+1 636 722 7111**. 24/7.
- **American Express International Service Center (collect):** **+1 336 393 1111**. 24/7.

If your card was issued by a bank (not directly by Visa, Mastercard, or Amex), the fastest route is the number printed on the back of the card. That line routes you to your own issuer's fraud desk and they can freeze the card in under a minute. Save this number in your phone under a searchable contact name before you travel.

## If you lose your passport

1. **Report it first** — go to the nearest police station and file a report for a lost or stolen passport. You will leave with the signed report. This is the document your embassy wants to see before issuing an emergency replacement.
2. **Call your embassy's main switchboard** — or its after-hours line if it is a weekend — and request an emergency passport appointment. US, UK, Australian, and Irish missions in Canada all issue same-day or next-day emergency travel documents; the fee varies.
3. **Bring** the police report, any photo ID you still have (a driver's license photo on your phone counts), passport photos (photo booths are at Shoppers Drug Mart and Canada Post outlets across Canada), and the payment method your embassy specifies.
4. **Departure clearance:** before flying out with an emergency travel document, you may need a short visit to Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) at the airport — your embassy will advise. Airlines departing YYZ, YUL, YOW, YYC, YVR, and YYJ see emergency documents regularly; CBSA officers know the format.

## If your phone is stolen

1. **Freeze the SIM** by calling your carrier's customer-service line from any other phone. Canadian carriers: **Bell** **611** (or **1-800-667-0123** from any phone); **Rogers** **611** (or **1-888-764-3771**); **Telus** **611** (or **1-866-558-2273**). Tourist eSIMs (Airalo, Saily, Holafly) can be suspended in-app.
2. **File a police report** with the device's IMEI (if you recorded it) and its serial number. Insurance claims require the report.
3. **Lock and wipe remotely** via Apple's Find My or Google's Find My Device from any browser. Do this before filing the police report — a wiped phone cannot leak your email and banking apps while you wait in line.
4. **Change passwords immediately** for any account whose app was installed on the phone: email, banking, social media. Start with email; everything else resets off it.

## If your credit card is stolen or skimmed

1. **Freeze the card** by calling the number on the back (or use the issuer's app's "lock card" feature, which works in seconds). This matters more than filing a police report first — every minute you wait is a minute the thief can use the card.
2. **Report to police** via the city non-emergency line or by calling the **Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501**. Insurance reimbursement and the card issuer's fraud-dispute process both want a report reference.
3. **Dispute the charges** with your issuer. Most European, US, Australian, and Japanese card issuers reverse fraudulent charges fully; the police-report number speeds the process.
4. **Watch for follow-on phishing** — a skimmed card often leads to email and SMS phishing pretending to be your bank. Never click a link in one of these messages; go to your bank's app or website directly.

## A closing note

This appendix is meant to be read once on the plane, screenshot for offline use, and forgotten about for the rest of your trip. Phone numbers change. Consulates move. Hospitals renumber their switchboards. If any number in this list fails, **911** will still work, and your embassy's website will still have the current line for your country.

Travel safely, travel calmly, and if the worst happens, remember: a stolen phone is an inconvenience, not a crisis. Filing a police report, freezing a card, and calling an embassy are ordinary procedures — Canadian police officers and consular staff handle these cases every day, and most travelers resolve everything within a few hours and return to their trip. Canada is not a dangerous country; it is a practiced one. Help is close, and English (or French, in Quebec and parts of New Brunswick and Ontario) is closer than you think.
