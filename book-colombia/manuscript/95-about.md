# About Tabiji {-}

Tabiji is a travel-safety research organization, and this is the sixteenth book we have written. We document scams, health risks, and local advisories for travelers across more than fifty cities worldwide, and we publish everything as a free public resource at **[tabiji.ai](https://tabiji.ai)**.

We started this series after noticing that most scam roundups online skip the one thing travelers actually need in the moment: the exact phrase, the exact move, that ends the interaction. Remembering a list of thirty-three scams is hard when you are tired and under pressure. Remembering six patterns is much easier. That is the shape we have tried to give this book.

Every scam documented here was drawn from Colombian press coverage — *El Tiempo*, *Semana*, *El Espectador*, *El Colombiano* (Medellín), *El País* (Cali), *El Heraldo* (Barranquilla and the Caribbean coast), and *El Universal* (Cartagena) — cross-referenced against Policía Nacional de Turismo reports, the US State Department Colombia country advisory, the UK Foreign Office Colombia travel advice, Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio consumer-complaint records, and firsthand traveler accounts from Colombia-focused travel communities. Where we have named perpetrators, arrest dates, or documented peso and dollar amounts, we cite them. Where a pattern has been reported but not formally investigated, we say so.

This edition reflects the 2026 scam landscape: the continued dominance of scopolamine (*burundanga*) drink-spiking across the Medellín and Cali nightlife districts; the 2025 surge of *paseo millonario* (express-kidnapping) taxis in Bogotá; the proliferation of fake Tinder and Bumble match setups as the pre-attack vector; the coexistence of DiDi, InDrive, and Cabify rideshare with a street-taxi economy in which Uber operates in a legal gray zone; and the persistent Cartagena walled-city restaurant USD-pricing gambit. We expect to revise portions of this book for the 2027 edition.

For new scams and live updates to the cases in this book, visit **[tabiji.ai/scams](https://tabiji.ai/scams)**. We maintain the database quarterly, and most corrections reach the site within a week of a credible report.

We hope you come home with stories about the *arepas de chócolo* at a *paisa* grandmother's table, a walled-city dinner in Cartagena, a salsa lesson in Cali that actually stuck, and a Metrocable ride over Medellín at dusk.

— Tabiji
