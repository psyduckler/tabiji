Medellín is the Antioquian capital, Colombia's second city by population (2.6 million in the urban area) and — thanks to a decade of investment in public transit, urban design, and neighborhood renovation — the country's most visible showcase for the *paisa* cultural revival. The Metro and the Metrocable aerial trams link the historic center to the hillside neighborhoods, the Museo de Antioquia and the Plaza Botero anchor the downtown, and the Comuna 13 graffiti walks, the Parque Arví eucalyptus reserve, and the Guatapé and El Peñol day trips have become central to the international itinerary. Medellín's "eternal spring" climate — 22 °C year-round at 1,500 meters — is part of the draw.

Medellín also has the most sophisticated scopolamine-setup ecosystem in Colombia. The specific pattern — documented repeatedly across 2024 and 2025 by Colombia-focused travel communities, the US State Department's Medellín-specific country notice, and the Policía de Turismo de Medellín itself — runs like this: a foreign man, typically twenty-five to forty-five, matches with an attractive local woman on Tinder, Bumble, or Hinge. The date proceeds normally at a Parque Lleras bar in El Poblado or at a rooftop in Provenza. At some point the woman insists on ordering a round of *aguardiente* (the anise-flavored Colombian spirit) or a cocktail. She hands it to the man directly, or he steps away briefly and returns to a drink she has "already ordered." The drink contains scopolamine — the plant-derived Colombian drug nicknamed *burundanga* — which renders the victim fully conscious but entirely compliant and amnesic. The rest of the night is spent emptying his bank accounts via ATM withdrawals, buying goods at shops, and in some cases returning to his hotel to withdraw from the safe.

Reports of scopolamine-assisted robbery in Medellín tripled between 2023 and 2025. The US State Department explicitly advises American citizens to "never accept food or drinks from strangers, even if they seem friendly" and to "order drinks directly from a bartender and watch them poured." The defensive playbook is narrow: if you meet someone on a dating app, meet at a bar where you can order your own drinks from a bartender you can see, watch every drink poured directly, and do not accept any drink from your match's hands. If your date insists on bringing a drink, politely refuse and walk to the bar yourself. If you feel unexpectedly impaired from even a single drink — disoriented, compliant, sluggish — ask a stranger to call **123** or walk into the nearest hotel lobby, not back to your room.

Beyond scopolamine, Medellín has a smaller set of tourist-financial scams. The **Parque Lleras and Provenza restaurant strip** runs the standard Latin-American *propina voluntaria* auto-add at 10 percent, plus a dollar-denominated tourist menu; ask for the peso menu (*menú en pesos*) and pay with a foreign credit card. The **Comuna 13 graffiti-tour** ecosystem includes unlicensed "guides" on Facebook and Instagram who collect USD 20–50 deposits and either cancel or deliver a substandard forty-five-minute tour — book only with a verified operator via GetYourGuide, Viator, or your hotel concierge. The **Guatapé and El Peñol day trips** are almost always scam-free when booked via a Medellín hostel or an established aggregator, but they are vulnerable to WhatsApp "private host" deposit fraud if you find the listing on Facebook. The **JMC airport** (José María Córdova, forty-five minutes out of town via the Túnel Nuevo) taxi rank charges roughly COP 120,000 to central Medellín; DiDi, InDrive, and Cabify do the same trip for COP 80,000–90,000 if you walk to the designated rideshare zone.

Medellín is one of the more rewarding Colombian cities — the Metrocable alone is worth the visit. The specific defensive habits matter most in nightlife: never accept an unwatched drink, never meet a dating-app match alone at a venue they chose, and keep the Policía de Turismo Medellín line **+57 4 511-1111** saved in your phone. Daytime Medellín is safe in almost every neighborhood a tourist would visit.

Emergency: **123**; Policía de Turismo Medellín (Carrera 66 No. 48–110, +57 4 511-1111); Hospital Pablo Tobón Uribe **+57 4 445-9000**; US Embassy **+57 1 275-2000**.
