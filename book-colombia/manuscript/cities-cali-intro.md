Cali — officially Santiago de Cali — is the capital of Valle del Cauca, Colombia's third city (2.2 million in the metro area), and the self-declared salsa capital of the world. The Plaza de Caicedo anchors the downtown; the Granada, Menga, and San Antonio neighborhoods form the main dining and nightlife district; the Cerro de las Tres Cruces offers a panoramic hike above the Cauca Valley; and the Cristo Rey statue, visible from nearly everywhere in town, marks the western skyline. Cali's airport (CLO, twenty kilometers north in Palmira) has strong domestic connections and growing international routes. The Feria de Cali in late December draws significant international traffic.

Cali's scam ecosystem divides cleanly into two categories: the Medellín-style scopolamine setups that mirror Parque Lleras's pattern exactly, and a smaller cluster of transport and authority-impersonation incidents specific to the Valle del Cauca. Scopolamine in Cali follows the same template as Medellín: a Tinder or Bumble match, a drink in Granada, and a night that goes blank. The US State Department groups Medellín and Cali together in its country advisory for this reason. The defensive playbook is identical to Medellín's: watch every drink poured directly at the bar, never accept a drink from your date's hands, and if you feel unexpectedly compliant after a single drink, walk to a hotel lobby (not back to your room) and call **123**.

Cali's specific transport variant is the **CLO airport "meter broken" taxi trick**. The official airport-rank taxi posts a flat fare of roughly COP 75,000–85,000 to Granada or San Antonio; a street-hailed or rideshare-pretender taxi outside the terminal will quote COP 120,000–160,000 and claim either the meter is broken or there is a late-night surcharge. The countermeasure is to walk directly to the posted-flat-rate counter inside the terminal, take the ticket with the license plate recorded, and confirm the printed fare before the car moves. For travel between Granada, Menga, and San Antonio inside the city, DiDi and Uber (in ride-in-front-seat mode) are fully operational; InDrive is the cheapest for longer routes. Cali is the Colombian city where the "never a street taxi" rule is easiest to follow, because app coverage is excellent.

On foot, the **San Antonio fake-plainclothes drug search** is Cali's signature authority-impersonation con. It targets solo foreign male travelers walking between nightlife venues: two men in polo shirts flash unofficial laminated cards, claim they are *antinarcóticos* checking for drugs after an unspecified report, and ask to "see" the contents of pockets and wallets. Real Colombian Policía Antinarcóticos does not conduct sidewalk drug searches on tourists in nightlife districts. The countermeasure is to refuse politely, walk into the nearest well-lit shop or hotel lobby, dial **123**, and request a uniformed officer. Real officers arrive; fake ones leave.

A softer-edged local twist is the **fake salsa-lesson "private studio" upsell**, Cali's take on the tourist-tour fraud found in every major city. A tout at the Plaza de Caicedo or on Avenida Sexta pitches a "private studio lesson with a professional dancer" at USD 40–80 per lesson; the "studio" turns out to be a rented back room, the "professional dancer" is a friend or cousin, and additional charges stack onto the advertised price. Book salsa lessons only through your hotel, a Viator or GetYourGuide listing, or a hostel-recommended studio — Arrebato Caleño, El Manicero, and Son de Luz are well-established, verifiable operators.

Cali rewards preparation and an app-only transit rule. The daytime city is safe in every tourist neighborhood. Nightlife is where situational awareness matters most — go out with company, stay in well-lit bars with visible bartenders, and keep the Policía de Turismo Cali line **+57 2 885-3999** saved in your phone. Fundación Valle del Lili on Carrera 98 has a strong international-patient desk and is the emergency room of choice for any suspected scopolamine case.

Emergency: **123**; Policía de Turismo Cali (Avenida Colombia No. 4–47, +57 2 885-3999); Fundación Valle del Lili **+57 2 331-9090**; US Embassy **+57 1 275-2000**.
