San Andrés is the largest island of the **Archipiélago de San Andrés, Providencia y Santa Catalina**, Colombia's Caribbean overseas department 750 km north of the mainland and 230 km east of the Nicaraguan coast. The 26-square-kilometer island is the country's primary domestic beach destination and a designated **UNESCO Seaflower Biosphere Reserve**, with a year-round population of around 60,000 — predominantly Raizal (English-Creole-speaking Afro-Caribbean descendants) — and a tourist economy that doubles the headcount in high season. Visitors arrive almost exclusively through **Aeropuerto Internacional Gustavo Rojas Pinilla (ADZ)**, a 12-minute walk from the **Spratt Bight** beach hotel strip in the main town of North End. The island uses Colombian pesos, but USD is widely quoted at unfavorable internal exchange rates, and the local Raizal economy runs largely in cash.

San Andrés has a single mandatory scam that catches almost every first-time visitor: the **Tarjeta de Turismo** resale fraud. Every visitor to the archipelago is required by Colombian law to purchase a tourist card before boarding the flight — the official price is **USD 35 (or roughly COP 145,000), payable at the airline counter or at the official sales booth at the mainland departure airport**. Resellers in Bogotá, Medellín, and at the San Andrés airport on arrival sell the same card at USD 80–150 with claims of "express processing," "VIP queue," or a "required additional environmental fee." There is no express processing, no VIP queue, and no environmental fee on top of the COP 145,000. The card itself is a printed slip that the airline checks at boarding; replacement copies are issued free at the Migración Colombia desk on arrival if the original is lost.

Beyond the *Tarjeta de Turismo*, San Andrés runs three signature scam patterns. The first is the **ADZ airport-to-hotel taxi overcharge**: the official posted rate from ADZ to anywhere in North End is **COP 15,000–25,000 (about USD 4–6)**, and the airport itself is genuinely a 12-minute walk from the Spratt Bight hotel strip. The terminal touts quote COP 80,000–150,000 in cash to first-time arrivals, particularly in the evening when the Avianca and LATAM flights from Bogotá land within thirty minutes of each other. The defense is the official taxi rate board posted inside the arrivals hall (read it before exiting) and the Uber-equivalent **InDrive**, which operates on the island.

The second is the **golf-cart rental damage-deposit trap**. Renting a golf cart is the single most popular way to circle the island (the perimeter road is 30 km, drivable in about 90 minutes). Legitimate operators rent two-seater carts at COP 80,000–120,000 per day with no cash deposit beyond the rental fee and a passport photocopy. The scam variant: an unregistered operator at the Spratt Bight strip demands **a USD 200–500 cash damage deposit** at pickup, then refuses to return it at drop-off because of "scratches" or "wear" they themselves photographed before handing you the keys. Several r/Colombia and TripAdvisor reports in 2024 documented operators withholding entire deposits over pre-existing scuffs. Defense: **rent only from a hotel-vetted operator**, photograph every existing scratch before driving away, refuse any cash deposit (a credit-card hold is normal; cash is not), and never hand over your passport — only a photocopy is required by law.

The third is the **Johnny Cay boat tour bait-and-switch**. The legitimate cooperative shared-boat run from Spratt Bight to Johnny Cay (a small island a kilometer offshore, with a sheltered Caribbean swimming beach) costs **COP 30,000–50,000 round-trip** at the muelle ticket booth, with departures every 20 minutes from 9 AM to 4 PM. The scam: a beach tout sells a "VIP private boat tour with snorkeling, lunch, and three-island access" for COP 200,000–350,000 per person, then loads you onto the same shared boat with the same captain and the same single Johnny Cay stop. Lunch and snorkeling are billed separately on the cay. The defense is the cooperative ticket booth at the muelle, which displays a posted board with the per-seat rate and the daily departure schedule.

San Andrés rewards a few small habits. Buy the **Tarjeta de Turismo only at the airline counter** at your mainland departure airport (Avianca and LATAM both sell it), not from any reseller. Walk from ADZ to your hotel if you are staying in North End, or use InDrive. Rent golf carts only through your hotel concierge with no cash deposit. Buy boat seats at the muelle cooperative window. Carry a photocopy of your passport, never the original. The island is otherwise safe, the Raizal community is welcoming, and the Seaflower reef is among the best snorkeling in the Caribbean.
