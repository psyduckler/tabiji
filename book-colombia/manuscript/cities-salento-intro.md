Salento is the postcard town of Colombia's **Eje Cafetero** — the UNESCO-inscribed Coffee Cultural Landscape that spans the Quindío, Risaralda, and Caldas departments. The pueblo itself is small (eight thousand residents, painted *bahareque* houses on a colonial grid), but it sits at the gateway to the **Valle de Cocora**, the high-altitude valley of *Ceroxylon quindiuense* — Colombia's national tree, the wax palm, the tallest palm species in the world. The combination of the Cocora day hike, the working coffee *fincas* on the surrounding hillsides, and the all-day *trucha* lunch on Salento's Calle Real makes the town the single most-visited rural destination in Colombia. The closest airports are Pereira (PEI) and Armenia (AXM); both are about an hour by road, with public buses running roughly every 30 minutes from each terminal.

Salento's risks are not violent. The dominant pattern is **transport overcharging on the chokepoints between the airports, the pueblo, and the Cocora trailhead**. Every legitimate route has a posted cooperative price, and every scam variant in this chapter is a 3–10x markup on it. The Pereira (PEI) and Armenia (AXM) airport-to-Salento taxi runs about **COP 80,000–120,000 by metered taxi or Uber**; the no-meter terminal touts quote COP 250,000–350,000 in cash. The Salento-to-Cocora *Willys* jeep round trip runs **COP 8,000–10,000 per person each way** from the cooperative kiosk on Plaza de Bolívar, departing every 20 minutes; the fake "private Willys" pitch from a Calle Real street tout quotes COP 80,000–150,000 for the same 30-minute drive. The official Cocora Valley **horse rental** runs COP 60,000–80,000 for a 4-hour loop with a registered cooperative *arriero* (horseman); the unregistered mid-trail tout demands an additional COP 100,000–200,000 once you are an hour into the *bosque de niebla* and turning back on foot would mean another two hours of muddy descent.

The coffee-farm tour ecosystem runs a parallel scam pattern. The legitimate operators — **Finca El Ocaso**, **Don Elías**, **Las Acacias**, and a handful of others — post fixed per-person rates (COP 35,000–55,000 for a one-hour tour with tasting), accept walk-ins, and are bookable through the Salento *casa de la cultura* tourism office on Plaza de Bolívar. The scam variant: a Calle Real "agency" with no posted RNT number takes a deposit of COP 80,000–120,000 per person for an "exclusive private finca tour with traditional lunch," then either (a) drops you at a generic finca with no production at all, (b) reveals on the day that lunch is COP 40,000 cash extra, or (c) simply does not show up at the morning pickup point. Defense: **book directly with the finca by WhatsApp** (every legitimate finca lists a WhatsApp number on Google Maps and on its own Instagram), pay only on arrival, and never hand cash to a Calle Real "agency" that cannot show an RNT number.

Salento's **Calle Real bar strip** runs the same drink-spiking pattern as Bogotá's Zona T and Medellín's Parque Lleras, but at smaller scale and slower frequency. The pattern is identical: a friendly approach in a legitimate bar, a suggestion to move to a "better" second venue two streets over, a drink you did not see prepared, and a partial-amnesia walk back to your hostel with your phone, cash, and cards gone. The defense is the same: **order every drink yourself directly from the bartender, watch it poured, and refuse to relocate to a second bar with a stranger you have just met**. Travel in pairs and agree in advance that neither of you leaves Calle Real with anyone new.

Salento rewards a few small habits. Use Uber or InDriver from PEI or AXM (both apps work in the airports). Buy Cocora Willys tickets at the cooperative kiosk window on Plaza de Bolívar — never from a street tout. Verify any agency's RNT number on the **Registro Nacional de Turismo** website before paying a deposit. Drink only with people you arrived in town with. The pueblo is otherwise one of the most relaxed places in Colombia, and the Cocora hike — the most photographed landscape in the country — is genuinely worth the trip.
