# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication, but phone numbers do change, consulates move, and hospitals occasionally renumber their switchboards. If a number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus *Colombia* or the city name; you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results.

**Before you travel**, we strongly recommend you double-check your own embassy or consulate's current number directly on its official website — the US State Department's travel.state.gov, the UK's gov.uk, Canada's travel.gc.ca, Ireland's dfa.ie, or Australia's smartraveller.gov.au. Save the main switchboard and the after-hours duty-officer line to your phone before departure, and screenshot this appendix so you can read it in a taxi or a boat where your signal cuts out. Even a verified number can become a wrong number between editions.

## The numbers that work everywhere in Colombia

These are the lines to dial from any phone — mobile or landline, Colombian SIM or roaming — anywhere in the country. All of them are free.

- **Unified emergency (all services):** **123**. Dispatchers speak intermittent English in Bogotá and Medellín; Spanish elsewhere. This is the single number to call if you are not sure which service you need; the operator will route you to police, ambulance, or fire as appropriate. **123** works on any mobile phone in Colombia, even without a SIM and even on a locked screen.
- **Policía Nacional de Colombia:** **112** (also routes through 123). The national police, with unified jurisdiction.
- **Policía de Turismo (national):** **01 8000 518 000**. The tourist-specific division — call this first for any crime affecting a tourist if you have a moment and phone signal.
- **Ambulance (Cruz Roja):** **125** in most cities. For emergencies, **123** is faster.
- **Fire (Bomberos):** **119**.
- **Anti-kidnapping hotline (GAULA — Grupos de Acción Unificada por la Libertad Personal):** **165**. The dedicated line for *secuestro* cases, including *paseo millonario*.
- **Migración Colombia:** **01 8000 510 454** (national). For passport-stamp issues, visa extensions, and suspected fraud-by-fake-officials.
- **Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (SIC) — consumer complaints:** **01 8000 910 165** (national).
- **Anti-poisoning hotline (CIGTOX — Centro de Información y Gestión Tóxica):** **01 8000 941 414**.

## Card issuer emergency lines (callable from Colombia)

When you call, have your card number (or the last four digits), the approximate transaction amount, and the time of the fraudulent charge ready.

- **Visa Global Customer Assistance (collect from Colombia):** +1 303 967 1096. 24/7, English. Visa also maintains a Colombia toll-free line at **01 800 912 5713**.
- **Mastercard Global Service (collect from Colombia):** +1 636 722 7111. Colombia toll-free: **01 800 912 1303**.
- **American Express International Service Center (collect):** +1 336 393 1111. Colombia toll-free: **01 800 913 8383**.

If your card was issued by a bank (not directly by Visa, Mastercard, or Amex), the fastest route is the number printed on the back of the card. That line routes you to your own issuer's fraud desk and they can freeze the card in under a minute. Save this number in your phone under a searchable contact name before you travel — hunting for a number on a stolen card is impossible.

## If you lose your passport

1. **Report it first** — go to the nearest Policía de Turismo office or CAI and file a *denuncia* for a lost or stolen passport. You will leave with the signed and sealed report. Colombia also accepts online filings at **denunciavirtual.policia.gov.co**, but for passport loss, in-person is faster because of the document production required.
2. **Call your embassy's main switchboard** — or its after-hours line if it is a weekend — and request an emergency passport appointment. US, UK, Canadian, Irish, and Australian embassies in Bogotá all issue same-day or next-day emergency travel documents; the fee varies.
3. **Bring** the *denuncia*, any photo ID you still have (a driver's license photo on your phone counts), two passport photos (photo booths are common in Bogotá shopping centers and in the major airports), and the payment method your embassy specifies.
4. **Exit stamp from Migración Colombia**: before flying out with an emergency travel document, you will need to coordinate with Migración Colombia at the departure airport — airlines departing El Dorado (Bogotá) and José María Córdova (Medellín) see emergency documents regularly. Your embassy will provide a letter.

## If your phone is stolen

1. **Freeze the SIM** by calling your carrier's customer-service line from any other phone. Colombian carriers: **Claro** \*611 or \*111; **Movistar** \*611; **Tigo** \*123. If you use a foreign SIM, call your home carrier's international line — most have a 24/7 theft-reporting number that forwards from Colombia. Tourist eSIMs (Airalo, Saily, Holafly) can be suspended in-app.
2. **File a *denuncia*** at the nearest Policía de Turismo office with your device's IMEI (if you recorded it) and its serial number. Insurance claims require the sealed report.
3. **Lock and wipe remotely** via Apple's Find My or Google's Find My Device from any browser. Do this before filing the *denuncia* — a wiped phone cannot leak your email and banking apps while you wait in line.
4. **Change passwords immediately** for any account whose app was installed on the phone: email, banking, social media. Start with email; everything else resets off it.

Colombia operates a centralized stolen-phone IMEI registry (the BDA, Base de Datos Administrativa) — reporting your phone's IMEI makes it unusable on every Colombian carrier network, which significantly reduces the market for stolen devices.

## If your credit card is stolen or skimmed

1. **Freeze the card** by calling the number on the back (or use the issuer's app's "lock card" feature, which works in seconds). This matters more than filing a police report first — every minute you wait is a minute the thief can use the card.
2. **Report to police** with a *denuncia*. Insurance reimbursement and the card issuer's fraud-dispute process both want the sealed report.
3. **Dispute the charges** with your issuer. Most US, European, Australian, and Japanese card issuers reverse fraudulent charges fully; the *denuncia* speeds the process.
4. **Watch for follow-on phishing** — a skimmed card often leads to email and SMS phishing pretending to be your bank. Never click a link in one of these messages; go to your bank's app or website directly.

## A closing note

This appendix is meant to be read once on the plane, screenshot for offline use, and forgotten about for the rest of your trip. Phone numbers change. Consulates move. Hospitals renumber their switchboards. If any number in this list fails, **123** — the unified Colombian emergency line — will still work, and your embassy's website will still have the current line for your country.

Travel safely, travel calmly, and if the worst happens, remember: a stolen phone is an inconvenience, not a crisis. Filing a *denuncia*, freezing a card, and calling an embassy are ordinary procedures — Policía de Turismo officers and consular staff in Colombia handle these cases every day, and most travelers resolve everything within a few hours and return to their trip. Colombia is not a dangerous country for prepared tourists; it is a vibrant one with specific hazards. Help is close, and English is closer than you think.
