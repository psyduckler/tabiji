Varanasi is one of Hinduism's holiest cities and a chapter of three temple-and-river scams that work specifically because the visitor's politeness is doubled by the spiritual setting. Risk concentrates at Dashashwamedh Ghat during the evening Ganga Aarti, the boat launches all along the ghats from Assi to Manikarnika, and the maze-like lanes leading to the Vishwanath Temple complex. The Ganga Offering Shake-Down is the highest-recurrence scam here: a man in priestly garb places an oil lamp and flower offering on your lap during the Aarti, says a quick prayer pushing them toward the river, then opens a donation book showing ₹2,000 to ₹5,000 entries from international visitors and demands you match. The boatmen quote sub-market sunrise rides — ₹100 against a going rate of ₹300 to ₹500 per hour — then announce mid-ride "viewing fees," "burning-ghat stop fees," and "return surcharges" that turn the ride into ₹1,500.

The defenses are simple and protect against all three. At the Aarti, watch from the steps with your hands at your sides; never accept items placed in your hands by strangers — the items create the donation obligation. Before any boat leaves the dock, agree the exact total price including all stops and the return ghat in writing, and know that the fair private-boat rate is ₹300 to ₹500 per hour. Navigate the Vishwanath lanes yourself with Google Maps (the GPS works through the lanes, just slowly); decline "shortcuts" from strangers, especially when the shortcut routes you through a "family silk shop" selling polyester at Banarasi prices. Buy real Banarasi silk only at government emporiums like UP Handloom or certified handloom cooperatives where the weave is tagged.
