Udaipur is the gentlest chapter in this book — two scams, both low-to-medium severity, both clustered around the city's two most-photographed sites: Lake Pichola and the City Palace. The Lake Palace Boat Photography Scam runs at the lake ghats: a boatman quotes ₹200 for a sunset ride that should run ₹400 to ₹800 per hour for a private boat, positions perfectly in front of the Taj Lake Palace mid-ride, and pulls out a camera before you can decline. Twenty photos later, he is asking ₹500 each. The City Palace Miniature Painting Pitch happens in the art shops along the lanes leading from the City Palace entrance — a 20-minute demonstration of real Rajasthani brushwork is followed by a sales pitch for paintings at ₹5,000 to ₹50,000 that are usually factory reproductions printed onto similar paper.

The fix for both is the same: agree on the all-inclusive total price before any service starts. For the boat, that means a single quoted hourly rate covering the route, the duration, the return ghat, and any photography — with the boatman confirming verbally before you push off. For the miniature painting, enjoy the demonstration but do not feel obligated to buy; Udaipur has government-certified art cooperatives at the Shilpgram craft village outside the city and the Bhartiya Lok Kala Mandal museum where authentic miniature paintings from trained Rajasthan-School-of-Art practitioners are sold at fair prices.
