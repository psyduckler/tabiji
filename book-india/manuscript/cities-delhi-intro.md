Delhi is the volume's anchor city for a reason: six documented scams across three concentrations — the New Delhi Railway Station and Paharganj corridor, the Connaught Place steps and inner circle, and the monument approaches at Red Fort, Jama Masjid, and Qutub Minar. The scam pattern density per neighborhood is the highest in this book. Polo-shirt "Delhi Tourism" reps walk arrivals from the NDLS platform to a fake India Tourism storefront on Main Bazaar where a suited man rebooks the trip into a $300 to $500 package. Auto-rickshaw drivers approaching the Red Fort announce a "government holiday" or a "VIP visit" and pivot to a silk emporium where they earn a 30 to 40 percent commission. A boy of sixteen waiting at every Paharganj drop-off says your booked guesthouse is "full tonight" and walks you to his uncle's similar-named place that costs double.

The fastest defenses, in order of importance: the real India Tourism office is at 88 Janpath, Connaught Place, and not at any storefront in Paharganj — walk there yourself if you need help. Take Uber or Ola directly to the monument gates and verify yourself before believing any "closed" claim. Screenshot your booking confirmation with the address before you exit the train. At the Rajiv Chowk metro, use the official token vending machines at the counter — never accept help from a stranger who volunteers it. On the Delhi-Agra Yamuna Expressway, agree in writing on no unscheduled stops and pay tolls only at the toll plaza booth.
