Jaipur is the Pink City, the third leg of the Golden Triangle, and the densest single chapter in this book at seven documented scams. Risk concentrates in three zones: the Hawa Mahal lanes and Johari Bazaar gem district in the old city, the approach road and ramp at Amber Fort, and the Jaipur Railway Station arrivals area on its way to the Sindhi Camp bus stand. The Johari Bazaar Gem Export Pitch is the most elaborate scam in this volume — a charming local invites you for chai, explains an "incredible opportunity" to courier ₹45,000 of gems home for resale, and the "investment rubies" turn out to be cut glass. The Hawa Mahal Rickshaw Textile Detour is the most consistently reported — drivers at suspiciously low half-day rates loop you through suburban silk emporiums where prices are 5x to 10x Bapu Bazaar rates. At Amber Fort, the elephant-ride handler quotes ₹600 at the bottom of the ramp and announces the price was "per person" halfway up.

The five most useful defenses for Jaipur, in order: never buy gems as "investment" from an unsolicited source — the entire pitch is a scam, full stop; book rickshaws only through your hotel front desk or via Ola/Uber, with "no shops, direct to destination" stated at the start of every ride; check the official Amber Fort elephant-ride price board before approaching any handler (the regulated rate is around ₹900 for two riders); decline every "gift" offered to your wrist or hands without a price first (Hawa Mahal henna sellers and Amber Fort sympathy hustlers both rely on this opening); and use only the real Rajasthan Tourism Development Corporation (RTDC) office inside or directly adjacent to Jaipur Railway Station, not the look-alike "Government Tourist Information Office" storefronts on MI Road.
