Goa is unique in India for one structural reason: the local taxi union successfully blocked Uber and Ola for years, leaving a state-protected cartel that charges three to five times the going rate for any ride. The cartel is the framing for nearly every Goa scam in this book. Risk concentrates at Dabolim Airport's taxi rank, the strip from Aguada Fort to Calangute and Baga in North Goa, the Anjuna and Vagator beach-and-club zone, and the rental-shop strip around Baga. The Taxi Mafia Fixed-Rate Extortion is the headline scam — ₹800 to ₹1,200 for a 20-minute Dabolim-to-Baga ride that costs ₹150 in Mumbai. The Aguada Fort Scratch-Card Resort Membership Scam is the most expensive: scratch cards distributed near the fort lead to ₹6 lakh to ₹15 lakh timeshare pitches at Karma Group properties. The Anjuna Drug Sting and Police Bribe Setup is the most dangerous: a stranger sells, "police" arrive, and a ₹5,000 to ₹50,000 cash "fine" disappears the arrest.

The Goa playbook in five moves: pre-book taxis through your hotel before arrival; download Rapido (the bike-taxi app has partial North Goa coverage at fair rates) or use GoaMiles (the official Goa government taxi app); rent a scooter (₹300 to ₹500 a day for total transport independence — but photograph every panel, scratch, and scuff at pickup, with timestamps); refuse every scratch card or "promotional gift" from a stranger near tourist sites; do not purchase or accept drugs from any stranger in Goa, ever, regardless of context — the police-sting variant is widely documented and the only safe defense is the rule. If officers approach over a substance, demand to be taken to a police station formally and contact your embassy.
