Kolkata is the City of Joy, the capital of Bengali culture, and seven scams that span the city's biggest transit hubs and tourist corridors. The risk is concentrated at Howrah Station — India's busiest railway hub and the densest tout corridor in this book — and ripples out to Kalighat Temple, New Market on Lindsay Street, the airport (CCU) taxi rank, the Sudder Street backpacker hub, the rickshaw stands around Victoria Memorial, and the small shops and taxi stands of central Kolkata where the currency-note swap operates. The Howrah Station Ticket Trap is the marquee scam: a man in a quasi-official shirt tells arriving travelers the official ticket office is closed and the train is fully booked, then walks them to a "government-approved agency" charging three times the IRCTC rate. The Kalighat Temple Donation Scam works the same lever as Varanasi's Aarti: saffron-robed "priests" offer "VIP darshan," place flowers in your hand, lead you to a side shrine, and produce a worn donation book with foreign-name entries written in identical handwriting.

The five Kolkata defenses, prioritized: walk past every helper outside or inside Howrah Station to the official Indian Railways ticket counter, which is open 24 hours; book trains in advance on IRCTC.co.in or the Rail Connect app, which accept international credit cards; enter Kalighat Temple through the official Gate 2 public entrance and stand in the regular darshan line, declining every priest who approaches outside; call your hotel before getting in any taxi from CCU airport, and insist the driver take you to your booked address ("Take me there anyway — I'll check myself") if anyone tells you the hotel is "flooded" or "closed for renovation"; and photograph the serial number of any large bill before handing it over to a taxi driver, especially ₹500 or ₹2,000 notes, against the currency-swap sleight-of-hand at central Kolkata stands.
