Chennai is the gateway to the Tamil south, anchored by Marina Beach, Kapaleeshwarar Temple in Mylapore, the textile bazaars of T. Nagar, and the colonial corridor of Egmore. Five scams work along these corridors. The Chennai Central Auto Commission Loop is the highest-recurrence: drivers outside the station quote ₹50 to your hotel — a third of the real rate — then loop through silk and jewelry showrooms in T. Nagar collecting commission at every door, and demand ₹500 for the "extended" ride two hours later. The Kapaleeshwarar Temple Blessing Shakedown follows the donation-book pattern from Kalighat and Varanasi — a man in priestly garments drapes a flower garland and produces a notebook with ₹2,000 to ₹5,000 entries from "previous tourists." The T. Nagar Silk Saree Switcheroo is the most subtle: a shopkeeper unfolds a real Kanchipuram silk for you to feel, agrees the price, then swaps in a polyester piece during the elaborate tissue-paper wrap — the real silk you touched never leaves the counter.

Chennai defenses concentrate around five rules. Skip the auto-rickshaw queue at Chennai Central entirely and book Ola Auto or Uber Auto from inside the station's free WiFi, with "no shops, direct to destination only" stated at the start. At Kapaleeshwarar, keep your hands at your sides outside the main sanctum and decline every garland and tilak before they touch you. At Marina Beach, use the official Chennai Smart City parking app, demand a printed municipal rate card and a serial-numbered receipt before paying, and offer to call the Chennai traffic police helpline (103) if pressed by men in fluorescent vests. For silk: do the burn test on a thread before paying (real silk smells like burnt hair, synthetic smells like plastic), watch the packaging without looking away, and shop at government-certified emporia like Co-optex or VTB Silks where the silk is sealed in plastic before it reaches you. Book multi-day Mahabalipuram or Pondicherry tours only through Ministry of Tourism-registered operators, pay partial deposits never the full amount upfront, and use a credit card for chargeback protection.
