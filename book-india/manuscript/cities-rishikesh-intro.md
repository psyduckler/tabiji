Rishikesh is the spiritual capital of India and the volume's smallest chapter — two scams, both targeting the wellness-and-yoga visitor specifically. The town clings to the Ganga where it leaves the Himalayas, with the ashram cluster concentrated in Tapovan and Laxman Jhula, and risk almost entirely localized to those neighborhoods. The Tapovan Fake-Ashram YTT Trap is the headline scam: an ashram advertises a 200-hour Yoga Teacher Training for $500 over 28 days, the website is polished, the WhatsApp confirmation is quick, and the certification turns out to come from a made-up yoga alliance with no recognition in your home country. Classes have 60-plus students, the "guru" has no formal training, and the worst-rated ashrams have documented histories of targeting solo female travelers. The Rishikesh Fake-GST Billing scam runs across restaurants and small shops citywide — a 5%-to-18% GST line is added to bills, often without a valid GSTIN registration, and the "tax" goes into the owner's pocket rather than the government.

The two defenses are simple. For yoga: verify the school is registered with Yoga Alliance at yogaalliance.org before paying anything, read recent Google and TripAdvisor reviews focusing specifically on class size and quality, ask the school to connect you with recent graduates (legitimate operators will), and budget $800 to $1,500 for a real 200-hour YTT in Rishikesh — courses below that range are almost always factory-mill operations. For GST: ask for a proper printed invoice with the GSTIN number on every bill above ₹500, verify the GSTIN at gst.gov.in if the amount is significant, and refuse any "GST" line on a handwritten bill from a small eatery or street vendor — they are exempt under Indian tax law and the charge is invented.
