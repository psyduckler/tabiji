Bangalore is India's IT capital and the chapter that targets the business-traveler corridor: airport-to-IT-park transfers, Silk Board Junction commute lanes, Majestic Bus Stand and central transit, HSR Layout and Koramangala neighborhood streets, and the Commercial Street tourist shopping strip. Five scams cover these corridors. The Silk Board Auto Meter-Speed Trick is the most pervasive: drivers buy aftermarket meter chips that double or triple the per-kilometer tick, turning a ₹150 ride into a ₹350 demand. The HSR Layout Driver Hospital-Photo Hustle is the most emotionally engineered: at the end of an otherwise normal ride, the driver shows photos of a wife and newborn twins in a hospital bed and asks for ₹2,000 — Deccan Herald documented HSR drivers sharing identical photos for over a year. The Kempegowda Airport Prepaid-Tout Hustle uses men in semi-official vests to intercept arrivals before they reach the official KIAL counter, quoting ₹2,500 against the real rate of ₹800 to ₹1,200.

Bangalore defenses are clean: skip street autos entirely on routes from Silk Board, Majestic, and KR Market — book Ola Auto or Uber Auto through the app where the fare is fixed before you climb in; the Bangalore official auto rate is ₹30 base plus ₹15 per kilometer, useful as a sanity check; ask any sob-story driver for the hospital name and ward number in the first sentence — the act usually collapses on contact; walk past every approach inside Kempegowda Airport's terminal to the official KIAL prepaid taxi counter or the Ola/Uber pickup zone; and buy SIM cards only at the airport's branded Airtel and Jio counters or any official carrier store, with passport-based eKYC and a test call before leaving the counter — back-channel "tourist SIMs" at ₹1,500 are often registered against someone else's Aadhaar and may be deactivated within 24 to 48 hours.
