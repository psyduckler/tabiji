Agra is the marquee Golden Triangle stop and the city where five distinct scams cluster around two landmarks. The Taj Mahal east and west gates and the Sheikh Salim Chishti dargah at Fatehpur Sikri are the highest-density risk zones; Sadar Bazaar drop-offs and the Yamuna Expressway are the secondary corridors. The Marble Factory Commission Tour Overcharge runs daily out of Tajganj — a polite owner walks you past artisans, claims his family carved for the original Taj craftsmen, and lands a $400 to $2,500 quote on bowls that retail at Sadar Bazaar for ₹3,000 to ₹4,000. The Times of India and News18 documented foreign tourists who paid ₹37,000 and $2,500 to Taj-area shops in late 2025 and early 2026. A "Taj Mahal" Google ad sells $35 invalid passes through ticketstajmahal.com against the ₹1,300 official ASI rate — Agra cybercrime police filed an FIR against the operator in April 2026.

The cleanest defense: book Taj tickets only at asi.payumoney.com or asi.nic.in (verify the URL bar reads "asi" before paying), refuse every driver-suggested marble factory or "government emporium" detour, and hire Fatehpur Sikri guides only at the Archaeological Survey of India counter inside the monument — never from men loitering outside Buland Darwaza. The Agra Development Authority installed signboards in 2023 stating that no chadar at the dargah may cost more than ₹500. Save the Agra Tourist Police hotline (+91 9454402764) before arrival; the email so-tourism.ag@up.gov.in handles complaints from foreign visitors.
