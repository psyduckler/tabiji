Mumbai is India's financial capital, its Bollywood capital, and the city where seven distinct scams target the maximum number of corridors. Risk concentrates at Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (CSMIA), CST station and the Gateway of India, the Andheri West and Bandra dating-app venues, the Colaba Causeway market strip, the Gateway of India ferry terminal serving Elephanta Island, and the booking office at CSMT. The CST Black-Taxi Meter Scam runs daily on the kaali peeli (black-and-yellow) ranks at the major tourist drop-offs — drivers quote a price that "somehow" doubles at the destination, claim the meter was "running on night rate," or produce a laminated rate card with confusing multipliers. The Mumbai Dating App Café Bill Scam became national news in 2024 when 22 victims were identified at just two Andheri pubs, with one man losing ₹61,000 at The Godfather Club; women involved are paid accomplices who receive a cut of the inflated bill.

The Mumbai defenses are concentrated and consistent: use Ola or Uber over kaali peeli wherever possible (the fare is shown upfront and tracked); use only the official prepaid taxi counter inside Mumbai Airport's arrivals hall — the polo-shirt "helpers" claiming your hotel is "closed" pay themselves ₹500 to ₹2,000 per delivered tourist; for Tinder/Bumble first meets, pick the venue yourself (a well-known, highly reviewed public place) and check it on Google Maps and TripAdvisor before going (no reviews is a red flag); buy Elephanta Island tickets only at the official MTDC counter near the Gateway of India (₹200 round trip), not from touts quoting ₹500 to ₹800 for "VIP" boats; book trains in advance on IRCTC.co.in or use the Foreign Tourist Quota counter on the ground floor of CSMT with your passport — never the "travel agents" two blocks away.
