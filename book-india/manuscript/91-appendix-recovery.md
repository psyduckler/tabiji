# Appendix: The Recovery Playbook {-}

If you have been scammed in India, the next fifteen minutes matter more than the rest of the day. This appendix walks you through the steps in order — the first quarter-hour, the first hour, the first day, the first week. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. This one tells you what to do when avoidance failed.

## In the first 15 minutes {-}

**Get to a safe, public place.** A hotel lobby, a shopping mall, a uniformed-officer area at a railway station. Do not stay at the scam location. Do not engage further with the scammer.

**Photograph everything you still can.** The receipt or bill, the storefront, the rickshaw number, the auto driver's permit if visible, the rental shop's signage. If you are inside a hotel or restaurant, photograph the menu and any printed prices on the wall.

**Note the exact time and location.** Pin-drop on Google Maps if you can — the GPS coordinates are useful evidence.

**Call your card issuer.** If you paid by card, call the bank's 24/7 international number on the back of the card *before* doing anything else. Most international cards offer a 60-day chargeback window for fraudulent or misrepresented charges. The earlier you call, the easier the dispute.

## In the first hour {-}

**File a police report — the FIR.** Indian police complaints are formally called *First Information Reports* (FIR). Go to the nearest police station that has jurisdiction over the scam location. Bring your passport, a printed copy of your booking or receipt if any, and the photographs from step 1. Ask for an FIR specifically — not just an "incident report" or a verbal note. The FIR is what your travel insurance, your card issuer, and your embassy will ask for. India has online FIR filing at [citizenservices.gov.in](https://www.citizenservices.gov.in/) for some categories.

**Call the Tourist Police helpline at 1363.** This is the all-India multilingual tourist police helpline, available in twelve languages. They cannot recover money for you, but they can dispatch a local officer to mediate at the scam location, often with surprising speed.

**For digital and online fraud (fake hotel listings, lookalike ticket sites, UPI transfers gone wrong):** call the cybercrime helpline at **1930** or report online at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in/). The cyber cell can sometimes freeze the receiving account if you call within the first few hours.

## In the first 24 hours {-}

**Contact your embassy or consulate** if your passport was involved (theft of belongings, hostage-style detention by a vendor, drug-sting style escalation). The US Embassy in New Delhi is at Shantipath, Chanakyapuri (+91 11-2419-8000). The British High Commission is on Shantipath as well. The Australian, German, Canadian, and most European embassies cluster in Chanakyapuri. Most have 24-hour duty officer lines for arrested or stranded citizens.

**Open a travel-insurance claim.** Most travel insurance policies cover scam-related losses up to a per-incident limit if you have an FIR. The insurer will need: the FIR copy, the original booking or receipt (or the screenshot of the lookalike site), and a written statement of what happened.

**Document what was lost.** Make a list while it is fresh. Cash amounts in INR and USD. Card transactions. Bookings that were never honored. Items you bought that turned out to be counterfeit (gems, "antiques," "Banarasi silk").

## In the first week {-}

**Follow up on every open thread.** Card chargeback (call back if you have not received an interim credit). FIR (call the station for an update — Indian FIRs do progress, but they need a polite phone call every few days). Cyber cell (the case number is your touchpoint). Insurance (most insurers want documents within seven days).

**Consider a consumer-court complaint** for scams above ₹20,000 in losses. The District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission accepts foreign tourists' complaints under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Filing fees are minimal (₹100 to ₹500 for most claims). The hearing is scheduled within 90 days; awards are typically delivered within 12 months. This is most effective for scams against named businesses (a hotel that defrauded you, a tour agent who never delivered, a rental shop that fabricated damage).

**Write the review.** Tripadvisor, Google Maps, Booking.com — wherever the scam was. Future travelers will read it. Specific details (the exact name of the shop, the date, the amount) make these reviews defensible against takedown.

## What not to do {-}

- **Do not pay any "fine" in cash on the spot.** Real Indian fines are paid at a station or via challan, never to an officer's hand.
- **Do not return to the scam location to confront.** If you need to retrieve property, go with the police, not alone.
- **Do not delete the chat history, the booking confirmation, or the original receipt** — even if the scam was mortifying. Those are the evidence trail your card issuer and insurance will need.
- **Do not pay a "fixer" who promises to resolve the case.** This is itself a known scam variant.

You will probably not need this chapter. If you do, work the list in order. The first fifteen minutes are the most important; the rest follows from there.
