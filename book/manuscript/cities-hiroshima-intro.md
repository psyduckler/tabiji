Hiroshima is a quiet city by day and a lively one by night, and the scams here divide along those lines. The nightlife district of Nagarekawa and Shintenchi has the same kind of bar-hustler problem as Tokyo and Osaka, although on a smaller scale. Daytime scams cluster around food and the island of Miyajima.

Hiroshima's famous okonomiyaki restaurants include a handful of operators who overcharge foreign tourists and alter receipts. Photograph the menu before you order, and ask for an itemized receipt before you pay. Miyajima, the island with the famous floating torii gate, has three separate issues you should know about: deer that have become aggressive around food, restaurants that process credit card transactions three times while claiming each one failed, and souvenir shops that charge significantly more than the same shops at Hiroshima Station. The fix for all three is simple: finish any food before you board the ferry, watch your card terminal closely at every restaurant, and buy your souvenirs at the station rather than on the island.

The peace memorial and the approach to Itsukushima Shrine are both completely safe, and the city does not have the organized-crime reputation of Tokyo or Osaka. A careful evening and a careful day on Miyajima will cover most of what this chapter warns you about.
