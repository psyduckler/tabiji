# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication. Phone numbers do change — if a number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus "phone" and you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results.

## National (anywhere in Japan)

- **Police:** 110. Say "English please" clearly if you do not speak Japanese; dispatchers in tourist areas will bring a translator onto the line.
- **Fire and ambulance:** 119.
- **JNTO 24-hour Japan Visitor Hotline:** 050-3816-2787 (from Japan) or +81-50-3816-2787 (from overseas). Available in English, Chinese, Korean, and Japanese, around the clock.
- **Consumer Hotline for Tourists:** 03-5449-0906. Weekdays 10:00–12:00 and 13:00–16:00. English, Chinese, Korean, Thai, Vietnamese. This is the line to use for restaurant, bar, hotel, and transport disputes.
- **Consumer Affairs national hotline:** 188. Dial from any phone in Japan; no area code needed. Routes to the nearest regional consumer center. Japanese-language first; ask for English and you may be transferred.
- **Yorisoi multilingual helpline:** 0120-279-338 (toll-free). Daily 10:00–22:00. After the Japanese greeting, press 2 for non-Japanese-language support. Covers English, Chinese, Korean, Tagalog, Spanish, Portuguese, Thai, Vietnamese, Nepali, and Indonesian.
- **TELL English-language lifeline (toll-free):** 0800-300-8355. 9:00–23:00 Monday through Thursday; 9:00 Friday through 02:00 Sunday.
- **TELL call center:** 03-5774-0992. 9:00–23:00 daily.

- **Mastercard Japan toll-free (for overseas-issued cards):** 00531-11-3886. 24/7.
- **Mastercard International collect:** +1-636-722-7111.
- **American Express Japan toll-free:** 0120-020-120. 24/7, English and Japanese.
- **American Express International Service Center (Japan):** +81-3-3220-6100.
- **JCB PLAZA Call Center (for JCB cards issued outside Japan):** +81-3-6625-8379 (toll call — charges apply). 24/7. English, Chinese, Korean, Thai.

If your card was issued by a bank (not directly by Visa / Mastercard / Amex / JCB), the fastest route is to call the number printed on the back of your card. That line routes you to your own issuer's fraud desk and they can freeze the card in under a minute.

## English-speaking medical care

### Tokyo

- **St. Luke's International Hospital** (Akashicho, near Tsukiji / Ginza): 03-3541-5151. Full international patient department; walk-in available for urgent cases.
- **Tokyo Midtown Clinic** (Roppongi Station, directly connected): 03-5413-0080. English-speaking staff; appointment recommended.
- **Himawari — Tokyo Metropolitan Medical Information Service:** 03-5285-8181. Daily 9:00–20:00. English, Chinese, Korean, Thai, Spanish. Finds you a hospital or clinic in Tokyo that speaks your language.
- **Himawari emergency phone interpretation:** 03-5285-8185. Weekdays 17:00–20:00; weekends and holidays 9:00–20:00. Three-way interpretation between you and your attending doctor.

### Osaka

- **Akane Dotonbori Clinic** (near Namba Station, Dotonbori): 06-6213-3311. English- and Chinese-speaking; walk-ins accepted, extended hours including weekends.
- **Osaka International Clinic** (24/7 house-call and hotel-visit service): +81-70-9208-0747. Doctor or nurse comes to your hotel. Appointment required. Useful at night when nothing else is open.

### Kyoto

- **Kyoto University Hospital** (Sakyo ward): 075-751-3111. International coordination required for non-insured patients; the hospital partners with medical coordinators who can arrange treatment.
- In a true emergency, dial 119 for an ambulance — the dispatcher will route you to the nearest hospital that can accept you.

### Sapporo

- **Hokkaido University Hospital** (Kita-ku, central Sapporo): 011-716-1161. Large teaching hospital with international patient services.
- **Sapporo Medical Communication Hotline:** 011-211-2121. 24/7. Free phone interpretation in 21 languages, and assistance with hospital reservations.

### Hiroshima, Fukuoka, Nara, Okinawa, Yokohama

Smaller cities have fewer dedicated international clinics. Your fallback is to dial 119 for an ambulance and say "eigo onegai shimasu" (English please), or ask your hotel's front desk to call on your behalf. Major city hospitals almost always have at least one English-speaking doctor on staff, especially in their emergency departments. For Yokohama, St. Luke's in central Tokyo is only 30 minutes away by train and worth the trip for non-urgent care.

## Where to find a koban (police box) in each city

Japan's koban system is one of its most practical assets. Every major intersection in a tourist district has one, and many are open twenty-four hours. You do not need an appointment, and you do not need to speak Japanese to be helped — koban officers will call a language service onto the line with you.

- **Tokyo:** Kabukicho (directly inside the entertainment district), Shibuya Station Hachiko exit, Asakusa temple approach.
- **Kyoto:** Kyoto Station main hall, Gion (near Yasaka Shrine), Arashiyama Togetsukyo Bridge area.
- **Osaka:** Namba Station, Shinsaibashi shopping arcade, Kansai Airport.
- **Fukuoka:** Hakata Station (both exits), Tenjin intersection, Nakasu entrance.
- **Hiroshima:** Hiroshima Station, Peace Memorial Park, Hondori shopping arcade.
- **Nara:** Kintetsu Nara Station, Todai-ji approach.
- **Sapporo:** Sapporo Station south exit, Susukino main intersection, Odori Park.
- **Okinawa:** Naha Airport, Kokusai-dori, Chatan entertainment district.
- **Yokohama:** Yokohama Station central exit, Chinatown Zenrinmon gate, Minato Mirai tourist center.

## A note on calling 110 with limited Japanese

If you dial 110 and cannot explain what is happening in Japanese, say "English please" clearly and wait. Police dispatchers in tourist areas are trained to recognize this request and will bring a translator onto the line. State your location as a landmark or station name rather than as a street address — "Shibuya Crossing" is more useful to an English-speaking dispatcher than a ward address that is hard to pronounce. If you are calling from a mobile phone, the dispatcher can also pull your approximate location automatically, so even a rough "I am near Shibuya" is enough to get help moving.
