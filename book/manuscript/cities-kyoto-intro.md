Kyoto is one of the most visited cities in Japan, and for good reason. It is also one of the most tourist-trap-heavy, and a handful of the scams in this chapter are unique to Kyoto's particular mix of old-world beauty and packed streets. The city itself is safe, and violent crime is almost unheard of, but the places where tourists cluster have developed a small economy of overcharging, hustling, and donation-book tricks that you should know about before you arrive.

The scams in Kyoto cluster in four areas. The first is the nightlife strip of Pontocho and Gion, where hustlers outside bars offer "first drink free" and the bill arrives many times higher. The second is the geisha alleys of Gion, where photography tensions have produced both real ¥10,000 fines enforced by the local council and fake "manager" shakedowns run by opportunists. The third is the main tourist walks, especially near Kinkaku-ji, Kiyomizu-dera, and the Arashiyama bamboo grove, where restaurants with no visible prices charge tourists several times what locals pay. The fourth is Kansai Airport and Kyoto Station, where unlicensed taxis operate from the taxi queues.

Kyoto rewards planning. Book your restaurants and tea ceremonies on Tabelog or Google Maps in advance, not on the street, and most of the city's worst tricks never come up. The temples, the gardens, and the long walk up to Fushimi Inari are exactly as lovely as you have been told.
