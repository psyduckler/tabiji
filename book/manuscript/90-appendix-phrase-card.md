# Appendix A: Japanese Exit-Phrase Card {-}

> **Editorial note (to be resolved before final publication):** This phrase card is a best-effort draft. Native-speaker review is required before publication. If you are traveling with someone fluent in Japanese, have them check the phrases you plan to use.

These are the phrases most likely to end a scam in Japan. They are arranged by situation. For each phrase you will see the **Japanese in kanji**, a romaji pronunciation guide in italics, and the **English translation**.

A practical suggestion: before your trip, take a screenshot of this appendix on your phone, and bookmark any phrase you think you are most likely to need. If you can, say the first three or four out loud a few times. You will feel much less awkward using them when the moment comes.

## Asking before you commit

**How much is it?**
いくらですか？
*Ikura desu ka?*

**Please show me the price list.**
料金表を見せてください。
*Ryoukin-hyou wo misete kudasai.*

**How much is the seat charge and cover fee?**
席料とお通しはいくらですか？
*Sekiryou to otoshi wa ikura desu ka?*

**Do you accept credit cards?**
クレジットカードは使えますか？
*Kurejitto kaado wa tsukaemasu ka?*

## Declining and leaving

**No, thank you.**
いいえ、結構です。
*Iie, kekkou desu.*

**I am leaving now.**
もう帰ります。
*Mou kaerimasu.*

**I do not want this.**
これは要りません。
*Kore wa irimasen.*

**Please let me through.**
通してください。
*Tooshite kudasai.*

## Responding to a scam in progress

**I am calling the police.**
警察を呼びます。
*Keisatsu wo yobimasu.*

**I would like to call my card company.**
カード会社に電話します。
*Kaado-gaisha ni denwa shimasu.*

**I was scammed. Please help.**
詐欺にあいました。助けてください。
*Sagi ni aimashita. Tasukete kudasai.*

**Please give me an itemized receipt.**
明細書をください。
*Meisai-sho wo kudasai.*

**I did not order this.**
これは注文していません。
*Kore wa chuumon shite imasen.*

**This is not what I agreed to.**
話が違います。
*Hanashi ga chigaimasu.*

## Getting help

**Where is the nearest police box (koban)?**
一番近い交番はどこですか？
*Ichiban chikai kouban wa doko desu ka?*

**I need an official taxi.**
正規のタクシーに乗りたいです。
*Seiki no takushii ni noritai desu.*

**Please dial 110.**
110番を呼んでください。
*Hyaku-too-ban wo yonde kudasai.*

**Is there someone who speaks English?**
英語が話せる人はいますか？
*Eigo ga hanaseru hito wa imasu ka?*

**Excuse me, I have to go.**
すみません、行かなければなりません。
*Sumimasen, ikanakereba narimasen.*

## Reading yen amounts on a bill

| Amount | Kanji | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|
| ¥1,000 | 千円 | *sen-en* |
| ¥5,000 | 五千円 | *go-sen-en* |
| ¥10,000 | 一万円 | *ichi-man-en* |
| ¥50,000 | 五万円 | *go-man-en* |
| ¥100,000 | 十万円 | *juu-man-en* |
| ¥1,000,000 | 百万円 | *hyaku-man-en* |

If the number on your bill is a multi-digit price like ¥130,000, the kanji will read "juu-san-man-en" (literally, thirteen ten-thousands). Any bill that ends in "-man-en" is in the tens-of-thousands range. If a cashier tells you a price you do not understand, ask them to write it out ("suuji de kaite kudasai" — *please write the number*) and they will almost always oblige.

## A note on tone

The phrases above are polite, not aggressive. In Japan, polite firmness is stronger than raised voices. An "iie, kekkou desu" delivered calmly is understood as an absolute refusal, and no elaboration is expected. If you find yourself needing stronger language, the phrase "警察を呼びます" (*keisatsu wo yobimasu*) changes the atmosphere in the room immediately. Most scam operators will back down the moment the word "keisatsu" is spoken clearly.
