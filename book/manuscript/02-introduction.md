# How to Use This Book {-}

Japan is one of the safest countries in the world, and for most tourists it stays that way. In an average week in Tokyo you can walk almost anywhere, at almost any hour, and meet only kindness. We want you to come home with stories about the food, the bullet trains, and the hot springs, not about a ¥500,000 bar tab or a taxi that took you the long way around.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents sixty specific scams that can trip up first-time visitors, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been reported by real travelers, cross-referenced against embassy advisories and local news, and confirmed against recent police reports.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First you will see the name of the scam, the category it falls into (nightlife, transport, sightseeing, and so on), and how serious and how common it is. Then a plain description of how the scam unfolds, with real yen amounts where we have them. Then a short section on how to avoid it, and where it happens. If you can read one entry, you can read them all.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. Most scams in Japan are variations on six simple patterns, and if you learn the patterns, you will be able to spot a new scam that is not even in this book. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **Japanese exit-phrase card**. Short phrases you can say, word for word, in the moments where your politeness would normally get you into trouble. Take a screenshot of it on your phone.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A one-page flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens, you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. Police, ambulance, tourist hotlines, embassies, and the nearest English-speaking hospitals, organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and the three appendices. Skim the chapters for the cities you are visiting. Take a screenshot of the phrase card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book ever becomes relevant during your trip, open it again in the taxi or in your hotel room and check what to do.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.

Safe travels.
