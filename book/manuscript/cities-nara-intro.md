Nara is small, beautiful, and unusually calm even by Japanese standards. The most serious "scam" in this chapter is not really a scam in the usual sense. It is the famous deer of Nara Park, which have become genuinely aggressive during mating season, and which injured over a hundred tourists in 2024. The other scams here are small-city versions of patterns you will see elsewhere: overpriced souvenirs, a ryokan bait-and-switch pattern, and taxi drivers who take long routes.

The key to Nara is that almost everything important is within walking distance. Kintetsu Nara Station to Todai-ji is about twenty minutes on foot through lovely streets. You rarely need a taxi, and skipping them avoids the entire taxi problem at once. The souvenirs along Sanjo-dori and Higashimuki-dori are typically marked up two or three times over what you would pay at the Kintetsu Nara department store or a Don Quijote branch. Save your shopping for the end of the day on the way back to Kyoto or Osaka.

The deer deserve respect. They are wild animals with a population of over a thousand in the park alone. Feed them quickly, keep your hands raised and empty when you are out of crackers, and avoid the big stags with full antlers during the rut in September and October.
