Okinawa is a different kind of place. The beaches, the reef, and the Ryukyuan culture are all wonderful, and the pace of life is slower than anywhere else in Japan. The scams, however, are more varied than you might expect from a resort destination. Naha has its own bottakuri bar scene in the Matsuyama entertainment district, Naha Airport has the usual unlicensed-taxi problem, and rental car returns are routinely directed to overpriced partner gas stations.

Two Okinawa-specific patterns are worth flagging. The first is the "juicy bar" scene around the US military bases in Chatan and Kin-cho, which has a documented history of drink-spiking and card-fraud targeting service members. If you are traveling with a family member who is active or retired military, read the Okinawa chapter carefully together. The second is the "ore-ore" phone fraud, in which someone posing as a police officer cold-calls hotel rooms, especially on the outer islands, claiming your bank account has been used in a fraud investigation. This is a well-established Japanese scam that has shifted to target tourists, and losses per incident are large.

The practical defense for Okinawa is to use the Yui Rail monorail wherever you can, take a taxi only from an official queue, and never trust a cold-call from anyone claiming to be the police or a bank.
