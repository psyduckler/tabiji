Sapporo is Japan's northernmost major city and the gateway to Hokkaido's ski season, food, and national parks. The scams in Sapporo cluster almost entirely in one district, Susukino, and are of two kinds: the familiar nightlife bottakuri pattern (hustlers, girls bars, and champagne-heavy bills) and a less-familiar pattern aimed at women, in which host clubs encourage "tsukebarai" tab-debts that can lead to much more serious situations. The second is worth reading carefully if you are traveling with a daughter or visiting Susukino at night yourself.

Outside Susukino, Sapporo's other scams are mostly around food aimed at tourists: the Nijo Market kaisendon overcharge, the tourist-oriented all-you-can-eat crab-buffet chains, and the famous Ramen Yokocho alley, where prices run twenty to thirty percent above what you will pay at the better ramen shops nearby. None of these are dangerous, and most are avoidable with five minutes on Tabelog or Google Maps before you eat.

A practical note for Sapporo: if you rent a car, check your hotel parking setup carefully before you arrive. Several hotels in Chuo-ku have stacker garages with strict vehicle-size limits, and they will not refund your parking fee if your rental does not fit.
