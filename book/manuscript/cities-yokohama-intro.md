Yokohama is Tokyo's next-door neighbor, and most visitors to Tokyo also spend a day or two here for the Chinatown, the bay, and the easy escape from the crowds. The scams are unfortunately concentrated in exactly those tourist areas.

Chinatown accounts for two of Yokohama's six documented scams: an all-you-can-eat restaurant with hidden leftover surcharges, and the sweet-chestnut sample that becomes a forced sale. Both are easy to avoid once you know to expect them. The more serious pattern is in Fukutomicho and the adjacent Isezakicho, where a documented drink-spike scam produced 249 reported victims and roughly ¥147 million in losses in just eleven months of 2023. This is the most dangerous nightlife pattern in any of the cities in this book. The short version: do not follow anyone into a bar in Fukutomicho after dark, for any reason.

Yokohama Station's west exit has an aggressive hustler pattern of the same kind as Tokyo's Kabukicho or Fukuoka's Hakata Station, and the area around Noge has a hostess-bar overcharging problem common to several Japanese cities. The rule is the same as elsewhere: Tabelog or Google Maps before you sit, printed menu with prices, and a polite but firm refusal of anyone with a clipboard who approaches you on the street.
