Fukuoka is a relatively small, relatively calm city, and you may be tempted to let your guard down. Do not. A cluster of organized scams operates in the Nakasu and Tenjin nightlife districts and at Hakata Station's east exit, and they are among the most aggressive in Japan. Fukuoka police prosecuted a twenty-person hustler network in 2019 and another ring in 2024. The scams have not gone away.

If you avoid the east exit of Hakata Station (use the west exit, Hakata-guchi, instead), avoid following anyone into a bar in Nakasu, and avoid any bar arrangement that comes through a matching app, you will almost certainly avoid the most expensive trouble Fukuoka has to offer. The yatai food stands by the river are a wonderful Fukuoka tradition, but the ones in Nakasu have developed a reputation for charging tourists double. Eat the yatai in Tenjin instead, especially around Nagahama, where the pricing is straightforward and the quality is better.

Short-term rentals in Fukuoka deserve special attention. A pattern of hosts demanding cash deposits "in the fridge" keeps appearing in reviews. Book through Booking.com or Airbnb only, pay through the platform, and never transfer money outside the app.
