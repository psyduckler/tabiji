The Dental Tourism Field Guide

Cover direction prototypes — for review

About these prototypes: These are functional concept sketches built in SVG to communicate visual direction. They are not production-ready cover designs. A real cover designer would refine typography (custom serif lockups, proper kerning), replace SVG illustrations with real photography for Direction 1, apply professional color theory, and produce a final spec for print, ebook, and audio formats. The purpose here is to compare directions, pick one or two finalists, and brief a designer.

Direction 1 — Kitchen Table Quote

The estimate on the table at the moment cost shock lands.

Photography-driven $$$ to produce

Close-up overhead view of an unfolded printed dental estimate on a wooden kitchen table. Echoes the book's opening line ("a dental quote made your stomach drop") visually before a reader has read a word. Avoids dental-imagery cliches entirely.

Most emotionally precise. Differentiated from the entire category shelf.
Highly screenshot-worthy for social marketing.
Requires real photography ($1,500–$4,000 custom shoot) to look polished.
SVG version here is a placeholder; real version needs editorial photography.

Closest comparables: The Cost of Hope (Bennett), An American Sickness (Rosenthal).

Direction 2 — Field Guide Aesthetic

Reference manual you keep at hand.

Type-driven $ to produce

Leans explicitly into "field guide" as a genre. Deep forest green, generous margins, restrained gold accents, a small clipboard ornament, "Volume One" framing for series extensibility. Counterpoints the noisy clinic-marketing aesthetic of the dental tourism category.

Cheapest direction by far. Stacks beautifully if Tabiji publishes more field guides.
Ages slowly. Reads clearly at thumbnail size on Amazon.
If executed poorly, can look generic. Needs a typographer with strong instincts.

Closest comparables: On Writing Well, The Elements of Style, Penguin Modern Classics paperbacks.

Direction 3 — The Pause

Slow down. You have leverage.

Concept-driven $$ to produce

A single graphic element — the universal pause symbol — anchored by the "20:00" timer beneath it. Maps directly to the book's central thesis (the 20-Minute Safety Pause, the leverage framework). Mostly negative space. Calm, distinctive.

Concept covers travel well in social media — readers screenshot them.
Distinct from category cliches; signals the book's core promise immediately.
Risks being read as too abstract by Amazon shoppers scanning for dental info.

Closest comparables: Thinking, Fast and Slow, Algorithms to Live By.

Direction 4 — Type-Driven Authority

No imagery. The title is the cover.

Type-driven $ to produce

Bold serif title occupies most of the cover. Two-color palette (cream + oxblood). The strongest pull quote ("Hope is important. It is not enough to sign consent.") appears as a quote-mark element near the bottom. Confident, serious, modern-publishing aesthetic.

Cheapest direction. Reads at any size, even mobile thumbnail.
Easiest for the publisher imprint to brand consistently.
Relies entirely on title strength. Risks looking academic if too restrained.

Closest comparables: Why We Sleep, Bad Blood, most Riverhead/Penguin Press nonfiction.

Direction 5 — Treatment Plan, Redacted

What the quote does not tell you.

Editorial / investigative $$ to produce

A treatment plan document with key items (price, warranty, refund policy, treating dentist) blacked out with redaction bars. Title overlaid as a stamped diagonal element. Investigative-journalism feel.

Memorable thumbnail. Editorial, distinct, signals consumer-rights angle hard.
Tonal mismatch risk: redaction skews more aggressive than the book's actual calm-protective voice.
Could attract exposé-seeking readers who'll be disappointed by the actual measured prose.

Closest comparables: investigative nonfiction like Dark Money, Bad Pharma.

How to use this comparison

  1. Look at thumbnails first. Resize this browser window narrower until each cover renders at about thumbnail size (200×300px). The cover that still reads clearly at that size is the cover that will sell on Amazon.
  2. Cross-check against the actual shelf. Search Amazon for "dental tourism" and "dental implants book." Pick the direction that stands out most against your real competition, not the abstract category.
  3. Pick two finalists for the designer. Recommended pairing: Direction 1 (emotionally precise) + Direction 2 (safe professional). A good designer should produce two refined comps from these in 2 weeks for $1,500–$3,500.
  4. Skip Direction 5 unless you want to reposition the book as investigative. The voice mismatch is real.
