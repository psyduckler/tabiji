# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication, but phone numbers do change, consulates move, and hospitals occasionally renumber their switchboards. If a number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus *Türkiye* or the city name, and you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results.

**Before you travel**, we strongly recommend you double-check your own embassy or consulate's current number directly on its official website — travel.state.gov (US), gov.uk (UK), travel.gc.ca (Canada), dfa.ie (Ireland), smartraveller.gov.au (Australia). Save the main switchboard and the after-hours duty-officer line on your phone before departure, and screenshot this appendix so you can read it on the Istanbul metro or a gulet boat where your signal cuts out.

## The numbers that work everywhere in Turkey

These are the lines to dial from any phone — mobile or landline, Turkish SIM or roaming — anywhere in the country. All of them are free.

- **Polis İmdat (National Police):** **155**. English-capable in all major tourist cities. For foreign-visitor issues, ask for the **Turizm Polisi** (Tourist Police) — specialized English-first desks at Istanbul, Antalya, Bodrum, Marmaris, Fethiye, Kuşadası, and Alanya.
- **Jandarma (rural and highway police):** **156**. Covers the countryside, highways, airports' exterior zones, and smaller towns (Cappadocia, Ephesus outskirts, Pamukkale, Side).
- **Medical emergency / ambulance:** **112**. Pan-European emergency line. Dispatchers speak English.
- **Fire:** **110**.
- **Consumer Protection (Ticaret Bakanlığı):** **175**. For merchant-misrepresentation and consumer-fraud complaints.
- **Ministry of Culture and Tourism tourism-complaint hotline:** **177**. For disputes with Ministry-licensed tour operators, hotels, carpet shops, and guides.
- **Roadside assistance / highway patrol:** **154**.

## If you lose your passport

1. **Report it first** — go to the nearest *Polis Karakolu* and file a *şikayet tutanağı* (kayıp pasaport — lost passport). You will leave with the signed and sealed report. This is the document your embassy wants to see before issuing an emergency replacement.
2. **Call your embassy's main switchboard** — or its after-hours line if it is a weekend — and request an emergency passport appointment. US, UK, Canadian, Irish, and Australian consulates in Istanbul all issue same-day or next-day emergency travel documents; the fee varies.
3. **Bring** the *şikayet tutanağı*, any photo ID you still have, passport photos (photo booths are in most subway stations and at major shopping centers), and the payment method your embassy specifies.
4. **Exit-stamp clearance**: before flying out with an emergency travel document, airlines departing Istanbul Airport, Sabiha Gökçen, Antalya, Bodrum, and Dalaman see emergency documents regularly; Turkish border police know the format.

## If your phone is stolen

1. **Freeze the SIM** by calling your carrier from any other phone: Turkcell **532**, Vodafone **542**, Türk Telekom **444 1 444**. If you use a foreign SIM or eSIM, use the app to suspend.
2. **File a *şikayet tutanağı*** at the nearest police station with your device's IMEI (if you recorded it) and serial number.
3. **Lock and wipe remotely** via Apple Find My or Google Find My Device.
4. **Change passwords immediately** for any account whose app was installed on the phone. Start with email.

## If your credit card is stolen or skimmed

1. **Freeze the card** by calling the number on the back (or use the issuer's app's "lock card" feature). Every minute matters.
2. **Report to police** with a *şikayet tutanağı*. Insurance reimbursement and the card issuer's fraud-dispute process both want the case number.
3. **Dispute the charges** with your issuer. The *şikayet tutanağı* speeds the process.
4. **Watch for follow-on phishing** — a skimmed card often leads to SMS and WhatsApp phishing pretending to be your bank. Never click a link in these messages; go to your bank's app directly.

## A closing note

This appendix is meant to be read once on the plane, screenshot for offline use, and forgotten about for the rest of your trip. Phone numbers change. Consulates move. Hospitals renumber their switchboards. If any number in this list fails, **155** — Polis İmdat — will still work, and your embassy's website will still have the current line for your country.

Travel safely, travel calmly, and if the worst happens, remember: a 3,000-lira Sultanahmet restaurant bill is an inconvenience, not a crisis. Filing a *şikayet tutanağı*, freezing a card, and calling a consulate are ordinary procedures — Turkish police and consular staff handle these cases every day, and most travelers resolve everything within a few hours and return to their trip. Turkey is not a dangerous country. Help is close, and English is closer than you think.
