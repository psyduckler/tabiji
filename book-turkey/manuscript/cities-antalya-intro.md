Antalya — Turkish Riviera capital, Taurus-Mountain backdrop, and the Mediterranean's largest tourist volume city — runs a scam scene concentrated in four zones: **Kaleiçi** (the old town inside the Roman walls), the **Konyaaltı** and **Lara** beach strips, **Antalya Airport (AYT)**, and **Düden Falls** north of the city.

The **Kaleiçi restaurant tout** is the volume approach. The old-town lanes west of Hadrian's Gate and south of the Saat Kulesi (clock tower) are packed with restaurants, and a meaningful share employ a tout on the street corner whose job is to walk tourists to a venue with a tourist-menu priced 40–80% higher than the posted-menu places on the same row. The defense is the universal refusal: ignore every "Hi, my friend — where are you from?" opener and eat at places where the menu is visibly posted in Turkish lira on the door, not walked-up to by a friendly man with a laminated photo card.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. **Antalya Airport (AYT) flat-rate taxi touts** and the night-tariff doubling — the regulated metered fare from AYT to Kaleiçi or Lara is 250–400 lira; touts quote 800–1,200, and the "night tariff" (doubled rate) is sometimes run during daylight. The **Konyaaltı and Lara beach jet-ski damage-deposit holds** run the Bodrum-Marmaris script on the Antalya beaches. The **Kaleiçi antique-shop "Ottoman coin" overcharge** sells modern-struck coins as Byzantine/Ottoman genuine at 400–1,500 lira each. And the **Düden Falls freelance "photo guide"** demands 150+ lira for photos the tourist didn't ask for.

A safe default: take the **Antray tram** (line AntRay) from AYT Airport to Ismet Paşa (28 lira, 35 minutes — the tram doesn't go directly to Kaleiçi but gets you to a short walk); from there, DiDi or a BiTaksi city taxi; eat in Kaleiçi at posted-menu places only (the side streets off Kaleiçi Meydanı are honest); rent jet skis only against a credit-card hold; buy antiques only from shops with a tax-ID number and a written provenance receipt; ignore Düden Falls photo-guide approaches; and call **155** or the Antalya Tourist Police at **+90 242 247 2400**. The Antalya Turizm Polisi desk is at **Cumhuriyet Caddesi 4, Kaleiçi**, a short walk from Hadrian's Gate.
