# Appendix B: Post-Scam Recovery Playbook {-}

If something in this book has happened to you, this appendix is where you start. The actions below are organized by urgency, not by severity. Even if you are not sure yet how serious the scam was, working through the first two timelines will put you in the best possible position to recover your money, your evidence, and your peace of mind.

## In the first fifteen minutes

Leave the venue. If you are physically inside a place where you were scammed — a carpet shop, a Sultanahmet restaurant with a surprise bill, a tout-run "Turkish Night" dinner show, a jet-ski rental return desk — do not argue, do not try to negotiate, and do not pay with a second form of payment. The single most important thing is to get yourself out of the environment and somewhere quiet.

Do not sign anything, including receipts or credit-card slips. A signed slip is much harder to dispute than one you refused to sign. If staff are blocking your exit, dial **155** (Polis İmdat — Turkish National Police, 24/7) or walk into the nearest Starbucks, Mado, Simit Sarayı, or a hotel lobby and ask the staff to call police. For medical emergencies, **112** is the pan-European ambulance dispatch.

Screenshot everything you can, as discreetly as possible: the bill, the sign outside the venue, the street intersection (a Google Maps dropped-pin is ideal), the shop's business-license certificate (Turkish retail shops are required by law to display an *Açılış ve Çalışma Ruhsatı* in a visible location), the card terminal, and the face of the person who served you if you can do it without escalating. The information you capture in these fifteen minutes is the evidence you will use for weeks afterward.

If your bag or wallet was just stolen in the street, shout *hırsız!* ("thief!") — Turkish bystanders and nearby shopkeepers will often intervene. If you are on the Istanbul metro or tram, the station supervisor's office is the closest authority; station staff can call police and hold CCTV footage.

## Within the first hour

Walk to the nearest **Turizm Polisi** (Tourist Police) desk if one is within ten minutes; otherwise, to the nearest regular *Polis Karakolu* (police station). Say "*Şikayette bulunmak istiyorum*" — "I want to file a report." You will walk out with a *şikayet tutanağı* — a printed, stamped report with a case number. This is the document your credit-card issuer and travel insurer will demand.

The major city Turizm Polisi desks (addresses in Appendix C) operate in English by default:
- **Istanbul:** Yerebatan Caddesi 4/6, Sultanahmet. +90 212 527 4503.
- **Antalya:** Cumhuriyet Caddesi 4, Kaleiçi. +90 242 247 2400.
- **Bodrum:** Atatürk Caddesi 3. +90 252 313 1234.
- **Marmaris:** İskele Meydanı 2. +90 252 412 1020.
- **Fethiye:** İskele Meydanı 1. +90 252 614 1569.
- **Kuşadası:** Barbaros Hayrettin Paşa Bulvarı 4. +90 256 614 0910.
- **Alanya:** Damlataş Caddesi 1. +90 242 513 1020.

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism also operates a nationwide tourist-complaint line: **+90 212 518 8754** (Istanbul-based, forwards regional). It can arbitrate disputes with Ministry-licensed businesses.

**Freeze the card that was used.** International emergency lines for the major card issuers are listed in Appendix C. These numbers work from Turkey and route you to an English-speaking representative. Have your card number (or the last four digits) and the approximate transaction amount ready before you call. Additionally, call your bank's fraud line directly — fraud desks sometimes freeze faster than the Visa/Mastercard emergency lines.

## Within the first twenty-four hours

**File a chargeback with your card issuer.** Use the *şikayet tutanağı* case number as documentation. Chargebacks initiated within twenty-four hours of the transaction have a significantly higher success rate than those filed a week later. Visa and Mastercard both offer strong chargeback rights for unauthorized or materially misrepresented charges — one reason we recommend credit over debit for every Turkey trip.

Call the **Tourist Information hotline** at your specific city (numbers listed per-city in Appendix C), or the national **Ministry of Culture and Tourism** complaint line. These services can arbitrate disputes with Ministry-licensed businesses (hotels, tour operators, licensed guides, licensed carpet shops with tax-ID registration), and sometimes produce restitution that police cannot.

If your passport was photographed, held, or taken by anyone involved, notify your embassy or consulate immediately. Passport data tied to a scam can show up in fraud attempts weeks later, and your embassy will advise on whether a replacement is needed. US, UK, Canadian, Irish, and Australian embassies in Ankara and consulates in Istanbul handle these cases routinely — full contact list in Appendix C.

Email your travel-insurance provider with a summary of what happened, timestamps, the *şikayet tutanağı* case number, and photographs of the bill. Even if you are not sure whether the loss is covered, opening the claim early protects your position.

If the scam involved an off-platform booking (a WhatsApp balloon tour, a beach-chalkboard gulet cruise, a carpet-shop bank transfer), open the dispute on the booking platform (Booking.com, Agoda, Airbnb, Viator, GetYourGuide) as well as with your card issuer.

## Within the first week

**File a formal consumer-protection complaint** with the **Ministry of Trade Consumer Protection Directorate** (*Ticaret Bakanlığı Tüketici Koruma Genel Müdürlüğü*) at **tuketici.ticaret.gov.tr** or by calling **175** (the consumer-complaint hotline, Turkish-speaking but with English callback available for tourist cases). This is the Turkish equivalent of the US FTC consumer-protection agency. It does not recover individual losses but its regulatory actions against repeat-offender tour operators, carpet shops, and hotels are one of the few effective pressures on the scam economy.

For **transport overcharging** — licensed taxis, tour minibuses, and registered ride-hail drivers — file a parallel complaint with the municipal transportation authority:
- **Istanbul:** IETT at **444 1871** (inside Turkey) or **+90 444 1871** from abroad.
- **Antalya:** Antalya Ulaşım A.Ş. at **+90 242 331 1700**.
- **Bodrum / Marmaris / Fethiye:** Muğla Provincial Directorate of Transportation.

With the license-plate number (always photograph it before getting out), these authorities can identify the driver, issue fines, and sometimes produce compensation.

For **banking and payment disputes**, escalate to the **Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency** (BDDK) at **+90 212 214 5000**, or to the **Turkish Central Bank Consumer Protection Department**.

Submit the full travel-insurance claim with all evidence gathered so far. Include the business's name, the address, the exact transaction details, the *şikayet tutanağı* case number, and the chargeback reference.

Consider leaving a neutral, factual review on Google Maps or TripAdvisor. Describe what happened, the amounts involved, and the date, without emotional language or accusations. Do not name individual staff members; describe the business by its official name and location only. Turkish defamation law is strict — stick to verifiable facts and receipts.

## If your phone or SIM was stolen

Call your carrier immediately to suspend the line. The three major Turkish carriers:
- **Turkcell:** dial **532** (in Turkey) or +90 532 532 0000 (from abroad).
- **Vodafone Turkey:** dial **542** or +90 542 542 5442.
- **Türk Telekom (Mobil):** dial **444 1 444**.

If you are on a tourist eSIM (Airalo, Saily, Holafly), the app will let you suspend the line in a few taps; do that before the *şikayet tutanağı*.

File a *şikayet tutanağı* at the nearest police station and include your phone's **IMEI** number (the fifteen-digit serial; find it on the original box, in your Apple or Google account, or by dialing \*#06# *before* the theft). The IMEI lets police flag the device and is required for any insurance claim.

Lock and wipe remotely via Apple's Find My or Google's Find My Device. Do this before the police report — a wiped phone cannot leak your email and banking apps while you wait in line.

Change passwords immediately for any account whose app was installed on the phone. Start with email; everything else resets off it.

## If the scam involved drink-spiking or physical harm

Go to a hospital before you go to the police. Toxicology tests have a short window and cannot be run retroactively. A list of 24/7 hospitals with English-speaking staff in each city is in Appendix C. At the hospital, ask for a toxicology screen (*toksikoloji taraması*); Turkish private international-capable hospitals (Acıbadem, Memorial, Medical Park, American Hospital Istanbul) run these routinely and will print a report you can use as evidence.

For **drug-planting scams** — where a stranger claims to have "found" a substance in your possession — stay calm, do not resist, and dial **155** (Polis) or **156** (Jandarma in rural areas) before any money changes hands. Real narcotics cases in Turkey go through official processes with written documentation, photographed evidence, and a *şikayet tutanağı* you sign. Demands for cash to "make it go away" are the signature of a scam, not of legitimate enforcement.

**Emergency numbers in Turkey:**
- **155** — Polis İmdat (National Police)
- **156** — Jandarma (rural/highway police)
- **112** — medical emergency / ambulance (pan-European)
- **110** — fire
- **175** — Consumer Protection complaint hotline
- **177** — Ministry of Culture and Tourism tourism-complaint central line

Your embassy can help coordinate English-speaking medical care and, if your passport was involved, coordinate a replacement. Consular registration — STEP (US), Smartraveller (Australia), LOCATE (UK), ROCA (Canada), Irish consular registration — is worth doing before you travel, specifically for situations like this.
