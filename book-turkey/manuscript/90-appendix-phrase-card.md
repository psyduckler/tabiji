# Appendix A: Turkish Exit-Phrase Card {-}

These are the phrases most likely to end a scam in Turkey. They are arranged by situation. For each phrase you will see the **English translation**, the **Turkish** spelling (Latin alphabet with Turkish diacritics), and a practical pronunciation guide in italics.

A practical suggestion: before your trip, take a screenshot of this appendix on your phone, and bookmark any phrase you think you are most likely to need. Turkish vendors in tourist areas almost always speak some English, but saying even a single Turkish phrase — especially "no thank you" and "meter, please" — signals you are not an easy mark.

## A note on pronunciation

Turkish is **phonetic**: every letter is always pronounced the same way, and stress is usually on the last syllable. Once you know the alphabet, you can read Turkish aloud. The tricky letters for English speakers:

- **c** is "j" as in "jam" — *cami* (mosque) = "jah-mee"
- **ç** is "ch" as in "chin" — *çay* (tea) = "chai"
- **ğ** is silent; it lengthens the preceding vowel — *dağ* (mountain) = "daah"
- **ı** (undotted i) is "uh" as in "sofa" — *ılık* (warm) = "uh-luhk"
- **i** (dotted) is "ee" — *iyi* (good) = "ee-yee"
- **ö** is the French "eu" or German "ö" — round your lips for "o" but say "e"
- **ş** is "sh" as in "ship" — *teşekkürler* (thanks) = "teh-sheh-koor-ler"
- **ü** is the French "u" or German "ü" — round your lips for "oo" but say "ee"
- **r** is rolled lightly, never silent
- **j** is "zh" as in "measure"
- **y** is always a consonant "y" as in "yes"

Stress: almost always on the last syllable. *Merhaba* (hello) is "mer-ha-**ba**."

## A note on politeness

Turkish politeness is short and warm. **Lütfen** ("please") at the start or end of a sentence, and **teşekkürler** or the more formal **teşekkür ederim** ("thank you") at the end, are enough to flag you as polite. There is no complex particle system you must learn.

## Asking before you commit

**How much is it?**  
Ne kadar?  
*Neh ka-DAR?*

**How much does it cost?**  
Fiyatı ne kadar?  
*Fee-ya-tuh neh ka-DAR?*

**Please start the meter.**  
Taksimetreyi açın, lütfen.  
*Tak-see-MET-rey-yi a-chuhn, LOOT-fen.*

Say this the instant you get into a taxi. If the driver refuses, get out. Alternatively, use **BiTaksi** — it is cheaper, meters itself, and now accepts foreign credit cards.

**Is this the day tariff?**  
Bu gündüz tarifesi mi?  
*Boo GOON-dooz ta-ree-feh-see mee?*

The meter has a small number in its upper-right corner — **1** is day (*gündüz*, 06:00–00:00), **2** is night (*gece*, 00:00–06:00). If it shows 2 during daylight you are being doubled; say "*gündüz tarifesi lütfen*" — "day tariff, please" — and watch the display change.

**Please show me the menu.**  
Menüyü görebilir miyim?  
*Meh-nee-oo-yoo geur-eh-bi-LEER mee-yim?*

**Do you have an itemized price list?**  
Yazılı fiyat listeniz var mı?  
*Ya-zuh-LUH fee-YAHT lee-steh-neez VAR muh?*

Use before any transaction that might be priced by weight or "special": fish, carpet, saffron, jewelry.

**What is the price per gram?**  
Gram başına fiyat ne kadar?  
*GRAHM bah-shuh-na fee-YAHT neh ka-DAR?*

Use before saffron, tea, gold, or any per-weight item.

**Do you accept credit cards?**  
Kredi kartı kabul ediyor musunuz?  
*KREH-dee kahr-TUH ka-BOOL eh-dee-yor moo-soo-nooz?*

Most larger Turkish venues accept foreign Visa and Mastercard. If a shop insists on cash, ask why — it is frequently a chargeback-avoidance tell.

## Declining and leaving

**No, thank you.**  
Hayır, teşekkürler.  
*Hai-UHR, teh-sheh-koor-LER.*

The single most useful phrase in this appendix. Firm, polite, understood everywhere. Walking away is not rude.

**I'm not interested.**  
İlgilenmiyorum.  
*EEL-gee-len-MEE-yor-um.*

Stronger than *hayır, teşekkürler*. Use when an approach keeps talking after the first refusal.

**Please leave me alone.**  
Lütfen beni rahat bırakın.  
*LOOT-fen beh-NEE rah-HAT buh-ra-KUHN.*

Used firmly but without raised voice.

**Don't touch me.**  
Bana dokunma.  
*BAH-nah doh-KUN-mah.*

Step back. Most touts disengage at this point.

**I'm waiting for my friend.**  
Arkadaşımı bekliyorum.  
*Ar-ka-dah-shuh-MUH beh-KLEE-yor-um.*

Socially-acceptable pause while you wait out an approach.

## Responding to a scam in progress

**I'm calling the Tourist Police.**  
Turist Polisini arıyorum.  
*Too-REEST Po-lee-see-nee ah-RUH-yor-um.*

This is the firmest card in the deck. **155** is the Turkish National Police (*Polis İmdat*), 24/7. **Turizm Polisi** (Tourist Police) desks exist in Istanbul, Antalya, Bodrum, Marmaris, Fethiye, Kuşadası, and Alanya; the numbers are in Appendix C. Say the phrase, hold up your phone, and dial 155.

**I'll call my bank.**  
Bankamı arayacağım.  
*BAHN-ka-muh ah-RAH-yah-jahm.*

Say this if a restaurant, shop, or taxi driver disputes a credit-card transaction.

**I was scammed. Please help.**  
Dolandırıldım. Yardım edin lütfen.  
*Do-lahn-duh-RUHL-duhm. Yahr-DUHM ee-DEEN LOOT-fen.*

The verb *dolandırmak* ("to defraud / to scam") is the specific Turkish term. It communicates what happened with more urgency than any English translation. Use this with a police officer, a hotel front-desk clerk, or any bystander you want to enlist.

**I'd like an itemized bill.**  
Detaylı fatura alabilir miyim?  
*Deh-tai-LUH fah-too-rah ah-lah-bee-LEER mee-yim?*

Forces the establishment to account for every item — a legitimate place hands it over; a tourist trap stalls.

**I didn't order this.**  
Bunu sipariş etmedim.  
*Boo-NOO see-pa-REESH et-meh-deem.*

Use calmly about any unrequested dish, bottle, or "service" that appears on your bill.

**This is not what we agreed.**  
Anlaştığımız bu değil.  
*An-lahsh-tuh-uh-muhz BOO deh-yeel.*

For taxi fares over a quoted price, jet-ski "damage" claims, and tour-operator price-creep.

## Getting help

**Where is the nearest police station?**  
En yakın polis karakolu nerede?  
*EN yah-KUHN po-LEES ka-ra-ko-LOO NEH-reh-deh?*

**I want to file a report.**  
Şikayette bulunmak istiyorum.  
*Shee-kah-YEHT-teh boo-LOON-mahk ees-TEE-yor-um.*

The formal police complaint produces a *şikayet tutanağı* (stamped report) — required by your credit-card issuer and travel insurer.

**Does anyone speak English?**  
İngilizce bilen var mı?  
*EEN-gee-leez-jeh bee-LEN VAR muh?*

Major Turkish tourist-police desks have at least one English-speaking officer on duty.

**Please call 155.**  
155'i arayın lütfen.  
*Yüz ell-ee-BEH-shee ah-rah-YUHN LOOT-fen.*

**Help! Thief!**  
İmdat! Hırsız!  
*EEM-daht! HUHR-suhz!*

Shouted in a crowded market or street, this is understood by every bystander. The word *hırsız* (thief) carries immediate legal weight.

## Reading Turkish lira amounts

Turkish prices are written with the abbreviation "TL" (short for *Türk Lirası*) either after or before the number, or occasionally with the Unicode lira symbol when the font supports it. Decimal convention uses a comma (4,50 TL = four lira fifty kuruş) and a dot for thousands (1.500 TL = 1,500 TL) — the opposite of English conventions.

- Small-denomination coins: 1, 5, 10, 25, 50 kuruş (increasingly rare); 1 TL coin
- Paper notes: 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200 TL (a new 500-TL note was introduced in 2025)
- The lira has lost significant value against the US dollar since 2021; verify on xe.com in the week before you travel.

| Amount | Written | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 lira | bir lira | *BEER lee-rah* |
| 5 lira | beş lira | *BESH lee-rah* |
| 10 lira | on lira | *OHN lee-rah* |
| 50 lira | elli lira | *EL-ee lee-rah* |
| 100 lira | yüz lira | *YOOZ lee-rah* |
| 500 lira | beş yüz lira | *BESH yooz lee-rah* |
| 1,000 lira | bin lira | *BEEN lee-rah* |

If a vendor quotes a figure you don't understand, ask them to write it:

**Please write the number down.**  
Rakamı yazın, lütfen.  
*Rah-kah-MUH YAH-zuhn, LOOT-fen.*

## A note on tone

The phrases above are polite, not aggressive. Turkish conflict-resolution culture rewards calm — raising your voice at a bazaar shopkeeper or a taxi driver almost always makes things worse. A calm *hayır, teşekkürler* is already a complete refusal. If you need to escalate, *ilgilenmiyorum* ("not interested") and *bana dokunma* ("don't touch me") are both firm without being rude, and *Turist Polisini arıyorum* ("I'm calling the Tourist Police") is the firmest card in the deck.

One last thing worth saying out loud: you do not have to be fluent to use any of this. You just have to say the key word clearly, keep your voice level, and not walk back your no. Scammers read your body language before they read your grammar — and a calm *hayır, teşekkürler* with the Turkish on your lips is, in itself, proof you know enough to make the interaction harder than they expected.
