# The Scams: City by City {-}

The chapters that follow document every scam reported in thirteen Turkish destinations. Cities are ordered by how most visitors actually travel: starting with Istanbul, the flagship and the country's densest scam ecosystem, then inland to Cappadocia (the balloon-tour and fairy-chimney circuit), over to the Aegean via İzmir, down through Ephesus / Kuşadası and the resort triangle (Bodrum, Marmaris, Fethiye), east along the Turkish Riviera (Antalya, Alanya, Side), inland to Pamukkale's travertine terraces, and finishing at Konya — the Mevlana mausoleum and a quieter last chapter.

If you're hitting only a few of these, skip to the chapter you need. If you're doing a longer loop, skim them all — the same core scams recur with local variations. The Sultanahmet shoe-shine brush drop runs identically on the Antalya Kaleiçi harbor promenade. The Grand Bazaar carpet-shop "just for tea" pitch runs in the Bodrum harbor shops and the Kuşadası cruise-arrival strip. The Bodrum jet-ski damage-deposit scam runs on every Aegean resort beach from Marmaris to Alanya. The airport flat-rate taxi refusal starts at Istanbul Airport (IST) and repeats at Sabiha Gökçen (SAW), Antalya (AYT), Bodrum (BJV), and Dalaman (DLM). Learn the pattern in one city, and you'll spot it the instant you step off the plane in the next.

## The Thirteen Cities at a Glance {-}

![Istanbul — Blue Mosque and Hagia Sophia across Sultanahmet at dusk](assets/cities/istanbul.jpg)

**Istanbul** — Turkey's flagship city and the densest approach-scam ecosystem on the eastern Mediterranean. The Sultanahmet shoe-shine brush drop that the Turizm Polisi have printed warning cards about since 2012; the Grand Bazaar carpet-shop "just for tea" pitch that leaves tourists with €1,500 kilims they didn't plan to buy; the Istiklal Caddesi "house cocktail" overcharge; the IST and SAW airport flat-rate taxi touts quoting 2,500–3,500 lira for metered 800–1,200 lira runs; and the Galata Bridge restaurant "let me show you a real Turkish place" that ends at a venue with no posted prices.

![Cappadocia — hot-air balloons over the Göreme fairy chimneys at sunrise](assets/cities/cappadocia.jpg)

**Cappadocia** — Fairy chimneys, cave hotels, and Turkey's iconic sunrise balloon fleet. The WhatsApp-booked balloon tour that turns out to be a non-flying ticket because "the wind was bad"; the Göreme cave-hotel "upgrade fee" added at check-in for rooms already booked; Uçhisar and Ürgüp "tile master" workshop overcharges on Iznik reproductions; and Kaymaklı Underground City freelance "guide" approaches demanding 500+ lira after tours that are not actually guided by a licensed *turist rehberi*.

![İzmir — Konak Square clock tower at golden hour](assets/cities/izmir.jpg)

**İzmir** — Aegean capital and Turkey's third-largest city, with a calmer scam scene than Istanbul. Kordon waterfront restaurant tourist-menu overcharges; Konak Square "free city tour" approaches that end at a commission shop; İzmir Adnan Menderes Airport (ADB) flat-rate taxi touts; and the old Alsancak district's "Turkish coffee house" cover charges that aren't disclosed on the posted menu.

![Ephesus — Library of Celsus facade at late afternoon](assets/cities/ephesus.jpg)

**Ephesus** — Roman ruins, the Library of Celsus, and one of the Mediterranean's most-visited archaeological sites. Freelance "official guides" at the main gate demanding 500–800 lira for tours they're not licensed to give; the "Terrace Houses combined ticket" confusion where touts sell add-ons that duplicate the base entry; Selçuk-based minibus drivers offering "Ephesus tour" packages that substitute four carpet-shop stops for meaningful time at the site; and the post-visit "handmade olive-wood" gift-shop kickback circuit.

![Kuşadası — Pigeon Island causeway at sunset](assets/cities/kusadasi.jpg)

**Kuşadası** — Aegean cruise port and the gateway to Ephesus. Cruise-arrival taxi touts quoting flat fares to Ephesus that are triple the regulated rate; the "Pigeon Island free tour" approach that ends at a single leather shop; Barlar Sokak (Bar Street) cover-charge scams where the advertised "free entry" becomes a 200-euro bottle-minimum; and the harbor-area "Turkish carpet workshop" visits bundled into shore-excursion packages that routinely run 2–3× direct retail.

![Bodrum — Castle of St Peter above the harbor at golden hour](assets/cities/bodrum.jpg)

**Bodrum** — Aegean peninsula, Crusader castle, and the summer jet-ski scam capital of Turkey. Gümbet, Yalıkavak, and Turgutreis beach jet-ski damage-deposit cycles that *Hürriyet Daily News* has covered every summer since 2018; the Yalıkavak marina sunset-restaurant unposted cover charges; gulet-boat day-cruise downgrades where €80 per person becomes a 30-person budget trip; and the Bodrum Milas Airport (BJV) flat-rate taxi touts.

![Marmaris — crescent bay and forested peninsula at blue hour](assets/cities/marmaris.jpg)

**Marmaris** — Mediterranean-Aegean crossroads, yacht-charter hub, and the Rhodes ferry port. The same jet-ski damage-deposit cycle as Bodrum; Içmeler minibus-to-Marmaris tourist fare inflation; Marmaris Bar Street (Barlar Sokak) drink-spike and inflated-bill pattern; Dalaman Airport (DLM) flat-rate taxi flats; and the "Turkish night" dinner-show tourist packages sold on the harbor that deliver a different (cheaper) venue than advertised.

![Fethiye — Ölüdeniz blue lagoon and Babadağ paragliding ridge](assets/cities/fethiye.jpg)

**Fethiye** — Ölüdeniz blue lagoon, the Babadağ paragliding ridge, and one of the Mediterranean's best-known small towns. Tandem-paragliding landing-fee add-ons ("helmet cam rental" €150 after a €70 advertised flight); the Fethiye 12-Islands day-cruise hidden-fee boat where "free lunch" and "free snorkel gear" become €15 each on board; Ölüdeniz beach jet-ski damage deposits; and the Paspatur old-town restaurant unposted-cover-charge scene.

![Antalya — Kaleiçi harbor and Taurus Mountains silhouette](assets/cities/antalya.jpg)

**Antalya** — Turkey's Riviera capital and the Mediterranean gateway. The Kaleiçi restaurant-tout script that's Sultanahmet ported south; Antalya Airport (AYT) flat-rate taxi touts and the night-tariff doubling; Konyaaltı and Lara beach jet-ski damage-deposit holds; Kaleiçi antique-shop "Ottoman coin" overcharges; and the Düden Falls freelance "photo guide" demanding 150+ lira for photos tourists didn't ask for.

![Alanya — Red Tower above Cleopatra Beach at golden hour](assets/cities/alanya.jpg)

**Alanya** — Castle-crowned peninsula, Cleopatra Beach, and the Riviera's eastern anchor. The Cleopatra Beach jet-ski damage-deposit cycle; Alanya harbor "Pirate Boat" tours that quote one price on the dock and add surcharges at sea; Damlataş Cave "photography guide" shakedowns; Gazipaşa Airport (GZP) flat-rate taxi quotes; and the old-town Red Tower "Turkish night" dinner-show packages with hidden bar minimums.

![Side — Temple of Apollo columns at sunset](assets/cities/side-turkey.jpg)

**Side** — Antalya-coast Roman town built around a spectacular temple-on-the-sea. The theater-area harbor restaurant tourist menus with no posted prices; "Roman coin" antique-shop scams where the coin is a modern strike; beach umbrella and lounger pre-payment scams where the boy who collected the cash is not employed by the venue; and Side-to-Manavgat-Waterfalls minibus packages with a single-carpet-shop-stop kicker.

![Pamukkale — white travertine terraces at dawn](assets/cities/pamukkale.jpg)

**Pamukkale** — Calcite terraces, the ancient city of Hierapolis, and one of the Mediterranean's most-photographed sites. Freelance "Pamukkale guide" touts at the Denizli bus station selling tours that substitute a brief travertine visit for the advertised full Hierapolis exploration; the "Cleopatra Pool" add-on sold separately from the base entry at inflated prices; Denizli Cardak Airport (DNZ) transfer-tout scams; and the post-visit "hand-loomed Pamukkale cotton" gift-shop kickback.

![Konya — Mevlana Museum turquoise dome at golden hour](assets/cities/konya.jpg)

**Konya** — Central-Anatolian Seljuk capital, home of the Mevlana mausoleum, and a quieter destination with a narrower scam footprint. Mevlana-area carpet-shop approaches offering "special Mevlevi-order prayer rugs" at 3× normal kilim prices; the Konya-based "whirling-dervish ceremony" tickets sold by touts at 3× the official Mevlana Cultural Center price; Konya Airport (KYA) flat-rate taxi quotes; and the central-Konya restaurant tourist menu priced in euros when the lira menu is significantly cheaper.
