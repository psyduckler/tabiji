Istanbul runs Turkey's oldest and most rehearsed approach-scam ecosystem, and almost all of it converges on three corridors: **Sultanahmet Square** (the Hagia Sophia / Blue Mosque / Topkapı axis), the **Grand Bazaar** (Kapalıçarşı) and the **Spice Bazaar** (Mısır Çarşısı), and **Istiklal Caddesi** running south from Taksim Square to the Galata Tower. The *Turizm Polisi* (Tourist Police) have been posting English-language warnings at the Hagia Sophia exit and at the Grand Bazaar's main entrances for over a decade, and the patterns have been covered repeatedly in *Hürriyet Daily News*, *Daily Sabah*, and on r/Istanbul.

The **shoe-shine brush drop** is the signature Istanbul approach. A man walking ahead of you through Sultanahmet or across the Galata Bridge drops a brush; the reflexively helpful tourist picks it up and hands it back, and the shoe-shiner insists on "thanking you" with a free shine — which then arrives at 400–800 lira, or €25+, for a three-minute polish. The script has been in continuous operation since at least the early 2000s and is still running as of this edition. The defense: if someone drops a brush ahead of you, do not pick it up. Keep walking.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Taksim "let's have a drink" approach** is the highest-cash-loss scam in the city: a well-dressed Turkish man in his thirties strikes up a conversation in English on Istiklal, mentions a great local bar foreigners never find, and walks you down a Beyoğlu side street to a basement venue where two drinks become an 8,000–20,000-lira bill enforced by two large men positioned between you and the door. The **fake-plainclothes-police ID check** runs after dark on the quieter streets west of the Grand Bazaar and on the approach roads toward Topkapı: two men in civilian clothes flash laminated badges, claim a counterfeit-currency or drug check, and lift cash and credit cards from your wallet under the cover of "verifying" your money. And the **Istanbul Airport (IST) flat-rate taxi** touts inside the terminal quote 700–1,200 lira for rides that meter out at roughly 250–350 lira via the O-3 highway as this edition goes to press — the Istanbul Metropolitan Municipality fare calculator at *ibb.gov.tr* is the reference, and the M11 metro is under 80 lira and takes 50 minutes from IST to Kağıthane (transfer to the M7 line toward Mahmutbey and then the tram to Sultanahmet).

A safe default: take the Istanbul M11 metro from IST or the Havabus shuttle from Sabiha Gökçen (SAW) into central Istanbul; use **BiTaksi** (the Turkish ride-hail app, now accepts foreign cards) for every city taxi ride; refuse every "I know a great local bar" pitch from a stranger on Istiklal — every late-night cocktail venue worth visiting in Beyoğlu is on Google Maps with prices and reviews; if anyone in plain clothes asks to "check" your passport, wallet, or banknotes, walk into the nearest hotel lobby or restaurant before producing anything; buy Grand Bazaar carpets only from shops with tax-ID numbers (*vergi numarası*) visible and a chargeback-able credit card accepted; and call **155** (Polis İmdat) or the Istanbul Turizm Polisi direct line at **+90 212 527 4503** from inside a Starbucks, Mado, or hotel lobby the moment an approach escalates. The Turizm Polisi central desk is at **Yerebatan Caddesi 4/6, Sultanahmet** — a three-minute walk from the Blue Mosque.
