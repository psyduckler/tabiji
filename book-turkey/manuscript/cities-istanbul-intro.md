Istanbul runs Turkey's oldest and most rehearsed approach-scam ecosystem, and almost all of it converges on three corridors: **Sultanahmet Square** (the Hagia Sophia / Blue Mosque / Topkapı axis), the **Grand Bazaar** (Kapalıçarşı) and the **Spice Bazaar** (Mısır Çarşısı), and **Istiklal Caddesi** running south from Taksim Square to the Galata Tower. The *Turizm Polisi* (Tourist Police) have been posting English-language warnings at the Hagia Sophia exit and at the Grand Bazaar's main entrances for over a decade, and the patterns have been covered repeatedly in *Hürriyet Daily News*, *Daily Sabah*, and on r/Istanbul.

The **shoe-shine brush drop** is the signature Istanbul approach. A man walking ahead of you through Sultanahmet or across the Galata Bridge drops a brush; the reflexively helpful tourist picks it up and hands it back, and the shoe-shiner insists on "thanking you" with a free shine — which then arrives at 400–800 lira, or €25+, for a three-minute polish. The script has been in continuous operation since at least the early 2000s and is still running as of this edition. The defense: if someone drops a brush ahead of you, do not pick it up. Keep walking.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Grand Bazaar "just for tea" carpet-shop pitch** walks you off a main alley into a back room where a patient shopkeeper unrolls ten kilims and serves you apple tea while a junior salesperson blocks the door; bills run €800–€3,000 for pieces worth a quarter of that retail. The **Istanbul Airport (IST) flat-rate taxi** touts inside the terminal quote 2,500–3,500 lira for rides that meter out at roughly 1,500–2,200 lira as this edition goes to press (the M11 metro is under 80 lira and takes 50 minutes from IST to Kağıthane; transfer to the M7 line toward Mahmutbey and then the tram to Sultanahmet). And the **Sultanahmet "let me help you find a restaurant" approach** pulls arriving tourists off the tram toward a venue with a menu that has no posted prices and a surprise 2,800-lira sea bass.

A safe default: take the Istanbul M11 metro from IST or the Havabus shuttle from Sabiha Gökçen (SAW) into central Istanbul; use **BiTaksi** (the Turkish ride-hail app, now accepts foreign cards) for city rides; buy Grand Bazaar carpets only from shops with tax-ID numbers (*vergi numarası*) visible and a chargeback-able credit card accepted; ignore every Sultanahmet restaurant tout and eat on posted-menu streets (Küçük Ayasofya Caddesi for local prices, Hocapaşa Sokak for set-menu tourism without the scam); and call **155** (Polis İmdat) or the Istanbul Turizm Polisi direct line at **+90 212 527 4503** from inside a Starbucks, Mado, or hotel lobby the moment an approach escalates. The Turizm Polisi central desk is at **Yerebatan Caddesi 4/6, Sultanahmet** — a three-minute walk from the Blue Mosque.
