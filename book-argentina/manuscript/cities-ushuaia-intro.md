Ushuaia — "the end of the world" — is the capital of Tierra del Fuego Province and the southernmost city on Earth, sitting 3,200 kilometers south of Buenos Aires on the Beagle Channel. Population is roughly 75,000, swollen by a seasonal influx of Antarctic-cruise passengers between October and March. Malvinas Argentinas International Airport (USH) is five kilometers west of downtown; the commercial pier accepts cruise ships up to twelve hundred passengers. Tourist activity concentrates along Av. Maipú (the waterfront), San Martín (the main shopping street, two blocks inland), and at Parque Nacional Tierra del Fuego (twelve kilometers west) — plus the Beagle Channel boat tours and the Pira Tours penguin excursions on Isla Martillo.

Ushuaia has one of the most distinctive scam ecosystems in the country. The signature scam — repeatedly flagged by foreign travelers in 2025 — is **airport-taxi dual-pricing by language**. Spanish-speakers are quoted ARS 6,500 for a five-kilometer ride from USH to downtown; English-speakers are quoted USD 50 flat. This is not a "misunderstanding" but an explicit two-tier pricing system that operates across the city's taxi fleet. The defensive habit is simple: always confirm the meter is running (say "*taxímetro, por favor*"), and if the driver refuses, step out.

The second layer is the **"last-minute Antarctic cruise" scam**. Real last-minute Antarctic-cruise agencies do exist — Freestyle Adventure Travel and Ushuaia Travel Agency among them — and they genuinely have unsold cabins at 30–60 % off rack rate, bookable on the spot forty-eight hours before departure. But fake WhatsApp-based agencies targeting tourists arriving without pre-booked Antarctic plans have proliferated since 2023, collecting USD 2,000 deposits for voyages that do not exist. Book only with established operators (Quark Expeditions, Hurtigruten, Oceanwide, Silversea, One Ocean) via their own websites, or with the physical, Av. San Martín-facing agencies that have been operating for decades.

The third layer is the **Av. Maipú restaurant centolla (king crab) USD-pricing gambit**: tourist-facing restaurants quote king-crab plates at USD 60–80, while the legitimate per-kilo price at the pier is ARS 35,000–45,000 (roughly USD 25–32). The waiter's "no change for dollars" line seals the markup.

Emergency: **911**; Comisaría del Turista Ushuaia (+54 2901 442-1773); Hospital Regional Ushuaia (Maipú and 12 de Octubre) handles cruise-medical cases.
