El Chaltén is Argentina's trekking capital — a micro-village of under three thousand year-round residents in the north of Los Glaciares National Park, at the foot of Monte Fitz Roy (3,405 meters) and Cerro Torre (3,133 meters). The town was founded in 1985 as a border-security settlement and has expanded, reluctantly, into a summer hiker's base. There is no airport; travelers arrive by a three-hour bus ride north from El Calafate on Ruta Nacional 40 and Ruta Provincial 23. The main operators are Chaltén Travel, Marga Taqsa, and Cal-Tur — all book through **plataforma10.com** or direct at their El Calafate terminal counters.

What makes El Chaltén unusual in this book is that the core activity — hiking Laguna de los Tres, Laguna Torre, the Loma del Pliegue Tumbado, or the Huemul Circuit — is almost entirely scam-free. The trails are free, well-signed, and administered by park rangers who do not take payments. The risks here cluster at three specific junctures: the restaurant, the bus terminal, and the short-term rental.

The restaurant scam is the **Parrilla La Oveja Negra 88,000-peso dinner** — an October 2025 incident, widely reported in traveler forums, that has become the canonical example of Argentine bill-padding. A party of four ordered standard parrilla items, received a bill with unlisted "cubierto" per-person charges, "panera especial" bread they did not request, and a "propina sugerida" that the credit-card terminal pre-filled. The owner has never publicly denied the account. The general defensive habit applies: photograph the Spanish-language menu before ordering, read the bill line by line, and dispute any charge not on the posted menu.

The bus-terminal scam is the "fee collector" in the arrivals hall who inflates a legitimate ARS 500–1,000 municipal charge into ARS 5,000 or asks you to sign a clipboard "for the trip" that's actually booking a phantom excursion. Pay the posted municipal fee at the window if required, and decline any other approach.

The rental scam is the **WhatsApp short-term rental** where a "landlord" asks for USD cash on arrival for an apartment that Booking.com shows does not exist. Book only through Booking, Airbnb, or Expedia; verify on Google Street View.
