Mar del Plata — locally just "Mardel" — is Argentina's flagship Atlantic beach city, a 600,000-resident port on the Buenos Aires coast that swells to over two million during the eight-week *temporada* peak between December 26 and the first weekend of February. The city's identity is built on summer: the Bristol and Playa Grande beaches, the Costanera Sur seafood strip, the sea-lion colony at the Escollera Sur, the Casino Central, and the parrilla rows along Av. Constitución. Buses from Buenos Aires arrive at the Estación Terminal de Ómnibus on Av. Champagnat after a six-to-seven-hour run; flights land at Aeropuerto Astor Piazzolla (MDQ), a small single-arrivals airport ten kilometers north of the centro.

The defining Mar del Plata scam — and the reason this chapter exists at all — is **temporada apartment rental fraud**. The 2026 Costa Atlántica rental-fraud surge is documented across 0223.com.ar, Infobae, and Diario El Norte: fake Facebook Marketplace, Instagram, and WhatsApp listings demanding USD wire or Western Union deposits, with photos stolen from real Booking.com properties and "owners" who vanish once payment clears. One Santa Fe family arrived on January 21 to find their booking did not exist and got a mocking selfie from the scammer the next day. **Never wire any deposit to an off-platform rental.** Book Mar del Plata stays through Booking.com, Airbnb, or Hotels.com with full payment on the platform — for longer stays, use the Argentine real-estate portals ZonaProp or ArgenProp and verify every address via Google Street View and a video walk-through before paying.

The second layer is the **temporada-window restaurant and taxi markup**. Costanera Sur and Bristol parrillas run aggressive English-menu / Spanish-menu dual pricing during the December 26 – January 31 peak — La 100, Revista Noticias, and 7 Caníbales have all published 2025–2026 reckonings showing the local-vs-tourist gap. Always ask for the "menú en pesos" before ordering and read every line of the bill. At the Estación Terminal de Ómnibus and Aeropuerto Astor Piazzolla, refuse every "fixed price, no meter" tout — Cabify operates legally in Mar del Plata and bypasses the meter game entirely, and Diario La Capital tracks the regulated taxi tariffs through the Concejo Deliberante.
