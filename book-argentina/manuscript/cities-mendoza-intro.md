Mendoza is Argentina's wine capital, the country's fourth-largest metropolitan area (roughly 1.1 million), and — by every measure — the single most-visited non-Buenos-Aires destination for first-time visitors. The wine circuit splits into three zones: Maipú (closest to the city, bike-friendly, mostly mid-range bodegas); Luján de Cuyo (thirty minutes south, home to Catena Zapata, Chandon, and a dozen boutique estates); and the Uco Valley (ninety minutes south, the ultra-premium zone: Monteviejo, Salentein, O. Fournier, The Vines). The Cordillera de los Andes — with Aconcagua at 6,961 meters as South America's highest peak — rises dramatically to the west.

Tourists typically arrive at Gobernador Francisco Gabrielli International Airport (MDZ), take a remis or Uber into the city, base themselves in the downtown Plaza Independencia grid or the Chacras de Coria pre-colonial village, and spend three to five days bouncing between bodegas. The scam ecosystem follows the wine-tour traffic precisely. Hotel-concierge bodega-tour packages run 3x the direct-book rate at Ampora Wine Tours or Trout & Wine; the Maipú "friend's cousin bike rental" pitch ends with a flat tire and no helmet (rent from Mr. Hugo Bikes or Kahuak, not from the pitch); the Peatonal Sarmiento "¡cambio!" touts play the same cards as Florida Avenue in Buenos Aires; and the annual Vendimia (harvest festival, first weekend of March) turns every mid-range hotel into a last-minute booking fraud target.

One Mendoza-specific note worth highlighting: **Aconcagua Provincial Park** and the ski resort at **Las Leñas** both have their own scam layers — Aconcagua mountaineering operators with incomplete permits, Las Leñas lift-ticket resellers — but neither falls into the core tourist-shopping-and-wine experience this book covers.
