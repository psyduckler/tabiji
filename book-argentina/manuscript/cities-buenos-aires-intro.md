Buenos Aires is Argentina's capital, its cultural heart, and — by a wide margin — the country's densest tourist-scam ecosystem. Fourteen million people share the greater metropolitan area. Three million of them live in the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires proper, where every year something like six million foreign visitors pass through Ezeiza International Airport (EZE), Jorge Newbery's Aeroparque (AEP), and the Retiro and Constitución bus terminals. Visitors cluster around a handful of neighborhoods: Microcentro (the Florida Avenue commercial strip), Recoleta (cemetery, parrillas), Palermo (Soho and Hollywood), San Telmo (tango and the Sunday fair), La Boca (Caminito), and Puerto Madero (the waterfront redevelopment). The scam clusters follow the tourist clusters exactly.

This is also the city where the Milei-era economic shock is most visible to visitors. The **blue dollar** market — the informal exchange rate that for two decades ran 50–100 % above the official peso-to-dollar rate — collapsed in mid-2024 when currency controls were relaxed. By 2026, the blue dollar often trades *below* the official rate. This has transformed the Florida Avenue *arbolito* (cambio tout) from a legitimate-if-grey bargain into a straight loss before counterfeit notes even enter the picture. The classic "save money by exchanging USD on the street" advice is now actively wrong.

For older travelers, the practical risk profile in Buenos Aires is overwhelmingly financial rather than violent. Violent crime against foreign visitors in Palermo, Recoleta, Puerto Madero, and the Microcentro daytime is very rare. What trips up tourists here are counterfeit peso notes from cambio touts, Ezeiza "Meet & Greet" remis overcharges, San Telmo taxi long-route gambits, Plaza de Mayo distraction pickpockets, and WhatsApp-apartment deposit fraud. The single best defensive habit is to avoid exchanging money on the street, use Western Union at Plaza San Martín (or the thirty-plus other Buenos Aires branches) for USD-to-peso conversion, download Uber or Cabify before arrival, and save the **Comisaría del Turista** (Av. Corrientes 436, +54 11 4346 5748, English-speaking 24/7) into your phone.
