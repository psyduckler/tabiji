# How to Use This Book {-}

Argentina is a traveler's paradise — Buenos Aires nightlife, Mendoza vineyards, Patagonian glaciers, Iguazú's thunder, and the world's southernmost Antarctica departures all within one passport stamp. It is also, thanks to three straight years of triple-digit inflation and the 2024 Milei-era economic shock, one of the most financially confusing destinations on earth for a foreign traveler. In 2022, walking into a Florida Avenue *cueva* with a $100 bill could triple your pesos overnight; in 2026, that same transaction is frequently a net loss before counterfeit notes even enter the picture. The official rate and the blue rate sometimes cross. ATM caps are low. USD dollars sit in restaurants' cash registers. Ride-hailing apps are legal — but in Córdoba, taxi drivers still throw stones at Uber vehicles approaching the airport. None of this is in the guidebooks yet.

We want you to come home with stories about the bife de chorizo, the Perito Moreno glacier, a tango milonga in San Telmo, and a night of Malbec in Mendoza — not about a 10,000-peso counterfeit note from a Florida Avenue tout, a motochorro phone-snatch outside the Monumento a la Bandera in Rosario, or an 88,000-peso bill at a Parrilla La Oveja Negra in El Chaltén.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents sixty-six specific scams that trip up first-time visitors to Argentina, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been reported in the Argentine press (Clarín, La Nación, Página/12, Perfil), cross-referenced against Policía Federal, provincial Policía del Turista, or Defensoría del Consumidor records, and confirmed by recent traveler accounts.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First, the name of the scam, the category it falls into (pickpocket, transport, overcharging, tourist trap, and so on), and how serious and how common it is. Then two short sections: **How this scam works** — a plain narrative of how it unfolds in real time, with real peso and dollar amounts where we have them — and **How to avoid it**, the specific moves that shut it down. Each entry ends with where it happens and a four-panel comic showing the exchange, so you can spot the pattern visually, not just in words. The comics are drawn in the spirit of Quino's *Mafalda*, the newspaper strip that has been describing porteño daily life since 1964.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. Most scams in Argentina are variations on six patterns. Learn the patterns and you will spot a new scam the moment it starts — even one that is not in this book. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will then read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters, you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **Rioplatense Spanish exit-phrase card**. Short phrases you can say, word for word, at the moments when your politeness would normally get you into trouble. Argentine Spanish uses *vos* and *che* and has a distinctive cadence — the pronunciation guides here are approximate, but the key words will carry the meaning even with a foreign accent. Take a screenshot of it on your phone.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A step-by-step flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam. Most scam books tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens, you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. Policía Federal, provincial Policía del Turista, Bomberos (firefighters), SAME (ambulance service), Defensoría del Consumidor, embassies, and the nearest English-speaking medical care, organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and skim the three appendices. Read the chapters for the cities you will be visiting. Take a screenshot of the phrase card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book turns out to apply during your trip, open it again in the *colectivo* or in your hotel room and check what to do.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.

*¡Hola, che — buena suerte!* — hello and good luck.
