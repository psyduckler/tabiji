Puerto Iguazú sits at the junction of three national currencies: the Argentine peso, the Brazilian real, and the Paraguayan guaraní all circulate openly along Calle Av. Victoria Aguirre and the Hito Tres Fronteras overlook where the Iguazú and Paraná rivers meet. The town is small (population around 85,000), purpose-built to service tourism to Iguazú Falls, and reachable via Cataratas del Iguazú International Airport (IGR) seven kilometers east of downtown. From Puerto Iguazú, the park has two faces: the Argentine side (Parque Nacional Iguazú, seventeen kilometers east) with the four-day Cataratas catwalk system and the Garganta del Diablo boardwalk; and the Brazilian side (Foz do Iguaçu), a taxi-and-bridge crossing away via the Tancredo Neves international bridge.

The scam profile here is shaped by the triple-border geography. The signature scam is the **"Ciudad del Este Day Trip"** — a downtown tour-agency package marketed to tourists who want to visit Paraguay's main commercial city across the Friendship Bridge. The drivers frequently skip the Argentine and Paraguayan migraciones stamps to save ten minutes on the outbound journey. When the tourist later exits Argentina for good (typically through Ezeiza), the missing exit stamp triggers a USD 100 overstay fine — paid to Migraciones, not to the original driver. Defensive habit: if crossing to Ciudad del Este, insist the driver stop at both migraciones checkpoints, and keep your passport in your own hand at every stop.

The second layer is the **IGR airport "private transfer" overcharge**. Legitimate hotel remises run ARS 25,000–30,000 for the seven-kilometer ride; arrivals-hall "private" touts quote ARS 60,000–100,000 (roughly USD 40–70). The Gran Meliá Iguazú (the park-interior hotel, formerly Sheraton Iguazú) runs its own shuttle for registered guests.

The third is the **"Both Sides One Day" bundle** at downtown tour agencies: a USD 350 package that repackages a USD 142 direct-book itinerary (Argentine park ticket + Jungle Tour + Brazilian park ticket + transport). The direct-book path is always cheaper, usually by half.

Emergency: **911**; Comisaría del Turista Puerto Iguazú (+54 3757 421-003); Parque Nacional Iguazú administration (+54 3757 491-444).
