Rosario is Argentina's third-largest city (population around 1.3 million), the birthplace of the Argentine flag (raised by Manuel Belgrano on February 27, 1812, at a spot now marked by the massive Monumento Nacional a la Bandera), and — less proudly — the country's acknowledged narcotráfico capital. Tourist traffic concentrates around two axes: the riverfront Costanera along the Paraná, and the downtown area south of Plaza 25 de Mayo. Ignacio de Loyola neighborhood, Martín Fierro, and Las Heras stay residential and welcoming.

The scam ecosystem here has a distinctive signature that's different from Buenos Aires or Córdoba: the **motochorro** (motorcycle phone-snatcher). Rosario's narcotráfico economy has produced small gangs of teenage riders who operate in pairs on 125cc motorcycles — one rides, one snatches — and specifically target the perimeter of the Monumento Nacional a la Bandera, the surrounding Parque a la Bandera, and the Peatonal Córdoba pedestrian street where foreign visitors walk with phones out. The pattern is so well-documented that the municipal Policía de la Provincia de Santa Fe now operates an English-speaking tourist liaison through the Rosario Tourism Board (+54 341 480-2230).

The second layer is rental fraud. The "Paganini Inmobiliaria" class of scam — where a foreign traveler pays a month's rent plus deposit by wire transfer to a WhatsApp contact who turns out to be someone completely unrelated to the actual property — has been written about in the Rosario press since 2019 and continues in 2026. The defensive habit is the same one that applies in Buenos Aires, Mendoza, and Córdoba: pay only through Booking.com, Airbnb, or Expedia, and verify the property on Google Street View before the wire leaves your bank.

Tourists who stick to the Costanera, the Monumento a la Bandera, the main plazas, and Central and Norte neighborhoods rarely run into trouble beyond the scams catalogued here. Barrio Tablada, Villa Gobernador Gálvez, and certain outer neighborhoods are best skipped on a first visit.
