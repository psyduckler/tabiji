# The Scams: City by City {-}

The chapters that follow document every scam reported in eleven Argentine destinations. Cities are ordered by how most visitors actually travel: starting with Buenos Aires, the capital and the country's densest approach-scam ecosystem, then west to Córdoba and Rosario, to the Mendoza wine country, north to Salta and the altiplano, south through the Patagonian circuit (Bariloche, El Calafate, El Chaltén, Ushuaia), to the subtropical falls at Puerto Iguazú, and finishing with the Paraná Delta at Tigre.

If you're hitting only a few of these, skip to the chapter you need. If you're doing a longer loop, skim them all — the same core scams recur with local variations. The Florida Avenue "¡cambio!" tout cluster that intercepts tourists on the Buenos Aires pedestrian mall runs identically at Peatonal Sarmiento in Mendoza and at the Salta bus terminal. The restaurant "cubierto" overcharge documented on Paseo Victorica in Tigre repeats on Av. Maipú in Ushuaia and along Calle Balcarce in Salta. The airport-taxi flat-rate overcharge that starts at Ezeiza arrivals repeats at COR, ROS, FTE, BRC, and every major Argentine airport. Learn the pattern in one city, and you'll spot it the instant you step out of the next *colectivo*.

## The Eleven Destinations at a Glance {-}

![Buenos Aires — Casa Rosada at golden hour](assets/cities/buenos-aires.jpg)

**Buenos Aires** — The capital, the Casa Rosada, and the country's densest approach-scam ecosystem. The Florida Avenue *arbolitos* who shout "¡cambio, cambio!" and hand back counterfeit 10,000-peso notes under the Milei-era post-blue-dollar regime; the Ezeiza arrivals-hall "Meet & Greet" remis touts who quote ARS 80,000–150,000 for a ride Manuel Tienda León completes for ARS 35,000; the Plaza de Mayo bird-dropping distraction pickpocket script; the San Telmo taxi "caramba" long-route gambit; and the WhatsApp-apartment "landlord" who collects a 50 % deposit and disappears.

![Córdoba — Manzana Jesuítica and Plaza San Martín](assets/cities/cordoba-argentina.jpg)

**Córdoba** — Jesuit heritage, the Sierras, and Argentina's most aggressive airport-taxi mafia. COR airport taxis that physically block Uber and Cabify vehicles from the arrivals curb (and throw stones at rideshare cars approaching the drop-off, per Clarín's 2025 coverage); the Villa General Belgrano Oktoberfest accommodation scam that lets you pre-pay for a Güemes room and arrive to a locked door in October; the Nueva Córdoba motochorro (motorcycle phone-snatcher) who targets sidewalks around the Plaza España; and the Güemes Saturday-market tourist-menu markup on the same pizzas sold 30 % cheaper three streets over.

![Rosario — Monumento a la Bandera above the Paraná](assets/cities/rosario.jpg)

**Rosario** — Argentina's flag-birthplace on the Paraná, Che Guevara's hometown, and the country's most methodical motochorro ecosystem. The Monumento a la Bandera perimeter phone-snatch on motorcycles (video-documented by Rosario12 and Clarín); the ROS Fisherton airport "USD 45 flat" quote where an Uber would be USD 15–18; the Paganini-style inmobiliaria rental-deposit fraud targeting foreign long-stayers; and the Peatonal Córdoba cambio-tout cluster near the Plaza 25 de Mayo.

![Mendoza — Andean vineyards under the Cordillera](assets/cities/mendoza.jpg)

**Mendoza** — Malbec, the Andes, and the country's wine-tour capital. The Maipú self-guided bike-wine-tour "friend's cousin rents bikes for half price" pitch that ends with a flat tire, no helmet, and no refund; the Uco Valley hotel-concierge bodega-tour markup at 3x the direct-book Ampora or Trout-and-Wine rate; the Vendimia-season hotel-booking fraud where properties accept Booking deposits and then cancel on check-in; and the Peatonal Sarmiento "¡cambio!" tout who picks up where Florida Avenue leaves off.

![Salta — Cerro San Bernardo and the colonial cathedral](assets/cities/salta.jpg)

**Salta** — Colonial northwest, the Andes altiplano, and the Tren a las Nubes. The Tren a las Nubes reseller-markup where an ARS 187,500 official extranjero fare becomes USD 350–500 in a tour-agency bundle (the TripAdvisor "$700 for 3 persons" complaint is the canonical example); the Calle Balcarce empanada tourist-menu "premium" charge at triple the non-tourist rate; the Cafayate wine-pairing "Torrontés reserve" upsell at USD 60–80 for a wine the bodega sells for USD 15–25; and the SLA airport "flat fare" tout quoting USD 60+ for what the taxi counter prints at EUR 42.

![Bariloche — Nahuel Huapi and Cerro Catedral](assets/cities/bariloche.jpg)

**Bariloche** — Swiss Alps in Patagonia, and the country's rental-car smash-and-grab capital. The Circuito Chico rental-car window-break at Cerro Campanario, Llao Llao, Punto Panorámico, and Bahía López (a half-dozen documented break-in spots that the local Policía de Río Negro openly acknowledges as organized crime); the chocolate-shop "artisan" pricing on Av. Bartolomé Mitre vs. the factory-outlet price 40 % lower; the Cerro Catedral ski-lift "last-run" overcharge; and the Villa La Angostura summer-rental deposit-fraud targeting WhatsApp bookings.

![El Calafate — Perito Moreno glacier face](assets/cities/el-calafate.jpg)

**El Calafate** — Perito Moreno, Lago Argentino, and the gateway to Los Glaciares. The Hielo y Aventura third-party reseller that charges USD 350 for an ice-trek the concessionaire sells for ARS 384,000 direct; the "park entrance included" tour that turns out to exclude the ARS 45,000 foreign-extranjero gate fee; the FTE airport "private transfer" overcharge on a USD 5–7 VES shuttle ride; and the Av. del Libertador restaurant tourist-menu markup running 40–60 % over the Spanish-menu price the waiter won't offer.

![El Chaltén — Monte Fitz Roy granite spires](assets/cities/el-chalten.jpg)

**El Chaltén** — Fitz Roy, Cerro Torre, and Argentina's most notorious restaurant-bill-padding hot spot. The Parrilla La Oveja Negra 88,000-peso dinner (the 2025 anchor incident that became regional shorthand for bill-padding); the bus-terminal fee ambiguity where a legitimate ARS 500–1,000 municipal charge is sometimes inflated to ARS 5,000 by scam collectors in the arrival hall; the Chaltén Travel vs. Marga Taqsa "cheaper bus" pitch that books you on a phantom company; and the Patagonia-Norte short-stay rental fraud that demands USD cash on arrival.

![Ushuaia — Les Eclaireurs lighthouse on the Beagle Channel](assets/cities/ushuaia.jpg)

**Ushuaia** — The end of the world, the Beagle Channel, and Antarctic cruise central. The USH airport taxi dual-pricing where Spanish speakers pay ARS 6,500 and English speakers pay USD 50 for the same ride (the signature Ushuaia scam, widely reported in 2025); the "last-minute Antarctic cruise" WhatsApp agency that collects USD 2,000 deposits for voyages that do not exist; the Av. Maipú centolla (king crab) USD-pricing gambit that quotes USD 80 for what locals pay ARS 35,000; and the Piratour penguin-walk "Isla Martillo private" upsell on a regulated Hielo Azul boat.

![Puerto Iguazú — Garganta del Diablo thundering falls](assets/cities/puerto-iguazu.jpg)

**Puerto Iguazú** — The falls, the subtropical frontier, and three overlapping national currencies. The Triple Frontier "Ciudad del Este day trip" that runs an illegal border crossing without migraciones stamps and leaves the traveler with a USD 100 overstay fine at the real Argentine exit; the IGR airport "private transfer" tout at ARS 60,000–100,000 for a ride the hotel remis runs at ARS 25,000–30,000; the Gran Meliá Iguazú (formerly Sheraton) "corporate rate" email-phishing scam; and the downtown "Both Sides One Day" tour-agency bundle that repackages a $142 direct-book itinerary for $350.

![Tigre — lanchas at Estación Fluvial](assets/cities/tigre.jpg)

**Tigre** — The Delta, Paseo Victorica, and the Tren de la Costa. The unlicensed-lanchero WhatsApp "private Delta tour" that never arrives (municipal fiscalización actively targets these, but the supply keeps coming); the Airbnb-host "Tigre Day Tour" upsell at USD 120 for an experience Sturla sells direct at ARS 27,500; the Puerto de Frutos artisan-market tourist-menu markup at 40–80 % over the Av. Liniers outer-stall price; and the Tren de la Costa service-suspension confusion where travelers arrive at Estación Maipú expecting a train that was pulled three weeks earlier.
