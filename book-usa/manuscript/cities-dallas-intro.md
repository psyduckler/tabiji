Dallas runs lean on tourist scams compared to a coastal megacity, but the four that dominate here are all confidence plays rather than pickpocketing, and two of them can cost you real money. Every documented scam falls into the same bucket: a stranger manufacturing urgency, a "deal," or a fake authority figure, then cashing in before you can think twice. The two that hurt are the DFW and Love Field Unlicensed Airport Taxi Scam, in which freelance drivers waiting outside baggage claim charge three to six times the metered fare, and Dallas Concert Ticket Resale and Fake Promoter Fraud, a category serious enough that in February 2026 a Dallas man drew 75 months in federal prison for a $1.1 million promotion fraud impersonating promoters for Bad Bunny and Beyoncé.

The trouble clusters in three predictable places. The airport curbs come first: both DFW terminals and Love Field's baggage-claim exits are where unlicensed drivers intercept tired arrivals. Downtown's walkable strips come second, where Downtown Dallas Aggressive Panhandling and Phone-Access Scams have shifted from passive freeway-ramp signs to organized groups working set intersections on shift patterns through the West End, Deep Ellum, Uptown's McKinney Avenue, and the Klyde Warren Park perimeter. Third is the road itself across North Dallas, the Dallas North Tollway service roads, US-75 ramps, and Lemmon Avenue near Love Field, where the "Ran Out of Gas" Fake Jewelry Roadside Distraction Scam pushes worthless gold-look jewelry as collateral for cash.

A few defaults keep you clean. Skip the curbside hustle entirely: order Uber or Lyft from the app, use the official yellow-taxi queue with posted flat rates, or just take the DART Orange Line from DFW into downtown for $3, and photograph the plate before your bags go in. Walking downtown, never hand an unlocked phone to a stranger, and never accept jewelry from anyone roadside. And buy concert tickets only through Ticketmaster, AXS, or the venue box office on a credit card, never via Zelle, Cash App, or Venmo to a Facebook seller.
