Austin's seven documented scams share a single signature: they all wear the costume of a legitimate service. There are no pickpocket rings or distraction crews here. Instead, every scheme on this list is a tourist-trap built on a real transaction you were going to make anyway — paying for parking, hailing a ride, buying a festival wristband, booking a room — with a counterfeit version slipped in at the moment your guard is down. Risk spikes hard around the city's big event weekends, when hotels sell out and surge pricing makes the fake option look like the smart one: SXSW, ACL, Formula 1 weekend, and UT football days are when nearly all of these run hottest.

The clusters are easy to map. Downtown's nightlife corridor — East 6th (Dirty Sixth), Rainey Street, Red River, and the Warehouse District — is where the after-midnight scams live: the Fake Rideshare Driver at Night leans out of an undecaled car asking "are you waiting for a ride?", pedicab drivers run the Pedicab Surprise Fare with no meter and no set rate, and Bar Crawl and Event Ticket Fraud sells $30 "VIP" wristbands to bars that charge no cover anyway. The same streets feed the parking traps — the Fake QR Code Parking Meter Scam (29 fraudulent stickers found on downtown pay stations) and the Fake Parking Lot Attendant Scam, where a yellow vest collects $20 cash at an unstaffed lot. Out at Zilker Park and the Convention Center, the ACL and SXSW Fake Ticket and Wristband Scam and the Fake Vacation Rental Listing Scam target festival crowds with too-cheap deals.

A few defaults neutralize most of this. Make every rideshare driver say your name first and match the plate before you open the door — never volunteer it. Pay parking only through the official Park ATX app or a card in the meter slot, never a QR code, and book lots ahead on SpotHero. Settle a flat total before any pedicab moves. And book rooms and festival passes only on the platform, through official sources — never a wire, Zelle, or Venmo to a stranger.
