# The Scams: City by City {-}

The chapters that follow document 238 scams reported across forty-one US cities. Cities are ordered by region rather than by any single itinerary — beginning in the Northeast, then working down through the Southeast, into the Midwest, across the South-Central states, up into the Mountain and Southwest desert, out to the West Coast, and finally across the water to the Pacific islands and the Caribbean.

If you are traveling to only a handful of these cities, skip directly to the relevant chapters. If you are visiting more, skim them all — the same handful of plays repeats from coast to coast with only the local color changed. The fake rideshare driver waiting at the airport curb in Orlando is the same man working the lot in Austin and Nashville. The costumed-character photo shakedown in Times Square is the same hustle on the Las Vegas Strip. Surprise resort fees, counterfeit event and theme-park tickets resold outside the gate, and the fake QR code stuck over a parking meter all turn up city after city. Learn the pattern once and you will spot it everywhere.

A quick word on what counts as a "scam" here. This book treats the everyday tourist economy as the threat surface: not violent crime, but the overpriced pedicab, the bar tab that doubles, the "official" currency booth with hidden fees, the timeshare ambush, the phantom vacation rental, and the pickpocket crew that works distraction in a crowd. Each chapter opens with a short orientation to the city, then walks through its reported scams one by one — how each works, how to avoid it, and exactly where it happens.

## The Seven Regions at a Glance {-}

**Northeast** — Dense, transit-heavy old cities where the hustles cluster around landmarks and stations: New York City, Boston, Philadelphia, and Washington, DC.

**Southeast** — The biggest block in the book, spanning historic squares, mountain towns, theme-park sprawl, and beach strips: Charleston, Savannah, Asheville, Atlanta, Nashville, Memphis, Gatlinburg, Myrtle Beach, Orlando, Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Key West, and New Orleans.

**Midwest** — Big-city downtowns and a heartland entertainment town, where parking, ticket, and rideshare cons dominate: Chicago, Minneapolis, St. Louis, and Branson.

**South-Central** — Texas and the Gulf, built around live-music crowds, festival wristbands, and event parking: Austin, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, and Galveston.

**Mountain/Southwest** — Desert resort and red-rock destinations where resort fees, timeshare pitches, and rigged street games run hot: Denver, Las Vegas, Phoenix, and Sedona.

**West Coast** — California, Oregon, and Washington, from theme parks and wine country to waterfront tourist traps and rental-car smash-and-grabs: Los Angeles, Anaheim, San Diego, San Francisco, Napa Valley, Carmel, Portland, and Seattle.

**Pacific & Caribbean** — The island destinations, where the timeshare ambush, the rental-car return fee, and the equipment-damage shakedown await: Honolulu, Maui, and San Juan.
