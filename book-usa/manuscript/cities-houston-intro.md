Houston is a car town, and almost every scam aimed at visitors here rides shotgun. Of the four documented schemes, all four orbit rideshare and parking, and two of them rate as high-severity — the kind that drain a bank account, not just a wallet. There are no museum-district pickpockets or fake-charity clipboards to worry about; the danger lives in the gap between landing at the airport and pulling up to your hotel, and in the lots around a stadium on event night. This is a sprawling metro where you will Uber or Lyft everywhere, and the scammers know it.

The sharpest cluster is at the airports. At both George Bush Intercontinental (IAH) and Hobby, the IAH & Hobby Airport Rideshare & Fake-Driver Scam puts unlicensed drivers at baggage claim and the arrivals curb, posing as your booked pickup and inventing a fare. The same trip can then sprout a Post-Ride 'Vomit Scam' Fake Cleaning-Fee Charge days later, or worse — the high-severity Fake 'Uber Support' Verification-Code Account Takeover, where a spoofed call talks you into reading out a code and hands your account to a stranger. These reach beyond the airport into downtown drop-offs, Midtown and Montrose late-night rides, and the Galleria on weekends. The second hot zone is NRG Park and the Toyota Center on game and rodeo days, where the Rodeo Houston & NRG Stadium Event Parking Scam funnels drivers into vacant yards and tow-prone strips for $40 to $80 cash.

Three defaults keep a first-timer clean. Match the plate, car, and driver name in the app before you open any door, and walk past anyone who approaches you inside the terminal. Pre-pay parking on the official venue website and ignore the freelancers waving you into a yard. And never, ever read a verification code aloud to a caller — Uber never asks.
