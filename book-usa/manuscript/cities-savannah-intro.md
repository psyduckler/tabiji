Savannah's tourist-scam profile is small but sharply concentrated: four documented schemes, weighted toward two of the highest-stakes categories a visitor faces, money and transport. Two of the four carry a high severity rating, and both hit you where you're least defended. The first is the wallet-out moment at the airport; the second is the card you hand over after a River Street dinner. What drives the pattern is the city's geography of arrival and indulgence. Tourists land tired at one airport, funnel into one Historic District, and eat and drink along one cobblestone strip, so scammers don't need volume; they need to stand in the right doorway.

That doorway starts at Savannah-Hilton Head International (SAV). The "SAV Airport Taxi Overcharge & Rideshare Confusion" scam runs inside the arrivals hall, where touts call out "Downtown Savannah?" or "Hilton Head?," claim the meter is broken, and walk you to an unmarked car for a cash flat rate roughly double the real fare. The denser threat is downtown: "Downtown Savannah Restaurant Credit-Card Skimming" clusters along the River Street restaurant strip and City Market, where a card taken out of sight for "processing" comes back cloned, with fraudulent charges surfacing days later in another state. The same promenade hosts the lower-stakes "River Street 'Monk' Fake-Buddhist Bracelet-Donation Scam," a saffron-robed figure who slips a bracelet onto your wrist at Factors Walk, then demands a "donation." Out in the rentals, "Savannah Airbnb & Vacation-Rental Deposit Fraud" works both ends: fake "security inspectors" at Historic District and Tybee Island doors, and off-platform "managers" collecting deposits by Zelle or Venmo.

For a first-timer, three defaults cover almost everything. Use only the Uber or Lyft app at the marked Ground Transportation curb, and verify the plate and driver name before opening a door. Pay every restaurant and gas-pump bill by contactless or phone wallet so the card never leaves your hand. And book lodging only through Airbnb, VRBO, or Booking.com, never off-platform, and never open the door to an unscheduled "inspector."
