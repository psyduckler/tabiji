Key West runs a low-violence, high-extraction kind of risk: all six documented scams here are tourist-traps, and not one involves a stranger reaching into your pocket. The danger is the bill. A walkable party island that empties cruise ships onto a single famous street, then surrounds you with meters, open bars, and "free" samples, is built to separate cheerful, sun-warmed visitors from their money one charge at a time. The categories that dominate are predatory pricing and high-pressure sales — the Pedicab Meter Manipulation, the Duval Street Bar Overpouring and Tab Padding, the Cosmetics Shop High-Pressure Scam — plus one genuinely costly online trap, the Vacation Rental Listing Fraud, the only scam here rated high severity.

Nearly everything clusters on or just off Duval Street and the cruise-ship corridor running down to Mallory Square. Duval itself is the gauntlet: cosmetics shops where a salesperson rubs cream on your hand and walks you toward a four-figure "one-time deal" (one visitor was charged $958.90), open-air bars in the 400-600 blocks pouring strong drinks and quietly padding the tab, and pedicab drivers who never name a rate, take the long way, and present a $60 meter for a ten-minute ride. Park a car anywhere near here and the Parking and Towing Trap waits — deliberately confusing signage on private lots that look public, then a tow that balloons from $135 to $400. Out on the harbor docks and the kiosks by Mallory Square, the Sunset Sail Bait and Switch packs 120 people onto a catamaran sold to you as an intimate champagne cruise.

Three safe defaults cover most of it. Agree the total fare out loud before you get into any pedicab — flat rate only, never "we'll see." Skip street parking entirely: use the city garage at Grinnell and Caroline, or leave the car and ride the free Duval Loop bus. And book lodging only through Airbnb, VRBO, or Booking.com with platform payment — never wire a deposit to an owner, no matter how charming the cottage.
