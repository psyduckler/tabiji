San Juan's scam landscape is dominated by logistics, not pickpockets. Across the six schemes that recur here, the through-line is the friction of arriving, driving, and parking on an island where the rules are real but loosely enforced — and where opportunists fill the gaps. Transport and accommodation fraud carry the heaviest hits: the Puerto Rico Rental Car "Sixt" / "NextCar" Damage & Deposit Scam can turn a pre-existing scratch into a $2,000–$5,000 post-return claim, and the San Juan Airbnb Off-Platform Booking & "PR Robbery" Pattern targets visitors with fake Zelle-only listings and lockbox break-ins, both rated high severity. Around them sit a cluster of moderate, common hustles that catch first-timers in their first hour and their first parking spot.

The trouble concentrates in three zones. At Luis Muñoz Marín airport (SJU), the San Juan Airport Taxi & Rideshare Overcharge plays out at the baggage-claim curb — drivers quote a "flat $45" to Old San Juan when the regulated rate is $21, or insist Uber isn't allowed (it is, at Zone E). Inside Old San Juan, the "$500 Parking Ticket" & Street-Parking Scam runs on confusing residential-only blocks along Calle San Sebastián and Calle del Cristo, where vest-wearing "attendants" pocket cash for free spaces that get towed anyway. Out in Condado and Isla Verde, resort lobbies and the cruise pier host the Timeshare Presentation Hustle, dangling "free tours" and dinner vouchers that lead to three-to-five-hour sales pressure.

For a first-timer, three defaults cover most of it. Take Uber from Zone E or pay only the posted ATP flat rate — refuse any driver who quotes higher. Skip Old San Juan street parking entirely and use the official Paseo Covadonga ($8) or Doña Fela ($5) lots, paying any genuine ticket only at sanjuan.pr.gov, never a website printed on the slip. And book lodging through the platform itself, never by wire or Zelle.
