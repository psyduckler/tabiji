Galveston runs on two engines that scammers love: a cruise terminal that processes hundreds of thousands of sail-day travelers, and a beach economy built around the Seawall. Across the island we track six recurring scams, and none of them are pickpocket-and-run violence. They are extraction plays — overcharges, fake fees, and document panic — aimed at people who are rushed, unfamiliar with how paid parking and cruise check-in actually work, or willing to pay anything to make a problem disappear. Four of the six are tourist-trap or food cons; two, both rated high severity, live at the Port of Galveston and prey on cruise passengers specifically.

The clustering is tight. Seawall Blvd is the worst block: the Seawall Parking Ticket Trap & PayByPhone Confusion hits drivers who skip the app and get $45–$85 tickets in the mail weeks later, or who hand cash to "parking attendants" who have no legitimate reason to exist on a public beach. A few hundred feet away, the Pleasure Pier Tourist Trap Overcharge sells $39 wristbands, $14 beers, and $40 photo packages, and Seawall and Pier 21 restaurants run the Gulf Coast Shrimp & Seafood Fraud, plating imported farm-raised shrimp under "wild Gulf" labels marked up three to five times. At the cruise terminal, the Off-Site Parking Lot Scam dangles "$5/day" lots that vanish with your deposit, and the Passport & Last-Minute Documentation Scam charges panicked sailors $1,500–$5,000 for "same-day passports" that do not exist.

For a first-timer, three defaults cover most of it. Download the PayByPhone app before you arrive and pay tickets only at galveston.paybyphone.com or the Municipal Court — never a URL left on your windshield. Book cruise parking directly at portofgalveston.com ($10–$15/day) and ignore Marketplace lots. And check your passport six months out, because the airport-style "expediting" booths at the pier are pure fiction.
