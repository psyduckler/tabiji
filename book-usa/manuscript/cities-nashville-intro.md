Nashville's scam economy runs on one thing: a relentless tide of bachelorette parties, concert crowds, and music tourists who come to spend and don't come to scrutinize. All seven scams covered here fall under the same banner — the tourist trap — and they cluster tightly around the city's nightlife and parking. Four of the seven are high-severity, the kind that drain a credit card or strand you in a stranger's car, and they share a single tell: a friendly, hospitality-flavored framing designed to keep you from asking the obvious question until the money has already moved.

Lower Broadway, the neon honky-tonk strip, is the epicenter. This is where Broadway Bar Tab Inflation turns eight basic drinks into a $340 tab at bars that post no prices, and where the Bogus Bar Crawl Wristband ($40 for an "event" the listed bars have never heard of) and the Pedal Tavern and Party Vehicle Overcharge (a $200 Instagram quote that balloons to $500 in "fuel surcharges" and mandatory gratuity) prey on group weekends. At 2 a.m., the same crowd spills out into the Fake Rideshare Driver Pickup — impersonators who call out an overheard first name and demand $80 to $150 in cash mid-ride, a hustle Nashville criminalized in 2024. Park in Midtown, 12 South, or Music Row and you risk the Fake Parking Ticket QR Code Scam, an MNPD-confirmed phishing operation; shop at Opry Mills and crews with Square readers run the Parking Lot Fake Fundraiser Drain, tapping $5 "donations" into $2,000 charges. Near the Ryman and Bridgestone, scalpers move Counterfeit Concert Tickets that scan as duds.

The defenses are short and worth memorizing. Ask any bartender for the price card before you order. Verify a rideshare by asking the driver "Who are you here for?" and matching the plate in the app before you open the door. Never scan a QR code on a parking ticket — go straight to nashville.gov. And never tap your card or send a Venmo payment to anyone working a sidewalk, a parking lot, or a screenshot.
