New York's scams aren't the pickpocket-in-a-crowd kind so much as the manufactured-confrontation kind: six common plays that all run on the same engine, getting you committed to something — a CD in your hand, a seat at a table, kids mid-hug with Elmo — before the price appears. Half of them concentrate in transport and tourist-trap territory, which is exactly where a first-timer spends day one. Nobody is trying to steal from you quietly. They're trying to surround you, raise their voice, and let the discomfort of a public scene do the collecting.

Times Square is the densest cluster. On 42nd Street between 7th and 8th, the CD Hustle works by pressing a "free mixtape" into your hand, then demanding $20 with two or three crew members closing the gap behind you. A few blocks up on the 46th and 47th Street plazas, the Character Photo Shake-Down does the same with costumed Elmo and Spider-Man: a "free" family photo becomes $20 per person, per character, the moment the shutter clicks. Battery Park is the other hot zone — men in fake "GUIDE" vests sell $60 Fake Statue of Liberty Tickets for boats that never dock, and tell families the free public park itself costs $20 to enter. At Central Park's 59th Street and Columbus Circle entrances, the Pedicab Price Trap offers a "we'll work it out" ride that ends in a $180 bill and a cab parked across the sidewalk.

Three defaults keep you clear. Never accept anything handed to you on the street — hands in pockets, keep walking. Buy Statue of Liberty tickets only at statuecruises.com and ride only from the official Battery Park dock; the Staten Island Ferry is a free alternative view. And agree on an exact written price before you board any pedicab — or skip it for Uber, a metered yellow cab, or the subway, where tapping your own contactless card with OMNY beats any stranger selling a "pre-loaded" MetroCard.
