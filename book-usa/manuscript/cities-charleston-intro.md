Charleston runs gentle on violent crime and heavy on the polite hustle. Of the six scams logged here, five are tourist traps that work by turning your good manners against you, and only one — the airport ride — involves a stranger flatly overcharging you. The driver is the city's compact, walkable historic core, where a captive crowd of cruise passengers and retirees flows through a few hundred yards of cobblestone, and where the line between a "Southern charm" interaction and a shakedown is deliberately blurred.

The trouble clusters in three places. The first is City Market and the Meeting–Market Street intersection, home to the "oldest scam in the book": someone weaves a palmetto-frond rose in ten seconds, presses it into your spouse's hand "as a gift," then demands $10 to $30 for the art, with fake-charity buckets, grab-your-hand fortune tellers, and unsolicited "photographers" working the same blocks. The second is the dining strip along Meeting, Market, and King Streets, where a 2025 scandal found 91% of "local shrimp" was actually imported farm-raised — sold at a $28–$38 local premium — and where bills arrive padded with auto-gratuity, tip-on-tax, and one viral 523% tip. The third is the carriage-and-timeshare gauntlet: hotel concierges mark up $34 carriage rides to $65–$95, while "Great Vacations LLC" on Meeting Street trades a "free harbor cruise" for a 90-minute pitch that runs three to five hours toward a $10,000–$40,000 sale.

Three defaults keep a first-timer clean. Refuse every unsolicited "gift" — keep your hands in your pockets near City Market, and hand the rose straight back. Book carriage and walking tours direct (Palmetto Carriage Works, Bulldog Tours), never through a curb tout or concierge. And from CHS airport, take Uber or Lyft from Garage Level 3 with the fare on-screen ($22–$35), ignoring the "flat $55" drivers circling baggage claim.
