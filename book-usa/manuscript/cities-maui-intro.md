Maui's scams are not the pickpocket-and-distraction kind you brace for in a crowded city. Across the six documented here, the money is taken slowly, through paperwork, "free gifts," and pressure tactics aimed squarely at visitors and retirees. Five of the six are transport or tourist-trap schemes, and the sixth is the gravest of all. The island's geography concentrates the risk: nearly everyone arrives by air and drives, which is exactly why the worst offenders cluster around the rental counter and the resort sales office rather than any street corner.

It starts at Kahului Airport (OGG). The Maui Rental Car "Damage Claim" scam is the high-severity standout — operators like Avis, Fox, and Payless levy $1,500–$4,500 post-return charges for pre-existing scratches, fabricated dings, or even a "sand damage" vacuuming fee if any beach sand is visible. The same lot breeds OGG Airport Long-Term Parking & Transportation Scams: "$8/day" lots that vanish with your deposit, and "rental car shuttle" touts charging $25 a head to phantom discount lots. Twenty-some miles west and south, the Kaanapali, Lahaina, and Wailea resort strips are timeshare country, where Hyatt Vacation Club, Westin, and "Hawaiian Island Experiences" dangle Maui Luau Tour "free ticket" hooks to lure retirees into multi-hour, six-figure sales pitches. Out on the Road to Hana, bait-and-switch operators and unofficial "viewpoint access fees" round out the picture. Gravest of all, in the North Kihei and Lahaina wildfire-recovery zones, the Post-Lahaina "Ironside Claims" scam sends fake insurance reps door to door demanding cash "processing fees."

For a first-timer, three defaults cover most of it. Book rentals through Costco Travel or AutoSlash, then shoot a timestamped video walk-around of all four sides at pickup and dispute any damage claim within 48 hours via your card. Book luaus and Hana tours direct with named operators — never the "free" or "VIP" version. And remember that no real insurer or charity collects cash at your door; verify any wildfire fund at charitynavigator.org first.
