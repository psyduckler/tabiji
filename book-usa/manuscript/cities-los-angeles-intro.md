Los Angeles runs seven scams, and what's striking is how little of it is pickpocketing or sleight of hand. Almost everything here is a tourist trap built on social pressure and the predictable rhythms of a sightseeing day: a costumed character's arm around your shoulder, a CD pressed into your palm, a bracelet knotted onto your wrist before you've registered the touch. The city's geography does the sorting. Scams cluster tightly around the three or four places every first-timer is guaranteed to go, and the operators have run these scripts on the same sidewalks for over a decade. The two that can actually hurt your trip, a smashed rental window and a phishing site dressed up as a parking meter, are the quiet ones nobody warns you about until the bank calls.

Hollywood Boulevard between Highland and La Brea is the densest hunting ground. The TCL Chinese Theatre forecourt and the Hollywood and Highland complex draw the costume-character photo scam, in which a "manager" materializes to demand $20 to $50 the moment the photo exists; the fake mixtape and free CD hustle, in which paired hustlers flank you, one with a Venmo QR code already up; and clipboard promoters pitching fake celebrity-home tour vans that bill $40-plus with surprise "fuel surcharges." The Venice Beach Boardwalk between Rose and Washington is the same energy with sand: the bracelet scam, a three-second wrist grab followed by a public-shaming pursuit if you refuse, and velvet-draped psychic-reading tables that pivot a free reading into a $100 "curse" only they can lift. The real money loss happens in the parking lots, where the fake parking-meter QR code phishing scam and rental-car smash-and-grabs both concentrate at Griffith Observatory, Venice, the Santa Monica Pier, and Universal.

Three defaults cover most of it. Never scan a QR sticker on a meter; pay with coins or a card in the slot, or use PayByPhone or ParkMobile downloaded straight from the app store. Agree on an exact dollar price before any photo, CD, bracelet, or tour, because once contact is made the script depends on your embarrassment. And leave nothing in a rental car, not in the trunk, nowhere, and peel the rental stickers off the glass before you park.
