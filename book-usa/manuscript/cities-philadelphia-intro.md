Philadelphia runs a low-pressure scam scene compared to a Barcelona or a Rome: six documented schemes, none of them organized-crime-scale, almost all of them exploiting the same blind spot. Tourists don't realize how much of historic Philly is free, and the cons price the gap. The two biggest categories are ticket fraud and tourist-menu padding, both built on convincing you to pay for something that costs nothing or close to it. The one genuinely dangerous outlier is short-term-rental fraud, the only high-severity entry on the list, where a fake "host" pockets an off-platform deposit before you've left home.

The clusters track the tourist trail almost exactly. Around Independence Mall, the Liberty Bell & Independence Hall "Skip-the-Line" Ticket Scam resells free National Park Service tickets for $20–$60 (the Bell is a walk-in, Independence Hall is a $1 reservation on recreation.gov), while well-rehearsed panhandling and the occasional three-card-monte "cup game" work the Center City Pickpockets & Street Scams beat through nearby SEPTA platforms at 15th Street and City Hall. A mile up the Parkway at the museum, the Rocky Steps Tour Touts hawk $20–$50 "professional photos" and $60–$90 "Rocky walking tours" for a 15-minute climb that's free. And the food trail — Reading Terminal Market and especially the South Street strip — hides the Tourist-Menu Overcharge, where laminated-photo stalls charge $18–$25 for a cheesesteak that runs $12–$14 at John's Roast Pork.

For a first-timer, three defaults cover most of it. From the airport, the licensed taxi to Center City is a published $28.50 flat rate (or SEPTA's Airport Line train at $6.75) — never take a ride from anyone soliciting inside the terminal. Book any "skip-the-line" historic ticket only on the official nps.gov or recreation.gov, scrolling past the sponsored Google results. And book lodging only through Airbnb or VRBO with on-platform payment — refuse every Zelle, Venmo, or off-platform ID-scan request, no matter how good the rate.
