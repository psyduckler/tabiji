Myrtle Beach runs six documented scams, and they share a single logic: this is a high-volume, drive-in beach town built on extracting one more fee from a vacationing family that just wants sand and a parking spot. The damage skews toward money rather than danger — half the entries are nightlife-and-lodging money grabs, the rest are tourist-trap overcharges and one transport hustle — but the dollar amounts are real. The standout is the Wyndham / Capital Vacations timeshare presentation hustle, the only high-severity scam on the list and one of the most aggressive sales markets in the country: a "free golf package" or "free Ripley's tickets" becomes a three-to-five-hour pressure session aimed at a $10,000-to-$40,000 signature.

The scams cluster in three predictable zones. Along Ocean Blvd, the "$200 Parking Scam" runs out of private side-street lots with handwritten "$10 all-day" signs that quietly bolt on $75–$200 "overtime fees" and tow your car ten miles inland for a cash-only $300–$500 release; legitimate city meters run $2/hour via ParkMobile. On the Boardwalk between Plyler Park and Pier 14, adult handlers send children to push pizza flyers and guilt you into a "$2–$5 tip," alongside unsolicited henna, beach-chair, and photographer touts. And at Broadway at the Beach — also where timeshare kiosks and hotel-lobby "concierge" desks lurk — restaurants run 40–70% above comparable local spots and "combo packages" bundle no actual discount.

First-timer defaults: park only at city-operated lots or the Boardwalk Paid Parking ($5/day flat), and verify any "ticket" at cityofmyrtlebeach.com before paying a cent. From MYR airport, take Uber/Lyft or the metered taxi queue ($12–$28 to Ocean Blvd) and refuse any baggage-claim "flat $35–$50" offer. Book lodging only through Airbnb, VRBO, or Booking.com — never Zelle or Venmo — and refuse every "free gift" tied to a 90-minute presentation.
