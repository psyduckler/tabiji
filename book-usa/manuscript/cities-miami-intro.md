Miami's scam economy runs on a single assumption: you came for the beach, you're paying in dollars, and you're never coming back. All seven of the scams documented here are tourist-trap plays rather than violent crime, but five of them carry a high severity rating, and almost every one shares the same engine — the moment of leverage arrives only after you've already committed, when the bag is in the trunk, the tab is open, or the rose is in your date's hand. There's no language barrier and no unfamiliar currency to blame; the friction is entirely manufactured by venues and vendors who know a one-time customer won't dispute the bill.

The pressure concentrates in three zones. South Beach is the epicenter: on Ocean Drive and Collins Avenue, sharply dressed promoters run the "VIP Promoter" Bait-and-Switch, steering you to a table with an undisclosed $500 minimum, while the beachfront restaurants pull the Menu Bait-and-Switch — a 20% "service charge" stacked on a "suggested gratuity" line plus $8 bread you didn't order. Inside the same bars and pool clubs, Credit Card Skimming captures your card the moment a bartender carries it out of sight, and on the patios the Rose Seller Guilt Trip weaponizes your dinner date. Miami International Airport is the second hot spot, where unofficial yellow cabs run the Luggage Abandonment play — holding your suitcase hostage for an inflated fare — and "official" currency kiosks bury a 15–20% spread behind the advertised rate. Wynwood is the gentlest of the three, but the Art District Gallery Hustle still lures visitors off the free murals with free wine and "investment-grade" prints.

A few defaults neutralize most of this. Take Uber or Lyft exclusively from the airport so no one controls your luggage, and never let a trunk close on your bags before you're seated. Tap Apple Pay or Google Pay at every bar so your card never leaves your hand. Book any club directly through its official website where the cover and table minimum are written down, and ask for a full menu with prices before you sit anywhere on Ocean Drive. And since Miami runs on U.S. dollars, skip the exchange kiosks entirely and pull cash from a real bank ATM.
