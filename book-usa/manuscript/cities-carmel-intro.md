Carmel-by-the-Sea is one of the gentlest entries in this book, and that is precisely what makes it disarming. There are no pickpockets working the galleries and no fake police shaking down rental cars. All three documented scams here are low-severity tourist traps, and every one of them runs on the same fuel: a wealthy, browsing-on-vacation crowd with money to spend and emotion overriding arithmetic. Nobody robs you in Carmel. They simply nudge you, warmly, into paying more than you meant to.

The clustering follows the village's small footprint. On the downtown streets and Ocean Avenue, the Parking Time Trap catches first-timers off guard: there are no meters anywhere, which feels like a gift right up until you return three hours later to a citation, because nearly every street carries a strict two-hour limit enforced by chalk-marking officers and signage that is easy to miss. The tasting rooms along San Carlos, Lincoln, and Dolores Streets run the Wine Tasting Upsell, where generous complimentary reserve pours soften you up for a 600-dollar case and a club membership at prices 30 to 50 percent above what the same bottle costs in Monterey. And in the art galleries along Ocean Avenue and Dolores, the Gallery Price Inflation leans on talk of "rising stars" and "investment value" to move pieces at four-figure prices the secondary market does not support.

The safe defaults are easy. Skip the street-parking gamble and drive straight to the free all-day lot on Junipero Avenue near Sunset Center, just south of Ocean Avenue; if you do park on the street, set a phone timer and note your cross streets, since Carmel has no street addresses. In tasting rooms and galleries alike, the rule is the same: decide your budget before you walk in, decline the extra pours, and never buy on the spot. Take the artist's name, sleep on it, and compare prices. The Carmel Art Association cooperative is a markup-free place to start.
