St. Louis is a low-stakes city for tourist scams in the conventional sense — there are no pickpocket rings working the Arch and no fake-ticket hustlers at Busch Stadium. What it has instead is a car culture, and nearly every scam here happens through a windshield or a phone screen. Of the four documented patterns, two are aggressive intersection approaches and two are higher-severity money grabs — a fake-rideshare operation at the airport and a phishing text sweeping the whole state. The through-line is simple: in St. Louis you are most exposed when you are stopped at a red light, standing at an arrivals curb, or holding your phone.

The clusters are predictable. The Hanley Road exit off I-64, the Brentwood Boulevard interchange, and the Forest Park perimeter are where the window approaches live. This is the home of the locally famous "Purple Shirt" Intersection Window-Approach Scam — men in branded "Saving the Lost" shirts who yell at drivers to roll down at red lights, open with a religious line, and pivot to a tap-to-pay card reader within ten seconds. The same corners host the Forest Park "Stranded Stranger" con, where a woman feigns a dead phone and an empty tank and hands over "gold" jewelry as collateral she'll never reclaim. The other danger zone is Lambert International, where Fake Uber & Rideshare Impersonation operators intercept real app riders at Terminal 1 and 2 arrivals and run unlicensed trips at inflated fares.

The safe defaults follow directly. At any intersection, keep your windows up, your doors locked, and your eyes forward — never tap a stranger's card reader and never accept jewelry as a promise. At the airport, verify the plate, make, and driver name in the app before you open a door, or skip rideshare entirely and take MetroLink downtown for two to four dollars. And the Missouri DOR never texts about traffic tickets: any "FINAL NOTICE" pay-now link is a scam, so delete it without tapping.
