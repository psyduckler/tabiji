San Diego's scam problem is not pickpockets or muggings — it's the slow, smiling overcharge. All seven scams documented here fall into a single category: the tourist trap, the kind run by someone who looks licensed, sounds friendly, and reveals the real price only after you can no longer say no. What drives it is a high-volume vacation economy stacked on top of attractions that charge premium prices. When a zoo ticket runs $90 and a downtown cocktail can hit $23, a 40-percent "discount" or a verbal $30 quote sounds believable rather than suspicious — and that believability is the whole mechanism.

The trouble concentrates in three zones. The Gaslamp Quarter downtown is the densest: pedicab drivers run the Pedicab Bait-and-Switch, quoting "$30" verbally and then flipping over a hidden per-person, per-five-minute card that turns a six-block ride into a $120 demand; bars practice Gaslamp Quarter bar overcharging with no printed menus, "market pricing," and an automatic 22-percent service fee stacked under your tip; and men in vests run the Parking Phishing and Fake Valet Scam, pocketing $30 cash at a public self-park lot and handing you a worthless handwritten ticket. The waterfront resorts of Mission Bay and Hotel Circle are recruitment grounds for the Timeshare Presentation Trap, where free SeaWorld tickets buy you a four-hour hostage sale. And down at La Jolla Cove and the Mission Beach boardwalk, the Unlicensed Beach Vendors and Photo Scams turn a "free" sea-lion photo into a $40 confrontation. Plan a Tijuana day trip and you'll meet the Tijuana Border Crossing Extortion at San Ysidro, where hustlers sell line-cutting that buys nothing.

For a first-timer, a few defaults neutralize most of this. Skip pedicabs entirely — the Gaslamp is small, and Uber or Lyft is faster than the dispute. Photograph any rate card or drink menu before you commit, and walk if there isn't one. Buy attraction tickets only from official sites, never Craigslist. And remember that the city never texts parking citations — pay through ParkMobile, and a "valet" with no business behind him is just a man in a vest.
