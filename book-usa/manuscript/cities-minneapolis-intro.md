Minneapolis is a low-stakes city for scams, but a persistent one. All three documented schemes here are nuisance-grade rather than financial ruin, yet they share a common engine: they target people who are in motion, distracted, or behind the wheel, and they bank on a Midwestern reflex to be polite and stop. Every one falls under the tourist-trap bucket and every one is rated common, so you will not stumble into a sophisticated identity-theft operation in the Twin Cities, but you should expect to be approached at least once if you spend time near the freeways or at a summer event.

The scams cluster in three predictable places. The highways are the worst offenders: the I-94 & Highway On-Ramp Fake "Out of Gas" Ring works the on-ramps through Minneapolis and St. Paul, plus the 494/62 Minnetonka interchange and I-35W ramps near downtown, where a "stranded" stranger offers you a ring or watch as fake collateral for gas money. That same 494/62 corner is the most-reported spot for Twin Cities Highway-Exit & Uptown Aggressive Panhandling, which also pushes into Uptown, the Hennepin-Lyndale intersection, and the Target Center perimeter on event days. Come late August, the action moves to the fairgrounds, where the Minnesota State Fair "CD Artist" Strongarm Sale Scam runs the Grandstand walkways, International Bazaar, and Snelling and Dan Patch corridors—someone shoves a CD into your hands, demands a signature, then demands payment.

The safe defaults are simple. Never stop your car for a roadside stranger and never roll the window down at a ramp; if someone genuinely looks in danger, call the State Patrol at 911 from a distance. At the fair, keep your hands in your pockets, refuse anything handed to you, and never sign a stranger's clipboard. Anywhere downtown or in Uptown, say "no" firmly and keep walking without breaking stride—pausing is the whole game.
