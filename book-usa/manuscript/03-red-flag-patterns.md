# The Seven Universal Red-Flag Patterns {-}

The 238 scams in this book may sound different from each other, but almost all of them are variations on just seven patterns. Learn the patterns and you do not need to memorize every scam. You will recognize a new one the moment it starts to unfold, even if it has never been written down.

Read each of these once. Then let them settle in your head the way you remember a home-safety tip or a road sign.

## Pattern 1: The Manufactured Urgency

The first pattern is a clock that does not really exist. "This rate is today only." "I can only hold these tickets for ten minutes." "The presentation is about to close — sign here and you keep the free park passes." The whole point is to move money before you have time to step away, compare a price, or sleep on it. Real businesses are happy to let you think it over. A scam cannot survive your second thought, so it manufactures a deadline.

You will see this most aggressively in the timeshare machine that runs in every resort city in this book. In Las Vegas it is the **Timeshare Vacation Club Pitch** working the Strip sidewalks and the Convention Center, where "free" show tickets or a buffet are the bait for a ninety-minute high-pressure close. In Orlando it is the **Timeshare Presentation Trap** on International Drive and at Westgate's presentation centers; in Honolulu, **The Waikiki Timeshare Presentation Ambush** on Kalakaua Avenue; in Anaheim, the **Timeshare Presentation Near Disneyland**; in Charleston, the **'Great Vacations LLC' & Meeting Street Timeshare Hustle**; in Phoenix and Scottsdale, the **Timeshare Presentation Hustle** at the Waterfront resorts. The same engine drives Miami's **South Beach Nightclub 'VIP Promoter' Bait-and-Switch** on Ocean Drive, where a "free table tonight only" becomes a four-figure minimum once you are inside, and Key West's **Cosmetics Shop High-Pressure Scam** on Duval Street, where a "free sample" turns into a closed door and a hard sell.

The pattern breaks the moment you treat the deadline as the tell. No legitimate ticket, room rate, tour, or club table evaporates because you walked away to check it. Say "I never decide today" and mean it — then leave the room, the booth, or the sidewalk. Book timeshare-adjacent "deals," show tickets, and club tables only through the venue's own box office or a platform you can dispute on. If a stranger is offering you something free to get you to sit down, the free thing is the hook and the ninety minutes is the catch.

## Pattern 2: The Phantom Authority Fee

The second pattern is a fee that wears a costume. A "city" parking ticket on your windshield. An "official" airport currency window. A folding table that looks like a ticket booth in front of a monument that does not charge admission. The fee feels governmental, so you pay it the way you would pay a real one — quickly, without arguing, because arguing with the government feels expensive. Almost none of it is real.

You will see this on windshields and phones as the fake-citation wave: Washington, D.C.'s **Fake Parking Ticket QR Code** slipped under wipers in Georgetown and around the National Mall, Boston's **Fake Parking Ticket Text Scam**, and Nashville's **Fake Parking Ticket QR Code Scam** in Midtown and on Music Row — each one a counterfeit "violation" that routes your payment to a stranger. You will see it at the money window as Miami's **Fake 'Official' Currency Exchange with Hidden Fees** at the airport and on South Beach, where an "official rate" buries a punishing spread. You will see it as a person in an official-sounding role: Washington's **Fake Tour Guide Solicitation** outside the White House and at the Capitol steps, and San Antonio's **Alamo Mission 'Ticket' & Historic Site Tour Confusion**, where someone sells "tickets" to a site that is free to enter. And you will see it as the rental counter's invented surcharge in Honolulu's **Rental Car Return Terminal Fee Scam** at HNL and the Waikiki Beach kiosks' **Equipment Damage Fee Shakedown**, where "damage" you did not cause appears on the bill.

The pattern breaks on verification, never on speed. A real parking citation is issued by the city and is payable on the city's own website — type the official `.gov` address yourself; never pay a ticket by scanning a QR code or texting a link off the paper. Withdraw cash from a bank ATM and pay by card rather than trusting any "official exchange." Confirm before you go which monuments and missions are free to enter (most federal sites are), and buy tickets only at the site's posted box office or its official site. When anyone in a quasi-official role names a fee, ask for the printed price card or the agency name — there rarely is one — and walk to the staffed counter to confirm what you actually owe.

## Pattern 3: The Off-Screen Payment Pivot

The third pattern is the moment the transaction jumps off any platform that could protect you. A QR code instead of the city meter. Venmo, Zelle, Cash App, or "just tap your card here" instead of a real terminal. Cash, gift cards, or a wire instead of a checkout page. Every legitimate payment leaves a trail you can dispute; this pattern's entire purpose is to get your money somewhere a chargeback cannot follow.

You will see the QR version at the curb as Los Angeles's **Fake Parking Meter QR Code Scam** and the fake-citation QR codes in Washington, Nashville, and Boston above — a sticker slapped over a real meter, or a fake ticket, that sends you to a lookalike payment page. You will see the app version in Phoenix's **Old Town Scottsdale 'Skin' & Venmo-PIN Scam**, where a friendly stranger maneuvers you into a Venmo screen and your PIN, and in Seattle's **Downtown 'Kids' Charity' Donation & Tap-to-Pay Scam** outside Lumen Field, where a clipboard "donation" becomes a contactless tap for an amount you never see. You will see the cash-and-wire version in every phantom-rental scam in this book — Boston's, Savannah's deposit fraud, Key West's **Vacation Rental Listing Fraud**, Orlando's **Vacation Rental Phantom Listing**, and the Disney-area **Ticket OfferUp / Craigslist Resale Scam** in Anaheim — where the listing is real-looking and the demand for a Zelle deposit or wire is the whole crime.

The pattern breaks when you refuse to leave the rails. Pay parking only at the physical meter, the city's app you downloaded yourself, or a kiosk — never a QR code printed on a sticker or a slip of paper. Never send Venmo, Zelle, Cash App, gift cards, or a wire to anyone you have not met for goods, tickets, deposits, or "donations"; those rails were built for friends, not strangers, and have no buyer protection. Keep every booking on the platform until check-in, and watch your own screen during any tap-to-pay. If someone is steering you off the app, the steering is the scam.

## Pattern 4: The Free Gift That Isn't

The fourth pattern manufactures a debt out of thin air. Someone hands you a CD, ties a bracelet on your wrist, pins a flower to your jacket, drops a toy in your child's hands, "blesses" you, raps your name, or asks you to sign a petition — and the instant you have accepted it, the tone flips and a payment is demanded. The "gift" was never a gift. It was a contract you did not know you signed, designed to weaponize your politeness.

You will see the costumed version everywhere tourists gather for photos: the **Times Square Character Photo Shake-Down** in New York, the **Costumed Character Photo Shakedown** on the Las Vegas Strip near Caesars and the LINQ, the **Hollywood Walk of Fame Costume Character Photo Scam**, and the **Disneyland 'Parrot Scammer' & Downtown Disney Photo Hustle** in Anaheim — a free pose, then a hand out and a hovering "tip." You will see the object-in-hand version as the **CD Hustle** in New York and the **Free CD / Mixtape Hustle** in Las Vegas and Los Angeles; the bracelet and bead versions in San Francisco's **Fake Buddhist Monk Bracelet Hustle**, Seattle's and Savannah's "monk" bracelet-donation scams, Chicago's and Washington's **Friendship Bracelet and CD** force, Venice Beach's bracelet vendors, New Orleans's **Bead and Bracelet Force**, and Charleston's **Palmetto Rose** hustle; the flower version in Miami's **Rose / Flower Seller Guilt Trip Scam**. You will see it as a "blessing" in Los Angeles's **'Psychic Reading' or Spiritual Blessing Pressure**, and as a friendly word in New Orleans's **Freestyle Rap Welcome** and **Name-Spelling Bet**. The petition table runs the same play with a signature: San Francisco's **Fake Charity Clipboard Solicitation**, Chicago's **Fake Charity and Petition Hustle**, and Nashville's **Parking Lot Fake Fundraiser Drain**.

The pattern breaks on a single rule: keep your hands in your pockets and your wallet closed. Do not accept anything anyone hands you on the street — not a CD, not a bracelet, not a rose, not a toy for your kid. If something is already in your hand or on your wrist, set it down or hold it back out and say "no, thank you" while you keep walking; you owe nothing for an item you never asked for. Decide in advance which causes you support and give to them online, never to a clipboard. The warmth is the wind-up; the demand is the pitch.

## Pattern 5: The Curbside Impostor

The fifth pattern meets you at the worst possible moment — jet-lagged, bag in hand, at the airport curb or outside the arena after the show — and offers a ride. The driver is friendly, the car looks fine, and the price is never run on a meter or an app. By the time the doors close, you are negotiating from inside a stranger's vehicle, and the fare, the route, and the "fees" are all his to invent.

You will see this at nearly every airport in this book: the **Fake Taxi and Rideshare at O'Hare** in Chicago, the **Fake Rideshare and Airport Shuttle** at Orlando's MCO, the **SeaTac Airport Fake Uber & A1 Taxi Overcharge** in Seattle, the **Sky Harbor (PHX) Rideshare & Taxi Scams** in Phoenix, the **SAV Airport Taxi Overcharge** in Savannah, the **CHS Airport Rideshare & Taxi Overcharge** in Charleston, the **SAT Airport Rideshare, Taxi & 'Airport Parking' Tow Scam** in San Antonio, and Miami's **Taxi Luggage Abandonment and Fare Inflation**, where a driver holds your suitcase hostage until the number climbs. You will see it after closing time in Nashville's **Fake Rideshare Driver Pickup** on Lower Broadway and at the airport. And you will see the same "ride" pitch in human-powered and stretched-out forms: the **Pedicab Price Trap** in New York, the **Unlicensed Pedicab Price Gouging** in Washington, the pedicab meter games in Key West and the overcharges in Boston, and the Las Vegas **Overpriced Limousine / Party Bus Bait-and-Switch** at the airport and Strip pickup zones.

The pattern breaks before you ever reach the curb. Decide your ride home before you land: a rideshare summoned in the app at the airport's designated pickup zone, or the official taxi line where the rate is metered or posted. Ignore anyone who approaches you offering a ride, a "shuttle," or a "better price" — real drivers do not solicit walking passengers in the arrivals hall. Confirm the license plate, make, and driver name against your app before the doors close, and never let your luggage into a trunk until the fare is settled and on a meter or a screen. For pedicabs, agree the total in writing and confirm whether the quoted number is per person or per block before you sit down — or simply walk.

## Pattern 6: The Vanishing Inclusions

The sixth pattern is the bill that grows after you have committed. The room rate that did not mention the "resort fee." The menu with no prices, or different prices than the board outside. The bar tab padded with drinks you did not order. The rental car returned with a "damage" claim. The gratuity already added, and then a tip line added on top. Each piece looks small and semi-plausible, and each one lands after the moment you could have walked away.

You will see the lodging version as Las Vegas's **Resort Fee / Hidden Hotel Charges**, the **Anaheim Disney-Area Hotel Resort Fee**, and the **Scottsdale & Desert Resort Dining Gratuity Surprise** in Phoenix, plus the off-platform booking fraud that hides fees in San Antonio's **'Alamo Homes' Airbnb Booking Fraud** and the phantom rentals across Boston, Savannah, Orlando, and Key West. You will see the table version as Miami's **Ocean Drive Restaurant Menu Bait-and-Switch**, New York's **Restaurant Menu Bait-and-Switch** in Little Italy, the **Riverwalk Restaurant Overcharge & 'Scenic' Menu Markup** in San Antonio, **Faneuil Hall and Quincy Market Overcharging** in Boston, Charleston's **Deceptive Menu** and its **Tip-on-Tax & Auto-Gratuity** overcharge, and the **Scenic Menu Markup** in Charleston's seafood venues. You will see the bar version as Nashville's **Broadway Bar Tab Inflation**, New Orleans's **Bourbon Street Shot Girls**, Key West's **Duval Street Bar Overpouring and Tab Padding**, and Miami's **VIP Promoter** minimum. And you will see the rental-counter version as the **'Damage Claim' & Alamo Upsell Scam** in San Antonio and the **Rental Car 'Damage' & Insurance Upsell** in Phoenix.

The pattern breaks before any money is committed. Read the full nightly price — resort fee, parking, "destination fee," and all — on the hotel's own page before booking, and get the all-in total in writing. Refuse any restaurant with no prices on the menu, and check the bill line by line against what you ordered and against the auto-gratuity already listed before you add a tip. Order and close your own bar tab; never run an open tab you cannot see. And photograph every rental car inside and out at pickup and return, declining "insurance" the counter pressures on you and disputing any surprise "damage" with your card issuer. Anything not on the written confirmation is a negotiation, not an obligation.

## Pattern 7: The Counterfeit Ticket

The seventh pattern sells you entry to something — a theme park, a ballgame, a concert, a tour, a ferry, a national monument — at a price that is just low enough to feel like a deal and just high enough to hurt. The ticket is a screenshot, a duplicate barcode, or a paper that scans red at the gate. By the time you discover it, the seller is gone and the real box office wants full price. The discount was the bait; the worthless ticket is the catch.

You will see it at the gates of the parks as Orlando's **Fake Discount Theme Park Tickets** on I-Drive and Route 192 and the Disney-area **Ticket OfferUp / Craigslist Resale Scam** in Anaheim. You will see it outside the stadiums and venues as Boston's **Fenway Park Ticket Scalping**, Nashville's **Counterfeit Concert Ticket Hustle** outside the Ryman and Bridgestone Arena, and Chicago's **Navy Pier and Event Ticket Scam** around Wrigley and the United Center. You will see it at the water as New York's **Fake Statue of Liberty Tickets** near the Staten Island Ferry terminal and Key West's **Sunset Sail Bait and Switch** at the Mallory Square kiosks. You will see it dressed up as a discount on the islands in Honolulu's **Fake Activity Discount and Pearl Harbor Ticket Scam** along Kalakaua and Kuhio Avenues. And you will see the tour-package version in Phoenix's **Grand Canyon Day-Trip Tour Package Overcharge** and San Antonio's **'Texas Winos' Wine Tour Scam**.

The pattern breaks the same way every time: buy from the source. Purchase theme-park, concert, sports, ferry, and monument tickets only from the official box office, the venue's own website, or its authorized resale partner — never from a sidewalk seller, a hotel-lobby "broker," a kiosk on the tourist strip, or a stranger on Craigslist, OfferUp, or Facebook Marketplace. Remember that Pearl Harbor's USS Arizona Memorial tickets are free through the official `recreation.gov` reservation and that a "discount" on them is a tell, not a deal. Book day tours and sunset sails through the operator directly or a platform that refunds fraud, and treat any pressure to pay a stranger by cash or app as proof the ticket is not real.
