Memphis runs a moderate scam risk that leans less on pickpockets and more on paperwork, parking, and street hustle. Of the six recurring schemes documented here, five rate moderate and all are common, splitting cleanly between tourist-trap setups (fake tickets, Graceland upsells, downtown solicitation) and money-grab plays around parking and suburban retail. The unifying thread is the official-looking detail that isn't official: a mailed citation, a windshield slip, a reflective vest, a check that looks real until it bounces two weeks later.

Most of it concentrates downtown around Beale Street, where the Beale Street Parking Scam plants unauthorized "attendants" in vests demanding $10-$20 cash on residential streets that have no attended parking at all, and the Downtown Panhandling and Gold Chain Hustle works the sidewalks from Second to Third — a stranger "finds" a gold-plated chain (zinc alloy, under $5) and sells it as real, sometimes pairing it with a "you took my wallet" accusation. The second hot zone is the mail and the web: the Red-Light Camera and Fake Traffic Ticket Mail Scam hits out-of-state drivers weeks after they leave, demanding $150-$300 with "late fees" when a genuine Memphis red-light ticket is $50. Out at the edges, MEM airport baggage claim draws flat-rate "$50 downtown" touts (the metered fare is $32-$42), and Wolfchase Galleria sees a "cash this check for me" con aimed at older shoppers.

For a first-timer, three defaults cover most of it. Verify any ticket — mailed or windshield — at memphistn.gov before paying a cent; the URL on the notice is the scam. Take Uber or Lyft from the MEM Economy Parking rideshare zone with the fare on your screen, and refuse anyone soliciting at the curb. And on Beale, ignore the vests and the chain, say a flat "no thank you," and keep walking.
