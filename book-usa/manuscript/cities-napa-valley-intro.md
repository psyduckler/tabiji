Napa Valley is not a pickpocket town. Across five documented scams here, almost none involve a stranger lifting your wallet — they involve your own credit card being talked into something larger than you agreed to. The risk is overwhelmingly financial and overwhelmingly soft: bait-and-switch tour bookings, "private driver" rides dressed up as wine tours, and recurring wine-club shipments that quietly auto-charge you for a year. The valley runs on legitimately high prices — $30 to $75 tasting fees, $149 to $249 shared tours, $450 to $800 day drivers — and scammers thrive in that fog, because when everything is expensive a $400 "behind-the-scenes" upsell or a $1,500 "luxury driver" site doesn't immediately look wrong.

The trouble clusters in three places. At the front end, on booking sites and hotel concierge desks, the Wine Tour Operator Bait-and-Switch works through Groupon, Facebook Marketplace, and shell outfits like "Napa Wine Tour Express" that take payment and vanish, while the Limo / Private Driver "Wine Tour Included" Upsell pushes unverifiable $1,500-plus sites and shared vans sold as private cars. Inside the tasting rooms of Oakville, Rutherford, and St. Helena, the Tasting Room "Reserve Tasting" Upsell and the Wine Club Recurring Shipment Trap turn a "complimentary members' pour" into a quarterly $150 to $600 commitment. And on the roads — especially return rides from wineries after 6pm — the Uber / Lyft Availability & Taxi Overcharge problem means surge quotes over $100 and SFO shuttle touts asking $250 per person.

For a first-timer, three defaults cover most of it. Book tours and drivers direct with named, verified operators — Platypus, Beau, Magnum, Napa Valley Limo Tours — never through Groupon or a marketplace listing. Never sign a wine club at the counter; evaluate it from home, where the same bottles sit on wine.com at retail. And don't trust a winery Uber to materialize at closing — arrange your return ride before you arrive, and pay everything by credit card so a disputed charge is a phone call, not a loss.
