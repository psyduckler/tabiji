Phoenix doesn't run the pickpocket-and-distraction playbook of a dense old-world capital. The six documented scams here are overwhelmingly about money moving out of your accounts: payment-app theft, inflated tour packages, high-pressure sales pitches, and rental-car fine print. Four of the six touch Scottsdale, and the two highest-severity ones — the Old Town Scottsdale "Skin" & Venmo-PIN Scam and the Phoenix Rental Car "Damage" & Insurance Upsell Scam — share a single weakness, which is a tourist who trusts a stranger or a counter agent with a phone, a signature, or a credit card before reading what's actually happening.

The hot zones are easy to map. Old Town Scottsdale — the pedestrian bar strip near Marshall Way and the Waterfront — is the center of gravity: it's where the "skin" hustlers ask to borrow your unlocked phone and quietly Venmo themselves $500 to $3,000, where golf-cart drivers offer "free rides" that turn into a Zelle shakedown, and where the Timeshare Presentation Hustle dangles a free round of golf or spa voucher in exchange for a "90-minute" pitch that runs three hours toward a $15,000–$50,000 sale. The second cluster is Phoenix Sky Harbor (PHX): drivers soliciting at baggage claim with a "flat $60 to downtown," limo touts quoting $100-plus, and the Rental Car Center's zero-excess insurance pressure and weeks-later "damage" claims. The third isn't a place but a pipeline — hotel concierges and tour-desk booths marking up Grand Canyon Day-Trip packages 30–80% over the $150–$250 you'd pay booking Viator or GetYourGuide direct.

First-timer defaults that neutralize most of this: never hand an unlocked phone to a stranger, and put a PIN on Venmo, Zelle, and Cash App before you land. Skip the baggage-claim hustle entirely — take the free PHX SkyTrain to the 44th Street/Washington light-rail station, then the $2 Valley Metro train downtown, or use the official Uber/Lyft curb and screenshot the fare first. And at every rental counter and resort restaurant, film a walk-around of the car before you drive off, and check the bill for an existing "gratuity" or "service charge" line before you tip again.
