Honolulu's scam problem is not pickpockets and muggers — it is the soft shakedown, the upsell, and the manufactured fee. All six of the schemes documented here fall under one umbrella: the tourist trap. Nobody is going to mug you on Kalakaua Avenue. Instead they will offer you something free, sell you something fake, or bill you for damage you never caused. The risk is driven by the sheer density of out-of-state visitors funneled into a few square blocks of Waikiki, a resort economy that profits from captive spending, and an unfamiliar landscape — Hawaii state permits, government-run memorials, residential parking zones — that scammers exploit because you don't know the rules.

The hot zone is Kalakaua Avenue and the Waikiki beachfront. This is where the Waikiki Timeshare Presentation Ambush lives: someone offers complimentary luau tickets or a sunset catamaran cruise, and the "90-minute" pitch swallows four hours of your day. It is also where the Fake Activity Discount and Pearl Harbor Ticket Scam runs — vendors hawking $40 tickets to the USS Arizona Memorial, which is free, or $80 catamaran tickets no real operator will honor. Down on the sand, near the Duke Kahanamoku statue and Kuhio Beach, the Waikiki Beach Equipment Damage Fee Shakedown turns a returned surfboard into a $200 "scratch" you can't disprove. Two more traps wait outside the strip: the $35-to-$50-a-night Waikiki Parking Trap (plus tow trucks in resident-only zones), and at HNL airport, the Rental Car Return Terminal Fee Scam, where a "wrong terminal" charge can hit your card weeks later for over $900.

For a first-timer, three defaults cover most of it. Nothing free on the street is free — book luaus and tours directly through the operator or your hotel concierge, and ask any vendor for their Hawaii state business permit; scammers walk away. Skip the rental car in Waikiki and use the bus, trolley, or Uber, since parking alone can run $350 a week. And photograph everything you rent or return — board, car, drop-off location — because in every one of these scams, the absence of a timestamped record is exactly what the con depends on.
