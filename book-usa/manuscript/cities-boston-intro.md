Boston runs lighter than the big con-artist capitals: seven documented tourist scams, and every one of them falls into the same family, the tourist trap. There's no organized street-hustle scene here. What you're up against instead is a city that monetizes geography, where standing too close to a famous landmark is the actual product being sold. The damage scales from a $60 pedicab squabble to a four-figure vacation-rental wire transfer, but the pattern is consistent: a real venue, a real price, and a fake middleman who has inserted himself between you and it.

The trouble clusters in three predictable zones. Around Fenway, the corridor along Yawkey Way, Lansdowne Street, and Kenmore Square fills two hours before first pitch with Fenway Park Ticket Scalping — cash-only sellers hawking a single Red Sox barcode they've screenshotted and resold to four people, where only the first scan turns the gate green. The Faneuil Hall and Quincy Market stretch at the head of the Freedom Trail is its own captive economy: Faneuil Hall and Quincy Market Overcharging buries 20% "service charges" in 6-point menu footnotes, the same street-performer crowds host the twelve-second "spill and clean" of Pickpocketing in Crowded Tourist Areas, and Boston Common's edge breeds Fake Discount Tour and Attraction Ticket Sales — lanyard-wearing men selling half-price whale-watch vouchers that have no record at the dock. Online and on event nights, the highest-stakes hits land: the Vacation Rental Scam, wiring "deposits" off-platform during Marathon and Red Sox weekends; the Fake Parking Ticket Text Scam, phishing card data by SMS; and pedicabs outside TD Garden turning "just a few bucks" into $60.

First-timer defaults are simple. Buy every ticket — game, tour, attraction, transit — from the official site, box office, or MBTA machine, never from a hand on the street. The city never texts parking notices, so pay only through the ParkBoston app or the meter slot, and never scan a QR sticker. Book lodging only inside Airbnb, Vrbo, or Booking.com, and the moment a "host" mentions wiring money to dodge platform fees, walk. And whether it's a pedicab or a restaurant, get the full price in dollars before you sit down.
