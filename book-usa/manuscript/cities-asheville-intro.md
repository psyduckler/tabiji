Asheville runs a low-to-moderate scam risk built on two engines: the crush of foliage-and-Biltmore tourism, and the long tail of Hurricane Helene recovery still grinding through 2025-2026. Five documented scams cluster tightly around money and lodging, namely parking traps, post-flood fraud, ticket resale, and short-term-rental cons, and almost none of them involve pickpockets or street violence. The danger here is paperwork: a windshield notice, a "FEMA" phone call, a too-good cabin listing. Get those right and Asheville is one of the gentler stops in this book.

The trouble concentrates downtown and around the Biltmore. On the streets around Pack Square, Biltmore Avenue, and the Grove Arcade, the Asheville Downtown Parking Scam is the one that snags most visitors: handwritten "$10 all-day" lots run by outfits like Preferred Parking Service LLC tack on $50-$200 "overtime fees" and aggressive tows, while tourists who park in residential Montford or West Asheville collect $75+ tickets even where no restriction is posted. Watch too for fake parking-ticket URLs slapped on windshields. Out at the estate, the Biltmore Ticket Resale & "Free Biltmore Tour" Scam marks the $77-$109 house ticket up to $140 through third parties, or dangles a "free tour" that is really a timeshare pitch. Closer to the recovery zones, the Hurricane Helene Post-Flood Scam and the Asheville STR & Airbnb Post-Helene Booking Fraud feed off the disaster, with phony FEMA "fees," exploitative "recovery tours," and cloned cabin listings demanding Zelle. On the sidewalks of Lexington Avenue and Pritchard Park, the low-grade "Cat Guy" panhandling is mostly a nuisance.

A few defaults cover the spread. Park only in City of Asheville garages (Civic Center $8, Aloft $10, Wall Street and Rankin Ave $12) or pay meters via ParkMobile, and settle any real ticket only at ashevillenc.gov, never a windshield link. Buy Biltmore tickets only at biltmore.com. Book lodging only through Airbnb, VRBO, or Booking.com's own payment system, never wire or Venmo. And remember the iron rule of Helene: FEMA never charges a fee, and its inspections are always free.
