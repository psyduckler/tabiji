Fort Lauderdale's scam landscape is shaped by two facts of local geography: it is a cruise-and-budget-airline gateway, and it is a beach town built for visitors with retirement money. Of the six recurring scams documented here, half are transport plays funneled through Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International (FLL) and Port Everglades, and the other half are the soft-fraud staples of a tourist economy: a timeshare hustle, aggressive beach parking, and hotel surcharges. None of it is violent. Almost all of it is a quiet tax on travelers who don't know the local rate, and the two that bite hardest, the cruise taxi overcharge and the ACA Group "Won a Vacation" timeshare presentation, both target older cruise passengers specifically.

The clustering is tight. The airport and the cruise port are the first danger zone: at FLL baggage claim and the Port Everglades terminal queue, drivers solicit "flat $40–$60" fares for a three-mile run that meters at $18, while "limo" touts quote $100+ for a ten-minute trip, and "Skysquad" and "VIP Assistance" booths at the Spirit check-in counter sell $45–$150 "concierge" service that amounts to pushing a luggage cart. The beach is the second zone: along the A1A strip and Las Olas Boulevard you'll find handwritten "$10 all-day" parking lots that tack on exit fees, fake payment-URL tickets on windshields, and "resort fees" of $25–$55 a night that never appeared in your booking. Las Olas and the beach resort lobbies are also where the timeshare "free vacation" promoters work.

For a first-timer, three defaults cover most of it. Skip every curb solicitor and walk to the official taxi queue or the designated Uber/Lyft zone, then confirm the fare on screen before the car moves. For the beach, park only in city garages like the Aquatic Complex or Beach Place ($2–$3 per hour) and pay any ticket exclusively at ftlauderdale.gov, never a URL on your windshield. And treat any "you won a free vacation" call or mailer as what it is: a three-to-six-hour sales ambush, not a prize.
