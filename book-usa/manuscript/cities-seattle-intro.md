Seattle is not a pickpocket city; it is a persuasion city. The four scams that recur here are all variations on the same tourist-trap theme: someone friendly, official-looking, or sympathetic intercepts you in a crowd and steers you toward your own wallet. The danger is not violence but momentum: a stranger who already knows your name, a cause you can't argue with, a card reader held out before you've decided anything. Two of the four rate as high-severity, and both work the same way; they exploit the half-second of politeness before you think to ask a question.

The trouble clusters in three places. The first is SeaTac Airport, where the congested Level 3 garage breeds the Fake Uber and A1 Taxi Overcharge: impostors read your assigned car's color and model off the app board, call it out, and walk you to an unmarked vehicle, while the taxi queue pushes "flat rate" quotes in place of the meter. The second is the downtown core around Lumen Field and Pike Place Market. On Seahawks and Sounders game days, the Kids' Charity Donation and Tap-to-Pay Scam puts a handheld card reader in your face for a high school team that doesn't exist; a block away on First Avenue and the Market cobblestones, the Monk Bracelet Donation Scam slips a trinket onto your wrist, then demands twenty to a hundred dollars for "blessings." The third zone is mundane and easy to forget: PCC and Trader Joe's parking lots, mall corners, and I-5 and I-90 exit medians, where the Mom-and-Kid Panhandling pattern works car windows during school hours.

Three defaults cover most of it. At SeaTac, ignore anyone who greets you by name and verify the plate, make, and driver in the app before any door opens, or just take Link Light Rail downtown for three dollars. On any Seattle sidewalk, never tap a handheld reader and never accept an object pressed into your hand; keep your hands in your pockets and keep walking. And never roll down your car window for a parking-lot story.
