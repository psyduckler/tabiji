Branson runs a different kind of risk than a big city: nobody is going to pick your pocket, but there is a coordinated machine built to separate older visitors from serious money. All five documented scams here are non-violent and financial, and the gravity is concentrated in two of them. The headline threat is the timeshare apparatus. The "$99 Branson Vacation Package" arrives by mail or email promising a hotel and show tickets, then traps you in a "90-minute orientation" that is really a three-to-five-hour pressure session pushing $10,000 to $40,000 in "vacation ownership." The Bluegreen and Capital Vacations Timeshare Hustle works the same way, dangling free Silver Dollar City tickets or a free lake cruise to get you in the room. If you take only one thing from this chapter: never respond to a "$99 Branson vacation" offer, and if you somehow end up in a presentation, walk out rather than sign.

The pressure clusters in a few predictable zones. Highway 76 — the Strip — is thick with promoter booths and "ticket" kiosks running the Show Ticket Resale and "Free Tickets" Marketing Scam, reselling seats at a $15 to $30 markup and pitching "free tickets" that lead straight back to a timeshare table. The Strip and Branson Landing are also where the Restaurant and Tourist-Trap Overcharge lives, with "Ozark buffet" spots charging $28 to $42 for grade-D food. Out at Table Rock Lake, Indian Point, and Lake Taneycomo, the Cabin Off-Platform Booking Fraud targets families with fake "25% off" listings demanding Zelle or wire transfer.

For a first-timer, three defaults keep you clean. Book every show direct — Dolly Parton's Stampede, Moon River Theatre, Silver Dollar City — never through a Strip booth or mailer. Book cabins only through Airbnb, VRBO, or Booking.com platform payment, and refuse any Zelle-only "last-minute deal." And if you ever sign a timeshare contract, use Missouri's five-day right of rescission immediately by certified mail through the state Attorney General (ago.mo.gov), not a paid "exit" company.
