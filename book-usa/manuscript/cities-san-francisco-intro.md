San Francisco's six documented scams all share one label — tourist trap — but they split into two very different threats: property crime that costs you real money, and a cluster of low-stakes street hustles that mostly cost you a few minutes and a little dignity. The headline danger is the rental car smash-and-grab, the only entry rated high severity here, and it is best understood not as bad luck but as the city's most reliable tourist tax. Thieves read out-of-state plates, assume the trunk holds luggage and cameras, and have the window broken and the bags gone in under sixty seconds. Everything else — cable car pickpocket crews, ATM panhandling distraction, fake monks, clipboard charities, and the sourdough markup — rides on the same fuel: dense, distracted, camera-out crowds in a handful of very predictable spots.

Those spots are easy to name because the same addresses keep recurring. Fisherman's Wharf and Pier 39 are the epicenter, stacking the smash-and-grab in the waterfront lots, the fake Buddhist monk bracelet hustle pressing laminated cards into your hands, and the overpriced sourdough and clam chowder tourist traps charging $35 for a bread bowl a local pays $12 for two blocks inland. Union Square and Market Street are where the fake charity clipboard solicitation steps into your path, while the Powell Street cable car turnaround and the F-Line streetcar are the pickpockets' home turf. Keep your guard up around the Tenderloin, Civic Center, and BART entrances, where aggressive panhandling near ATMs can mask distraction theft.

Two defaults handle most of this. First, do not rent a car — get around by Uber, Waymo, or transit, and if you must drive, park inside a paid garage and leave the vehicle visibly empty, glove box included. Second, treat anything pressed into your hands on the street as a hook: keep your hands in your pockets, keep walking, and never hand a credit card to a clipboard or a "monk." On the cable car, wear valuables in a front pocket or a body-side crossbody bag, and pull cash only from ATMs inside banks or hotels.
