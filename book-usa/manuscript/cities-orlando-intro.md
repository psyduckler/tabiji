Orlando's scam problem is almost entirely a tourist-trap economy: six well-documented schemes here, every one of them built to skim money from the 70-million-a-year crowd headed for Disney, Universal, and SeaWorld. There's no pickpocket or violent-crime angle to worry about; the danger is your wallet, and it gets opened in three predictable ways: counterfeit access to the parks, high-pressure pitches for "free" perks, and bookings that evaporate. Three of the six are high-severity, and they're the ones that cost the most: Fake Discount Theme Park Tickets (partially used or AI-counterfeited passes sold 30-50% below gate price), the Timeshare Presentation Trap (a "90-minute" session that runs three to four hours of aggressive closers), and the Vacation Rental Phantom Listing, where families fly cross-country to a Kissimmee address that doesn't exist or belongs to someone else.

The hot zone is the tourist spine. International Drive (I-Drive) and Route 192 through Kissimmee are thick with ticket kiosks, timeshare booths, and hotel-lobby solicitors running the ticket and presentation cons. The theme-park gates and their parking lots are their own front: Parking Lot Grifters and Ticket Scalpers offer to "watch your car" for $10-20, hawk "leftover" passes, or sell empty Disney gift cards at a fake 20% discount. Inside resort hotels along I-Drive and Lake Buena Vista, the Pizza Flyer Credit Card Phishing scam slides realistic takeout menus under your door to harvest card numbers, now paired with smishing texts posing as "Disney Parking." And at MCO airport's baggage claim, Fake Rideshare and Airport Shuttle drivers flash printed Uber logos and charge two to three times the app rate.

For a first-timer, the safe defaults are simple. Buy park tickets only from the official sites or a reseller showing the "Authorized Ticket Seller" logo. Book lodging only through Airbnb, Vrbo, or Booking.com on-platform, never via Venmo or Zelle. And at the airport, ignore anyone who approaches you, ride only from MCO's Level 2 rideshare zones, and verify the plate, make, and driver name in the app before you get in.
