Denver's seven documented scams all share one thing: they hunt the visitor who looks like a tourist. There's no elaborate con artistry here, just predictable patterns that key off a rental sticker, an out-of-state plate, a phone left face-up on a patio table, or a jet-lagged traveler who'll pay cash rather than argue at 11 PM. The risk concentrates in two arenas — the downtown core for petty theft and street hustles, and the airport-plus-mountains circuit for vehicle and rideshare predation — and the dollar stakes climb fast: a $20 gas-can sob story downtown, a $150 fake Uber from the airport, a $3,000 phantom "hail damage" bill weeks after you returned a clean rental.

Downtown, the 16th Street Mall is the epicenter. Distraction-and-grab teams work the patios from Stout to California — one person engages while an accomplice lifts your phone in the four-second window your attention is split — and the Union Station blocks log more crime per square mile than anywhere in the city. The same corridor draws sob-story panhandlers around Civic Center Station and east down Colfax (Denver's highest-crime stretch after dark), plus cannabis dispensary tourist traps that mark edibles up two to three times over the Broadway shops and invent a "tourist processing fee" with no basis in Colorado law. Out at the edges, Denver International is the launch point for two of the nastiest plays: rideshare impersonators who read your first name off your phone screen ("Sarah? Your Uber is here"), and off-airport rental lots like Routes that rush your inspection and bill you for damage after a spotless return. The break-in crews follow the rentals to I-70 trailhead lots and DEN long-term parking, smashing a window and clearing visible bags in under a minute.

First-timer defaults that neutralize most of this: never leave a phone, wallet, or bag on a patio table — keep it on your lap or clipped to your chair. At the airport, confirm the plate, make, and model in the app and make the driver say your name first; real Uber and Lyft drivers never take cash. Rent only from major brands at the main DEN Rental Car Center and photograph every panel before you drive off. And leave nothing visible in the car at any trailhead — strip the rental sticker and airport receipt off the dash before you park.
