Frequently Asked Questions

What kind of food can I expect in Casablanca's food halls?

Casablanca's food halls offer a wide spectrum, from traditional Moroccan street food like tagines, grilled seafood, and pastries at places like Marche Central and Quartier Habous, to international fast-casual options like sushi, burgers, and pizza found in mall food courts like Anfaplace and Morocco Mall. Gourmet markets such as Les Halles de L'Olivier also provide artisanal products and refined European-inspired dishes.

Are food halls in Casablanca suitable for families with children?

Absolutely! Many food halls, especially those in shopping malls like Anfaplace and Morocco Mall, are very family-friendly, offering diverse international menus that cater to various tastes. Even traditional markets like Marche Central can be an exciting sensory experience for children, though they might prefer simpler grilled options.

What is the typical price range for food at these venues?

The price range varies significantly. Budget-friendly options like Quartier Habous or local street food stalls can be as low as 30–100 MAD ($3–$10) per person. Mid-range options in mall food courts or casual eateries might be 70–200 MAD ($7–$20). Gourmet markets and more upscale dining promenades like Les Halles de L'Olivier or Marina Casablanca can range from 150–350 MAD ($15–$35) per person.

Do I need to make reservations for any of these food halls?

Generally, no. Most food halls and markets operate on a walk-in basis with self-service or first-come, first-served seating. For the dining promenades like Marina Casablanca, some of the more upscale standalone restaurants might recommend reservations, especially on weekends or for larger groups, but the casual eateries typically don't require them.

What are the typical opening hours for food halls in Casablanca?

Traditional markets like Marche Central usually open early in the morning (around 7–8 AM) and close in the late afternoon (around 5–6 PM), though some food stalls might stay open slightly later. Mall food courts typically follow mall hours, often from 10 AM to 10 PM. Gourmet markets and dining promenades might have varied hours, with most operating from late morning until late evening, some even past midnight.

Is vegetarian or vegan food readily available in Casablanca's food halls?

Vegetarian options are generally available, with many Moroccan dishes being naturally vegetable-based (e.g., vegetable tagines, lentil soup, salads, couscous). Vegan options can be more challenging but are increasingly found, especially in modern food courts or international eateries. Always ask if dishes are prepared with animal fats or broths to be sure.

Planning your Casablanca food hall tour

The ideal Casablanca food hall crawl takes you through three distinct experiences in one day.

Start with a morning visit to Marche Central and the adjacent Fish and Flower Market — arrive by 9 AM for the freshest seafood and most vibrant atmosphere. Have the vendors grill your catch on the spot, pair it with harira soup, and you have a legendary breakfast for under 100 MAD.

At lunch, head to Quartier Habous in the New Medina for traditional pastries and mint tea at Patisserie Bennis (since 1930), then explore the food alleys for a kefta sandwich. In the afternoon, explore the Maarif food hub around the Twin Center for patisseries and casual cafes.

For dinner, choose your vibe: the Marina promenade for upscale waterfront dining with Hassan II Mosque views, or La Corniche for casual beachside grills at sunset. On weekends, the Marche Solidaire in Dar Bouazza offers a unique artisanal food experience.

Budget roughly 200–400 MAD ($20–$40 USD) for a full day of food hall sampling across multiple spots, or 100–150 MAD ($10–$15) if sticking to traditional markets and budget venues.
