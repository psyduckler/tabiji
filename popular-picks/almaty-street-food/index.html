Quick answer

Almaty is Central Asia's street food capital, where Kazakh, Uyghur, Russian, and Dungan cuisines collide in bazaars and casual eateries. From sizzling shashlik to hand-pulled laghman noodles and flaky samsa pastries, this guide covers 10 of the best spots, with prices ranging from ₸300 to ₸3000 per dish.

Almaty isn't just Kazakhstan's largest city — it's the culinary crossroads of Central Asia. The former capital sits at the intersection of Kazakh nomadic traditions, Silk Road trading routes, and Soviet-era influences, creating a street food scene unlike anywhere else.

The city's signature street foods reflect this heritage: samsa (flaky meat pastries baked in clay tandoor ovens), shashlik (marinated meat skewers grilled over charcoal), laghman (hand-pulled noodles in savory broth brought by Uyghur immigrants), and manty (steamed dumplings with spiced lamb). Add in baursak (puffy fried dough), kurt (portable dried cheese balls), and kumis (fermented mare's milk), and you have a cuisine built for nomads and travelers.

The Green Bazaar, operating since 1875, remains the beating heart of Almaty's food culture — a two-story labyrinth of spices, dried fruits, fresh meats, and food stalls serving everything from simple samsa to elaborate plov feasts. Beyond the bazaar, dedicated shashlik houses, Dungan noodle shops, and modern Kazakh restaurants offer deeper dives into specific cuisines.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most popular street food in Almaty?

Samsa is arguably the most popular street food in Almaty — savory pastries filled with meat, onions, and spices, baked in a traditional tandoor oven. You'll find samsa stands on nearly every corner, and they're cheap, satisfying, and perfect for eating on the go. Other must-try street foods include shashlik (grilled meat skewers), laghman (hand-pulled noodles), and manty (steamed dumplings).

Where is the best place to try street food in Almaty?

The Green Bazaar (Zelyony Bazaar) is the ultimate destination for street food in Almaty. This historic market dating back to 1875 has hundreds of food stalls offering samsa, laghman, shashlik, plov, and traditional Kazakh snacks like kurt (dried cheese balls) and baursak (fried dough). The food stalls on the market's perimeter serve hot meals all day.

How much does street food cost in Almaty?

Street food in Almaty is very affordable. Expect to pay ₸300-500 ($0.65-1.10) for samsa, ₸800-1500 ($1.75-3.30) for a bowl of laghman, ₸1000-2000 ($2.20-4.40) for shashlik skewers, and ₸500-1000 ($1.10-2.20) for manty. A full street food meal typically costs ₸1500-3000 ($3.30-6.60).

What is laghman and where can I find the best in Almaty?

Laghman is a Central Asian noodle dish featuring hand-pulled noodles in a savory broth with meat and vegetables. The dish was brought to Almaty by the Uyghur community and is a symbol of the city's multicultural heritage. For the best laghman, try Dungan House for authentic Uyghur-style preparation, or the food stalls at Green Bazaar for a quick, affordable bowl.

Is street food safe to eat in Almaty?

Yes, street food in Almaty is generally safe. Stick to busy stalls with high turnover, which ensures fresh food. The Green Bazaar and established restaurants like Shashlik Dvor are reliable choices. Avoid pre-cooked food that's been sitting out, and watch for freshly grilled or cooked-to-order items. Samsa fresh from the tandoor and shashlik straight off the grill are always safe bets.

What is beshbarmak and can I find it as street food?

Beshbarmak is Kazakhstan's national dish — boiled meat (traditionally horse or lamb) served over flat noodles with onion sauce. While it's typically a sit-down restaurant dish due to its ceremonial nature, you can find simplified versions at food stalls in Green Bazaar. For the authentic experience, visit Qaimaq or Gakku restaurant, which specialize in traditional Kazakh cuisine.

What are the best vegetarian street food options in Almaty?

While Kazakh cuisine is meat-heavy, vegetarians can enjoy kartoshka samsa (potato samsa), non bread, baursak (fried dough), kurt (dried cheese balls), and vegetable laghman (request without meat). The Green Bazaar also has excellent dried fruits, nuts, and fresh produce. Some restaurants like Navat offer vegetable plov and manti with pumpkin filling.

When is the best time to eat street food in Almaty?

For the freshest street food, visit markets like Green Bazaar in the morning (8-11am) when vendors are restocking and cooking fresh batches. Shashlik spots are best in the evening when the grills are fired up and the atmosphere is lively. During Ramadan, many food stalls open after sunset. Summer months (June-August) offer the best outdoor dining experience.
