Casablanca is Morocco's most cosmopolitan city, and its wine scene reflects that sophistication perfectly.

From dramatic oceanfront terraces on the Corniche to intimate French bistros in Gauthier and glamorous Art Deco lounges in Anfa, the city offers a surprising depth of wine experiences. Morocco has been producing wine for centuries, and local labels like Domaine du Val d'Argan, Volubilia, and Ksar d'Atlas hold their own against European imports. Whether you are sipping a Gris de Boulaouane rosé by the Atlantic or discovering a reserve Syrah in a candlelit brasserie, Casablanca rewards those who look beyond the mint tea.

Frequently Asked Questions

What kind of wine is produced in Morocco?

Morocco has a long history of winemaking, primarily producing red wines, rosés, and some whites. Common grape varieties include Carignan, Cinsault, Grenache, Syrah, Cabernet Sauvignon, and Merlot for reds and rosés. For whites, you will often find Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, and Chenin Blanc. Moroccan wines are increasingly gaining international recognition for their quality and unique terroir.

Is it easy to find wine in Casablanca?

Yes, it is relatively easy to find wine in Casablanca, especially in restaurants, hotels, and dedicated wine shops or supermarkets in wealthier neighborhoods. While Morocco is a Muslim country, it has a significant tourism industry and a more liberal attitude towards alcohol compared to some other Muslim-majority nations. Upscale establishments and international hotels almost always serve alcohol.

What is the typical price range for a glass of wine in Casablanca?

The price for a glass of wine in Casablanca can vary significantly depending on the venue and the wine's origin. In a casual bar or restaurant, a glass of local wine might cost between 60–90 MAD ($6–9 USD). At more upscale establishments or for imported wines, prices can range from 90–150 MAD ($9–15 USD) or more per glass.

Are there any specific Moroccan wines I should try?

Absolutely! For reds, look for labels like Domaine de Sahari, Volubilia, or Ksar d'Atlas, often made with Syrah, Cabernet Sauvignon, or a blend. For rosés, Gris de Boulaouane is a popular and refreshing choice. For whites, Domaine Val d'Argan (especially their Sauvignon Blanc) and some Fes Blanc varieties are highly recommended. Do not hesitate to ask your sommelier for local recommendations.

Do wine bars in Casablanca serve food?

Most wine bars and restaurants in Casablanca that serve wine will also offer food. This can range from light bites and tapas, perfect for pairing with wine, to full-course meals. Many of the establishments listed above are primarily restaurants with excellent wine programs, ensuring you have delicious food options alongside your wine selection.

What is the general atmosphere like in Casablanca's wine bars?

The atmosphere in Casablanca's wine bars is quite diverse, reflecting the city's cosmopolitan nature. You can find everything from lively Spanish-style tapas bars with live music to chic, modern lounges, elegant French bistros, and luxurious hotel bars with stunning views. Dress codes can vary from casual smart to elegant, so it is good to check beforehand, especially for upscale venues.

Planning your Casablanca wine tour

The ideal wine bar crawl in Casablanca takes you from the Corniche to the city centre and back, sampling Morocco's best wines alongside international classics.

Start with a sunset aperitif at Le Cabestan Ocean View or Le Petit Rocher — both offer breathtaking Atlantic views that pair perfectly with a chilled Moroccan rosé or white. Move on to dinner at Bistrot Chic in Gauthier for a proper French wine-and-food pairing, or head to Le Relais de Paris for the legendary entrecôte with Bordeaux.

For a livelier evening, La Bodega delivers tapas and sangria with live music, while Le Gatsby offers Roaring Twenties glamour with premium wines. End the night at Le Boudoir for a quiet nightcap, or splurge at Vertigo at the Four Seasons for a celebratory glass of Champagne. Do not miss Rick's Café for the one-of-a-kind cinematic experience with live piano.

Budget roughly 500–1,500 MAD ($50–$150 USD) for a full evening sampling 2–3 spots, or 250–450 MAD ($25–$45) for a single stop at a budget-friendly venue like La Bodega or La Cocina.
