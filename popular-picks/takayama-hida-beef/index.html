Hida beef restaurants in Takayama offer a range of dining experiences, from street food to multi-course meals, but for the best experience, try yakiniku. Prices are generally lower than in Tokyo, and the freshness is unparalleled, making Takayama the perfect place to savor this premium wagyu.

Hida beef (飛騨牛) isn't just "Japan's other wagyu" — for many travelers who've tried it, it ranks above Kobe. The beef comes from Japanese Black (Kuroge Washu) cattle raised in the mountain air of Gifu Prefecture, with a BMS of 5 or higher to qualify for the brand. In Takayama — the cultural capital of the Hida region — you eat this beef at the source.

Prices are lower than Tokyo, freshness is higher, and the range of preparations is extraordinary: slow-grilled yakiniku over charcoal, silky sukiyaki in sweet soy broth, chef-cooked teppanyaki, street skewers for ¥800, and even a knockout Hida beef burger. This guide covers every tier.

Frequently asked questions

What is Hida beef and is it worth trying in Takayama?

Hida beef (飛騨牛) is a premium brand of Wagyu from Gifu Prefecture, Japan. It must be A4 or A5 grade with a BMS (Beef Marbling Score) of 5 or higher. Reddit travelers consistently rate it on par with or even above Kobe beef — fattier, juicier, and deeply flavorful. Trying it in Takayama, the heartland of Hida cattle, means the freshest possible product at lower prices than you'd pay in Tokyo.

What is the cheapest way to try Hida beef in Takayama?

The most affordable Hida beef experience is street food: Hidagyu no Komori sells single skewers for ¥600–¥800. Centre4 Hamburgers offers a Hida beef burger for around ¥1,200–¥1,500. For a sit-down meal, Aji no YOHEI's Hida Beef Hida Soba Set at ¥3,500 is excellent value — you get teppanyaki beef plus local soba in one course.

What's the difference between yakiniku, sukiyaki, and teppanyaki Hida beef?

Yakiniku means you grill the beef yourself over a tabletop charcoal or gas grill — more hands-on and interactive. Sukiyaki is a hot pot where thin slices cook in a sweet soy-based broth, then dipped in raw egg. Teppanyaki is chef-cooked on an iron griddle, often tableside. All three are excellent ways to enjoy Hida beef — yakiniku gives the most control over doneness, sukiyaki offers the richest flavor melding, and teppanyaki is the most theatrical.

Do I need to reserve Hida beef restaurants in advance?

For high-end spots like Hidatakayama Hidagyu Sakana and Nakabashi Wakachiya during peak season, reservations are strongly recommended. Hidagyu Maruaki is famously popular and often fully booked on weekends. Budget spots like Hidagyu no Komori (street food) and Centre4 Hamburgers don't require reservations. For mid-range restaurants, arriving right when they open (11am or 5pm) usually guarantees a seat without a reservation.

Is Hida beef the same as Kobe beef?

No — Hida beef and Kobe beef are different regional brands of Japanese Wagyu. Kobe beef comes from Tajima cattle raised in Hyogo Prefecture; Hida beef comes from Kuroge Washu (Japanese Black) cattle raised in Gifu Prefecture. Both are A4/A5 grade Wagyu, but Hida beef is often described as having a slightly higher fat content and a more robust, beefy flavor. In Takayama, you're eating it at the source, which means exceptional freshness.
