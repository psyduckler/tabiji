Quick answer

Berlin is arguably the vegan capital of the world — and the numbers back it up: over 300 fully vegan or vegetarian restaurants, a #2 global ranking on HappyCow, and an attitude toward plant-based eating that is more mainstream than niche.

The Vietnamese wave brought 1990 Vegan Living and Mom's Creation, while Italian purists can get wood-fired Neapolitan pizza at La Stella Nera.

Frequently asked questions

Is Berlin a good city for vegans?

Absolutely. Berlin is widely considered one of the best vegan cities in the world, ranking #2 on HappyCow's global list. It has over 300 fully vegan or vegetarian restaurants, plus dedicated vegan supermarkets like Veganz. You will find options ranging from Michelin-recognized fine dining to casual street food, covering every cuisine and budget.

Are vegan restaurants in Berlin expensive?

Berlin's vegan restaurants span every budget. Street food and fast-casual spots like Voner (EUR 5-12) and Brammibal's Donuts (EUR 3-6) are very affordable. Mid-range restaurants like 1990 Vegan Living and Alaska Bar run EUR 9-18 per person. Fine dining at Frea, Kopps, or Lucky Leek ranges from EUR 25-100+ for tasting menus. Compared to other European capitals, Berlin is remarkably affordable.

Do I need reservations for vegan restaurants in Berlin?

For fine dining restaurants like Frea, Kopps, and Lucky Leek, reservations are essential, especially on weekends. These places often book out weeks in advance. For casual spots like Voner, Brammibal's, Lia's Kitchen, and Vincent Vegan, you can walk in. Mid-range restaurants like 1990 Vegan Living do not take reservations, so arrive early or be prepared to wait.

What kind of vegan food can I find in Berlin?

Berlin's vegan food scene is incredibly diverse. You can expect innovative modern European tasting menus, traditional German comfort food reimagined (schnitzel, currywurst), excellent Vietnamese tapas and bowls, wood-fired Neapolitan pizza, Spanish-style tapas, gourmet burgers, doner kebabs made with seitan, and outstanding vegan donuts and pastries. The city also has dedicated vegan ice cream shops, ramen bars, and sushi restaurants.

What is the best neighborhood in Berlin for vegan food?

Friedrichshain and Kreuzberg have the highest concentration of vegan restaurants, including Voner, 1990 Vegan Living, Lia's Kitchen, and many others. Mitte is home to fine dining options like Frea and Kopps. Prenzlauer Berg has Lucky Leek and Das Forsters. Neukolln offers La Stella Nera and Alaska Bar. Virtually every Berlin neighborhood has excellent vegan options.

Are there vegan-friendly grocery stores in Berlin?

Yes, Berlin has dedicated vegan supermarkets like Veganz, as well as extensive vegan sections in conventional supermarkets (Lidl, Rewe, Edeka) and organic stores (Bio Company, Denn's Biomarkt). The Turkish markets along Maybachufer are also great for fresh produce. Many corner shops (Spatis) now carry vegan snacks and plant milks.

What are must-try vegan dishes unique to Berlin?

You must try a vegan doner kebab from Voner (seitan-based, a Berlin institution since 2006), vegan donuts from Brammibal's (Europe's first all-vegan donut shop), and vegan currywurst from any of the street food vendors. For a special experience, the zero-waste tasting menu at Frea or the seasonal courses at Lucky Leek showcase Berlin's innovative plant-based fine dining scene.

Can non-vegans enjoy these restaurants too?

Absolutely. Many of Berlin's best vegan restaurants attract a mixed crowd of vegans and non-vegans alike. Places like Kopps, Lucky Leek, and Frea regularly impress meat-eaters with their creativity and flavor. Voner's doner kebab is often described as indistinguishable from the original. The food quality at these places stands on its own, regardless of dietary preference.

Planning your Berlin vegan food tour

The ideal vegan food crawl in Berlin takes you through three distinct experiences in one day.

Start your morning at Brammibal's Donuts on Maybachufer (the original location) — grab a salted caramel donut and walk the Turkish market if it is Saturday. For lunch, head to Friedrichshain for a Vöner döner or Vietnamese tapas at 1990 Vegan Living. In the afternoon, explore Kreuzberg and stop at Lia's Kitchen for a burger or La Stella Nera for early dinner pizza.

For a special evening, book ahead at Frea (zero-waste tasting menu in Mitte), Lucky Leek (Michelin Bib Gourmand in Prenzlauer Berg), or Kopps (elegant multi-course in Mitte). End the night with vegan tapas and cocktails at Alaska Bar in Neukölln.

Budget roughly EUR 30–50 for a full day of casual vegan eating across multiple spots, or EUR 80–150 if including a fine dining dinner. Berlin's excellent U-Bahn and S-Bahn make hopping between neighborhoods effortless.
