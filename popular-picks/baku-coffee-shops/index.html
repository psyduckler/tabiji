Baku's coffee scene blends Ottoman-era Turkish coffee tradition with a booming specialty wave. From third-wave roasters near Fountains Square to atmospheric Old City cafes, this guide covers the 10 best spots for a perfect cup in Azerbaijan's capital.

Quick answer

For the best specialty coffee in Baku, head to United Coffee Beans near Fountains Square. For the best overall cafe experience blending Azerbaijani culture with coffee, Ali & Nino in the Old City is unbeatable. Prices range from AZN 3 to AZN 15 per drink.

Baku's coffee scene has exploded in recent years, transforming from a tea-dominated culture into one of the Caucasus region's most exciting cafe cities. While traditional Azerbaijani tea culture (served in the iconic pear-shaped armudu glass) remains deeply important, a new generation of baristas and roasters has put Baku firmly on the specialty coffee map.

The city's cafe scene spans a remarkable range: third-wave specialty roasters sourcing beans from Ethiopia and Colombia, Ottoman-inspired Turkish coffee houses, elegant European-style patisseries, and homegrown chains that have become part of daily Baku life. The best area for cafe-hopping is the stretch between Fountains Square and the Old City (Icherisheher), where you can walk between a half-dozen excellent spots in under 15 minutes.

What makes Baku unique is the price-to-quality ratio. A world-class specialty latte here costs AZN 7-10 (roughly $4-6), a fraction of what you would pay in London or Dubai. Combined with the stunning architecture and Caspian Sea views, a Baku coffee crawl is one of the best-value cafe experiences in the region.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the typical prices for coffee in Baku?

Coffee in Baku is very affordable. A standard espresso or Americano costs AZN 3-6 (roughly $1.80-$3.50), while specialty lattes and cappuccinos run AZN 6-10 ($3.50-$6). Turkish coffee is even cheaper at AZN 2-4. Premium spots like Entree sit at the higher end, while Coffee Mood and The Coffee Hut offer excellent value.

Do coffee shops in Baku offer free Wi-Fi?

Yes, nearly all modern coffee shops in Baku provide free Wi-Fi. The Coffee Shop Baku, CafeCity, and Coffee Mood are especially well-suited for remote work with reliable connections and outlets. Ask staff for the password, which is usually posted near the counter.

What is the best area in Baku for cafe-hopping?

The stretch between Fountains Square and the Old City (Icherisheher) has the highest concentration of quality cafes. Nizami Street (Torgovaya) is also excellent. You can walk between a half-dozen top spots in under 15 minutes. The Boulevard waterfront has scenic options like Dalga Coffee.

Do Baku cafes serve traditional Azerbaijani tea?

Absolutely. Most cafes serve both coffee and traditional Azerbaijani black tea in the armudu (pear-shaped glass), often with lemon or cherry jam (vishnya). Ali & Nino is especially known for bridging tea and coffee culture. Some places also offer samovar-style service.

Is tipping expected at Baku coffee shops?

Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory. Rounding up the bill or leaving AZN 1-2 on a coffee order is a nice gesture. At upscale spots like Entree, 10% is generous. Many modern cafes have tip jars. Some restaurants add a service charge automatically, so check the bill.

Can I pay by credit card at Baku coffee shops?

Most modern coffee shops in central Baku accept Visa and Mastercard. Chains like Coffee Mood, CafeCity, and MADO all take cards. Some smaller independent spots may be cash-only, so carry some AZN. Contactless payment is increasingly common at newer places.

What time do coffee shops open and close in Baku?

Most open between 8:00-9:00 AM and stay open until 11:00 PM or midnight. Baku has a strong evening cafe culture, so busy cafes well after 10 PM are normal, especially on weekends. Summer hours often extend later, particularly near Fountains Square.

Are there vegan-friendly options at Baku coffee shops?

Vegetarian options like pastries and salads are widely available. Vegan options are growing but limited compared to Western cities. Plant-based milks (soy, almond, oat) are available at specialty shops like United Coffee Beans and The Coffee Shop Baku. Traditional Azerbaijani cafe food tends to be meat-heavy, so specialty coffee shops are your best bet.
