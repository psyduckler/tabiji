12 Best Bakeries in Baltimore

Baltimore's culinary scene boasts an impressive array of bakeries, offering everything from traditional European pastries to innovative American treats and artisanal breads. Whether you're craving a flaky croissant, a rich cannoli, or a gourmet doughnut, Charm City has a spot for you.

Quick answer

Baltimore is quietly one of the best bakery cities on the East Coast — a place where century-old traditions meet a new wave of artisan bakers.

Frequently asked questions

What kind of bakeries can I expect to find in Baltimore?

Baltimore's bakery scene is wonderfully diverse, ranging from historic Italian pastry shops in Little Italy and sophisticated French patisseries in the Harbor East area to artisan bread makers and creative gourmet doughnut shops. You'll also find classic American bakeries, vegan and gluten-free friendly cafes, specialty cupcakeries, and charming neighborhood spots with decades of history.

Are there good options for vegan or gluten-free baked goods in Baltimore?

Yes, several bakeries and cafes in Baltimore cater to dietary restrictions. Sweet 27 in Remington is particularly well-regarded for its extensive menu of both vegan and gluten-free baked goods and savory dishes. Other places like Ovenbird Bakery often have limited vegan and gluten-free selections, so it's always good to check their daily offerings.

What is the average price range for pastries and baked goods in Baltimore?

The price range varies significantly. A single pastry or coffee from a budget-friendly or traditional bakery like Fenwick Bakery might cost $2-7. More upscale French patisseries or gourmet bakeries like Pâtisserie Poupon and Sacré Sucré typically charge $5-15 per item, especially for elaborate cakes or specialty macarons. Expect to pay around $5-10 per person for a casual snack at most bakeries.

Which neighborhood is best for finding bakeries in Baltimore?

Several neighborhoods offer excellent bakery options. Fells Point and Little Italy are prime spots for European-style bakeries like Sacré Sucré and Vaccaro's. Harbor East has Ovenbird Bakery for artisan breads. Hampden is home to both Dangerously Delicious Pies and Common Ground. Remington has Sweet 27 for the vegan and gluten-free crowd. Federal Hill features Midnite Confections Cupcakery.

Do Baltimore bakeries offer savory as well as sweet options?

Absolutely! Many bakeries offer a fantastic selection of savory options. Dangerously Delicious Pies is famous for its savory pies and quiches. Ovenbird Bakery does outstanding sandwiches on house-baked bread. Common Ground offers savory bagel sandwiches and breakfast items. Even Pâtisserie Poupon has savory croissants and quiches alongside their sweet pastries.

Are Baltimore bakeries typically open early for breakfast?

Yes, many of Baltimore's bakeries open early. Diablo Doughnuts opens at 6 AM and sells out early. Fenwick Bakery and Common Ground both open at 7 AM. Pâtisserie Poupon opens at 8 AM. Some spots like Dangerously Delicious Pies are afternoon and evening focused. Check specific hours before planning your visit, especially on weekends when schedules may differ.

What is the best bakery in Baltimore for a special occasion cake?

For special occasion cakes, Pâtisserie Poupon is the top choice for elegant French-style wedding and celebration cakes. Midnite Confections Cupcakery has a perfect 5.0 rating on WeddingWire and is beloved for custom cupcake towers and cakes. Rosendorff's is the go-to for Jewish celebrations, especially challah and personalized cakes. Vaccaro's does stunning Italian-style cakes as well.

Is it worth visiting bakeries outside of downtown Baltimore?

Definitely. Some of Baltimore's best bakeries are slightly outside the downtown core. Fenwick Bakery in Hamilton/Parkville has been a beloved institution for over a century. Rosendorff's in Pikesville is a must for challah and Jewish baked goods. Diablo Doughnuts in Overlea is worth the drive for their creative artisan doughnuts. Bread & Circuses in Towson offers a unique bistro-bakery experience.

Planning your Baltimore bakery tour

The ideal Baltimore bakery crawl takes you through three distinct neighborhoods in one day.

Start your morning at Pâtisserie Poupon in Harbor East for a croissant and coffee, then walk to Ovenbird Bakery nearby for a sourdough loaf. Head to Little Italy for a cannoli at Vaccaro's. In the afternoon, drive up to Hampden for a pie slice at Dangerously Delicious and a pastry at Common Ground. If you have time, detour to Fenwick Bakery in Parkville for old-school doughnuts, or Diablo Doughnuts in Overlea (go early). End your evening back in Fells Point with macarons from Sacré Sucré.

For dietary restrictions, Sweet 27 in Remington is a must-visit. For special occasions, Midnite Confections in Federal Hill does custom orders. Budget roughly $25–40 for a full day of bakery sampling across multiple spots.
