Brno is the gateway to South Moravia — the region that produces 96% of Czech wine — and the city's wine bar scene has exploded in the past decade.

Unlike Prague, where Czech wine bars compete with international cocktail lounges, Brno's scene is rooted in the vineyards 30 minutes to the south. You can drink wines from Pálava, Znojmo, Velké Pavlovice, and Slovácko that never leave the region. The range stretches from centuries-old monastery cellars to sleek natural wine bars run by sommeliers, and from French boutique shops to Spanish tapas joints with Rioja by the glass.

We researched local wine blogs, TripAdvisor, Google Reviews, expat forums, and community threads to identify the spots that Brno residents, wine enthusiasts, and savvy visitors actually recommend. Every venue was verified for current hours, ratings, and wine selection as of April 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best wine regions near Brno?

Brno is the gateway to South Moravia, which produces 96% of Czech wine. The four main sub-regions are Pálava (known for aromatic whites and the indigenous Pálava grape), Znojmo (crisp Riesling and Grüner Veltliner from loess soils), Velké Pavlovice (the largest area, with both reds and whites), and Slovácko (bold reds and traditional wine cellars). The Pálava and Mikulov areas are just 45 minutes south by car.

How much does a glass of wine cost in Brno wine bars?

A glass of Moravian wine in Brno typically costs 60–150 CZK ($2.50–$6.50 USD). Draft (cask) wines at traditional vinotékas can be as low as 40–80 CZK per glass. Premium pours at upscale spots like Kohout NA VÍNĚ or Klára Bára may run 150–300 CZK. Bottles for purchase at wine shops start around 150 CZK for local producers.

What grape varieties should I try in Brno?

For whites, try Pálava (the indigenous aromatic grape with lychee and rose notes), Riesling (Ryzlink rýnský), Grüner Veltliner (Veltínské zelené), Pinot Blanc (Rulandské bílé), and Sauvignon Blanc. For reds, seek out Frankovka (Blaufränkisch), Svatovavřinecké (Saint Laurent), and Zweigeltrebe. Natural wine bars like Kaple and justWINE also offer excellent orange and skin-contact wines.

When is the best time to visit Brno for wine?

Late spring through early autumn (May–October) is ideal. In September, you can try burčák (partially fermented young wine) — a seasonal delicacy sold at wine bars and street stalls. The annual Moravian wine harvest festivals take place in September and October. In April, the Czech Winery Open House event lets you visit dozens of wineries with free tastings.

Can I buy wine bottles to take home from Brno wine bars?

Yes. Most Brno wine bars also function as vinotékas (wine shops) and sell bottles for takeaway. Vinotéka U Tří knížat, Franck & Thierry, and justWINE all have extensive retail selections. Bottles from quality Moravian producers start at 150–250 CZK ($6–$11). Some bars offer a discount for bottles purchased to go versus on-premises consumption.

Are Brno wine bars suitable for non-Czech speakers?

Brno is a university city with a growing expat community, so most wine bars in the center have English-speaking staff. Klára Bára, Kohout NA VÍNĚ, Don Pintxos, and justWINE are all expat-friendly. At smaller traditional vinotékas outside the center, Czech may be the primary language, but pointing at bottles works everywhere.

What food pairs well with Moravian wine?

Moravian wines pair beautifully with local cheeses (especially Niva blue cheese and Olomoucké tvarůžky), cured meats, and traditional párky (sausages). Crisp whites like Grüner Veltliner go well with lighter Czech dishes. Don Pintxos pairs Spanish wines with tapas and jamón. Protože můžu offers excellent crêpes and cheese boards alongside their wines.

Is it worth taking a day trip from Brno to visit wine villages?

Absolutely. The wine villages of Mikulov, Valtice, and Popice are 30–45 minutes south of Brno by train or car. In Valtice, the National Wine Salon in the Château cellar lets you taste the top 100 Czech wines for 599 CZK. Mikulov has cobblestone streets lined with wine cellars. You can also rent bikes and ride the Moravian Wine Trails connecting villages through the vineyards.

Planning your Brno wine bar tour

The ideal wine bar crawl in Brno takes you through three distinct neighborhoods in one evening.

Start in the afternoon at Vinotéka U Tří knížat on Jánská for a glass of fresh cask wine and a crash course in Moravian varieties. Walk to Don Pintxos on Dominikánské náměstí for Spanish tapas and a Rioja. Then head to Starobrňenská for Protože můžu's atmospheric 17th-century cellar, and finish on the same street at VINÁRIUM for pure Moravian wines on tap.

For a different route, start at Kaple near Lužánky Park for natural wines, walk to JUNGLE by justWINE on Dvořákova for biodynamic pours, and end at Klára Bára on Čápkova for the award-winning Moravian selection.

Reservations: Essential at Kohout NA VÍNĚ for dinner. Recommended at Don Pintxos on weekends. Walk-ins are fine everywhere else.

Payment: Cards accepted everywhere. Cash useful at traditional vinotékas. Expect to spend 300–800 CZK ($13–$35) per person for a good evening of wine tasting with snacks.

Getting around: All city-center bars are walkable within 15 minutes of each other. Trams #1, #2, and #6 connect the outer venues. Bolt and Liftago are the local ride-hailing apps.
