Quick answer

Hanoi's phở scene features bowls ranging from ₫35,000 to ₫90,000, with Phở Gia Truyền Bát Đàn being a top recommendation for its pure, no-frills perfection. Hanoi-style phở emphasizes a clear, aromatic broth simmered for hours, setting it apart from its southern counterpart and making it a must-try culinary experience. We analyzed hundreds of Reddit posts from r/VietNam, r/hanoi, r/travel, and r/JapanTravelTips to find the phở spots that actual Hanoi residents and repeat visitors recommend over and over.

Hanoi is the birthplace of phở — and the city takes its national dish very seriously. Unlike the sweeter, herb-loaded southern version, Hanoi-style phở is all about the broth: clear, aromatic, simmered for hours with beef bones, charred ginger, star anise, and cinnamon. The noodles are flat and silky. The garnishes are minimal. The experience is pure.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best phở in Hanoi?

Based on local consensus Phở Gia Truyền Bát Đàn is the most consistently recommended phở in Hanoi. It's been operating since the 1970s and locals consider it god-tier. Phở 10 Lý Quốc Sư is a close second, especially for tourists who want an accessible Old Quarter experience. For something unique, Phở Thìn Lò Đúc offers a stir-fried beef style that's unlike any other phở.

What is the difference between phở bò and phở gà?

Phở bò is beef phở — the most iconic version, with a rich bone broth simmered for hours with star anise, cinnamon, and charred ginger. Phở gà is chicken phở — lighter, cleaner, and often served with shredded chicken and a more delicate broth. Hanoi is most famous for phở bò, but phở gà is equally beloved by locals, especially in summer when a lighter bowl hits the spot.

How much does a bowl of phở cost in Hanoi?

A bowl of phở in Hanoi typically costs ₫35,000–₫60,000 ($1.40–$2.40 USD) at local street stalls. Tourist-facing spots or famous names like Phở Thìn Lò Đúc can charge ₫70,000–₫90,000 ($2.80–$3.60 USD). Sit-down restaurants in upscale areas may charge ₫80,000–₫120,000. Even the most expensive phở in Hanoi is remarkably cheap by international standards.

When is the best time to eat phở in Hanoi?

Phở is traditionally a breakfast food in Hanoi — most locals eat it between 6:00–9:00 AM. Many famous phở shops open at 6 AM and sell out by 10–11 AM. Some shops also serve in the evening. Going early (before 7:30 AM) means shorter lines at famous spots like Bát Đàn, and the broth is at its freshest. A few places like Phở Chiều specialize in afternoon/evening phở.

What should I add to my phở in Hanoi?

Northern-style phở in Hanoi is served with fewer garnishes than the southern version. You'll typically get fresh herbs (ngò gai, hành lá), lime wedges, chili, and sometimes quẩy (fried dough sticks) for dipping. Unlike Saigon phở, Hanoi phở does NOT traditionally come with bean sprouts or hoisin sauce — adding those is considered sacrilege by purists. A squeeze of lime and some fresh chili is all you need.

Is Phở Thìn overrated?

This is hotly debated on Reddit. Many locals say Phở Thìn Lò Đúc was excellent under the original owner but has declined since. The stir-fried beef style is genuinely unique and worth trying once — it's a different experience from traditional phở. However, at ₫70,000–₫90,000, it's nearly double the price of local spots. local consensus: try it for the experience, but don't expect the best broth in Hanoi.

What's the best time of day to eat phở in Hanoi?

While traditionally a breakfast dish, phở can be enjoyed throughout the day. Many iconic spots like <a href="#pho-gia-truyen-bat-dan"><strong>Phở Gia Truyền Bát Đàn</strong></a> are morning-centric, but you can find excellent bowls at lunch, or even late afternoon at places like <a href="#pho-chieu"><strong>Phở Chiều</strong></a>. For the freshest broth and most authentic buzz, mornings are often best.

How do I identify a good, authentic phở spot in Hanoi?

Look for places with a steady stream of local customers, especially during peak morning hours. A large, bubbling pot of bone broth at the front (like you might see at <a href="#pho-vui"><strong>Phở Vui</strong></a>) is usually a sign of rich flavor and freshness. Don't be deterred by humble appearances; the best phở often comes from simple, long-standing stalls.

Is Phở Thìn Lò Đúc really that controversial, and what makes it unique?

Yes, <a href="#pho-thìn-lo-duc"><strong>Phở Thìn Lò Đúc</strong></a> sparks debate among phở purists because it breaks from tradition. Instead of just blanching, the beef is quickly stir-fried with garlic and ginger before being added to the broth, resulting in a fattier, richer, and distinctly savory flavor profile that some adore and others find too unconventional. It's a must-try for variety and its place in Hanoi's phở history.

Are there vegetarian or vegan phở options available in Hanoi?

While traditional phở in Hanoi is heavily meat-based (beef or chicken), vegetarian and vegan options are becoming more common in some modern cafes and specialized restaurants. However, at the classic phở stalls on this list, it's generally not available as the broth is meat-based. If you're seeking plant-based options, it's best to specifically ask for 'phở chay' (vegetarian phở) at larger establishments or dedicated vegetarian eateries.

What is phở cuốn and where can I try it in Hanoi?

Phở cuốn is a unique, non-soup variation of phở where fresh phở noodle sheets are rolled up like spring rolls with stir-fried beef, herbs, and a dipping sauce. It's a refreshing alternative, especially popular for a lighter meal or snack. The most famous place to try this delicacy is <a href="#pho-cuon-hung-ben"><strong>Phở Cuốn Hưng Bền</strong></a> in the Ba Đình district, where it's practically perfected.

What's the difference between Phở 10 Lý Quốc Sư and other Phở Lý Quốc Sư locations?

The original and most famous is <a href="#pho-10-ly-quoc-su"><strong>Phở 10 Lý Quốc Sư</strong></a>, known for its consistent quality and prime Old Quarter location. Other spots bearing 'Lý Quốc Sư' in their name, such as <a href="#pho-ly-quoc-su-cathedral"><strong>Phở Lý Quốc Sư (Cathedral)</strong></a>, might be official branches or separate establishments leveraging the brand's fame. While quality can be similar across locations, the original at 10 Lý Quốc Sư is often considered the benchmark for its iconic status and bustling atmosphere.
