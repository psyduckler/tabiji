Quick answer

Portland's thrifting scene is legendary, offering an eclectic mix of vintage treasures, budget-friendly finds, and curated fashion that reflects the city's unique, independent spirit. Whether you're hunting for a rare vinyl, a designer piece at a steal, or simply a sustainable way to refresh your wardrobe, PDX has a pre-loved paradise waiting for you.

Top verdicts

  • House of Vintage: Anyone seeking authentic, high-quality vintage pieces from various eras, ready to spend time exploring multiple vendors.
  • Buffalo Exchange (Hawthorne): Fashion-forward individuals looking for trendy, gently used clothing, or those wanting to sell their own items for cash or store credit.
  • Red White & Blue Thrift Store: Thrill-seekers who love the process of a deep dive, willing to spend time sifting through racks for incredible deals on everyday items.

The city's rich history of counter-culture and commitment to sustainability has fostered a thriving second-hand market. From bustling flea markets and massive warehouses to boutique vintage shops, Portlanders have long embraced the art of the reuse, making thrifting not just a hobby, but a deeply ingrained part of the local lifestyle and economy.

Expect to discover a diverse array of thrifting experiences, from sprawling bargain outlets where goods are sold by the pound, to highly-curated vintage emporiums specializing in specific eras or styles. You’ll find everything from mid-century modern furniture and unique home decor to upcycled clothing and high-end fashion, catering to every taste and budget.

Frequently asked questions

What is the typical price range for thrift stores in Portland?

The price range varies widely, from as low as $1 per pound at places like Goodwill Outlet (The Bins) for general goods, to $200-$300 for high-end, authentic vintage pieces at specialized boutiques like Ray's Ragtime. Most mid-tier resale shops offer items in the $15-$90 range, depending on the brand and condition. You can find excellent value for every budget across the city.

Are Portland's thrift stores generally well-organized?

Organization levels differ significantly. Curated vintage boutiques like Rerun and Buffalo Exchange are typically well-organized by style, size, and type, offering a pleasant shopping experience. Larger bargain stores like Red White & Blue Thrift and especially Goodwill Outlet (The Bins) are less organized, requiring more effort to sift through items. It's part of the 'hunt' experience at these bigger stores.

Do Portland thrift stores offer designer or high-end brands?

Yes, many of Portland's more curated thrift and resale shops, such as Buffalo Exchange and Crossroads Trading, frequently carry designer and popular high-end brands. Even larger thrifts can occasionally yield a lucky designer find, though it requires more diligent searching. Specialty vintage shops like House of Vintage may carry vintage designer pieces from past eras.

What kind of unique items can I expect to find besides clothing?

Beyond clothing, Portland's thrift stores are excellent sources for unique home decor, vintage furniture, books, records, art, kitchenware, and quirky collectibles. Stores like Village Merchants specialize in eclectic home goods, while multi-dealer shops like House of Vintage offer a broad array of non-clothing items, making them perfect for furnishing and decorating on a budget.

Are there specific days to get the best deals at Portland thrift stores?

Many larger thrift stores, like Red White & Blue, have rotating daily or weekly color tag sales offering significant discounts (e.g., 50% off). Goodwill Outlet (The Bins) rolls out new bins throughout the day, so timing your visit can be key. For curated shops, new inventory often arrives on specific weekdays, making those good times to check for fresh items before they get picked over.

What's the best way to get around Portland for a thrifting tour?

Driving is often the most convenient way to hit multiple, geographically spread-out thrift stores, especially if you plan on buying larger items. However, Portland also has an excellent public transport system (TriMet buses and MAX light rail) that can get you to many popular thrifting neighborhoods like Hawthorne and NW 23rd. Biking is also a great option for exploring concentrated areas with several shops.

Can I sell or consign my used clothes at Portland thrift stores?

Yes, many of the trendy resale shops in Portland, such as Buffalo Exchange and Crossroads Trading, offer cash or store credit for gently used, current-style clothing. Each store has specific buying policies regarding brands, condition, and season, so it's best to check their websites or call ahead before bringing in your items.

Are there any ethical considerations when thrifting in Portland?

Thrifting is inherently sustainable as it promotes reuse. Many local thrift stores and vintage boutiques in Portland are independent and support local communities. Some larger chains may have different operational models. If ethical sourcing is a primary concern, focus on local, independently owned vintage shops or non-profit thrifts that directly benefit local charities.
