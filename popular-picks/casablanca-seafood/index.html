Quick answer

Casablanca is Morocco's undisputed seafood capital — a bustling Atlantic port city where the day's catch moves from fishing boats to restaurant tables in hours, not days.

Best overall
Restaurant du Port de Pêche (Chez Madame Ali)
Top pick
Restaurant du Port de Pêche — the iconic portside grill where you pick your catch fresh off the ice.

Top verdicts

  • Restaurant du Port de Pêche (Chez Madame Ali): Authentic portside seafood straight from the fishing boats
  • L'Ecailler: Classic French-style seafood platters in an elegant setting
  • Le Cabestan Ocean View: Romantic sunset dinner with panoramic Atlantic views

From the chaotic energy of the Port de Pêche where fishermen unload sardines, sole, and sea bream at dawn, to the elegant Corniche restaurants where grilled lobster arrives with Atlantic sunset views, Casablanca offers a seafood spectrum unmatched in North Africa. The Marché Central lets you play chef — pick your fish, hand it to a grill master, and eat it minutes later. And Dakhla oysters, farmed in Morocco's deep south, rival anything from Brittany.

We analyzed travel blogs, TripAdvisor reviews, local food guides, and community discussions to find out which spots actual locals, expats, and savvy travelers recommend. Every place was verified for current hours, ratings, and menu accuracy as of April 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What kind of seafood is commonly found in Casablanca?

Casablanca, being on the Atlantic coast, offers a wide variety of fresh seafood. Common catches include sardines, mackerel, sea bream (dorado), sole, cod, hake, tuna, sea bass, squid (calamari), octopus, shrimp, prawns, and various shellfish like oysters from Dakhla. The daily catch ensures a diverse selection year-round.

Is it safe to eat street food or market seafood in Casablanca?

Generally, yes, especially at reputable market stalls or popular local eateries. Look for places with high turnover, where the seafood is displayed on ice and looks vibrant and fresh. Always ensure the food is cooked thoroughly and served hot. The Marché Central is a great spot for this authentic and typically safe experience.

What's the best way to order seafood in a traditional Moroccan restaurant?

In many traditional or casual seafood restaurants, especially near the port or markets, you often get to choose your fish directly from a display of fresh catches on ice. You can then specify how you'd like it cooked — typically grilled (à la plancha or charbon) or fried (frit). Don't hesitate to ask for recommendations or how it's usually prepared.

Are seafood restaurants in Casablanca expensive?

Casablanca offers a range of seafood dining options to suit all budgets. You can find incredibly fresh and affordable grilled fish at local portside eateries or market stalls for as little as 100–200 MAD per person. High-end restaurants with ocean views and gourmet preparations can easily cost 400–800+ MAD per person for a full meal.

What are some typical Moroccan seafood dishes I should try?

Beyond simple grilled fish, look for Seafood Tagine (slow-cooked in an earthenware pot with vegetables and spices), Seafood Pastilla (a savory pastry often made with vermicelli and mixed seafood), and Harira with seafood (a hearty lentil and chickpea soup with added fish or shrimp). Don't miss out on fresh calamari or shrimp fritters often served as starters.

Do I need reservations for seafood restaurants in Casablanca?

For upscale restaurants like Le Cabestan Ocean View, L'Ecailler, Basmane, or those in luxury hotels (Four Seasons, Sofitel), reservations are highly recommended, especially for dinner or on weekends. For more casual spots, particularly by the port or in markets, reservations are usually not needed, but be prepared for potential waits during peak hours.

What is the best time of year for seafood in Casablanca?

Casablanca's seafood is excellent year-round thanks to the Atlantic's consistent supply. However, sardine season peaks from June to November, and oysters from Dakhla are best from October to March. Winter months (December–February) bring excellent sole and sea bream. The fishing port is busiest in the early morning, so portside restaurants have the freshest catches at lunch.

How do I get to the fishing port restaurants from the city center?

The Port de Pêche is a short taxi ride (10–15 MAD) or a 15-minute walk from the city center and the old medina. The tram stops at Marché Central, from where the port is about 10 minutes on foot heading north toward the coast. Most drivers know Restaurant du Port de Pêche by name. For the Corniche restaurants (Le Cabestan, Le Petit Rocher, Basmane), a taxi from the center costs 30–50 MAD.

Planning your Casablanca seafood crawl

The ideal seafood crawl in Casablanca takes you from the market to the port to the Corniche in one unforgettable day.

Start your morning at the Marché Central (Restaurant Zerktouni, #11) — arrive early to see the market in full swing, select your fish, and have it grilled on the spot. For lunch, head to the Port de Pêche for the iconic Chez Madame Ali (#1) or Ostrea's oyster bar (#4), both steps from where the boats unload. Walk to the nearby Old Medina for a leisurely afternoon tea at La Sqala (#5), then cross to Rick's Café (#10) for a cocktail in movie-legend surroundings.

For dinner, taxi to the Corniche for sunset views at Le Cabestan (#3) or Le Petit Rocher (#7). For a splurge, Bleu Casa at the Four Seasons (#12) delivers the full luxury experience. Budget roughly 300–600 MAD ($30–$60) for a full day of seafood sampling, or 800–1,500 MAD ($80–$150) if including a high-end dinner.

Payment: Most upscale restaurants accept credit cards. Port and market restaurants are cash-only — bring Moroccan dirhams (MAD). Tipping: 10–15% is customary. Getting around: Petit taxis (red taxis) are affordable and plentiful. The Corniche is a 30–50 MAD ride from the center.
