Frequently asked questions

Where is the best specialty coffee in Zurich?

MAME Coffee on Josefstrasse in District 5 is widely considered Zurich's best specialty coffee shop. Founded by Emi Fukahori, it roasts single-origin beans in-house and offers a unique tasting flight of three coffees.

Are Zurich coffee shops expensive?

A flat white or cappuccino in Zurich typically costs CHF 5–7 (about $5.50–$7.75). Filter coffee and espresso are slightly cheaper at CHF 4–5. Zurich is one of the world's most expensive cities, but specialty coffee prices are comparable to other major European coffee cities.

Can I work from coffee shops in Zurich?

Yes — many Zurich coffee shops are laptop-friendly. Kraftwerk, Bros Beans & Beats, and Auer & Co. are popular with remote workers. Some smaller shops like Bean Bank Coffee are takeaway-only, so check before setting up.

What is Zurich's coffee culture like?

Zurich's coffee scene has evolved rapidly from traditional Swiss café culture to a thriving third-wave specialty scene. District 4 (Langstrasse area) and District 5 (Kreis 5) are the epicenters, with roaster-cafes, multi-roaster concepts, and barista championship winners running small shops.

What coffee drink should I order in Zurich?

The flat white is the most popular specialty drink in Zurich. Swiss coffee culture traditionally favored Café Crème (a long espresso with hot water), but the specialty scene has moved toward milk-based drinks and pour-over filter coffee.

Which Zurich neighborhood has the best coffee shops?

District 4 (Kreis 4, around Langstrasse) has the highest concentration of specialty coffee shops, including miró, slurp., Ombak, and Commercial — The Project. District 5 (Kreis 5) follows with MAME and Auer & Co.
