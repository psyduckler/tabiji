Bukhara's Lyabi-Hauz plaza is the beating heart of one of the Silk Road's most storied cities — a 17th-century pool flanked by ancient madrasahs and mulberry trees where merchants, scholars, and travelers have gathered for centuries.

Today, the square is ringed with chaikhanas (teahouses) and restaurants serving Bukhara plov made with sesame oil and raisins, lamb shashlik charred over open coals, and endless pots of green tea. The dining scene ranges from budget teahouses where a full meal costs under $3 to upscale lounges with live music and terrace views.

We analyzed traveler reviews across TripAdvisor, Google, Lonely Planet, and travel blogs — spanning 2020 to 2026 — to find which spots actual visitors recommend. Every place was verified for current hours, ratings, and menu accuracy as of April 2026.

Planning your Bukhara teahouse tour

The ideal Bukhara food day takes you through four distinct experiences from morning to night.

Start with breakfast at Bolo Hauz Chaikhana near the Ark Citadel — plov and green tea for under $3. Mid-morning, walk to the Silk Road Tea House for spiced tea with cardamom and saffron, then stop at Cafe Wishbone for the best coffee in Uzbekistan. For lunch, head to Chaykhana Lyabi Hauz or Labi Hovuz for shashlik by the pool.

In the evening, book Ayvan Restaurant for the finest dining experience with pool views, or Joy Chaikhana Lounge for live Uzbek music starting at 8 PM. For a special occasion, Doston House's fixed-menu feast is an unforgettable cultural experience.

Reservations: Only needed for Ayvan and Joy at dinner. Cash (UZS) is king; some restaurants accept cards but do not rely on it. Getting around: All 12 spots are walkable from Lyabi-Hauz within 15 minutes. Budget roughly 100,000–300,000 UZS ($8–$25 USD) for a full day of food exploration.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes Lyabi-Hauz a special place to eat shashlik and drink green tea?

Lyabi-Hauz is the heart of Bukhara's Old City, an ancient plaza centered around a historic pool that was once a vital water source and meeting point on the Silk Road. Dining here under ancient mulberry trees, surrounded by centuries-old madrasahs and khanqas, immerses you in the city's rich history and vibrant culture, creating an unforgettable sensory experience.

What is shashlik and how is it traditionally prepared in Bukhara?

Shashlik refers to skewered and grilled meat, a staple in Central Asian cuisine. In Bukhara, it is typically made with lamb, beef, or chicken, marinated overnight in a mixture of onions, spices, and sometimes vinegar or lemon juice. It is cooked over an open flame until tender and slightly charred, usually served with fresh onions and a side of flatbread (non).

What types of green tea are commonly served in Bukhara?

In Bukhara, green tea (Kok Choy) is ubiquitous and served hot, without milk or sugar, as a gesture of hospitality. Common varieties include pure green tea with a subtle grassy or floral note, and sometimes flavored with local herbs like mint or lemon. Spiced teas with ginger, cardamom, cinnamon, and saffron are also popular at specialty tea houses like the Silk Road Tea House.

How much does a meal cost at restaurants near Lyabi-Hauz?

Budget chaikhanas like Bolo Hauz serve plov for around 30,000 UZS ($2.50 USD). Mid-range restaurants near Lyabi-Hauz charge 50,000–120,000 UZS ($4–$10 USD) for a full meal with shashlik, tea, and bread. Upscale spots like Ayvan and Joy Chaikhana Lounge range from 100,000–200,000 UZS ($8–$16 USD).

What is the best time of day to visit Lyabi-Hauz for dining?

The best time to dine at Lyabi-Hauz is in the late afternoon or early evening. As the sun begins to set, the temperature cools and the ancient buildings glow in warm light. Many restaurants and chaykhanas come alive with soft lighting and a lively atmosphere. Joy Chaikhana Lounge offers live Uzbek music from 8 to 10 PM.

Are there vegetarian options available near Lyabi-Hauz?

While traditional Uzbek cuisine is meat-heavy, many restaurants in Bukhara now offer vegetarian options. Minzifa is known for its vegetarian-friendly menu. You will also find vegetable shashlik, fresh salads, vegetarian soups, bread, rice dishes, and stuffed grape leaves (dolma) at most restaurants.

Is it safe to eat street food or shashlik from local vendors in Bukhara?

Generally yes. Bukhara is considered safe for food, and many local vendors offer delicious and hygienic meals. Look for places with high turnover where food is cooked fresh in front of you. The established restaurants around Lyabi-Hauz are all reliable.

What else should I order besides tea and shashlik in Bukhara?

Bukhara plov (osh) is the signature dish, made with sesame oil for a lighter texture, often with chickpeas and raisins. Also try samsa (baked meat pastries), lagman (pulled noodle soup), manti (steamed dumplings with lamb and onion), and non (traditional flatbread). For dessert, try halva and traditional Uzbek sweets with your tea.
