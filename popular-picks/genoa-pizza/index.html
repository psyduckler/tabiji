Quick answer

Genoa, a city of historic port charm and labyrinthine caruggi, might not be the first place that springs to mind when you think of Italian pizza, but it certainly holds its own. While Naples boasts the birthplace of pizza, Genoa presents a unique and compelling pizza landscape, deeply intertwined with its Ligurian culinary identity and a growing appreciation for diverse styles.

Top verdicts

  • Pizzeria da Teo: Authenticity seekers who crave a classic Neapolitan pizza experience in a bustling, central location.
  • Pizzeria Pingusto: Diners looking for a modern, energetic ambiance and gourmet pizza options beyond the traditional.
  • Pizzeria Savo: Travelers and locals seeking a taste of traditional Ligurian flatbreads and a genuine local experience without breaking the bank.

The history of pizza in Genoa, much like the city itself, is one of adaptation and regional pride. While the classic Neapolitan style has found a strong foothold, Genoa's own contribution often leans towards the beloved 'focaccia,' a precursor to modern pizza that dates back centuries. Here, you'll find flatbreads topped with local ingredients, a testament to Liguria's simple yet profoundly flavorful cuisine.

Today, the Genovese pizza scene is a delightful mosaic. From traditional wood-fired Neapolitan pizzerias crafting perfectly blistered crusts and San Marzano tomato bases, to establishments specializing in the thicker, more robust Ligurian-style focaccia-pizza, and even modern eateries experimenting with gourmet toppings and sourdough bases, there's a pizza for every preference. Expect fresh seafood toppings, fragrant pesto, and vibrant local produce.

Frequently asked questions

What is Genoa's unique contribution to the Italian pizza scene?

Genoa is famous for its focaccia, a thick, airy flatbread that can be considered a regional precursor to modern pizza. Many local pizzerias, like Pizzeria Savo, offer 'focaccia-pizza' topped with Ligurian specialties like pesto, onions, or local cheeses, distinct from the thinner, chewier Neapolitan style.

Is it easy to find vegetarian or vegan pizza options in Genoa?

Yes, Italian pizzerias traditionally offer several vegetarian options, such as Margherita (without anchovies), Marinara (tomato, garlic, oregano), and pizzas with various vegetables (verdure). Vegan options are also increasingly available; you can often request pizzas without cheese ('senza formaggio') or look for specific vegan-friendly establishments.

What is the typical price range for pizza in Genoa?

A standard individual pizza in Genoa typically ranges from €8 to €15. More gourmet or specialized pizzas might go up to €20-€25. For a sit-down meal with a drink, expect to pay between €12 and €25 per person, depending on the venue and what you order.

Are 'pizza al taglio' (pizza by the slice) places common in Genoa?

Yes, 'pizza al taglio' or 'focacceria' spots are very common in Genoa, especially in the historic center and near busy areas. These are excellent for a quick, affordable snack or lunch. Places like Pizzeria Savo offer various slices, often sold by weight, allowing you to try multiple flavors.

What local ingredients should I look for on a Genovese pizza?

When in Genoa, definitely try pizzas featuring local Ligurian ingredients. Pesto (basil, pine nuts, garlic, Parmesan, olive oil) is a must-try topping. Other common local additions include fresh basil, Taggiasca olives, artichokes, and local cheeses like stracchino or crescenza.

Is Genoa known for Neapolitan pizza, or other styles?

While Genoa has a strong tradition of Ligurian focaccia, authentic Neapolitan pizza has also gained significant popularity and respect. Many pizzerias, such as Pizzeria da Teo, specialize in classic Neapolitan style, adhering to strict traditional methods to produce high-quality, wood-fired pizzas.

Can I expect gluten-free pizza options in Genoa?

Some pizzerias in Genoa are starting to offer gluten-free (senza glutine) pizza bases, especially larger or more modern establishments like Pizzeria Pingusto. It's always best to inquire directly with the restaurant beforehand or look for signage indicating 'senza glutine' options.

How do I choose between a focacceria and a traditional pizzeria in Genoa?

Choose a focacceria or 'pizza al taglio' spot if you're looking for a quick, casual bite, often for lunch or a snack, focusing on Genoa's unique Ligurian style. Opt for a traditional pizzeria if you prefer a sit-down meal with classic round pizzas, particularly for the authentic Neapolitan experience.
