12 Best Viewpoints in Bali

Bali, the Island of the Gods, isn't just about beaches and temples; it's a paradise of panoramic vistas. From dramatic volcanic landscapes and lush rice terraces to stunning ocean cliffs and tranquil lakes, Bali offers an endless array of spectacular viewpoints that capture the island's diverse beauty.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time of year to visit Bali for clear views?

The dry season, from April to October, is generally the best time for clear views in Bali. During these months, there's less rainfall and humidity, resulting in crisp skies perfect for enjoying mountain vistas, rice terraces, and ocean panoramas without obstructions.

Are there any viewpoints in Bali accessible without a long trek?

Yes, many stunning viewpoints in Bali are easily accessible. Pinggan Village Viewpoint offers Mount Batur views without trekking, while the Twin Lakes Viewpoint is right off the main road. Tegalalang Rice Terrace has accessible sections, and most temple viewpoints like Uluwatu and Tanah Lot only require a short walk from parking.

Do I need a guide for viewpoint treks like Mount Batur?

For treks like Mount Batur, hiring a local guide is generally compulsory and highly recommended for safety and navigation, especially for sunrise treks in the dark. For other viewpoints or shorter walks like Campuhan Ridge, a guide is not necessary.

What should I bring when visiting Bali's viewpoints?

Essential items include comfortable walking shoes, a camera, sunscreen, a hat, and plenty of water. For sunrise viewpoints at higher altitudes (like Kintamani), bring a light jacket as it can be quite chilly. For temple visits, sarongs are often provided or can be rented to cover knees and shoulders.

Are viewpoints in Nusa Penida easily accessible from Bali?

Viewpoints in Nusa Penida like Kelingking Beach and Diamond Beach are accessible via a 30-45 minute fast boat ride from Sanur (Bali). Once on Nusa Penida, it's best to rent a scooter or hire a private car with a driver, as the roads can be rough and distances between viewpoints are significant.

Are there any viewpoints that are free to visit in Bali?

Yes, several beautiful viewpoints in Bali are free or only require a small parking donation. The Campuhan Ridge Walk in Ubud is entirely free. The Twin Lakes Viewpoint and Jemeluk Bay Viewpoint typically only incur a minimal parking fee, making them great budget-friendly options.

Which viewpoint in Bali is best for sunrise?

Mount Batur is the most iconic sunrise viewpoint in Bali, with the sunrise trek being a bucket-list experience. However, if you want to avoid the strenuous trek, Pinggan Village offers equally stunning sunrise views of the Batur caldera without any hiking. Both require a very early start (2-3 AM departure from Ubud).

Can I visit multiple Bali viewpoints in one day?

Yes, several viewpoints can be combined into day trips. A popular northern route includes Twin Lakes Viewpoint, Wanagiri Hidden Hills, and Sekumpul Waterfall. In the south, combine Uluwatu Temple (sunset) with nearby beaches. On Nusa Penida, Kelingking Beach and Diamond Beach are commonly visited together as a day trip from Bali.

Planning your Bali viewpoints tour

The ideal Bali viewpoints itinerary spreads across 3–5 days, covering the island's major regions.

Day 1 — Ubud area: Start with the Campuhan Ridge Walk at sunrise (free, 6:30 AM), then visit Tegalalang Rice Terrace (open from 7 AM). In the afternoon, head to Tanah Lot Temple for sunset.

Day 2 — Kintamani sunrise: Choose between the Mount Batur sunrise trek (depart 2 AM, more strenuous) or Pinggan Village (depart 3:30 AM, no hiking). Spend the afternoon recovering or exploring the Batur hot springs.

Day 3 — Northern Bali: Combine the Twin Lakes Viewpoint, Wanagiri Hidden Hills, and Sekumpul Waterfall in a full-day northern Bali circuit. Start early for clear lake views.

Day 4 — Nusa Penida: Take the fast boat from Sanur and visit Kelingking Beach and Diamond Beach as a day trip. Book through your hotel for the best boat prices.

Day 5 — South & East: Visit Uluwatu Temple for the cliff walk and Kecak dance at sunset. Or head east to Amed for the peaceful Jemeluk Bay viewpoint and world-class snorkeling.

Budget: Allow IDR 500,000–800,000 ($30–$50 USD) per day for entrance fees, transport, and meals. The biggest expenses are the Mount Batur trek guide and Nusa Penida boat transfers.
