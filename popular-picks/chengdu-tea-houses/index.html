11 Best Tea Houses in Chengdu

Chengdu has more teahouses than any city in China — from century-old bamboo chair spots in People's Park to hidden suburban gems where ¥10 buys you bottomless tea and a front-row seat to real Sichuan life.

Quick answer

Chengdu's teahouses range from ¥10 for bottomless tea at local spots to ¥150–¥380 for premium experiences with Sichuan opera. For an authentic experience, Guanyin Pavilion Old Teahouse, priced at just ¥10, offers a glimpse into real Sichuan life in suburban Pengzhen. Weekday mornings are ideal for visiting most teahouses to avoid crowds.

Frequently asked questions

What is a typical Chengdu teahouse experience like?

You sit in a bamboo chair, order tea served in a gaiwan (lidded bowl), and a server pours hot water from a long-spouted brass kettle. You can refill endlessly, play cards or mahjong, get your ears cleaned, read a newspaper, or simply watch the world go by. Tea costs ¥10–¥50 and you can stay for hours.

How much does tea cost in a Chengdu teahouse?

Prices range from ¥10 at old-school local spots like Pengzhen's Guanyin Pavilion to ¥35–¥50 at popular tourist teahouses like Heming. Premium experiences with Sichuan opera performances can cost ¥150–¥380. Most neighborhood teahouses charge ¥15–¥25 for bottomless tea with hot water refills.

What tea should I order at a Chengdu teahouse?

The most popular local teas are Zhuyeqing (竹叶青, Bamboo Leaf Green from Emei Shan), Bitan Piaoxue (碧潭飘雪, jasmine-infused green tea), Longjing (Dragon Well), Tieguanyin (Iron Goddess), and chrysanthemum tea. Zhuyeqing and Bitan Piaoxue are the most distinctly Sichuan choices.

What is gaiwan lid language in Chengdu teahouses?

In traditional Chengdu teahouses, you communicate through the position of your gaiwan lid: lid leaning against the bowl or face-up means I need more water; lid face-down on your chair means I am stepping away, do not clear my table; lid flat and tight on the bowl means I am finished. Tapping your index and middle fingers twice on the table says thank you when they pour.

Is Heming Teahouse worth visiting or too touristy?

Heming is worth visiting at least once — it is over 100 years old and genuinely atmospheric, especially on weekday mornings. But locals note it has gotten crowded and lines can be long on weekends. For a more authentic experience, try Jinqinlao near Fuqin or make the trip to Pengzhen's Guanyin Pavilion.

Can I watch Sichuan opera at a Chengdu teahouse?

Yes! Several teahouses combine tea service with Sichuan opera performances featuring face-changing (变脸), shadow puppetry, and traditional music. Shu Feng Ya Yun on Qintai Lu is the most famous. Daqi Teahouse near Tonghuimen and Shunxing Old Teahouse also offer opera shows with tea service.

When is the best time to visit a Chengdu teahouse?

Weekday mornings (8–11 AM) are ideal for the most authentic, uncrowded experience — this is when local retirees gather. For Sichuan opera shows, evening performances (7–9 PM) are standard. Avoid weekend afternoons at popular spots like Heming, which get packed with tourists.
