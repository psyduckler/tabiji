Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between Belgian and American fries?

Belgian frites are double-fried — first at low temp to cook through, then at high temp for crispness — and traditionally served in a paper cone with mayo. The result is fluffier inside with a sturdy exterior, distinct from thinner American fast-food fries.

What sauces do locals order with frites in Antwerp?

Mayonnaise is the most common, but locals often choose andalouse (a tomato-mayo with peppers), samurai (mayo with sambal), or cocktail sauce. Some shops offer 30+ sauces — pointing at a numbered photo board is normal.

How much should a cone of frites cost?

Expect €3–€4 for a standard small cone (klein) and €4.50–€6 for a large (groot). Sauces cost €0.50–€1 each. Frituurs (fry shacks) are cheaper than restaurants serving frites as a side.

Are frites stands open late?

Most frituurs close by 9–10 PM, but a few near nightlife areas (around Groenplaats, De Coninckplein) stay open until 1–2 AM on weekends. Check Google Maps for current hours since they vary.

Do any frituurs offer gluten-free or vegan options?

The fries themselves are usually vegan and gluten-free (cooked in beef fat at traditional shops, but many now use vegetable oil — ask). For sauces, ask for vegan mayo or stick to ketchup. Cross-contamination from breaded items is common.

Is it better to eat frites standing or sitting?

Standing at the counter or eating from the cone while walking is the local style. Most frituurs have only a small standing bar; full sit-down meals with frites are at brasseries, not stands. The cone-on-the-go experience is part of the tradition.

Are there vegetarian-friendly fry shops in Antwerp?

Yes — most modern frituurs cook in vegetable oil, but traditional shops still use beef fat (rundsvet) for that distinct flavor. Ask 'plantaardig olie?' (vegetable oil?) before ordering. Frietboutique and several newer spots commit to vegetable oil only.
