Almaty's bakery scene reflects the city's unique position at the crossroads of Central Asia and Europe. From polished French patisseries lining Dostyk Avenue to Soviet-era konditerskie selling nostalgic Napoleon cakes, and from Italian focaccia cafes to the intoxicating smell of fresh tandoor nan at the Green Bazaar, this city serves up baked goods for every palate and budget.

The former capital of Kazakhstan has seen a bakery renaissance in recent years. International brands like PAUL have arrived alongside homegrown artisan operations, while traditional bazaar bread stalls continue to thrive as they have for generations. A growing cafe culture means many bakeries double as stylish hangout spots with excellent coffee programs.

We analyzed local food blogs, Google reviews, TripAdvisor rankings, Instagram food accounts, and expat community recommendations to build this list, verifying hours, menus, and pricing as of April 2026. Prices are listed in Kazakhstani tenge (KZT), with 1 USD roughly equal to 470 KZT.

Frequently Asked Questions

What kind of bakeries can I find in Almaty?

Almaty has a remarkably diverse bakery scene. You will find high-end French patisseries like Delice and PAUL, European-style cafes, traditional Kazakh and Soviet-era konditerskie with classics like Napoleon cake and medovik, Italian bakeries serving focaccia and ciabatta, specialty macaron shops, and artisan sourdough bakeries. The city also has numerous tandoor bread stalls at markets selling freshly baked nan and lepyoshka.

What traditional Kazakh baked goods should I try?

Start with baursaki (puffy fried bread served with tea), tandoor-baked nan (flatbread with sesame or nigella seeds), and shelpek (thin fried bread traditionally made on Fridays). For sweets, try chak-chak (fried dough soaked in honey), medovik (honey cake), and Napoleon (layered puff pastry with custard cream). These are beloved Soviet-era classics that remain hugely popular throughout Kazakhstan.

How expensive are bakeries in Almaty?

Prices range widely. Traditional konditerskie and market bread stalls are very affordable at KZT 200-800 per item (roughly $0.40-1.70 USD). Mid-range European-style bakeries charge KZT 1,000-3,000 per pastry. Premium French patisseries like PAUL or Delice range from KZT 2,000-5,000 for individual pastries. A full cafe meal with pastry and coffee runs KZT 3,000-8,000 at most spots.

Where can I find the best croissants in Almaty?

PAUL Kazakhstan is widely considered to have the best traditional butter croissants in Almaty, staying true to French technique with imported butter. Delice Patisserie is a close second with excellent laminated pastries. Bread Factory also bakes solid croissants daily. For the freshest selection, visit any of these spots before 10 AM as popular items sell out by midday on weekends.

Are there gluten-free or vegan bakery options in Almaty?

Dedicated gluten-free and vegan bakeries are still rare in Almaty, but the situation is improving. Some modern cafes and bakeries now offer select gluten-free cakes and vegan pastries. Bread Factory and Biskvit Patisserie occasionally have options. For custom orders, several bakeries will accommodate dietary restrictions with advance notice. Health food stores in the Dostyk Avenue area also stock some GF baked goods.

What is the best time to visit for fresh items?

For the freshest croissants, breads, and morning pastries, arrive between 8-10 AM. Most bakeries do their first bake early morning. At the Green Bazaar, nan bread is baked continuously starting from 7 AM and is best grabbed still warm. Weekends are busier and popular items at places like Delice and PAUL can sell out by noon. Afternoon visits work well for cakes and custom orders.

Can I find traditional tandoor-baked nan bread easily?

Nan bread is a staple in Almaty and easy to find. The best place is the Green Bazaar (Zelyony Bazaar), where multiple bakers produce warm nan throughout the day. You will also find nan stalls near smaller neighborhood bazaars across the city. A large round nan typically costs KZT 200-400 and makes an excellent edible souvenir that keeps for several days.

Do Almaty bakeries accept credit cards?

Most established bakeries and patisseries accept Visa and Mastercard. PAUL, Delice, Biskvit, Granaio, and other modern cafes all take cards and often support contactless payment via Kaspi QR (Kazakhstan's dominant mobile payment system). However, traditional market stalls and smaller konditerskie often prefer cash (tenge) or Kaspi transfers. Keep some cash on hand for the Green Bazaar.
