Bali's natural wine scene has exploded in recent years, transforming the island from a cocktail-and-beer destination into a serious player in Southeast Asia's wine culture.

Led by passionate sommeliers and expat restaurateurs, a wave of wine bars and wine-forward restaurants has swept through Canggu, Seminyak, and beyond. Whether you are seeking a dedicated natural wine bar with funky orange wines and minimal-intervention pours, or a restaurant where the wine list is just as thoughtful as the food menu, Bali now has options to rival many European capitals.

We researched across local food blogs, expat community forums, and traveler reviews to identify the 12 best spots for natural, organic, and biodynamic wines on the island. Every venue was verified for current hours, wine selections, and accessibility as of April 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is natural wine?

Natural wine is made with minimal intervention, typically from organically or biodynamically farmed grapes. It involves spontaneous fermentation using native yeasts, no added sulfites (or very minimal), and no fining or filtration, aiming for a pure expression of the grape and terroir.

Is natural wine expensive in Bali?

Prices for natural wine in Bali can vary widely. Entry-level natural wines by the glass start around IDR 100,000–150,000 ($7–10), comparable to conventional wines. Unique or imported bottles can run IDR 800,000+ ($55+), reflecting artisanal production and import costs. Many bars on this list offer affordable by-the-glass options.

Are there any local Balinese natural wines?

While Bali has local wines (like Hatten Wines), truly natural or biodynamic winemaking is still a nascent concept in Indonesia, primarily due to climate and regulatory challenges. Most natural wines found in Bali bars are imported from traditional wine-producing regions like France, Italy, and Australia.

How do I choose a good natural wine if I am new to it?

Ask the staff for recommendations. Many natural wines have unique profiles — funky, cloudy, or effervescent. Describe your usual wine preferences or what you are eating, and let the sommelier guide you. Tasting flights at places like Vin+ are a great way to explore without committing to a full bottle.

Do natural wine bars in Bali offer food?

Yes, almost all natural wine bars and restaurants on this list offer food. This ranges from small plates and tapas designed to complement the wine (PNNT, Bar Souvenir) to full-service dining menus (Mason, The Slow, Indigo). Cantina specializes in Italian wine and cheese pairings.

What is the best area in Bali for natural wine bars?

Canggu is the epicenter of Bali's natural wine scene, with 10 of the 12 spots on this list located there. The Berawa and Batu Bolong areas of Canggu have the highest concentration. Seminyak offers Shelter and Vin+ as strong alternatives, while Ubud is emerging with a few notable spots.

Planning your Bali wine bar tour

The ideal natural wine crawl in Bali takes you through Canggu's diverse wine scene in a single evening.

Start with a late afternoon glass at Cantina in Berawa — the affordable Italian wine bar is perfect for easing into the evening with a cheese board. Head to Mason on Batu Bolong for a wood-fired dinner with curated wines, then walk to Skool Kitchen for a sunset glass by the ocean. End the night at PNNT or Bar Souvenir for dedicated natural wine exploration.

For a full-day experience, begin with brunch and wine at Shelter in Seminyak, explore the 250+ label collection at Vin+, then head to Canggu for dinner and drinks. Santanera's rooftop bar La Terraza is perfect for sunset cocktails between venues.

Budget roughly IDR 500,000–1,000,000 ($35–$70 USD) for an evening of wine bar hopping across 2–3 venues, including food. For a dedicated wine dinner at upscale spots like The Slow or Indigo, budget IDR 800,000–1,500,000 ($55–$100) per person including wine.
