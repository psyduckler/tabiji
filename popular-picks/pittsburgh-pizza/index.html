Quick answer

Pittsburgh’s pizza landscape is as rich and diverse as its history, a testament to the city's strong Italian-American roots and an ever-evolving food scene. From old-school pizzerias serving up generations-old recipes to modern establishments pushing the boundaries of artisanal pies, Pittsburgh has firmly established itself as a serious contender in the national pizza conversation.

Top verdicts

  • Caliente Pizza & Draft House: Those looking for an exciting pizza experience with a wide variety of styles and toppings, perfect for groups or a date night.

  • Mineo's Pizza House: Families and anyone craving a classic, no-frills, consistently delicious Pittsburgh-style pizza experience.

The Steel City's pizza story began with Italian immigrants bringing their culinary traditions. Over decades, these traditions blended with local tastes, giving rise to unique 'Pittsburgh-style' pizzas, often characterized by a thicker, chewier crust and a generous hand with toppings, designed to satisfy the hardworking appetites of the region.

Today, you'll find an impressive spectrum of pizza styles, including classic New York-style slices, crispy Neapolitan wood-fired masterpieces, hearty Detroit-style squares, and distinctive Sicilian variations. Whether you prefer a simple margherita or a complex pie piled high with gourmet ingredients, Pittsburgh offers a slice for every palate.

Frequently asked questions

What makes Pittsburgh-style pizza unique?

Pittsburgh-style pizza often features a thicker, chewier crust than New York-style, but not as deep as Detroit. It commonly has a slightly sweeter sauce and a generous amount of cheese, often baked directly onto the crust. Many classic Pittsburgh pizzerias also offer square-cut pies, a nod to Italian-American traditions.

Are there good gluten-free pizza options in Pittsburgh?

Yes, many Pittsburgh pizzerias now offer quality gluten-free crusts. Caliente Pizza & Draft House, Pizza Taglio, and Driftwood Oven are known for their excellent gluten-free options that don't compromise on flavor or texture. Always confirm with the restaurant directly about cross-contamination precautions if you have severe allergies.

What's the best time to visit for fewer crowds?

To avoid the biggest crowds, aim for weekday lunches (11 AM - 1 PM) or early dinners (4 PM - 6 PM). Weekend evenings, especially Fridays and Saturdays, are universally the busiest times for popular pizza spots across Pittsburgh. Planning your visit during off-peak hours can lead to a more relaxed dining experience and shorter wait times.

Is Pittsburgh a good city for a pizza crawl?

Absolutely! Pittsburgh's distinct neighborhoods, each with unique pizza offerings, make it ideal for a pizza crawl. You can focus on a single neighborhood like Lawrenceville or Squirrel Hill, or branch out to explore different styles across the city. Public transport and ride-shares make getting between neighborhoods convenient.

Do any pizza places in Pittsburgh offer vegan options?

The vegan pizza scene in Pittsburgh is growing! Several places, including Caliente Pizza & Draft House and Spirit, offer delicious vegan cheese and plant-based topping options. It's always a good idea to check their menus online or call ahead to confirm their current vegan offerings.

What's the deal with the Mineo's vs. Aiello's rivalry?

The Mineo's vs. Aiello's debate is a classic Pittsburgh rivalry, particularly strong in Squirrel Hill. Both are long-standing Italian-American pizzerias known for their traditional pies. Mineo's is often lauded for its slightly thicker, less sweet crust and robust sauce, while Aiello's is praised for its sweeter sauce and consistent quality. Locals usually pick a side and stick with it!

Are reservations necessary for these top pizza spots?

For most of the classic, no-frills pizza shops, reservations are not accepted as they focus on walk-ins and takeout. For more upscale or sit-down focused establishments like Driftwood Oven or Pizza Taglio, especially on busy nights, making a reservation is highly recommended to secure a table and avoid a wait.

Is parking difficult at Pittsburgh pizza restaurants?

Parking can vary significantly by neighborhood. In dense areas like Squirrel Hill, Lawrenceville, or the Strip District, street parking might be metered and limited, especially during peak hours. Residential areas may offer more free street parking. It's always a good idea to factor in potential parking challenges when planning your visit.
