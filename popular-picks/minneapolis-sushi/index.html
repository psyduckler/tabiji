Quick answer

Minneapolis's sushi scene has blossomed into a surprising haven for discerning diners, offering everything from traditional Edomae mastery to innovative, locally-inspired rolls. Forget chilly winters; the Twin Cities' warmth truly shines through its fresh, artfully prepared sushi.

Best overall
Kado no Mise

Top verdicts

  • Kado no Mise: Sushi connoisseurs seeking an authentic, high-end Edomae omakase experience in an elegant setting.
  • Kyatchi: Diners looking for fresh, sustainable sushi, creative roll options, and a fun, energetic dining experience perfect for groups or a casual date.
  • Akira Back Minneapolis: Diners seeking a luxurious, trendsetting experience with unique fusion sushi and a vibrant, upscale atmosphere, ideal for special occasions.

While perhaps not the first city that comes to mind for sushi, Minneapolis has a long-standing appreciation for diverse cuisines. The influx of skilled chefs and fresh, high-quality ingredients, often flown in daily, has elevated the local sushi landscape significantly over the past two decades.

Here, you'll find a spectrum of styles: the purist's delight of traditional nigiri and sashimi, modern fusion creations that embrace unexpected flavors, and vibrant, elaborate rolls perfect for sharing. Whether you seek a quiet omakase experience or a lively bistro, Minneapolis delivers.

Frequently asked questions

What is Edomae-style sushi, and where can I find it in Minneapolis?

Edomae-style sushi refers to traditional Tokyo-style sushi, emphasizing carefully selected and prepared fish (often marinated, cured, or simmered) over elaborate rolls. The focus is on the fish's natural flavor and the chef's skill. In Minneapolis, Kado no Mise is the premier destination for an authentic Edomae omakase experience, focusing on purity and tradition.

Are there good sushi options for vegetarians or vegans in Minneapolis?

Yes, many sushi restaurants in Minneapolis offer a variety of vegetarian and vegan options. Most spots will have vegetable rolls (like avocado, cucumber, or asparagus) and often creative vegetarian specialty rolls. Wakame Sushi & Asian Bistro and Midori Sushi & Grill have extensive menus with many plant-based choices, and even upscale spots can accommodate with advance notice.

Which sushi restaurants in Minneapolis are best for a special occasion?

For a truly memorable special occasion, Kado no Mise offers an exquisite omakase journey, while Akira Back Minneapolis provides a chic, upscale fusion dining experience with a trendy atmosphere. Sushi Fix also presents a refined setting with creative, well-executed rolls and craft cocktails, perfect for celebrating.

Is all-you-can-eat sushi available in Minneapolis, and is it good quality?

Yes, Midori Sushi & Grill in Prospect Park is well-known for its all-you-can-eat sushi options. While AYCE spots prioritize quantity and value, Midori maintains a decent standard for its price point, making it a popular choice for those with a big appetite. Quality is generally considered good for the value.

What's the typical price range for sushi in Minneapolis?

The price range for sushi in Minneapolis varies widely. Casual spots like PinKU Japanese Street Food or Midori Sushi & Grill can range from $20-$45 per person. Mid-range restaurants like Kyatchi or Wakame typically fall between $40-$80. For premium omakase or high-end fusion experiences like Kado no Mise or Akira Back, expect to pay $70-$200+ per person, especially with drinks.

Do Minneapolis sushi restaurants source local fish?

Given Minneapolis's inland location, truly local sushi-grade fish (beyond freshwater options like trout, which are rarely used for traditional sushi) is not readily available. However, many restaurants, especially places like Kyatchi, prioritize sustainable sourcing and fly in high-quality, fresh seafood daily from reputable suppliers on the coasts or internationally to ensure freshness and flavor.

What should I know about tipping at sushi restaurants in Minneapolis?

Standard tipping etiquette in Minneapolis restaurants is 18-20% for good service. For exceptional service, especially at higher-end establishments or omakase experiences, a tip towards the higher end of that range or slightly more is customary. Check if a service charge is already included, particularly for larger groups.

Are there sushi spots in Minneapolis good for kids or families?

Yes, several sushi restaurants in Minneapolis are welcoming to families. Wakame Sushi & Asian Bistro offers a diverse menu that caters to various tastes, including non-sushi options for younger palates. Ichiban Japanese Steakhouse & Sushi Bar provides an entertaining hibachi experience alongside sushi, which is often a hit with kids. Casual spots like Midori Sushi & Grill also offer a relaxed atmosphere suitable for families.
