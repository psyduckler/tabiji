Guangzhou's night markets offer a culinary adventure for every budget, ranging from ¥5 to ¥200, with Huangsha Seafood Market being a top recommendation for its unique pick-your-own seafood experience. These vibrant markets showcase the city's diverse food scene, making Guangzhou a true culinary capital after dark.

Guangzhou — historically known as Canton — is where Cantonese cuisine was born, and the city takes its food culture seriously. While dim sum and roast goose dominate the daytime, the night markets reveal a different side of the city: smoky BBQ alleys, bustling seafood markets, and hole-in-the-wall noodle shops that have been perfecting a single dish for generations. Street food was technically banned in Guangzhou to maintain food safety standards, which means the vendors who survive have proper permits and take hygiene seriously. This guide covers 12 essential spots — from street-level night markets to the legendary restaurants that anchor Guangzhou's food streets.
