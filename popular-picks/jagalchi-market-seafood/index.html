Quick answer

For seafood near Jagalchi Market, Busan, expect to pay between ₩8,000 and ₩100,000, with the best value found outside the immediate tourist traps. Locals recommend venturing to spots like Cheongsapo for grilled shellfish or Millak for massive sashimi platters, steering clear of inflated prices within Jagalchi Market itself. We dug through hundreds of Reddit posts to find where Busan's seafood actually shines, from seaside grilled shellfish in Cheongsapo to massive sashimi platters at Millak, from budget pojangmacha feasts in Seomyeon to crab markets in Gijang — these are the spots that real residents and experienced travelers recommend.

Frequently asked questions

Is Jagalchi Market worth visiting for seafood?

Jagalchi Market is worth visiting for the experience and atmosphere — it's Asia's largest fish market. However, Reddit overwhelmingly warns that eating at the upstairs restaurants is overpriced and often disappointing. The consensus is: walk the 1F market for the spectacle, but eat at restaurants outside the main building or in neighborhoods like Cheongsapo, Millak-dong, or Bupyeong.

How much does sashimi cost in Busan?

A sashimi platter (회, hwe) for one person typically costs ₩30,000–₩50,000 at local restaurants outside tourist areas. At Jagalchi Market's upstairs restaurants, expect to pay ₩50,000–₩100,000+ for the same quality. Set menus at places like Gwangandatchi or Millak Raw Fish Center run ₩35,000–₩45,000 per person and include both raw and cooked dishes — much better value.

Where do locals eat seafood in Busan instead of Jagalchi?

Busan locals generally avoid Jagalchi Market for dining. Popular local alternatives include Millak Raw Fish Center near Gwangalli Beach for sashimi, Cheongsapo village for grilled shellfish by the water, Gijang Market for affordable crab, Bupyeong Market for budget seafood, and small neighborhood hwe restaurants throughout the city. The advice on Reddit is consistent: the further you get from Jagalchi, the better the value.

What seafood should I try in Busan?

Must-try Busan seafood includes: hwe (회, raw fish/sashimi) — especially gwangeo (flounder) and ureok (rockfish); jogae gui (조개구이, grilled shellfish); daege (대게, snow crab) from Gijang; san-nakji (산낙지, live octopus); and eomuk (어묵, fish cake) from local shops. For the adventurous, try gaebul (개불, spoon worms) and meongge (멍게, sea squirts).

Can I eat seafood solo in Busan?

Yes, but most sashimi platters are designed for 2+ people. Solo-friendly options include hoedeopbap (회덮밥, sashimi rice bowl) and mulhwe (물회, cold raw fish soup), which are commonly served as single portions. Seomyeon pojangmacha stalls are also great for solo diners — you can order small plates of various seafood at the bar.
