Frequently asked questions

What is Wiener Schnitzel?

Wiener Schnitzel is a thin, breaded and deep-fried veal cutlet that originated in Vienna. By Austrian law, a true 'Wiener Schnitzel' must be made from veal. If it's made from pork, the menu must label it 'Schnitzel Wiener Art' (Viennese-style schnitzel). The meat is pounded thin, coated in flour, egg, and breadcrumbs, then fried in clarified butter or lard until golden and puffy — the coating should billow away from the meat, creating air pockets.

How much does Wiener Schnitzel cost in Vienna?

A Wiener Schnitzel in Vienna typically costs €14–€25 depending on the restaurant. Budget-friendly spots like Schnitzelwirt and Reinthaler serve generous pork schnitzels for €10–€16. Mid-range Gasthäuser charge €16–€22. Upscale restaurants like Meissl & Schadn or Pfarrwirt charge €22–€35 for veal schnitzel. The veal version always costs more than pork — expect to pay €5–€10 extra for real Kalb (veal).

Should I order veal or pork schnitzel in Vienna?

Traditional Wiener Schnitzel is veal, and purists will insist on it. Veal is more tender and delicate, while pork is heartier and cheaper. Many beloved local spots serve pork schnitzel that's excellent — Figlmüller's famous oversized schnitzel is actually pork. If you want the authentic experience, order veal at least once. If budget matters, pork schnitzels at places like Schnitzelwirt or Gasthaus Kopp are fantastic and half the price.

What sides go with Wiener Schnitzel?

The classic side is Erdäpfelsalat (warm Viennese potato salad with a vinaigrette dressing — never mayonnaise). Lingonberry jam (Preiselbeeren) is the traditional condiment, and a lemon wedge is always served for squeezing over the schnitzel. Some places also offer Petersilerdäpfel (parsley potatoes) or a green salad. Fries are available but considered less traditional. A glass of dry Austrian white wine (Grüner Veltliner) or a local beer is the standard drink pairing.

Is Figlmüller worth the hype?

Yes, with caveats. Figlmüller has been Vienna's most famous schnitzel restaurant since 1905, and their plate-sized pork schnitzel is genuinely excellent — super thin, incredibly crispy, and much lighter than it looks. The downside: long queues (30–60 minutes without a reservation) and tourist-heavy crowds. locals are split — some dismiss it as a tourist trap, while others defend the quality. Our verdict: go once for the experience, but book a reservation to skip the line. For a more local vibe, try Gasthaus Kopp or Schnitzelwirt.

What is the difference between Wiener Schnitzel and Schnitzel Wiener Art?

Wiener Schnitzel is a protected designation in Austria, meaning it must exclusively be made from thinly pounded veal, breaded, and pan-fried. 'Schnitzel Wiener Art' (Viennese style schnitzel) or 'Schweineschnitzel' (pork schnitzel) indicates it's made from pork or another meat. For the authentic veal experience, head to places like <a href="#meissl-schadn"><strong>Meissl & Schadn</strong></a> or <a href="#skopik-lohn"><strong>Skopik & Lohn</strong></a>.

Is Figlmüller worth the hype/wait?

<a href="#figlmuller"><strong>Figlmüller</strong></a> is undoubtedly iconic, offering a quintessential Viennese schnitzel experience in a bustling, traditional setting. Its paper-thin, plate-sized schnitzel is famous. While often busy and popular with tourists, many visitors find the unique atmosphere and quality schnitzel to be well worth the hype and potential wait, especially if you secure a reservation in advance.

Can I find vegetarian or vegan schnitzel options in Vienna?

While traditional Wiener Schnitzel is meat-based, many Viennese restaurants are increasingly offering vegetarian alternatives, often made with celery root (Sellerieschnitzel) or mushrooms. Some places might offer plant-based options upon request. For dedicated options, check modern bistros, though traditional schnitzel spots like <a href="#schnitzelwirt"><strong>Schnitzelwirt</strong></a> will focus purely on meat.

What is the average price for a schnitzel in Vienna?

The price for schnitzel in Vienna varies significantly based on the restaurant and type of meat. Budget-friendly options like <a href="#schnitzelwirt"><strong>Schnitzelwirt</strong></a> can start from €10-€16 for pork schnitzel. Mid-range veal schnitzel at places like <a href="#figlmuller"><strong>Figlmüller</strong></a> or <a href="#gasthaus-poschl"><strong>Gasthaus Pöschl</strong></a> typically costs €18-€25. For upscale veal schnitzel at establishments like <a href="#meissl-schadn"><strong>Meissl & Schadn</strong></a>, expect to pay €22-€35 or more.

What should I drink with my schnitzel?

A classic pairing for schnitzel in Vienna is a crisp, refreshing local beer, often a light lager or Märzen. Many traditional Gasthäuser will have excellent draught beer options. Alternatively, a Grüner Veltliner, a dry white wine indigenous to Austria, also pairs wonderfully with the fried, savory notes of the schnitzel, offering a refreshing counterpoint.

Are there any schnitzel places open late in Vienna?

Most traditional schnitzel restaurants and Gasthäuser in Vienna typically close around 10 PM or 11 PM. For later options, you might have more luck in central areas, particularly around the 'Bermuda Triangle' where places like <a href="#salzamt"><strong>Salzamt</strong></a> offer a lively atmosphere and extend hours, but dedicated late-night schnitzel spots are less common. Always check specific opening times.
