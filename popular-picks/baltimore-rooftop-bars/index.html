Quick answer

Baltimore's rooftop bar scene stretches from the luxury heights of Harbor East's Four Seasons down to the cobblestoned waterfront of Fells Point, with every neighborhood in between offering its own elevated take on drinks with a view.

From hotel rooftops with dress codes to casual neighborhood decks where flip-flops are welcome, the Charm City punches above its weight in the outdoor drinking department.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best rooftop bar in Baltimore for views?

For breathtaking panoramic views of the Inner Harbor and Baltimore skyline, The Bygone (Harbor East) is widely considered the best. Its 29th-floor location provides an unparalleled vantage point for a truly memorable visual experience. Topside at Hotel Revival also offers excellent views of Mount Vernon and the Washington Monument.

Are Baltimore's rooftop bars open year-round?

Many rooftop bars in Baltimore, especially those with enclosed or partially enclosed spaces like The Bygone and Topside, operate year-round. However, smaller or more open-air rooftop decks and patios, such as The Tiki Barge, Blue Agave, or Federal House's upper deck, are often seasonal or weather-dependent. It's always a good idea to check their current operating status before visiting, especially outside of peak summer months.

Do Baltimore rooftop bars typically require reservations?

For upscale rooftop bars like The Bygone and Topside, reservations are highly recommended, particularly if you're seeking a specific table or visiting during peak hours. For more casual rooftop decks like those at Federal House or LB Skybar, reservations are usually not required, but seating can fill up quickly, especially on pleasant evenings or weekends.

What's the typical price range for drinks at Baltimore rooftop bars?

Prices vary widely depending on the venue. At upscale spots like The Bygone, cocktails can range from $18-25+. Mid-range options like Topside or Little Havana typically offer cocktails in the $14-18 range. More casual venues such as Federal House or Admiral's Cup might have drinks for $8-15, with beer prices generally lower across the board.

Are Baltimore's rooftop bars accessible for all?

Most established rooftop bars in hotels (like The Bygone or Topside) are ADA compliant with elevator access. However, some smaller neighborhood bars with elevated decks or 'rooftops' might have stairs only. It's advisable to call ahead if you have specific accessibility needs to confirm the venue's setup. Most will strive for accessibility where possible.

What neighborhoods in Baltimore have the most rooftop bars?

Federal Hill and Fells Point are two neighborhoods with a notable concentration of venues offering elevated outdoor drinking experiences, including true rooftops and popular upper decks. Harbor East and Mount Vernon also boast some of the city's most upscale and well-known rooftop bars, providing a mix of casual to luxury options.

What is the best time to visit Baltimore's rooftop bars?

Late spring through early fall (May through October) is peak season for Baltimore's rooftop bars, with warm evenings and long daylight hours. Sunset is the most popular time, offering golden-hour views over the harbor. Weeknights tend to be less crowded than weekends. Many rooftop bars close or reduce hours during winter months.

Can I do a rooftop bar crawl in Baltimore?

Absolutely. A great route: start at Topside in Mount Vernon for sunset cocktails, then head to The Bygone in Harbor East for the premium skyline experience. Walk to Fells Point for The Waterfront Hotel and Admiral's Cup. Or stay in Federal Hill and hit Blue Agave, Federal House, and Little Havana in quick succession. Most neighborhoods are walkable or a short rideshare apart.

Planning your Baltimore rooftop bar crawl

The ideal rooftop bar crawl in Baltimore takes you through three distinct neighborhoods in one evening.

Start in Mount Vernon at Topside for sunset cocktails with views of the Washington Monument — arrive by 6 PM on a Saturday for the best light. From there, head to Harbor East for The Bygone's 29th-floor experience (reservations recommended). After an hour of skyline views, take a quick rideshare to Fells Point and hit The Waterfront Hotel for live music and Admiral's Cup for late-night drinks.

Alternatively, stay in Federal Hill for a walkable crawl: start at Blue Agave for margaritas on the rooftop, move to Federal House for casual beers on the upper deck, then finish at Little Havana for harbor-view mojitos. All three are within a five-minute walk of each other on Light Street and Key Highway.

Budget roughly $50–80 per person for 3–4 stops with drinks, or $120+ if you include dinner at The Bygone or Rusty Scupper. Rideshare between neighborhoods runs $8–12.
