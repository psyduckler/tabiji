Cartagena's cafe culture blends colonial elegance with Caribbean warmth, offering everything from traditional Colombian aguas aromaticas to refined European-style afternoon tea services.

While Colombia is famous for its coffee, the tea and infusion scene in Cartagena deserves its own spotlight. Local cafes serve aromatic herbal blends made from fresh lemongrass, mint, and hierbabuena, while luxury hotels offer tiered platters of finger sandwiches and French pastries alongside premium loose-leaf teas. The best spots are concentrated in Centro Histórico and the bohemian Getsemaní neighborhood, all within easy walking distance.

Frequently asked questions

Are there dedicated traditional tea houses in Cartagena?

While Cartagena does not have many traditional "tea houses" in the English or Asian sense, it boasts a vibrant cafe culture with many establishments offering excellent tea selections. Luxury hotels often provide sophisticated afternoon tea services, and many cafes excel in specialty teas and local herbal infusions, ensuring a delightful experience for tea lovers.

What kind of teas can I expect to find in Cartagena?

You can find a variety of teas, from classic black and green teas to more exotic herbal infusions. Many cafes offer traditional Colombian aguas aromáticas made with fresh herbs like lemongrass, mint, or hierbabuena. Specialty cafes and hotels will also stock imported premium loose-leaf teas. The local aromatica tradition — steeping fresh herbs and fruit in hot water — is a uniquely Colombian experience worth trying.

Is afternoon tea a common offering in Cartagena?

Afternoon tea is not a widespread daily tradition for locals, but several high-end hotels, such as the Sofitel Legend Santa Clara and Hotel Casa San Agustín, offer elegant afternoon tea services, often with a French or international flair. These are typically special experiences that require reservations and come with a higher price point.

Can I find vegan or gluten-free tea snacks in Cartagena's tea houses?

Many modern cafes and patisseries in Cartagena are increasingly catering to dietary restrictions. While not all places will have extensive options, you can often find vegan pastries, gluten-free cakes, or fresh fruit to accompany your tea. It is always best to inquire with the staff directly about their offerings.

What's the best neighborhood for finding tea houses in Cartagena?

The Centro Histórico and Getsemaní neighborhoods are the best areas for finding tea houses and cafes. These historic districts are brimming with charming establishments, from cozy bookshop cafes to stylish patisseries and luxury hotel lounges, all within easy walking distance of each other.

What's the typical price range for tea in Cartagena?

The price for a cup of tea in Cartagena varies depending on the establishment. In local cafes or chains like Juan Valdez, a standard tea might cost between COP 8,000–15,000 (approx. $2–$4). Specialty loose-leaf teas in upscale cafes or luxury hotels can range from COP 15,000–35,000 (approx. $4–$9), and full afternoon tea services can be COP 70,000 (approx. $18) or more.

Planning your Cartagena tea tour

The ideal tea crawl in Cartagena takes you through the historic center and Getsemaní in a single afternoon.

Start your morning at La Brioche for a French-style breakfast tea with fresh croissants, or at Ely Cafe in Getsemaní for something more local. By mid-morning, head to Abacus Books and Coffee for a quiet herbal infusion surrounded by literature in a colonial building with exposed brick arches.

For lunch, visit Gato Negro for a casual tea and empanadas, then in the afternoon splurge on the full afternoon tea experience at the Sofitel Legend Santa Clara — reservations are recommended. Wind down your tour at Epoca Espresso Bar for a curated tea in their beautiful restored setting, or grab a balcony table at El Balcón Eat Drink Love for sunset views with an iced tea.

Budget roughly COP 80,000–150,000 ($20–$38 USD) for a full day sampling across multiple spots including the luxury afternoon tea, or COP 30,000–50,000 ($7.50–$12.50) if sticking to casual cafes.
