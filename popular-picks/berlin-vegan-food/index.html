Berlin is arguably the vegan capital of Europe — a city where plant-based eating is not a niche lifestyle choice but a mainstream culinary movement.

From Michelin-starred tasting menus at Cookies Cream to €5 seitan döners at Vöner, Berlin delivers the full spectrum of vegan dining. The city's multicultural DNA means you will find Vietnamese pho, Thai curries, Japanese ramen, and Spanish tapas — all fully plant-based. Neighborhoods like Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg and Neukölln are dense with options, while Prenzlauer Berg and Mitte house the fine dining scene.

Frequently asked questions

Is Berlin really that vegan-friendly compared to other major cities?

Yes, Berlin is widely considered one of the most vegan-friendly cities in the world, with over 100 fully vegan restaurants and cafes. HappyCow ranks it among the top 3 vegan cities globally. You will find plant-based options in nearly every culinary category -- from fine dining tasting menus to street-food doner kebabs, ramen bars, and even all-vegan supermarkets like Veganz.

What neighborhoods are best for finding vegan food in Berlin?

Friedrichshain and Kreuzberg are the vegan heartlands, with the highest density of plant-based eateries. Prenzlauer Berg has several upscale vegan spots including Lucky Leek and Foersters. Mitte houses fine dining options like Cookies Cream and Frea. Neukolln offers excellent casual vegan dining at spots like Alaska Bar and Brammibal's Donuts.

Is it expensive to eat vegan in Berlin?

Berlin vegan food spans every budget. Street food like Voner's seitan doner costs EUR 5-10. Mid-range restaurants like 1990 Vegan Living or Con Tho run EUR 10-18 per person. Fine dining at Lucky Leek or Cookies Cream ranges from EUR 50-100 for a tasting menu. Compared to other European capitals, Berlin's vegan scene is remarkably affordable.

Can I find traditional German dishes made vegan in Berlin?

Yes. Foersters in Prenzlauer Berg specializes in veganized German comfort food -- think plant-based schnitzel, roast beef, and potato salad. Several other restaurants offer vegan currywurst and doner. Berlin is one of the few cities where you can experience the full range of German cuisine reimagined entirely plant-based.

Do I need reservations for vegan restaurants in Berlin?

For casual spots like Voner, Brammibal's, and Lia's Kitchen, reservations are not needed. For mid-range restaurants like 1990 Vegan Living, walk-ins usually work on weekdays. For fine dining at Lucky Leek, Frea, or Cookies Cream, reservations are essential -- book at least a week ahead, especially for weekend dinners.

What is the best vegan street food to try in Berlin?

The quintessential Berlin vegan street food is the seitan doner at Voner in Friedrichshain -- a plant-based take on Berlin's iconic kebab. Brammibal's vegan donuts are another must-try, with creative daily-changing flavors. For something more substantial, the Vietnamese banh xeo at Con Tho or the tapas-style bowls at Li.ke Thai Vegan are excellent casual options.

Are there vegan-friendly supermarkets in Berlin?

Berlin has several fully vegan supermarkets including Veganz, which stocks everything from specialty cheeses to ready-to-eat meals. Conventional supermarkets like Edeka, Rewe, and Lidl have extensive vegan sections. DM and Rossmann drugstores carry vegan snacks, supplements, and cosmetics. You will have no trouble self-catering.

What is the single best vegan restaurant in Berlin for a special occasion?

For a special occasion, Lucky Leek in Prenzlauer Berg is the top choice. Chef Josita Hartanto serves a seasonal 3- to 7-course tasting menu with optional wine pairing in an elegant yet relaxed setting. It holds a Michelin Bib Gourmand. For a vegetarian (with full vegan menu) Michelin-starred experience, Cookies Cream is the other standout.

Planning your Berlin vegan food tour

The ideal vegan food crawl in Berlin takes you through three distinct neighborhoods and dining tiers in one day.

Start the day at Brammibal's Donuts in Neukölln for coffee and a salted caramel donut. For lunch, head to Friedrichshain for a seitan döner at Vöner or sharing plates at 1990 Vegan Living. In the afternoon, walk to Kreuzberg for a burger at Lia's Kitchen or banh xeo at Con Tho. For dinner, choose your tier: mid-range tapas and cocktails at Alaska Bar, or splurge on a tasting menu at Lucky Leek or Cookies Cream.

For the complete experience, add a vegan food walking tour (3 hours, around €50) which covers 4 stops with a local guide who knows the hidden gems. The U-Bahn and S-Bahn make it easy to hop between neighborhoods — Friedrichshain to Kreuzberg to Neukölln to Mitte to Prenzlauer Berg, all connected by quick train rides.

Budget roughly €40–60 for a full day of vegan eating across multiple spots, or €15–25 if sticking to street food and casual restaurants.
