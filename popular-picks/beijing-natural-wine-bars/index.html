Beijing's natural wine scene has grown from a niche curiosity into a genuine movement, powered by passionate importers, adventurous drinkers, and a wave of new bars that take low-intervention wine seriously.

The city offers everything from intimate hutong wine bars with hand-picked lists to sprawling bistros stocking 600+ labels. Neighborhoods like Sanlitun, the Gulou hutong area, and the embassy district around Liangmaqiao each have distinct wine cultures. Whether you want an orange wine on a rooftop terrace, a glass of pet-nat with Neapolitan pizza, or a rare Burgundy paired with jujube-wood smoked duck, Beijing delivers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is natural wine and why is it popular in Beijing?

Natural wine refers to wine made with minimal intervention in both the vineyard and the winery. This means organic or biodynamic farming, no artificial additives, and often spontaneous fermentation using wild yeasts. Its growing popularity in Beijing stems from a global movement towards healthier, more authentic food and drink, as well as a desire for unique, expressive flavors that differ from conventional wines.

How much does natural wine cost in Beijing?

A glass of natural wine in Beijing typically costs CNY 60–160 (about $8–22 USD). Bottles at wine bars range from CNY 250–800 or more depending on the producer and region. Casual spots like Tap Bar à Vin and Café Zarah offer glasses starting around CNY 48–80, while upscale venues like The Merchants and Solitude start around CNY 65–150 per glass with premium pours reaching CNY 1,000+.

Where are the best neighborhoods for natural wine in Beijing?

Sanlitun is the main bar district with venues like Bottega, Enoterra, and Buona Bocca clustered around Nali Patio. The Gulou-Dongcheng hutong area around the Drum Tower has charming spots like The Merchants and Café Zarah. The Chaoyang embassy district near Liangmaqiao has Solitude and Vin Vino. The CBD area around China World has Migas Mercado and Sip Siip.

Can I find natural wine in Beijing's traditional hutongs?

Absolutely. Beijing's hutongs are increasingly home to charming wine bars. The Merchants near the National Art Museum operates from a renovated space, Café Zarah sits on Gulou Dongdajie near Nanluoguxiang, and Fū Tapas & Wine Bar is a three-story hutong venue near Beixinqiao with organic-only wines and a rooftop terrace.

Do I need to make reservations at Beijing natural wine bars?

For popular or smaller natural wine bars, especially on Friday and Saturday evenings, reservations are highly recommended. Tap Bar à Vin has only about 12 bar stools, so it fills up fast. Larger venues like Bottega, Migas Mercado, and Buona Bocca usually have walk-in availability on weekdays. Many Beijing bars accept reservations via WeChat or phone.

What food pairs well with natural wine in Beijing?

Natural wines are incredibly versatile. In Beijing, you will find them alongside French bistro fare (The Merchants), modern Chinese dishes like jujube-wood smoked duck (Solitude), Neapolitan pizza (Bottega), Japanese-French tapas (Vin Vino), and organic tapas (Fū). The vibrant acidity and unique flavor profiles complement everything from rich meats to delicate vegetables.

Where can I buy natural wine to take home in Beijing?

Several venues double as retail shops. Solitude allows bottle purchases from its 600-label collection. Tap Bar à Vin sells bottles at retail prices. Cheers Wine shops across Beijing offer affordable options. You can also check WeChat groups and mini-programs from importers who specialize in natural wine for home delivery across Beijing.

Is Beijing's natural wine scene growing?

Yes, rapidly. China's bar economy saw a 90% surge in orders according to Meituan's 2024 Bar Guide, and wine bars have grown over 250%. New venues open regularly, including Sip Siip in 2024. The scene benefits from established importers like Felix Liu (Solitude) bringing in hundreds of natural labels, a growing community of wine enthusiasts, and a young urban demographic that values experiential dining.

Planning your Beijing wine bar tour

The ideal Beijing natural wine crawl takes you through three distinct neighborhoods in one evening.

Start in the Gulou hutong area in the late afternoon at Café Zarah for a glass on the courtyard terrace, then walk to The Merchants for a curated tasting of boutique and natural wines. As evening settles, taxi to Sanlitun and head to Bottega's Vesuvio loft for a Negroni and Italian wine, then pop next door to Enoterra for a candlelit glass. End the night at Buona Bocca for spritzes and cured meats.

For a dedicated natural wine evening, combine Solitude (deepest cellar) with Tap Bar à Vin (most intimate) — both are in the Chaoyang area and a short taxi ride apart. Alternatively, Fū Tapas in the Beixinqiao hutongs offers an organic-only experience with rooftop views.

Budget roughly CNY 300–600 ($40–80 USD) for an evening sampling 3–4 venues with a glass at each, or CNY 150–250 ($20–35) if sticking to budget spots like Tap, Zarah, and Vin Vino.
