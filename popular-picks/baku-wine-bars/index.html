Baku is quickly establishing itself as the Caucasus's most exciting wine destination — a city where 7,000 years of winemaking heritage meets a modern, cosmopolitan bar scene.

Azerbaijan's capital has seen a surge of wine bars in recent years, from specialist local-wine bars championing indigenous grape varieties like Madrasa and Bayanshire, to upscale hotel terraces pouring international grand crus with Caspian Sea views. The Old City (Icherisheher) hides intimate wine clubs within medieval caravanserais, while the Fountains Square area and seaside boulevard offer livelier, more contemporary options.

We researched dozens of local reviews, travel forums, and expert guides to compile this list of Baku's best wine bars. Every venue was verified for current hours, ratings, and wine selection as of April 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

What kind of wine is popular in Azerbaijan?

Azerbaijan has a rich history of viticulture, and local wines are gaining international recognition. Popular indigenous grape varieties include Madrasa (red), Bayanshire (white), and Saperavi (red, also common in Georgia but grown here). Local reds are generally full-bodied, while whites are often crisp and aromatic.

Is it expensive to drink wine in Baku?

The cost of wine in Baku varies significantly. Local Azerbaijani wines are generally quite affordable, with a good glass ranging from 8–15 AZN ($5–9). International wines, especially premium imports, can be more expensive, similar to prices in major European cities, ranging from 15–30+ AZN ($9–18+) per glass in upscale bars.

Are there any wine tours available from Baku?

Yes, several tour operators in Baku offer wine tours, typically to regions like Ismayilli, Shamakhi, and Gabala. These tours often include visits to local vineyards, wineries, and tasting sessions, providing a deeper insight into Azerbaijani winemaking traditions and modern practices. It is a great day trip option.

Do wine bars in Baku serve food?

Most wine bars in Baku offer a selection of small plates, tapas, or cheese platters designed to complement their wine offerings. Many establishments, especially those that also function as bistros or restaurants with a strong wine program, will have a full food menu, ranging from international cuisine to traditional Azerbaijani dishes.

What is the dress code for wine bars in Baku?

The dress code for wine bars in Baku generally ranges from smart casual to elegant, depending on the venue. For most casual to mid-range bars, neat attire is sufficient. For upscale venues like Eleven or Chinar, a more sophisticated dress code (e.g., smart casual or cocktail attire) is recommended, especially in the evenings.

Can I buy local Azerbaijani wine to take home?

Absolutely! Many wine bars, especially places like The Wine Factory or Le Chateau Wine & Co, also operate as wine shops where you can purchase bottles to take home. Additionally, supermarkets and specialty liquor stores throughout Baku offer a wide selection of local Azerbaijani wines at various price points, making for excellent souvenirs.

Planning your Baku wine bar crawl

The ideal Baku wine crawl covers three distinct experiences in one evening, moving from historic to modern.

Start in the Old City (Icherisheher) at Art Garden for an affordable glass in a XII-century caravanserai, then walk to Qaynana for a food-and-wine pairing with traditional Azerbaijani cuisine. Head through the city gates to Fountains Square and ROOM Wine & Dine for the biggest wine-by-the-glass selection in Baku. End the night at Eleven on the 11th floor for panoramic views over Baku Bay with wine in hand.

For a more focused local wine experience, start at The Wine Factory to taste and buy Azerbaijani wines, then hit Old City Wine Club for intimate tastings of small-producer wines, and finish at Le Chateau for live jazz and cheap local reds.

Budget roughly 80–150 AZN ($47–88 USD) for a full evening of wine tasting across 3–4 venues, or 40–70 AZN ($24–41) if sticking to budget spots with local wines.
