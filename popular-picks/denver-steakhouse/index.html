Quick answer

Denver’s steakhouse scene is a testament to the city’s thriving culinary landscape, offering a carnivorous journey that ranges from timeless elegance to modern, innovative cuts. As a city steeped in Western heritage, the art of grilling and enjoying a perfectly cooked steak holds a special place in the Mile High City’s heart, evolving with contemporary tastes while honoring its roots.

Top verdicts

  • Guard and Grace: Those seeking a high-energy, modern take on the classic steakhouse with top-tier service and an extensive wine list.
  • A5 Steakhouse: Discerning diners looking for unique cuts, exceptional Wagyu, and a lively, chic environment perfect for a date night or special occasion.
  • Elway's Downtown: Those seeking a traditional, luxurious steakhouse setting with reliably excellent prime cuts and white-glove service.

The history of steakhouses in Denver is intertwined with its ranching and mining past. Early establishments catered to hungry cowboys and prospectors, serving hearty, no-frills cuts. Over the decades, as Denver grew into a sophisticated urban center, its steakhouses transformed, embracing fine dining, extensive wine lists, and opulent decor, yet never losing sight of the primal joy of a great piece of beef.

Today, Denver’s steakhouses offer an impressive array of styles and cuts. You’ll find classic chophouses serving meticulously dry-aged prime beef with traditional sides, alongside modern steakhouses pushing boundaries with international influences, unique cooking techniques like wood-firing, and sustainable sourcing. From Wagyu to bison, bone-in ribeyes to tender filets, there’s a steak experience for every palate and occasion.

Frequently asked questions

What's the typical price range for a steakhouse meal in Denver?

The price range for a steakhouse meal in Denver can vary significantly. For a casual steak at a brewpub like Bull & Bush, you might spend $30-$60 per person. Mid-range options like Citizen Rail often fall between $50-$90, while high-end steakhouses such as Guard and Grace or Elway's typically start at $70-$80 and can easily exceed $150 per person, especially with appetizers, sides, and drinks.

Do Denver steakhouses offer more than just beef?

Yes, absolutely! While beef is the star, most Denver steakhouses offer a diverse menu. You'll commonly find fresh seafood, including oysters, lobster, and various fish dishes. Many also feature lamb chops, pork, chicken, and vegetarian options. Historic places like Buckhorn Exchange even specialize in exotic wild game like elk and buffalo.

Are there any steakhouses in Denver known for a unique ambiance?

Denver boasts several steakhouses with distinct ambiances. Buckhorn Exchange offers a truly historic, Western lodge feel with extensive taxidermy. STK Steakhouse provides a trendy, high-energy lounge atmosphere with a DJ. Guard and Grace is known for its modern, chic, and bustling fine-dining vibe. Each offers a different experience beyond just the food.

What kind of cuts of steak are popular in Denver?

Denver steakhouses offer a full range of popular cuts. You'll find classic New York Strips, Ribeyes (bone-in and boneless), Filet Mignons, and Porterhouses. Many upscale establishments also feature dry-aged steaks, Wagyu beef (both domestic and Japanese A5), and occasionally specialty cuts like Tomahawk or Picanha, especially at modern steakhouses like A5 Steakhouse.

Are Denver steakhouses generally good for groups or special occasions?

Many of Denver's steakhouses are perfectly suited for groups and special occasions. Places like Elway's Downtown, The Capital Grille, and Shanahan's Steakhouse offer elegant settings and private dining rooms ideal for celebrations or business gatherings. Even lively spots like STK Steakhouse can accommodate groups looking for a more vibrant, social experience.

Do I need to make reservations for Denver steakhouses?

For most of the highly-rated and upscale steakhouses in Denver, reservations are strongly recommended, especially for weekend evenings or if you have a specific time preference. Popular spots can book up weeks in advance. Casual steakhouses or brewpubs might accept walk-ins, but you could face a wait during peak hours.

Are there vegetarian or gluten-free options available at Denver steakhouses?

While steakhouses are meat-centric, most modern establishments in Denver are accommodating to dietary restrictions. You'll typically find robust side dishes that can be combined to form a vegetarian meal, and many offer gluten-free modifications or designated gluten-free dishes. It's always best to inform the restaurant of any dietary needs when making a reservation or upon arrival.

What's the dress code like for Denver steakhouses?

Dress codes vary by establishment. High-end steakhouses like Guard and Grace or Elway's typically encourage business casual or smart casual attire. Jackets are not usually required but are often seen. For places like Bull & Bush Brewery, casual attire is perfectly acceptable. When in doubt, it's always safer to dress a bit more on the nice side for a true steakhouse experience.
