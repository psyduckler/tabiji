Quick answer

Barbados is the fish cake capital of the Caribbean — a tiny island that punches far above its weight when it comes to street food, seafood, and culinary tradition.

Best overall
Cuz's Fish Shack
Top pick
Cuz's Fish Shack -- the iconic beachside fish cutter stand at Pebbles Beach.

Top verdicts

  • Cuz's Fish Shack: The quintessential Bajan fish cutter experience on the beach
  • Mustor's Restaurant: Authentic cou-cou and flying fish in a historic Bridgetown setting
  • Oistins Fish Fry: The ultimate Bajan food-and-culture experience — especially Friday nights

The Bajan fish cake — a deep-fried fritter of salted cod, herbs, and Scotch bonnet pepper — is as fundamental to Barbados as the arepa is to Colombia. You will find them everywhere: at roadside stands for under BBD $2, at market stalls in Cheapside, and alongside flying fish cutters at beachside shacks. Add in the legendary Oistins Fish Fry, world-class roti, and sit-down restaurants serving cou-cou and flying fish, and you have a food scene that rewards every budget and appetite.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Bajan fish cake and how is it different from a fish cutter?

A Bajan fish cake is a deep-fried fritter made from salted cod (or salt fish), flour, herbs, and Bajan seasonings. It is crispy on the outside and soft inside, usually eaten as a snack or appetizer. A fish cutter, by contrast, is a sandwich -- a piece of fried fish (often marlin, mahi-mahi, or flying fish) served in a Bajan salt bread bun with lettuce and pepper sauce. Both are street food staples, but they are different dishes.

Where can I find the best fish cakes in Barbados?

Hot Legendary Fish Cakes on Magazine Lane in Bridgetown is widely considered the best dedicated fish cake vendor on the island. Their freshly fried fish cakes are crunchy on the outside, soft and fluffy inside, and cost under BBD $2 each. The Cheapside Market vendors also serve excellent fish cakes. For a sit-down experience, Mustor's Restaurant on McGregor Street in Bridgetown has served authentic Bajan fish cakes since 1941.

Is the Oistins Fish Fry worth visiting?

Absolutely. The Oistins Fish Fry is the most iconic food experience in Barbados. Friday night is the biggest event, when hundreds of locals and visitors flock to the waterfront for freshly grilled flying fish, marlin, swordfish, and lobster. Vendors like Uncle George's and other stalls cook fish to order. Arrive by 6 PM on Friday to beat the rush. Saturday nights are also lively. Weeknights are quieter but still have vendors operating.

What is flying fish and how is it typically prepared in Barbados?

Flying fish is Barbados' national dish, a small, silvery fish abundant in Caribbean waters. It is most commonly prepared fried or steamed, seasoned with fresh herbs and Bajan spices, then served with cou-cou (cornmeal and okra porridge) or in a salt bread bun as a flying fish cutter. The combination of cou-cou and flying fish is the official national dish.

How much does street food cost in Barbados?

Street food in Barbados is very affordable. Fish cakes cost BBD $1-3 each (under $1.50 USD). Fish cutters (sandwiches) at places like Cuz's run BBD $15-30 ($7.50-15 USD). A full plate at Oistins Fish Fry with grilled fish, rice and peas, and macaroni pie costs BBD $25-50 ($12-25 USD). Roti at The Roti Den or Chefette costs BBD $15-25 ($7.50-12.50 USD). Budget roughly BBD $50-100 ($25-50 USD) for a full day of street food sampling.

Do street food vendors in Barbados accept credit cards?

Most smaller street food vendors and market stalls in Barbados operate on a cash-only basis, especially at places like Cheapside Market and Oistins Fish Fry. Carry Barbadian Dollars (BBD) in small denominations -- the exchange rate is fixed at 2 BBD to 1 USD. Larger establishments like Chefette, Shakers, and Brown Sugar accept credit cards. Some Oistins vendors now accept contactless payments, but do not count on it.

Is street food in Barbados safe to eat for tourists?

Yes, street food in Barbados is generally safe and a fantastic way to experience local culture. Look for vendors with visible cooking areas, fresh ingredients, and a steady stream of local customers. At Oistins, the fish is grilled or fried to order at high temperatures. Cheapside Market vendors have been operating for decades. Always choose cooked-to-order options where possible, and drink bottled or filtered water.

What are the best non-fish street food options in Barbados?

Beyond fish, Barbados has excellent roti (flatbreads filled with curried chicken, beef, or vegetables) at The Roti Den and Chefette. Pudding and souse (pickled pork with sweet potato) is a Saturday tradition. Macaroni pie is served as a side everywhere. Granny's in Oistins is famous for fried chicken necks and gizzards with Bajan hot sauce. At Cheapside Market, try breadfruit cou-cou and fried plantains.

Planning your Barbados food tour

The ideal Barbados food crawl covers three coasts in two days — but you can hit the highlights in a single day if you plan it right.

Start your morning at Hot Legendary Fish Cakes on Magazine Lane for freshly fried fish cakes (they close by noon). Walk to Cheapside Market for more fish cakes and fresh produce, then lunch at Mustor's for the national dish — cou-cou and flying fish. In the afternoon, head to Pebbles Beach for a marlin cutter at Cuz's Fish Shack. For dinner, take a taxi to Oistins for the Friday night Fish Fry (or any evening for a quieter experience).

If you have a second day, explore the west coast: lunch at The Roti Den for the best roti on the island, followed by Karibu for authentic Bajan home cooking. On the north coast, Fisherman's Pub in Speightstown serves excellent cou-cou and flying fish in a relaxed pub setting.

Budget roughly BBD $100–150 ($50–75 USD) per day for a comprehensive food tour across multiple spots, or BBD $40–60 ($20–30 USD) if sticking to street food and markets. The fixed exchange rate of 2 BBD to 1 USD makes budgeting easy.
