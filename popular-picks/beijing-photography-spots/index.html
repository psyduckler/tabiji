Quick answer

Beijing is one of the world's greatest cities for photography — a place where 3,000 years of history collide with 21st-century ambition at every turn.

Best overall
The Forbidden City
Top pick
The Forbidden City -- unparalleled imperial grandeur and the most iconic photography subject in Beijing.

Top verdicts

  • The Forbidden City: Imperial architecture, sweeping courtyards, and iconic rooftop silhouettes
  • Great Wall at Mutianyu: Sweeping landscape panoramas, watchtower compositions, and dramatic mountain backdrops
  • Temple of Heaven: Symmetrical architectural shots, local life scenes, and tranquil park atmospherics

Within a single day you can shoot the crimson walls of the Forbidden City at dawn, navigate centuries-old hutong alleys at midday, frame the space-age CCTV Headquarters at golden hour, and capture the shimmering reflection of the National Centre for the Performing Arts at night. From the wild grandeur of the Great Wall snaking through forested mountains to the paint-splattered walls of 798 Art Zone, Beijing delivers an extraordinary range of subjects for every style of photographer.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time of year for photography in Beijing?

Autumn (September to November) is generally considered the best season, offering clear skies, pleasant temperatures, and beautiful fall foliage, especially at the Great Wall and Summer Palace. Spring (April to May) is also good with blossoms, but can sometimes be hazy. Winter brings crisp blue skies and snow-dusted landmarks, while summer is lush but often humid and smoggy.

Are tripods allowed at most photography spots in Beijing?

Tripods are generally allowed in outdoor public spaces and at many tourist attractions, but always check for specific restrictions, especially inside temples, museums, or during crowded events. The Forbidden City may restrict large tripods during peak times for safety and crowd control. Jingshan Park and the Olympic Park area are tripod-friendly. For street photography in hutongs, a monopod or compact tripod is more practical.

What are some tips for street photography in Beijing's hutongs?

Be respectful of locals, ask for permission if photographing individuals directly, and carry a compact lens to blend in. Focus on capturing daily life, traditional architecture, and unique storefronts. Early mornings are often best for quieter scenes and soft light. The hutongs around Dashilar, Nanluoguxiang, and the Lama Temple area (Wudaoying Hutong) are particularly photogenic.

How can I best capture the Beijing skyline at night?

For historical skyline views, head to Jingshan Park before closing for an elevated perspective of the Forbidden City at golden hour. For modern skyscrapers, the CBD area around the CCTV Headquarters offers dramatic shots. The Olympic Park and the NCPA (The Egg) are spectacular when illuminated at night. Use a tripod, a wide-angle lens, and experiment with long exposures to capture light trails and reflections.

Is it easy to get around Beijing with photography gear?

Beijing has an efficient and extensive subway system that makes it relatively easy to navigate with your gear. Most central spots (Forbidden City, Jingshan, Temple of Heaven, Lama Temple, 798 Art Zone, CBD) are reachable by metro. For the Great Wall at Mutianyu, take the 916 Express bus from Dongzhimen or book a private transfer. Taxis and ride-hailing apps like Didi are also readily available.

What are the must-have camera settings for the Forbidden City?

Bring a wide-angle lens (16-35mm) for the vast courtyards and a telephoto (70-200mm) for architectural details like roof ornaments and watchtower corners. Shoot wide to capture the immense scale, then zoom in on stone textures, carved dragons, and golden roof details. Golden hour (shortly after opening or before closing) delivers the best light. Use a polarizing filter to cut haze and deepen the blue sky.

Are there cultural considerations for photography in Beijing?

Always be mindful and respectful, especially in religious sites like the Lama Temple and Temple of Heaven, where photography may be restricted or considered disrespectful in certain prayer areas. Avoid photographing military installations or government buildings. In general, ask before taking close-up photos of individuals. Drone photography is heavily restricted in central Beijing due to the government district.

Which Beijing photography spot should I visit first?

Start with the Forbidden City early in the morning for the softest light and thinnest crowds. Arrive right at opening (8:30 AM) and head straight to the less-visited side halls. After, walk to Jingshan Park for the panoramic overhead shot. If you only have one day, pair these with a late-afternoon visit to the Temple of Heaven and finish at the NCPA or Olympic Park for night shots.

Planning your Beijing photography tour

The ideal Beijing photography itinerary covers three distinct moods across two to three days.

Day 1 — Historic Beijing: Start at the Forbidden City right at opening (8:30 AM) for the best light and thinnest crowds. Exit through the north gate and climb Jingshan Park for the panoramic sunset shot. In the evening, walk to Houhai Lake for street photography and dinner.

Day 2 — Temples and Hutongs: Begin at the Temple of Heaven at 6 AM for morning park life and the iconic hall. After lunch, explore the Lama Temple and Wudaoying Hutong, then head to Dashilar for afternoon hutong street scenes.

Day 3 — Modern Beijing + Great Wall: Dedicate the morning to the Great Wall at Mutianyu (90-min drive, leave early). Return for evening shots at the Olympic Park (Bird's Nest at night) or the NCPA at blue hour. Save 798 Art Zone for a half-day on any trip extension.

Getting around: The subway covers most central spots efficiently. For Mutianyu, take the 916 Express bus from Dongzhimen or book a private transfer (roughly CNY 400–600 round trip). Budget CNY 200–400/day for entry fees, transport, and meals.

Best season: Autumn (September–November) offers clear skies and foliage. Spring (April–May) brings blossoms. Winter delivers crisp blue skies and snow-dusted landmarks. Avoid major holidays (Golden Week, Chinese New Year) when crowds are extreme.
