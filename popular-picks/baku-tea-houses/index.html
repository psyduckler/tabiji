Baku is one of the world's great tea cities — a place where every conversation begins and ends with a glass of strong black tea, served in a pear-shaped armudu glass with sweet preserves and warm hospitality.

The Azerbaijani tea ritual is more than a drink — it is a social institution. From the ancient caravanserais of Icherisheher (the walled Old City) to the modern lounges along the Caspian Boulevard, Baku's tea houses offer everything from traditional samovar service with homemade murabba (fruit preserves) to elegant fusion experiences with live music and panoramic views.

We researched dozens of tea houses, restaurants, and cafes across Baku — cross-referencing TripAdvisor reviews, Google ratings, local food blogs, and traveler recommendations — to find the 12 spots that best represent Baku's rich tea culture. Frequently Asked Questions

What is unique about Azerbaijani tea culture?

Azerbaijani tea culture is deeply rooted in hospitality and social gatherings. Tea is typically served strong and hot in small, pear-shaped glasses called armudu, always accompanied by various sweet preserves (murabba), dried fruits, and sometimes sugar cubes (gand). It is a symbol of welcoming guests and forms the centerpiece of social interactions, often enjoyed for hours.

What should I expect in a traditional Azerbaijani tea set?

A traditional Azerbaijani tea set usually includes a samovar (a metal container for boiling water), black tea served in armudu glasses, a bowl of fresh lemon slices, and a spread of various accompaniments. These typically include different fruit jams (like fig, cherry, or walnut jam), baklava, shakarbura, dried apricots, figs, and nuts. Sometimes, a plate of local cheeses and fresh herbs is also offered.

Are there modern tea houses in Baku, or are they all traditional?

Baku offers a mix of both. While traditional tea houses (chai khana or cay evi) in the Old City are iconic, there are also numerous modern cafes and lounges, especially along the Boulevard and in newer districts, that offer a contemporary take on tea service. These often feature stylish interiors, diverse tea menus, and fusion pastries, blending local traditions with international trends.

What is armudu and why is it used for tea?

Armudu is a traditional pear-shaped glass in which tea is served in Azerbaijan. Its unique shape is not just for aesthetics; it is designed to keep the tea hot at the bottom while allowing it to cool slightly at the top, making it comfortable to hold and drink. This shape also beautifully showcases the deep amber color of the tea.

Is it customary to tip in Baku tea houses?

Tipping is appreciated in Baku, especially in more upscale or tourist-oriented tea houses and restaurants. A tip of 5-10% of the bill is generally considered appropriate if service charges are not already included. For smaller, very traditional tea houses, rounding up the bill or leaving a small amount of loose change is common.

Can I find vegetarian or vegan options for tea accompaniments?

Absolutely. Traditional Azerbaijani tea accompaniments are naturally very vegetarian-friendly, largely consisting of fruit jams (murabba), dried fruits, nuts, and fresh lemons. For vegan options, you should stick to these fruit-based items and avoid pastries like baklava or shakarbura, which typically contain dairy or eggs. Always confirm with the staff if you have specific dietary requirements.

What is the best time of day to visit a tea house in Baku?

Tea is enjoyed throughout the day in Azerbaijan, but the best times for a tea house experience are mid-morning (10-11 AM) when locals have their first tea with breakfast pastries, and late afternoon (4-6 PM) when the social tea ritual is at its peak. Evening visits to Old City tea houses are also magical, with the historic setting beautifully lit.

How much does a tea house visit typically cost in Baku?

A traditional tea set with accompaniments at a budget-friendly tea house in the Old City costs AZN 5-15 (about $3-9 USD). Mid-range spots with full meals run AZN 20-40 ($12-24 USD). Upscale restaurants like Chayki or Mugam Club with live music and full dining can cost AZN 40-70+ ($24-42+ USD). Tea itself is often quite affordable even at premium venues.

Planning your Baku tea house tour

The ideal tea house crawl in Baku takes you through the Old City, along the Boulevard, and into the heart of Azerbaijani hospitality.

Start your morning with breakfast tea and fresh tandoor bread at Sehrli Tendir in the Old City. Mid-morning, walk to Art Garden for a traditional samovar tea set in the 12th-century caravanserai courtyard. After lunch, head up to Cay Bagi 145 (Tea Garden 145) for afternoon tea with panoramic views of the Maiden Tower and Caspian Sea.

In the evening, book a table at Mugam Club for live mugam music and a full Azerbaijani dining experience with tea. For a modern contrast, Zeytun Restaurant at Park Bulvar offers late-night tea with views of the illuminated Flame Towers. If you have a car, the weekend drive to Qosha Gala in Mardakan offers authentic family-style tea culture.

Budget roughly AZN 50-80 ($30-48 USD) for a full day of tea house sampling across multiple spots, or AZN 15-25 ($9-15 USD) if sticking to budget-friendly Old City tea houses like Sehrli Tendir and Cay Bagi 145.
