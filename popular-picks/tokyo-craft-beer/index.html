Frequently asked questions

Is craft beer popular in Tokyo?

Craft beer in Tokyo is a growing scene but still niche compared to the US, UK, or Australia. Japan's craft beer movement started in the mid-1990s when laws were relaxed to allow microbrewing, but growth has been slow and deliberate. The upside: bars that do exist are high-quality, lovingly run, and full of personality. Tokyo has an estimated 200+ craft beer bars and taprooms, with Shibuya, Shinjuku, Shimokitazawa, and Ryogoku as key neighborhoods.

How much does craft beer cost in Tokyo?

Expect to pay ¥800–¥1,500 ($5–$10 USD) per pint at most Tokyo craft beer bars. Happy hour specials can bring prices down to ¥500–¥700. Beer Club Popeye offers free snacks with each beer purchase during happy hour. Compared to London or New York craft beer prices, Tokyo is roughly equivalent — not cheap but not outrageous.

What are the best Japanese craft beer brands to try?

Top Japanese craft breweries include: Hitachino Nest (Kiuchi Brewery) — Japan's most internationally recognized craft brand, known for White Ale and Red Rice Ale. Yo-Ho Brewing (Yona Yona Ale) — the gateway Japanese craft beer for many. Baird Beer — Numazu-based with a legendary taproom. Minoh Beer — award-winning Osaka brewery. Shiga Kogen — from Nagano's ski country. Tokyo-based breweries like Y.Y.G., DevilCraft, and TDM 1874 are worth seeking out at their own taprooms.

What is the best area in Tokyo for craft beer?

Shibuya has the highest concentration of craft beer bars, including Goodbeer Faucets, Mikkeller Tokyo, ØL by Oslo Brewing, Stand Umineko, and Y.Y.G. Brewery all within walking distance. Shinjuku/Sendagaya has Watering Hole. Shimokitazawa has TDM 1874. Ryogoku has Beer Club Popeye. For a craft beer crawl, start in Shibuya and you can hit 4-5 bars on foot.

Can you do a craft beer bar crawl in Tokyo?

Absolutely. The best route: Start at Goodbeer Faucets (Shibuya, opens 4pm) → walk to ØL by Oslo Brewing (10 min) → Mikkeller Tokyo (5 min) → Stand Umineko SiB100 (3 min). That's 4 world-class beer bars in under an hour of walking. Alternatively, combine Watering Hole (Shinjuku) with a train to Shimokitazawa for TDM 1874 — 2 stops on the Odakyu line.

Are Tokyo craft beer bars foreigner-friendly?

Very much so. Many Tokyo craft beer bars are run by international owners (DevilCraft, Two Fingers, Mikkeller, ØL Tokyo) or have English-speaking staff. Beer Club Popeye and Goodbeer Faucets have English menus. The craft beer community in Tokyo is inherently international — expect to meet expats, tourists, and curious locals at any of these bars. Language is rarely a barrier.
