Quick answer

Auckland's Night Markets run every day of the week at different locations across the city, featuring street food from around the world at budget-friendly prices (most dishes under $10). The largest is Pakuranga on Saturday with up to 200 stalls.

Auckland's Night Markets have become a cornerstone of the city's food culture, bringing together cuisines from China, Korea, Japan, Thailand, Malaysia, Vietnam, the Philippines, India, and beyond — all at prices that won't break the bank.

Held in car parks beneath shopping centres across the city, these markets transform suburban Auckland into a buzzing street food paradise every evening. Expect sizzling skewers, steaming dumplings, Korean corndogs dripping with cheese, and sweet treats from churros to mochi — plus live entertainment from buskers, magicians, and dance troupes.

The markets operate on a rotating weekly schedule, so there's always one running somewhere in Auckland. Whether you're after the massive Saturday scene at Pakuranga, the central convenience of Silo Park, or the authentic Asian variety at Glenfield, this guide covers the 10 best locations to explore.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best food stalls to try?

Look for: Chinese dumplings ($5 for 15 is common), Korean corndogs, satay skewers, Malaysian laksa, Japanese takoyaki, Turkish gozleme, and for dessert — churros, mochi, and bubble tea. The famous hotdog store with the cheese option is consistently recommended. Ask locals in line for their favourites.

Should I bring cash or card?

Bring both, but have cash ready. Most stalls accept EFTPOS but some smaller vendors are cash-only, and card machines can have connectivity issues in the underground car parks. Having $20-40 cash ensures you won't miss out on anything.

What's the best time to arrive?

Arrive at 5:30pm when things are just getting rolling for best parking and shortest queues. Peak crowds are 6-8pm with families. After 7pm is more relaxed once younger families leave, but some popular stalls may sell out by then.

Are the markets wheelchair/stroller accessible?

Most markets are in flat car parks so generally accessible, though crowds can make navigation challenging during peak times. Silo Park being open-air is often the most accessible option.

Is there seating available?

Limited. Some markets have communal seating areas but they fill up fast. Many people eat standing or sitting on ledges. Expect to wait for a seat unless you go later (after 7pm) when family groups have departed.

What about ventilation in the car park markets?

The smoke from cooking stalls in enclosed car parks can be overwhelming for some. If this bothers you, Silo Park at Wynyard Quarter is the only fully outdoor option. Otherwise, arriving early or later when crowds are thinner helps.
