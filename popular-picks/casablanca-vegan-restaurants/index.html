Casablanca may not be the first city that springs to mind for plant-based dining, but Morocco's largest city has quietly built a compelling vegan scene that draws from both local tradition and global trends.

Moroccan cuisine is surprisingly vegan-friendly at its roots. Vegetable tagines slow-cooked with preserved lemons and olives, hearty bissara (fava bean soup), smoky zaalouk, and fresh salads are staples of the local diet. Modern Casablanca has layered health-focused cafes, organic kitchens, and international restaurants on top of this foundation, creating a dining landscape where plant-based eaters can thrive.

The best vegan-friendly neighborhoods cluster around Gauthier and Racine, where trendy cafes cater to a young, health-aware crowd. Anfa hosts upscale organic options, while the Ancienne Medina offers traditional dishes that are naturally plant-based. Along the Ain Diab corniche, casual spots and fine dining both provide ocean views with your meal.

We analyzed local reviews, expat food blogs, and community recommendations to build this guide, focusing on restaurants that either specialize in vegan cuisine or offer substantial, thoughtful plant-based menus rather than afterthought side salads.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Casablanca a vegan-friendly city?

While not traditionally a vegan hub, Casablanca is increasingly accommodating plant-based diets. A growing number of international restaurants, health-focused cafes, and establishments willing to adapt dishes make finding delicious vegan meals easier, especially in modern neighborhoods like Anfa, Gauthier, and Racine.

What are common accidentally vegan Moroccan dishes?

Traditional Moroccan cuisine offers several naturally vegan options including vegetable tagines (confirm no animal broth or butter), zaalouk (smoky eggplant dip), taktouka (cooked pepper and tomato salad), bissara (fava bean soup), and vegetable couscous. Always confirm ingredients with your server, as butter and meat stock are common additions.

How do I communicate being vegan in Casablanca?

In French, say 'Je suis végétalien(ne)' (I am vegan) and 'Sans viande, sans poisson, sans produits laitiers, sans oeufs, sans beurre, sans miel' (without meat, fish, dairy, eggs, butter, honey). In Darija you can say 'Bla l7em, bla 7lib, bla bid' (without meat, without milk, without eggs). Most staff in modern restaurants and tourist areas understand English.

Are there fully vegan restaurants in Casablanca?

Dedicated fully-vegan restaurants are still emerging in Casablanca, but several spots like Veggie and Nkoa focus heavily on plant-based menus. Many restaurants including L'Organic Kitchen, Acai Spot, and The Brunch Factory offer extensive vegan-friendly options and are happy to modify dishes. The scene is growing rapidly.

What is the typical price range for vegan meals in Casablanca?

Budget-friendly vegan meals like acai bowls, juices, and Moroccan salads run 40–100 MAD. Mid-range restaurants offering vegan burgers, bowls, or adapted tagines typically cost 80–180 MAD per person. Upscale dining with vegan-friendly options ranges from 200–500+ MAD per person. Overall, eating vegan in Casablanca is affordable compared to European cities.

Can I find vegan groceries and plant milk in Casablanca?

Yes. Major supermarkets like Marjane, Carrefour, and Acima stock plant milks (oat, soy, almond), tofu, and increasingly vegan alternatives. Organic shops in Gauthier and Anfa carry specialized vegan products. Fresh produce markets like Marché Central offer excellent quality fruits, vegetables, nuts, and olives at low prices.

Which Casablanca neighborhoods are best for vegan dining?

Gauthier and Racine are the most vegan-friendly neighborhoods, with a high concentration of modern cafes and international restaurants. Anfa has upscale health-focused spots. Ain Diab along the corniche offers casual options. The Ancienne Medina has traditional restaurants that can adapt Moroccan dishes. Maarif is a growing food scene.

Is Moroccan mint tea vegan?

Traditional Moroccan mint tea is made with green tea, fresh mint, and sugar, making it naturally vegan. However, some cafes may add honey instead of or alongside sugar, so ask for tea without honey if you want to be certain. Street vendors and traditional establishments almost always use plain sugar.
