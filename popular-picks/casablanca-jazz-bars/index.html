Casablanca may not be the first city that comes to mind when you think of jazz, but Morocco's largest metropolis has a surprisingly sophisticated live music scene.

From the cinematic nostalgia of Rick's Cafe to the panoramic heights of Sky 28, Casablanca's jazz bars span the spectrum from intimate piano lounges to full-band rooftop venues. The city's French colonial heritage blends with Moroccan hospitality to create an atmosphere that feels both cosmopolitan and distinctly North African. Hotel bars in particular are where jazz thrives — the Hyatt Regency, Four Seasons, Sofitel, and Kenzi Tower all host regular live music programs.

We researched local travel forums, community reviews, and travel guides to identify the venues where Casablanca's jazz scene comes alive. Every venue was verified for current operations, music programming, and accessibility as of April 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best night to experience jazz in Casablanca?

While some venues like Rick's Cafe offer live piano jazz nightly, many other bars feature their main jazz acts or live bands on Thursday, Friday, and Saturday evenings. Weekends are generally the best time to find the most vibrant music scenes and full bands.

Is there a dress code for jazz bars in Casablanca?

Most upscale jazz bars and hotel lounges in Casablanca, such as Sky 28 or the Four Seasons' Le Living Bar, prefer smart-casual attire. For places like Rick's Cafe or Le Cabestan, it's best to dress a bit more formally. More casual spots like La Bodega have a relaxed dress code.

Do I need reservations for jazz bars in Casablanca?

For popular and upscale venues like Rick's Cafe, Le Cabestan, or any hotel lounge, especially on weekend evenings, reservations are highly recommended. For smaller or more casual spots, you might be able to walk in, but it is always safer to call ahead to ensure availability and confirm live music schedules.

Are jazz bars in Casablanca expensive?

Prices vary widely depending on the venue. Upscale hotel bars and fine dining lounges can be quite expensive, with cocktails often costing 100–200 MAD (~$10–20 USD). More casual spots like La Bodega offer more budget-friendly options. Factor in the cost of drinks and potentially small bites.

Can I find traditional Moroccan music in Casablanca's jazz bars?

While most listed venues focus on jazz, international, or lounge music, some places like Maison B or even La Bodega might occasionally incorporate Moroccan influences or traditional instruments into their sets, especially fusion bands. However, dedicated traditional Moroccan music venues would be a separate category.

Are there any age restrictions for Casablanca's jazz bars?

Yes, in Morocco, the legal drinking age is 18. Most bars and lounges will strictly enforce this, especially for evening hours. You may be asked for identification at the entrance, so it is always advisable to carry a valid ID if you look under 25.

What is the Jazzablanca festival?

Jazzablanca is Casablanca's premier annual jazz festival, running since 2006. It brings international and local jazz artists to multiple stages across the city, typically held in late June or early July. It is one of the biggest jazz events in North Africa and attracts thousands of music lovers each year.

Is Casablanca safe for nightlife?

Casablanca is generally safe for nightlife, especially in upscale hotel bars and established venues on the Corniche and around Place des Nations Unies. Use standard precautions: take a licensed taxi or ride-hailing app (Careem or InDriver) home, avoid walking alone in poorly lit areas late at night, and keep valuables secure.

Planning your Casablanca jazz evening

The ideal jazz crawl in Casablanca takes you from an early sunset cocktail to a late-night lounge session.

Start at Sky 28 around 6 PM for sunset cocktails and the live jazz band, with panoramic views of the city transitioning to night. Around 8 PM, head to Rick's Cafe for dinner and the iconic piano experience — book ahead for a table near the pianist. After dinner, take a taxi to the Corniche for Le Cabestan's cliff-top cocktails with ocean sounds as a backdrop, or wind down at the Four Seasons' Le Living Bar for bespoke drinks in a plush setting.

For a more casual evening, La Bodega offers tapas and gypsy-jazz fusion from early evening, and Maison B picks up the energy after 10 PM with its lounge-to-club transition. If you prefer a refined, quiet experience, La Cascade at the Hyatt is perfect for a pre-dinner coffee with background piano jazz.

Budget roughly 400–800 MAD ($40–80 USD) per person for an evening covering 2–3 venues, including drinks and a light bite. Taxis between venues cost 20–40 MAD. Casablanca's nightlife typically gets going around 9 PM and runs until 1–2 AM on weekends.
