Quick answer

Sofia, Bulgaria's bustling capital, might not be the first city that springs to mind when you crave a slice, but its pizza scene is surprisingly diverse and utterly delicious. From authentic Neapolitan pies with blistered crusts to thin-crust Bulgarian interpretations piled high with local ingredients, the city offers a fantastic journey for any pizza enthusiast.

The love affair with pizza in Sofia has evolved significantly since its post-communist introduction. Initially, it was a novelty, often simplified for local palates. However, over the past decade, a wave of passionate pizzaiolos, many trained in Italy, have elevated the craft, bringing authentic techniques and high-quality imported ingredients to the forefront, alongside innovative local adaptations.

You'll find a wide array of styles across Sofia: classic Neapolitan with its soft, airy crust; Roman-style 'pizza al taglio' perfect for a quick bite; American-inspired New York slices; and the local 'pizza bulgare,' which often features a thinner crust and unique toppings like 'lukanka' (a spicy cured sausage) or 'sirene' (feta-like cheese).

Frequently asked questions

What is the typical price range for pizza in Sofia?

The price for a medium-sized pizza in Sofia generally ranges from 15 BGN to 35 BGN ($8-$19 USD). More upscale or authentic Neapolitan places might have pizzas up to 45 BGN ($25 USD), while budget-friendly options can start around 10-12 BGN ($5-$7 USD). Drinks and appetizers would be additional.

Are there vegetarian or vegan pizza options readily available?

Yes, most pizza places in Sofia offer several vegetarian options, typically including Margherita, Quattro Formaggi, or various vegetable combinations. Vegan options are becoming increasingly common, especially at modern or customizable spots like Pizza Lab, where you can select dairy-free cheese or abundant vegetable toppings. It's always a good idea to inquire with the staff.

What's the difference between Neapolitan and Bulgarian-style pizza in Sofia?

Neapolitan pizza in Sofia strictly follows Italian traditions: a soft, airy, often charred crust cooked quickly in a wood-fired oven, with minimal, high-quality toppings. Bulgarian-style pizza, on the other hand, tends to have a thinner, crispier crust and more generous, diverse toppings that might include local ingredients like lukanka sausage, sirene cheese, or yellow cheese (kashkaval).

Do I need to make reservations for pizza restaurants in Sofia?

For highly-rated, popular, or more upscale Italian restaurants, especially L'Opera and Napoletana on weekend evenings, reservations are strongly recommended to avoid waiting. For casual eateries, fast-casual spots, or chains like Pizza Lab, Victoria, or Happy Bar & Grill, reservations are generally not needed, and walk-ins are welcome.

Is tap water safe to drink in Sofia restaurants?

Yes, tap water in Sofia is generally safe to drink, and many restaurants will provide it upon request. However, bottled water is also widely available and often preferred by locals and tourists alike. Don't hesitate to ask for 'cheshmyana voda' (tap water) if you prefer.

Are gluten-free pizza options available?

Gluten-free pizza options are becoming more common in Sofia, particularly at restaurants catering to a broader international clientele. While not every pizzeria will offer them, it's worth checking with places like Pizza Lab, or larger chains, as they are more likely to have dedicated gluten-free crusts. Always confirm with the staff regarding cross-contamination concerns.

What's the best way to get around Sofia to visit these pizza places?

Sofia has an efficient public transport network of metro, trams, and buses that can take you near most locations. Walking is also a great option for central areas like Vitosha Blvd. For convenience, ride-sharing apps like Uber and Bolt are widely used, offering affordable and quick transportation across the city, especially to areas further out like Studentski Grad.

Can I expect English-speaking staff at pizza restaurants?

In most popular pizza restaurants, especially those in tourist-frequented areas or larger chains, you can expect to find English-speaking staff, particularly younger employees. While not guaranteed everywhere, communication should generally not be a significant issue, and menus are often available in English.
