Quick answer

Chicago isn't just a pizza town; it's a burger lover's paradise, boasting a vibrant and competitive patty landscape that ranges from high-brow gourmet creations to no-frills, perfectly executed classics. Whether you're after a greasy late-night snack or a sophisticated, multi-layered masterpiece, the Windy City has a burger joint calling your name.

Top verdicts

  • Au Cheval: Foodies and burger connoisseurs seeking an iconic, perfectly executed gourmet diner burger experience.
  • Small Cheval: Anyone craving a top-tier classic cheeseburger without the wait or formality of a full-service restaurant.
  • The Loyalist: Diners seeking a truly indulgent, high-end burger in a more refined, date-night appropriate atmosphere.

The burger's journey in Chicago mirrors the city's own development, from humble diner staples served to factory workers to today's elevated gastropub fare. Its enduring popularity is rooted in its versatility and universal appeal, crossing cultural and economic divides to remain a beloved comfort food for all.

You'll find an astonishing array of burger styles here: from the iconic smash burger with its crispy edges and melty cheese, to thick, juicy gastropub patties, inventive Asian-fusion creations, and even classic depression-era griddle burgers. The city's chefs are constantly pushing boundaries, experimenting with toppings, buns, and patty blends, ensuring there's always something new and exciting to discover.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between a smash burger and a regular burger?

A smash burger is made by pressing a ball of ground beef onto a hot griddle, creating a thin patty with crispy, caramelized edges and a juicy interior. A regular burger, or pub-style burger, is typically formed into a thicker patty before cooking, resulting in a more uniform texture and often a pinker center.

Are there any vegetarian or vegan burger options at these top spots?

While this guide focuses on meat burgers, many of the listed venues do offer vegetarian or vegan alternatives. Kuma's Corner, Bad Apple, and Mott St are known for having solid plant-based burger options. It's always best to check their current menus or call ahead to confirm availability and ingredients.

Is it customary to tip at burger restaurants in Chicago?

Yes, it is customary to tip at sit-down restaurants in Chicago, including most of the mid-range and gourmet burger spots. A tip of 18-20% for good service is standard. For fast-casual or counter-service places where you pick up your food (like Redhot Ranch or Portillo's), tipping is appreciated but not mandatory, typically a few dollars in a tip jar or 10-15% for exceptional service.

What are the typical price ranges for burgers in Chicago?

Burger prices in Chicago vary widely based on the venue type. At budget-friendly, classic spots like Redhot Ranch or Portillo's, you can expect to pay $7-$15 for a burger. Mid-range gastropubs and casual eateries like Kuma's or Small Cheval typically charge $15-$25. Gourmet and upscale options like Au Cheval, The Loyalist, or Mott St can range from $25-$40, often reflecting higher-quality ingredients and the overall dining experience.

How do Chicago burgers compare to those in other major U.S. cities?

Chicago's burger scene is renowned for its diversity and quality, often emphasizing a balance of classic techniques with innovative, chef-driven creations. While cities like NYC excel in classic deli-style burgers and LA has a strong In-N-Out culture, Chicago stands out with its robust gastropub offerings, unique cultural fusion burgers, and a strong appreciation for the 'elevated diner' burger epitomized by places like Au Cheval.

Are Chicago-style hot dogs and burgers related?

While both are beloved Chicago street foods, they are distinct. Chicago-style hot dogs are iconic for their unique combination of toppings (yellow mustard, chopped white onions, bright green sweet pickle relish, a dill pickle spear, tomato slices or wedges, pickled sport peppers, and a dash of celery salt). Burgers, while equally popular, don't adhere to a single 'Chicago style' in the same way, allowing for more variation in toppings and preparation.

Can I find late-night burger options in Chicago?

Yes, Chicago offers several great late-night burger options. Many bars and gastropubs, including some on our list like Au Cheval and Mott St (check their specific late-night menus), serve food well into the evening, often past midnight. Fast-casual spots or diners can also be reliable choices for late-night cravings.

What's the best neighborhood for a burger crawl in Chicago?

The West Loop is arguably the best neighborhood for a high-end burger crawl, boasting both Au Cheval and The Loyalist within walking distance. Wicker Park also offers excellent options with Small Cheval and Mott St. For a more diverse and spread-out experience, exploring different neighborhoods with public transport will yield the best results.
