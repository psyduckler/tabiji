15 Best Tapas Bars in Seville

Seville is the spiritual home of tapas. Not the Instagram-friendly small plates you get in Barcelona or Madrid — real tapas. Standing at a crowded bar, ordering rounds of montaditos and croquetas, spilling wine on your shoes, then walking two doors down to do it all again. A proper Seville tapas crawl is one of the best food experiences in Europe.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need reservations for tapas bars in Seville?

For highly popular modern tapas bars like <a href="#contenedor"><strong>ConTenedor</strong></a> or <a href="#espacio-eslava"><strong>Espacio Eslava</strong></a>, reservations are strongly recommended, especially for dinner. Many traditional spots, such as <a href="#el-rinconcillo"><strong>El Rinconcillo</strong></a> or <a href="#bodeguita-romero"><strong>Bodeguita Romero</strong></a>, usually operate on a walk-in basis, so arriving early is your best bet to grab a table or a spot at the bar.

What's the difference between traditional and modern tapas in Seville?

Traditional tapas, found at places like <a href="#casa-morales"><strong>Casa Morales</strong></a> or <a href="#antonio-romero"><strong>Antonio Romero</strong></a>, are classic, often simpler dishes reflecting Andalusian culinary heritage (e.g., jamón, patatas bravas, espinacas con garbanzos). Modern tapas, offered by <a href="#el-traga"><strong>El Traga</strong></a> or <a href="#la-brunilda"><strong>La Brunilda</strong></a>, feature innovative ingredients, fusion flavors, and creative presentations, pushing the boundaries of classic Spanish cuisine.

How much does a tapas meal cost in Seville?

The cost varies significantly. Budget-friendly traditional bars like <a href="#bar-vizcaino"><strong>Bar Vizcaíno</strong></a> or <a href="#bodeguita-romero"><strong>Bodeguita Romero</strong></a> can offer tapas for €2-€5 each. Mid-range modern places like <a href="#sal-gorda"><strong>Sal Gorda</strong></a> or <a href="#duo-tapas"><strong>Duo Tapas</strong></a> typically charge €6-€12. For a splurge experience at places like <a href="#canabota"><strong>Cañabota</strong></a> or <a href="#el-traga"><strong>El Traga</strong></a>, expect to pay €10-€20 per tapa, particularly for premium ingredients.

Can I do a tapas crawl in Seville, and how does it work?

Absolutely! A tapas crawl (or 'tapeo') is a quintessential Seville experience. The idea is to visit several bars, having one or two tapas and a drink at each, rather than a full meal at one place. It’s a great way to sample diverse flavors and soak in different atmospheres. Consider a route through the Arenal district with <a href="#bodeguita-romero"><strong>Bodeguita Romero</strong></a> and <a href="#antonio-romero"><strong>Antonio Romero</strong></a> for a classic crawl.

Are tapas portions always small, or are there larger options?

Tapas are indeed small, individual portions designed for sharing and sampling. However, most bars, including <a href="#blanco-cerrillo"><strong>Blanco Cerrillo</strong></a>, also offer 'media ración' (half portion) and 'ración' (full portion) options, which are larger plates suitable for sharing among a group or for a more substantial meal. Always check the menu or ask your server for clarification on sizes.

What are some must-try traditional tapas in Seville?

When in Seville, seek out classics like 'espinacas con garbanzos' (spinach with chickpeas), 'solomillo al whisky' (pork loin in whisky sauce), 'montadito de pringá' (stew meat sandwich), and 'jamón ibérico'. Many traditional bars, such as <a href="#el-rinconcillo"><strong>El Rinconcillo</strong></a> and <a href="#casa-morales"><strong>Casa Morales</strong></a>, are excellent places to try these time-honored dishes.
