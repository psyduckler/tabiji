Quick answer

Denver is rapidly establishing itself as a haven for plant-based eaters, with its vibrant food scene embracing vegetarian and vegan cuisine with open arms. From cozy cafes serving inventive brunches to upscale eateries crafting sophisticated plant-forward dinners, the Mile High City offers a delicious journey for those seeking meat-free options.

Top verdicts

  • City O' City: Anyone looking for reliable, delicious, and diverse vegetarian and vegan comfort food at any time of day.
  • Watercourse Foods: Those seeking a delicious vegan brunch or a comforting, yet elevated, plant-based dinner in a bright setting.
  • Somebody People: Diners looking for a stylish, modern vegan dining experience with innovative, seasonal dishes and creative cocktails.

The evolution of Denver's dining culture has seen a significant shift towards more inclusive and sustainable eating. Historically known for its steak houses, the city has undergone a culinary revolution, driven by a younger, health-conscious, and environmentally aware population. This has fostered a fertile ground for innovative chefs and restaurateurs to champion vegetarian and vegan menus, moving beyond simple salads to truly inspiring dishes.

Today, you'll find an incredible diversity in Denver's vegetarian offerings. Whether you're craving globally-inspired street food, classic American comfort fare, healthy and holistic bowls, or elegant fine dining experiences, the city delivers. Expect to see influences from Mediterranean, Asian, Latin American, and traditional American cuisines, all given a creative plant-based twist.

Frequently asked questions

Are there many vegan-only restaurants in Denver, or mostly vegetarian with vegan options?

Denver has a growing number of dedicated vegan restaurants, with notable examples including Watercourse Foods, Somebody People, Vital Root, and Meta Burger. Many other vegetarian-friendly establishments, like City O' City, also offer extensive and clearly labeled vegan menus, making it easy for strict vegans to find delicious options across the city.

Do Denver's vegetarian restaurants cater to other dietary restrictions like gluten-free?

Yes, most vegetarian and vegan restaurants in Denver are highly accommodating to other dietary restrictions, especially gluten-free needs. They often clearly label GF options on their menus or have staff who can guide you. It's always best to inform your server about any allergies or dietary requirements when ordering.

Is vegetarian dining in Denver expensive?

Denver's vegetarian dining scene offers options across all price points. You can find very affordable and delicious meals at places like Vital Root and Meta Burger (typically $15-25 per person), mid-range options at City O' City and Watercourse Foods ($20-40), and upscale experiences at Somebody People, Root Down, or Linger ($35-65), ensuring there's something for every budget.

Are these restaurants generally kid-friendly?

Many of the listed restaurants are kid-friendly, especially the more casual spots like City O' City, Watercourse Foods, Vital Root, and Meta Burger, which offer approachable menus and relaxed atmospheres. Upscale establishments like Root Down or Linger might be better suited for older children or special occasions.

Can I find healthy and light vegetarian options, or is it mostly comfort food?

Denver's vegetarian scene strikes a great balance. While you can certainly indulge in fantastic comfort food at City O' City or Watercourse Foods, places like Vital Root and Corner Beet specialize in healthy, fresh, and often organic dishes, including vibrant bowls, salads, and juices. Many other restaurants offer lighter options alongside heartier fare.

Do I need to make reservations in advance for most of these places?

For the more popular or upscale restaurants like Somebody People, Root Down, and Linger, especially for dinner or weekend brunch, reservations are highly recommended. For casual spots like City O' City, Watercourse Foods, Meta Burger, Vital Root, or Corner Beet, reservations are typically not necessary, but be prepared for potential waits during peak hours.

Are there vegetarian options available for different cuisines, like Asian or Mexican?

Absolutely! Denver's vegetarian scene is very diverse. You'll find delicious vegetarian and vegan Asian cuisine at Veggie Fun, Caribbean flavors at Bang Up to the Elephant!, Mediterranean at Somebody People, and globally-inspired dishes at Vital Root and Linger. Many general restaurants in Denver also offer excellent plant-based takes on Mexican and other international cuisines.

What's the best time of year to visit Denver for the food scene?

Denver's food scene is vibrant year-round. However, late spring through early fall (May-October) is particularly pleasant as many restaurants, including Root Down and Linger, open their outdoor patios, allowing for al fresco dining with beautiful mountain or city views. This period also coincides with the availability of fresh, local produce highlighted in many farm-to-table menus.
