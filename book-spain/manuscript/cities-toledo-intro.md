Toledo is 40 minutes by AVE from Madrid, a UNESCO World Heritage walled city, and one of the most day-trip-able monuments in Spain — which means its scam scene is dominated by freelance taxis and tour-guide touts at exactly the two choke points where day-trippers arrive: the Puerta de Bisagra and the AVE station.

The **AVE-station freelance-taxi flat-rate quote** is the volume scam. The regulated metered fare from Toledo AVE into the old city — a 2-km hop up the switchback road to the Plaza de Zocodover or the Alcázar — is 5.80 € at daytime rates. Off-rank freelancers quote "20 €, straight to your hotel" to day-trippers who don't want to queue. The official rank is directly outside the station exit; the municipal taxi fare chart is posted in Spanish and English.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. **Damascene-souvenir shops** on Calle del Comercio pitch "handmade *Toledano* steel" inlaid with gold thread; most pieces sold on the main strip are machine-stamped imports. Authentic Damascene workshops — where you can watch the etching and inlay — are clustered on Calle de Santo Tomé near the Museo del Greco. **Santo Tomé photo-tour "helpers"** around the *Entierro del Conde de Orgaz* (El Greco's masterpiece at Iglesia de Santo Tomé) offer to take your picture and demand a tip; the church has a posted "no photography inside" rule that a real guide would not suggest breaking. And **Plaza de Zocodover sketchy-guide approaches** attach to small groups at the Alcázar and collect 15 € each for a "walk" that never materializes. A safe default: take the official AVE-station taxi rank at the posted rate; buy Alcázar and Catedral tickets only at **catedralprimada.es** and the official Alcázar website; watch Damascene being made before buying it; ignore every clipboard and every "let me take your photo" approach; and call **091** or **112**. Toledo's municipal tourist-information office at Plaza del Consistorio handles complaints in English during office hours.
