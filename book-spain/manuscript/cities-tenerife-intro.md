Tenerife is the largest Canary Island, the biggest package-holiday destination in Europe's off-season, and — thanks to two decades of British and Irish reporting — home to some of the most-documented timeshare and rental scams in the Mediterranean rim. Most of it is concentrated in Los Cristianos, Playa de las Américas, and Costa Adeje on the sunny south coast.

The **timeshare "free gift" lure** is the volume scam. Young staff in branded T-shirts work the Las Américas and Los Cristianos promenades with a "free scratch card" that everyone wins; the prize is a "three-hour resort presentation" that turns into a high-pressure sales session for a Canaries timeshare you can't unwind without a 14-day EU-directive cooling-off period. British tabloid *The Sun* and *The Times* have documented the playbook in repeated 2024–2025 coverage. Polite refusal is sometimes enough; sustained refusal requires walking away mid-pitch.

The risk radiates out in four corridors. **Playa de las Américas jet-ski damage claims** at 800–1,500 € match the Ibiza and Mallorca script. **Mount Teide "private-guide" approaches** at the Teleférico del Teide cable-car base offer 30 €-per-head guided hikes that skirt the official park-ranger Volcán Teide guided walks (which require an advance permit — always). **Santa Cruz carnival-season taxi flat-fares** during the February carnival explode to double the posted rate if you don't watch the meter. And **Los Cristianos "Canarian cellar" wine-tour scams** sell all-day wine-country trips that spend forty minutes at a cellar and four hours at affiliated shops pressuring Canarian-wine boxes onto your credit card. A safe default: refuse every "free scratch card" on the promenade; book Teide National Park permits in advance at **reservasparquesnacionales.es**; photograph jet-ski and moped rentals at pickup; pay everything with a chargebackable credit card; and call **092** (Policía Local Arona covers Las Américas) or **112** for help in English. Costa Adeje's tourist-help desk is on Avenida Rafael Puig.
