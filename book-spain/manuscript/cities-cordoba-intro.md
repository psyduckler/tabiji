Córdoba is small, compact, and — for visitors who stay inside the Judería and at the Mezquita — one of the safer major Andalusian cities. What scams happen, happen in tight, predictable locations. The **Mezquita-Catedral entrance rosemary-sprig variant** at the Patio de los Naranjos runs the same script as Seville's Plaza de España: a sprig is pressed into your hand as a "gift," followed by a palm-reading and a 15–20 € ask. The volume is lower than Seville because the Mezquita plaza is narrower and Policía Local maintains a visible presence during peak hours, but the pattern is identical and the defense is the same: a firm "*no, gracias*" before your hand accepts anything.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. **Judería fake tour-guides** offer "official Judería walks" at 10–15 € per head that include no ticket to the Mezquita, the Sinagoga, or the Alcázar de los Reyes Cristianos — the three sites most tourists actually want to see. **Roman-Bridge photo "helpers"** offer to take your picture with the Torre de la Calahorra in the background, then demand 5–10 € per shot after returning the phone, often working as a distraction team: while you negotiate, a partner lifts from the bag you set down. And **Plaza de las Tendillas charity petitions** — the "deaf-and-mute" clipboard approach — run the same Madrid Plaza Mayor script but at lower volume during the lunch-hour lull. A safe default: buy Mezquita tickets only at **mezquita-catedraldecordoba.es**; refuse every rosemary sprig; politely decline unsolicited photo offers and hand your phone only to a fellow tourist on your own terms; ignore every clipboard; and call **091** (Policía Nacional) or **112** if an approach turns physical. The Córdoba SATE for tourist complaints is **+34 957 205 511** during office hours.
