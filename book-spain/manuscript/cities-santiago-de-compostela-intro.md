Santiago de Compostela is the end of the Camino and, for most pilgrims and tourists, the emotional high point of a Galician trip. It is also, statistically, one of Spain's lowest-volume scam cities — a combination of its relatively modest non-Camino tourist flow, the slow pace of Galician life, and the Cathedral-area Policía Nacional presence. What happens, happens in four narrow clusters: fake-*Compostela* certificate offers near the Obradoiro, Rúa do Franco per-person restaurant minimums that surprise two-person tables, knockoff pilgrim-shell jewelry sold as silver, and Cathedral-area "blessing" cash asks from non-clerical approaches.

The **fake-*Compostela* certificate scam** is the most persistent. The authentic *Compostela* — the Latin certificate attesting completion of the Camino — is issued free by the Cathedral's Oficina del Peregrino at Rúa Carretas 33, with a 3 € supplement for the upgraded-paper *Certificado de Distancia*. Street touts outside the Obradoiro and on Rúa das Hortas sell "ornate" certificates at 20 €, sometimes with a fake seal, sometimes as printed color copies of the real thing. Refusing is simple — the real office is visibly signposted and the queue moves in under thirty minutes.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. **Rúa do Franco per-person minimums** on the main restaurant strip — "menú del peregrino, 15 €" — sometimes carry an unlisted 2-person minimum charge, so two travelers pay 60 € for what looked like 30 €. **Knockoff pilgrim-shell jewelry** sold as silver on Plaza das Praterías turns out to be plated alloy. And **post-Mass cathedral-area "blessing" cash asks** by non-clerical approaches (the real clergy never asks for cash on the street) sometimes coax 5–10 € from pilgrims still in an emotional state. A safe default: collect your *Compostela* only at the Oficina del Peregrino; ask any Rúa do Franco waiter if the menu price is a per-person minimum before ordering; buy silver only from shops on Rúa do Vilar with visible hallmark certifications; ignore every "blessing" cash ask; and call **091** or **112** for help in English.
