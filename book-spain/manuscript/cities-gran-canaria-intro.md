Gran Canaria is Tenerife's quieter sister — smaller package-holiday market, less media coverage of timeshare scams, but a near-identical surface-area in the three main resort strips: Playa del Inglés, Maspalomas, and Puerto Rico. The scam mix skews heavily to beach-chair and sunset-cruise traps, with jet-ski and scooter-damage running at a lower volume than on Ibiza.

The **Maspalomas beach-chair per-hour trap** is the volume scam. The iconic chairs along the dunes in Maspalomas are quoted at "8 €" on the board; that means 8 € per hour, and the tally runs at sunset. Tourists who settled in at 2 p.m. are billed 48 € at 8 p.m. The defense is a two-word question before sitting: "*¿Por hora o todo el día?*" ("per hour or all day?").

The risk radiates out in four corridors. **Playa del Inglés jet-ski damage claims** at 800–1,500 € match the Canary mainland script. **Las Palmas taxi "special airport rate"** quotes a flat 45 € for a regulated 25 € ride from LPA Airport to the center. **Puerto Rico catamaran-cruise "alcohol included"** baits the headline and adds per-glass 10 € supplements after the initial included pour. And **Playa del Inglés scooter-scratch shakedowns** match Ibiza's — 400 € deposits returned minus 250 € for a mirror-scratch that existed at pickup. A safe default: confirm every beach-chair rate as per-hour-or-all-day in writing before sitting; take the Global **Bus 30 or Bus 1** from LPA Airport to central Las Palmas for 3.40 € rather than the off-rank taxi touts; photograph jet-ski and scooter rentals at pickup; and call **091** (Policía Nacional Las Palmas) or **112**. Gran Canaria's SATE-equivalent tourist-help for the south-coast resort strip operates from the Maspalomas Yumbo Centrum.
