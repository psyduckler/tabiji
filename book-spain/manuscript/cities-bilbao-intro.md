Bilbao is the Basque Country's biggest city and, by reported figures, among Spain's safest major urban destinations for tourists. The Ertzaintza (the Basque regional police) logs a fraction of Barcelona's or Madrid's pickpocket volume, and the Casco Viejo is well-policed during peak *pintxo* hours. What happens, happens in three tight clusters: verbal-tab uncertainty at *pintxo* bars in the old town, fake Ertzaintza-vest "ID checks" near Gran Vía Metro Bilbao, and taxi flat-fare quotes for the 20-minute run from BIO Airport into Abando station.

The **Casco Viejo *pintxo* verbal-tab scam** exploits a genuine regional tradition: in Basque *pintxo* culture, you eat from the counter plates and pay at the end, with the bartender trusting your count. Most bars run this honor system without issue; a small number of tourist-facing bars on Plaza Nueva round the count aggressively — a plate of four ends up billed as six, and the math happens in Basque-language shorthand you can't verify. The defense is to order from the kitchen menu rather than the counter, ask for a visible tally, and photograph what you've eaten.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Gran Vía Metro Bilbao fake-ID check** is the Basque variant of the Madrid Gran Vía fake-plainclothes scam: two men in hi-vis vests ask for your passport and cash "to verify a counterfeit report." The **BIO Airport flat-rate taxi** quote runs 40–50 € for a metered 20–25 € ride. And **Plaza Nueva sketchy-bar cover** tricks quote "no cover" and then transition to a 120–200 € bottle-minimum. A safe default: take the **Bizkaibus A3247** (3 €) from BIO to central Bilbao — fastest, cheapest, and scam-free; use the official taxi rank if you prefer; ignore any hi-vis vest "ID check" and walk directly into the Metro Bilbao station; order *pintxos* from the kitchen menu with prices visible; and call **112** (pan-European emergency) or the Ertzaintza on **944 066 000**. Bilbao's municipal tourist-help desk is in Plaza Circular, next to the Guggenheim-bound bus stop.
