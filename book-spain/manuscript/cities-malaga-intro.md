Málaga is the gateway to the Costa del Sol and one of Spain's fastest-growing cruise and airport hubs. Its scam ecosystem reflects the volume: airport-and-transport scams at the AGP arrivals terminal, beach-chair escalations on La Malagueta, rental-car fuel-gauge disputes at the car-hire cluster, and a low-rent "charity petition" scene on Calle Larios during peak shopping hours.

The **Málaga Airport freelance-taxi quote** is the volume scam. The regulated rank outside Terminal 3 charges a metered fare of roughly 20–25 € to central Málaga, with a 4 € airport supplement that is legal and posted. Off-rank freelancers approach solo or small-group arrivals inside the terminal with a "30 €, cash only" offer that tourists accept to avoid the line; Policía Local has signposted this in four languages at arrivals, but the approach persists. The **La Malagueta beach-chair per-hour trap** runs the same script as the Costa del Sol: "10 €" on the chair sign means 10 € per hour, and the vendor tallies at sunset. The **Málaga Airport car-rental fuel-gauge dispute** is the return-counter variant: the agent records the tank as 7/8 even when you've just filled up at the Cepsa station two minutes away, and bills the "missing" fuel at triple the pump rate.

A safe default: take the **Cercanías C-1 train** from the airport to María Zambrano station at 2.05 € — fastest, cheapest, and completely scam-free — or queue for the official taxi rank at Terminal 3; use the posted "official rate" card as your reference; photograph your rental car's fuel gauge at pickup and return and keep the Cepsa receipt; ask for the per-hour beach-chair rate in writing before sitting down; and call **091** or **112** for help in English. Málaga's SATE tourist-help number is **+34 951 926 010**.
