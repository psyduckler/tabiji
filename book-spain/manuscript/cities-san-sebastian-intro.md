San Sebastián (*Donostia* in Euskara) is Europe's third-highest Michelin-star-per-capita city after Kyoto and Tokyo, and its Parte Vieja is one of the greatest *pintxo* walks on earth. As a scam destination it is, by Ertzaintza figures, quiet — meaningfully quieter than Madrid or Barcelona. What happens, happens in three narrow clusters: overpriced "displayed" *pintxos* at a handful of tourist-strip bars in the Parte Vieja, La Concha beach-chair per-hour tallies, and Monte Igueldo funicular-area freelance "guides" who attach to groups.

The **Parte Vieja *pintxo* overpricing trap** is the volume scam. Genuine *pintxo* culture charges 2–4 € per piece, with hot kitchen orders (*pintxos calientes*) priced higher than displayed cold pieces. A small number of Calle 31 de Agosto and Fermín Calbetón bars price the "premium" displayed pieces — the ones with the visible jamón, the small steak skewer, the langoustine-on-bread — at 6–10 € per piece, without the price on the plate or the board. You build a plate of six in the dim evening light and the bill is 42 €. The defense is to stick to bars with a written *pintxo* list (common in San Sebastián, and a reliable legitimacy signal); **La Cuchara de San Telmo**, **Borda Berri**, and **Bar Nestor** are all trustworthy standard-bearers with published prices.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. **La Concha beach-chair per-hour tallies** charge "6 €" that means 6 € per hour; the evening tally runs 36 €. **Monte Igueldo funicular "guides"** attach to small groups at the funicular base station and demand 10–15 € tips for "guiding" to the overlook that is literally at the top of a single staircase. And **Boulevard-promenade "charity" clipboards** during summer high season run the same Madrid deaf-and-mute pocket-lift. A safe default: order *pintxos* only from bars with visible prices and keep a running tally on your phone; confirm beach-chair rates in writing; ignore every Igueldo tout (the funicular ticket already includes the scenic overlook); and call **112** or the Ertzaintza on **943 449 300**. San Sebastián's municipal tourism desk at Alameda del Boulevard 6 handles tourist complaints in English during office hours.
