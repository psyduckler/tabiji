Santorini is Greece's most photographed island — and its most expensive tourist trap. The caldera-view restaurants in Oia and Fira charge €15 for bread you never ordered, €8 for a cappuccino that costs €3 in Athens, and run the per-kilogram fish script with fish priced at €100–€140/kg where mainland prices are €40. The 2024 community warnings on r/GreeceTravel about Santorini overcharging are so consistent they could fill this chapter twice over.

The risk concentrates in three zones. Athinios port, where ferries arrive, runs the taxi-quote racket: €40 for the 15-minute ride to Fira when the legitimate fare is €25. Oia's caldera restaurants — the ones with the sunset views — run welcome-bread markup and fish-by-weight surprises that produce €200+ bills for two. Fira's main drag (Dekigala and the caldera path) runs souvenir-markup and the same restaurant patterns. For ATV rentals, small storefronts in Fira quote damage claims on pre-existing scratches, particularly at the end of your rental. A safe default: take the KTEL bus from Athinios to Fira (€2.50), eat one block back from the caldera at posted-price tavernas with 4.4+ Google ratings, photograph every scratch on any rental before driving off, and refuse all welcome bread and olives — "*Óchi, efcharistó*" and push them back.
