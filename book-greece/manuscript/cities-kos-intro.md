Kos is the third-largest Dodecanese island, immediately west of Bodrum, Turkey, and one of the heaviest UK + German package-tourism receivers in Greece. Three resort strips dominate the foreign visitor map: Kardamena on the south coast (the British party-tourism epicenter), Tigaki on the north coast, and Mastichari on the northwest where the daily Bodrum ferry departs. Kos Town itself — the old harbor and the Asclepieion archaeological site — is the cultural core. The scam economy is a hybrid of Mediterranean clip-joint patterns and the kind of UK-package-strip exploitation that the Daily Mail and the Mirror have flagged in recurring summer exposés.

The risk radiates from four corridors. At Kos Airport (KGS), the taxi cartel runs per-person flat-rate quotes that triple the legal metered fare for "hotel booking" transfers — the same ride at the meter would cost a quarter as much. On the Kardamena resort strip, scooter and ATV rental shops run a damage-deposit cycle: €200–€400 cash deposits "held" against pre-existing scratches, with returns "discovered" on the lower bumper or under the seat. The Mastichari–Bodrum day-trip ferry has its own variant — touts at the port confuse single-day visitors about Turkish visa requirements, then upsell €60+ "with-visa-assistance" packages over the standard €40 walk-on fare. In Kos Town's old quarter, tourist-menu cover-charge padding at the harbor cafés layers €10–€20 of unrequested bread, water, and "cutlery" onto bills. A safe default: book Kos Airport transfers through your hotel with a written quote, rent only from major airport brands (Avis, Europcar, Hertz, Sixt), confirm the Bodrum ferry visa rules at the official tourist information office before paying any "assistance" markup, and refuse all unrequested cover items at restaurants — Greek consumer law bans them.
