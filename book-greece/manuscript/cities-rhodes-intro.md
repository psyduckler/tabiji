Rhodes is the largest of the Dodecanese islands, and its UNESCO-listed Old Town — a complete medieval walled city built by the Knights of St. John — is one of the most remarkable historic sites in the Mediterranean. It is also one of the most concentrated tourist-menu zones in Greece. The Old Town's narrow cobbled streets run restaurant after restaurant with menus that show no prices, fish priced per kilogram at €80–€120, and welcome bread and olives added to every bill.

The risk concentrates in the Old Town's main arteries — Sokratous Street and the streets leading from the Palace of the Grand Master to the harbour. The patterns are the same as Athens and Santorini: per-kg fish, unordered bread, inflated coffee prices. Rhodes Town's New Town runs the taxi-overcharge pattern from the port and airport. For car and scooter rentals, the storefronts along Ialyssos Beach and in Lindos run the damage-claim script. A safe default: confirm fish prices in writing before ordering, eat at posted-price tavernas with long Google review histories (Ta Kardasia, Tamam, Hatzikelis), photograph every angle of any rental, and use Bolt or Beat for airport transfers rather than the port taxi rank.
