# How to Use This Book {-}

Greece is extraordinary. It is also, statistically, one of the most scam-prone destinations in the Mediterranean for first-time visitors. Athens's Plaka district runs a "friendly local bar" scam so old the Hellenic Police have posted warning signs at metro exits. Santorini's caldera-view restaurants charge €15 for bread you never ordered. Mykonos runs bill-padding schemes that make international news every summer. The Tourist Police (dial **171**) logs thousands of foreign-visitor complaints every year, most of them concentrated on a handful of harbours, archaeological sites, and island beach clubs.

We want you to come home with stories about the Acropolis, the sunset at Oia, the ferry rides through the Cyclades, and the grilled octopus at a village taverna — not about a €300 bar tab for four drinks or a €500 rental-car damage claim for a scratch that was already there.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents sixty-five specific scams that trip up first-time visitors to Greece, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been reported in the Greek press, cross-referenced against Tourist Police or Hellenic Police records, and confirmed by recent traveler accounts on r/GreeceTravel, r/greece, and the island-specific subreddits.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First, the name of the scam, the category it falls into (pickpocket, transport, overcharging, tourist trap, and so on), and how serious and how common it is. Then two short sections: **How this scam works** — a plain narrative of how it unfolds in real time, with real euro amounts where we have them — and **How to avoid it**, the specific moves that shut it down. Each entry ends with where it happens and — for Greece — a four-panel comic rendered in the style of ancient red-figure pottery so you can spot the pattern visually, not just in words.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. Most scams in Greece are variations on six patterns. Learn the patterns and you will spot a new scam the moment it starts — even one that is not in this book. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will then read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters, you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **Greek exit-phrase card**. Short phrases you can say, word for word, at the moments when your politeness would normally get you into trouble. Greek is not tonal like Thai, but pronunciation matters — the guides are approximate, and the key words will carry the meaning even with a foreign accent. Take a screenshot of it on your phone.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A step-by-step flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens, you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. Tourist Police, Hellenic Police, ambulance, Greek National Tourism Organisation lines, embassies, and the nearest English-speaking medical care, organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and skim the three appendices. Read the chapters for the cities and islands you will be visiting. Take a screenshot of the phrase card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book turns out to apply during your trip, open it again in the taxi or in your hotel room and check what to do.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.

*Kalí taxídi* — have a good journey.
