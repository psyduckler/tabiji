Athens runs the oldest and most rehearsed scam ecosystem in southeastern Europe, and almost all of it converges on a single district: the Plaka, the ancient neighborhood beneath the Acropolis. The "friendly local bar" gambit — a charming Greek who approaches you near Monastiraki Square, suggests drinks at a "real Athenian bar," and walks you to a clip-joint where four ouzos cost €400 — has been documented so often that the Hellenic Police have posted warning signs at metro exits. The first refusal at the first approach collapses the entire script.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The airport-taxi racket at Athens International — drivers quoting €60–€80 to Syntagma when the fixed metered fare is €40 (flat rate, posted at the rank) — peaks between 11pm and 6am when jet-lagged arrivals accept anything. The Acropolis approach runs the bracelet-and-rose crews: South Asian men with friendship bracelets, African men with roses, and pickpocket teams who work the distraction. Omonia and Monastiraki metro exits run ATM-shoulder-surfing and wallet-lift patterns documented by r/greece users weekly. A safe default: take the metro from the airport (€9, 40 minutes) instead of a taxi, use Beat or FreeNow rather than flagged taxis, decline any uninvited conversation near tourist sites, and call Tourist Police **171** the moment a bill is padded or an exit is blocked — English-speaking dispatchers answer 24/7 and most venues fold at the word "police."
