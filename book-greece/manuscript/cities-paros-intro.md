Paros is the Cycladic island that locals recommend when Mykonos and Santorini feel too crowded and too expensive. It is less aggressive than either — but the patterns are present. Naoussa's Old Port waterfront runs the same tourist-menu script: menus without prices, fish by the kilogram, welcome bread and olives. Parikia ferry port runs the taxi-overcharge quote: €40 for a five-minute ride when the Greek minimum fare is €5.

The risk concentrates in three zones. Parikia port runs the taxi cartel; the island has only about 50 registered taxis for millions of annual visitors, and drivers know it. Naoussa's Old Port harbourfront runs per-kg fish and welcome-bread markup. The ATV and scooter rental scene across Paros runs the damage-claim script, compounded by narrow mountain roads where minor damage is common. A safe default: pre-book a hotel transfer (€15–€25) rather than using the port taxi rank, take the KTEL bus (€1.80 to Naoussa), eat at Sigi Ikthios, Yemeni, or Tsachpinis with posted menus, rent from Europcar at the airport rather than small storefronts, and photograph every scratch before driving. For Antiparos, take the €2.50 public ferry yourself rather than a €90 "tour package."
