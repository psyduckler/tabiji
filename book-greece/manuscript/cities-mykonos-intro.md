Mykonos is the Greek island where tourist overcharging has become international news. DK Oyster, a beach restaurant in Platis Gialos, has been documented in the *Guardian*, *New York Post*, and dozens of Reddit threads for bills exceeding €400–€800 for meals that tourists believed cost €50. The Greek Consumer Ombudsman has investigated; the restaurant remains open. It is the extreme case, but the pattern — menus without prices, fish priced by the kilogram at €120–€180/kg, welcome bread and olives added at €15–€25, and bill totals that bear no relation to expectation — runs across the Mykonos waterfront.

The risk concentrates in three zones. Mykonos port and the New Port run the taxi cartel: "fixed rates" of €20–€30 for 2-km rides to Chora when the metered fare should be €8–€10. Little Venice and the Chora waterfront run the restaurant-markup script to its most aggressive extreme — posted menus rare, per-portion pricing rarer. The beach clubs at Psarou and Paradise Beach run €80–€150 "VIP sunbed" minimums that are disclosed only after you sit. For jet-ski rentals, Platis Gialos and Paradise Beach have documented damage-claim scams at €5,000–€20,000. A safe default: pre-book hotel transfers with a written quote, eat at community-verified restaurants with posted menus (Kiki's Tavern, Joanna's Nikos Place, Kalita), photograph every angle of any rental before use, and never sit at any restaurant or beach club without confirming prices in writing first.
