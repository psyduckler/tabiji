Milos is a small Cycladic island — about 5,400 residents — that became mainstream around 2020, when Sarakiniko's white-rock "lunar landscape" went viral on Instagram. Three sites carry the visitor flow: Sarakiniko on the north coast, the Kleftiko sea caves at the southwest tip (reachable only by boat), and the photogenic Plaka old town above Adamas port. The Hellenic Seaways high-speed ferry from Piraeus brings most visitors in 3 hours; cruise tenders from Adamas pier add a second arrival flow. The scam economy is shaped by the island's small size and even smaller taxi fleet — about seven licensed taxis serve thousands of summer ferry arrivals on peak days.

The risk radiates from three corridors. At Adamas port, the taxi cartel runs supply-driven price inflation on Pollonia and Sarakiniko transfers — the seven-taxi fleet quotes €40–€60 for runs the meter would price at €15–€25, and refuses to operate the meter when demand is high. The Kleftiko boat-tour market is the second concentration: catamaran operators charge €120–€180 for shared day cruises that overcrowd to the legal passenger limit, with "sell-out pressure" at Adamas tour desks pushing same-day bookings at peak prices. ATV and scooter rentals at Adamas run a damage-deposit cycle on the island's narrow unpaved roads to remote beaches (Tsigrado, Firiplaka, Provatas) where minor cosmetic damage is nearly inevitable. Lower-impact but common: Sarakiniko parking-lot cash solicitation by unlicensed "attendants," and Plaka old-town tourist-menu cover-charge padding at the sunset-view restaurants. A safe default: take the Milos Buses (€2 per disembarkation, milosbuses.com) for inter-village transport, book Kleftiko boat tours direct from named operators (Polco Sailing, Naya, Perseas, Volcano, Oneiro) with their published per-day price list, and rent only a small car (not an ATV) if independent transport is needed.
