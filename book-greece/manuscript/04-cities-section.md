# The Scams: City by City {-}

The chapters that follow document every scam reported in ten Greek destinations. Cities are ordered by how most visitors actually travel: starting with Athens, the mainland flagship and the country's densest scam ecosystem, then through the Cycladic islands (Santorini, Mykonos, Paros, Naxos), north to Thessaloniki and the gateway to Meteora, across to the Dodecanese (Rhodes), the Ionian (Corfu), and finishing on Crete (Heraklion, Chania).

If you're hitting only a few of these, skip to the chapter you need. If you're doing a longer island-hopping loop, skim them all — the same core scams recur with local variations. The welcome-bread cover charge that runs at Plaka tavernas in Athens replays identically at Naoussa's Old Port in Paros and at Oia's caldera restaurants in Santorini. The taxi meter-refusal and flat-rate overcharge pattern starts at Athens Airport and repeats at every ferry port, airport, and cruise terminal in this book. The rental-car damage-claim scam that runs in Heraklion storefronts operates identically in Chania, Naxos, and on Santorini's ATV circuit. Learn the pattern in one city, and you'll spot it the instant you step off the ferry in the next.

## The Ten Cities at a Glance {-}

![Athens — the Parthenon atop the Acropolis at golden hour](assets/cities/athens.jpg)

**Athens** — The Acropolis, Plaka, and southeastern Europe's oldest tourist-scam ecosystem. The "friendly local bar" clip-joint trap that runs from Monastiraki to Syntagma; the Athens Airport taxi flat-rate quote (€60–€80 for a €40 run); the Acropolis approach bracelet-and-rose distraction pickpocket crews; and the Plaka welcome-bread cover charge that adds €15 to every taverna bill.

![Santorini — blue-domed churches of Oia perched on the caldera cliff](assets/cities/santorini.jpg)

**Santorini** — Oia, Fira, and Greece's most photographed (and most overpriced) island. The Athinios port taxi overcharge (€40 for a €25 ride to Fira); the Oia caldera restaurant bill-padding with per-kg fish and €15 bread baskets; the Santorini ATV rental damage-claim scam on the island's steep roads; and peak-season hotel booking fraud where "Booking.com" messages request off-platform payment.

![Mykonos — the famous windmills at Little Venice at dusk](assets/cities/mykonos.jpg)

**Mykonos** — Little Venice, windmills, and bill-padding that makes international news. DK Oyster in Platis Gialos, named by the *Guardian* and *New York Post* for €400–€800 seafood bills; the Mykonos port fixed-rate taxi cartel (€20 for a 2-km ride); Psarou and Paradise Beach "VIP sunbed" minimums (€80–€150 disclosed only after sitting); and jet-ski damage claims at €5,000–€20,000.

![Paros — whitewashed houses of Naoussa fishing village at sunset](assets/cities/paros.jpg)

**Paros** — Parikia, Naoussa, and the Cycladic island locals recommend when Mykonos feels too aggressive. The Parikia port taxi overcharge (€40 for a 5-minute ride); the Naoussa Old Port restaurant per-kg fish and welcome-bread markup; the Paros ATV rental damage-claim scam; and Antiparos "private yacht" day-trip packages that charge €500 for a €15 DIY ferry trip.

![Naxos — the Portara gateway to the Temple of Apollo at sunset](assets/cities/naxos.jpg)

**Naxos** — The Portara, Kastro, and an island with one specifically named bad actor. Matha Rent a Car, flagged by r/GreeceTravel for damage claims and post-return credit-card fraud; the Plaka Beach "per chair per hour" pricing trap; the Naxos Chora waterfront welcome-bread markup; and storefront "traditional village tour" packages that cost €85 for a €15 bus-and-taverna loop.

![Thessaloniki — the White Tower on the waterfront promenade](assets/cities/thessaloniki.jpg)

**Thessaloniki** — The White Tower, Byzantine churches, and the gateway to Meteora. The SKG airport Uber/taxi overcharge (€24 charged on a €14 estimate); the Ladadika "VIP table" minimum-consumption bar trap; the Aristotelous Square bracelet-and-distraction pickpocket crew; and Meteora day-trip bait-and-switch packages with rushed visits and commission-restaurant stops.

![Rhodes — medieval walls and the Palace of the Grand Master](assets/cities/rhodes.jpg)

**Rhodes** — Medieval Old Town, the Palace of the Grand Master, and a concentrated tourist-menu zone. The Rhodes Old Town restaurant per-kg fish and welcome-bread markup; the Rhodes Town taxi overcharge from port and airport; the Ialyssos Beach scooter rental damage-claim scam; and the Lindos beach-club sunbed pricing ambiguity.

![Corfu — the Liston arcade with the Old Fortress in the distance](assets/cities/corfu.jpg)

**Corfu** — Venetian architecture, the Liston, and the most aggressive taxi overcharging in Greece. The Corfu port-to-town taxi flat-rate quote (€30 for an €8–€10 metered ride); the Corfu Town Liston tourist-menu coffee markup (€7 espresso); the east-coast resort scooter and quad rental damage-claim scam; and the Kavos nightlife strip drink-spiking warnings.

![Heraklion — the Venetian fortress of Koules guarding the harbour](assets/cities/heraklion.jpg)

**Heraklion** — Koules fortress, Knossos, and Crete's rental-car damage-claim epicenter. Carwiz specifically named in Greek market warnings; the Hersonissos and Malia nightclub "minimum consumption" trap; the Heraklion harbour restaurant welcome-bread markup; and the HER airport taxi overcharge (€30 for a €15 metered ride).

![Chania — the Venetian Lighthouse and Old Harbour at dusk](assets/cities/chania.jpg)

**Chania** — The Venetian Lighthouse, Old Harbour, and Crete's most beautiful (and most overpriced) waterfront. The Old Venetian Harbour tourist-menu markup (€8 coffee, €6 bread cover); the Chania Airport taxi overcharge (€50 for a €25–€35 metered ride); the Souda Shell station short-change scam; and the Leather Lane counterfeit and mass-produced goods markup.
