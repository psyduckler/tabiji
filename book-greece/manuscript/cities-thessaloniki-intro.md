Thessaloniki is Greece's second city — a Byzantine, Ottoman, and Jewish heritage layered into a modern port metropolis — and its scam ecosystem is smaller and less aggressive than Athens. But the patterns are present. The airport-taxi overcharge at Makedonia (SKG) runs the same script: €24 Uber charges on €14 estimates, €40 taxi quotes for €20 rides. The Ladadika nightlife district runs the "VIP table" minimum-consumption trap and the occasional clip-joint bar. The White Tower waterfront runs welcome-bread markup at tourist-zone restaurants.

The specific Thessaloniki risk is the Meteora day-trip bait-and-switch. Tour agencies near Aristotelous Square sell "Meteora from Thessaloniki — €55" packages that deliver 8 hours of bus driving, 3 hours at the monasteries, a commission-restaurant lunch, and a "souvenir shop" stop where the guide takes kickback. The real Meteora experience requires an overnight in Kalambaka — take the train from Thessaloniki (€20, 3 hours) and stay a night. For Thessaloniki itself, a safe default: take the 01X bus from SKG airport (€2), eat in Ano Poli or Kalamaria for local prices rather than Ladadika waterfront, confirm minimum consumption before sitting at any bar, and use FreeNow or Beat rather than street taxis.
