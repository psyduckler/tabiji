Corfu is the greenest of the Greek islands, with Venetian architecture, Albanian influences, and some of the most aggressive taxi overcharging in the country. The port-to-town taxi scam — €30 quoted for a 3-km ride that should cost €8–€10 metered — is documented so consistently on r/GreeceTravel that it appears to be a cartel. The "meter is broken" excuse is standard; the solution is to demand the meter or walk away.

The risk concentrates in three zones. Corfu port and airport run the taxi-overcharge script; the taxi operators are widely described as the most aggressive in Greece outside Mykonos. Corfu Town's Liston — the beautiful arcaded promenade — runs tourist-menu coffee and restaurant markup (€7 espresso, welcome-bread charges). The beach resorts on the east coast (Gouvia, Dassia, Ipsos) run scooter and quad-rental damage claims targeting cruise visitors on day rentals. A safe default: pre-book airport transfers through your hotel with a written quote, take the local bus from the port to town (€1.50), eat at posted-price tavernas in the back streets (Rex, Chrysomallis, La Cucina), and photograph every rental before use. The old town is walkable; you do not need a taxi or scooter to see Corfu Town itself.
