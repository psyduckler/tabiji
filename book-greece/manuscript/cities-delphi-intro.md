Delphi is the UNESCO oracle of antiquity, perched on the slopes of Mount Parnassus 180 km northwest of Athens. The archaeological site, the museum, and the Tholos at Athena Pronaia draw two distinct visitor flows: KTEL Fokidas bus passengers staying overnight in the village or in Arachova, and Athens-based tour groups doing a fourteen-hour bus-loop day trip. The scam economy targets the second flow with surgical precision — operators bundle the €15.50 KTEL bus seat into €120-plus "guided day tours" that compress three sites into a 90-minute walking loop, with a contracted Arachova lunch stop and a souvenir-shop kickback added on the way back.

The risk radiates from three corridors. At the Liosion Terminal B departure point in Athens, "private transfer" curb dispatchers sell €350-plus rides for what the public bus does for €15.50 single, with the upsell pitch heaviest on travelers heading to early-morning Athens flights after a Delphi day. At the Delphi site forecourt, "official guide" badges materialize at 09:30 most summer mornings — €60–€90 per person for content that hhticket.gr's official audio guide covers for €4. The most damaging single incident is rental-car break-ins at the Delphi site car park and at the Hosios Loukas monastery and Galaxidi-Itea waterfront lay-bys on the way back; thieves work the unattended lots while drivers walk a 90-minute ruins loop. A safe default: take the KTEL Fokidas bus from Athens (€15–€16.50 single per ktelbus.gr), stay one or two nights in Delphi village or Arachova, buy site and museum entry directly at the gate (€12 in summer), and carry passports and prior-trip shopping on your person — never leave anything in the rental car at any sightseeing lay-by.
