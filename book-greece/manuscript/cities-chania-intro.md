Chania is Crete's second city and, in many travelers' opinions, the most beautiful harbour in Greece. The Old Venetian Harbour — a horseshoe-shaped waterfront backed by 14th-century fortifications and a 16th-century lighthouse — is also Chania's concentrated tourist-menu zone. A cappuccino at a harbour café costs €6–€8 (€2.50 two blocks inland); dinner on the waterfront regularly runs €50–€90 per person for food that costs €20–€25 at a neighbourhood taverna. The cutlery-charge debate is real: Greek law prohibits unlisted cover charges, but enforcement is weak.

The risk concentrates in three zones. The Old Venetian Harbour runs welcome-bread markup and the highest per-item prices on Crete. Chania Airport (CHQ) runs the taxi-overcharge script: €50 quotes for a €25–€35 metered ride to Old Town. The Souda junction petrol station has been named specifically for short-changing tourist drivers. For car rentals, the Chania storefronts run the same damage-claim pattern as Heraklion. A safe default: take the KTEL bus from the airport (€2.50, 40 minutes), enjoy the harbour with a single coffee as the tourist premium, eat meals at Tamam, Chrisostomos, or Bougatsa Iordanis two blocks inland, photograph every rental, and pay petrol by credit card to eliminate the short-change opportunity.
