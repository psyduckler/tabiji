# Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication. Phone numbers do change — if a number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus *Malaysia phone* and you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results.

**Before you travel**, we strongly recommend you double-check your own embassy or high commission's current number directly on its official website (the US State Department's travel.state.gov, the UK's gov.uk, Canada's travel.gc.ca, Australia's smartraveller.gov.au). Save the main number and any after-hours duty-officer line on your phone before departure.

This appendix lists national numbers first, then one block for each of the ten cities covered in this book — in reading order: Kuala Lumpur, Melaka, Johor Bahru, Genting Highlands, Cameron Highlands, Ipoh, Penang, Langkawi, Kuching, and Kota Kinabalu.

## National (anywhere in Malaysia)

- **Emergencies (police, ambulance, fire, civil defence):** **999** from any phone anywhere in Malaysia. English-speaking operators are available 24/7 in the major cities. From a GSM mobile with no service, **112** also routes to 999 through whichever network has coverage.
- **NSRC — National Scam Response Centre:** **997**. 24/7 since September 2025. Operated jointly by Bank Negara Malaysia and PDRM; the correct first call for any financial-fraud incident, including unauthorized transfers, phishing, romance scams, and investment fraud. NSRC can freeze beneficiary accounts at partner banks in minutes if you call quickly.
- **MOTAC Tourist Helpline:** **1300-88-5050**. Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture general tourist information and complaint line. English available.
- **Bank Negara Malaysia BNMTELELINK:** **1-300-88-5465**. Central-bank consumer line for banking, e-wallet, money-changer, and card-issuer complaints. Business hours, English available.
- **JPJ (Jabatan Pengangkutan Jalan) national hotline:** **1-800-88-7723**. Road transport authority — the correct channel for illegal-taxi, unlicensed rideshare, and *teksi sapu* complaints.
- **KPDN (Kementerian Perdagangan Dalam Negeri dan Kos Sara Hidup):** file online at **eaduan.kpdn.gov.my** or via the **ez-ADU** mobile app. The federal consumer-affairs ministry; handles unmarked prices, restaurant overcharges, surcharge disputes, and misleading-trade complaints.
- **PDRM (Polis Diraja Malaysia):** **rmp.gov.my**. Royal Malaysia Police portal with balai-polis directory and online complaint forms.
- **MOTAC Tourism Malaysia:** **tourism.gov.my**.
- **Tribunal Tuntutan Pengguna Malaysia (TTPM, Consumer Claims Tribunal):** **ttpm.kpdnhep.gov.my**. Claims under RM 50,000.

## A note on calling 999

If you dial 999 from any Malaysian phone, the operator will ask which service you need — police (*polis*), ambulance, fire, or civil defence. English is available 24/7 in the major cities; in rural areas, operators may answer in Bahasa Malaysia first — say "English please" and you will be transferred. State your location as a landmark ("Pavilion KL main entrance," "Jonker Street in Melaka," "Pantai Cenang near Cenang Mall") rather than a GPS coordinate — Malaysian dispatchers work from landmarks. If you cannot speak (medical incapacitation, active threat), leaving the line open will still allow the operator to triangulate your approximate location from the cell tower.

For scam and fraud reports where emergency response is not needed, **997** (NSRC) is the correct line for financial incidents and **1300-88-5050** (MOTAC) is the correct line for tourist-service complaints. Both are English-capable.
