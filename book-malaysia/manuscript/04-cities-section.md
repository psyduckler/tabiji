# The Scams: City by City {-}

The chapters that follow document every scam reported in ten Malaysian destinations. Cities are ordered by how most visitors actually travel: starting with Kuala Lumpur, the KLIA gateway and the hub every domestic route radiates from; south to the UNESCO heritage core of Melaka and then down to Johor Bahru at the Singapore land border; back up to the KL day-trip pair of Genting Highlands and Cameron Highlands; north through Ipoh along the KL–Penang rail corridor; out to the UNESCO shophouses of George Town (Penang) and the northern islands of Langkawi; and finally across the South China Sea to the Borneo capitals of Kuching (Sarawak) and Kota Kinabalu (Sabah).

If you are hitting only a few of these, skip to the chapter you need. If you are doing a longer loop, skim them all — the same core scams recur with local variations. The Grab cancellation + teksi sapu airport overcharge at KLIA runs identically at PEN, LGK, KCH, and KKIA. The vacation-rental off-platform payment-fraud pattern runs in KL, Melaka, Langkawi, Cameron Highlands, and Kota Kinabalu. And the ATM home-currency-conversion (DCC) markup threads through every tourist-facing ATM from Genting's SkyAvenue to Pantai Cenang to Jesselton Point. Learn the pattern in one city, and you will spot it the instant you arrive in the next.

## The Ten Cities at a Glance {-}

![Stylized illustration of Kuala Lumpur — the Petronas Twin Towers and KL skyline at golden hour](assets/cities/kuala-lumpur.jpg)

**Kuala Lumpur** — Malaysia's capital, the KLIA / KLIA2 gateway, and the country's most-documented tourist-scam ecosystem. The KLIA and KLIA2 teksi sapu airport overcharge paired with the Grab cancel-and-cash pattern at arrivals; the Petronas Towers skybridge "fake sold-out" ticket tout working the KLCC concourse; the Central Market and Chinatown gem-investment confidence con where a "gem dealer" recruits a tourist for a duty-free export scheme; the fake plainclothes police passport and wallet inspection on Jalan Alor and Bukit Bintang; the Batu Caves garland, photographer, and temple-donation markup stacked on the stairs; and the fake MDAC (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card) website charging RM 145 for what is a free government form.

![Stylized illustration of Melaka — Christ Church and the Stadthuys Red Square at golden hour](assets/cities/melaka.jpg)

**Melaka** — The UNESCO-listed Dutch Red Square, Jonker Street, and a river-cruise economy running its own tourist-price layer. The QR-code receipt fake-payment scam that led to an April 2026 arrest of a Melaka Tengah couple; the Hello Kitty and Pikachu trishaw bait-and-switch (touts charging RM 80 against the MBMB-published rate of RM 25–RM 50); Jonker Street night-market food and durian overcharge; the Melaka River Cruise unofficial-ticket tout working the Taman Rempah jetty; the February 2026 Spider-Man and Frog costumed-beggar case involving a 7-year-old girl trafficked from Terengganu; and fake-donation charity solicitors circling the Stadthuys photo spots.

![Stylized illustration of Johor Bahru — Sultan Iskandar CIQ complex and the causeway at dusk](assets/cities/johor-bahru.jpg)

**Johor Bahru** — The Singapore land-border gateway, Sultan Iskandar CIQ, and a cross-border taxi underground that JPJ keeps hauling in. The illegal cross-border SG↔JB taxi at Sultan Iskandar CIQ (September 2025 JPJ seizure of four Singapore-registered MPVs); counterfeit luxury-goods stalls at Pasar Karat, Holiday Plaza, and along Jalan Wong Ah Fook; motorbike snatch-theft on Jalan Wong Ah Fook and into Taman Pelangi; the fake parking-fine QR code (bogus MBJB SPOT phishing stickers slapped over real meters); fake-banknote change at pasar malam; and the budget-massage upsell and cupping-blister setup on Jalan Dhoby and in Taman Pelangi.

![Stylized illustration of Genting Highlands — Resorts World and mountain mist](assets/cities/genting-highlands.jpg)

**Genting Highlands** — The KL day-trip casino and theme-park complex at 1,800 meters, with a scam mix tuned to captive-market pricing. The Gohtong Jaya "wild fish / market price" seafood bill-shock (RM 902 patin, RM 1,762 final bill in one viral case); fake Genting SkyWorlds Theme Park tickets sold on Shopee and Facebook; Resorts World fake "hotel staff" room-phone fraud paired with cloned booking domains; SkyCasino distraction chip-lift work and the May 2025 casino-begging arrests; the KL–Genting unlicensed shuttle-van souvenir-stop upsell; and ATM DCC home-currency conversion markup at First World and SkyAvenue cash machines.

![Stylized illustration of Cameron Highlands — BOH tea plantation terraces in morning mist](assets/cities/cameron-highlands.jpg)

**Cameron Highlands** — The backpacker highlands circuit through Tanah Rata, Brinchang, and the BOH estates, with a day-trip operator ecosystem that has drawn repeat enforcement. The Tanah Rata tour-van RM 1,470 day-trip overcharge (viral on AsiaOne); the December 2025 JPJ seizure of sixteen vehicles including an illegal tourist-ferrying school bus; strawberry-farm per-kg price-switch at Big Red and Raju's Hill; the Mossy Forest fake Forestry "conservation fee" at Gunung Brinchang; Brinchang homestay and hotel-impersonation booking fraud (the April 2026 Heritage Hotel alert); and the BOH Sungai Palas unofficial-guide and van-transport markup pattern.

![Stylized illustration of Ipoh — Concubine Lane shophouses and limestone cliffs behind Ipoh Old Town](assets/cities/ipoh.jpg)

**Ipoh** — Perak's capital, a key stop on the KL–Penang rail corridor, and home to a cluster of in-person photo and phone-impersonation scams. The Ipoh Old Town unlicensed-photographer RM 800 photo-fee pressure pattern; the October 2025 AEON Station 18 arrest linking a PRC-organized mall distraction-theft crew; the Macau-scam fake-PDRM phone impersonation (one Ipoh trader lost RM 1.2 million); Concubine Lane (Lorong Panglima) tourist-trap overcharge on drinks, snacks, and photo-prop rentals; the November 2025 Malay Mail traffic-police impersonation conviction (two men fined RM 5,000 each); and the cave-temple and Gua Tempurung guide, parking, and donation touts.

![Stylized illustration of Penang — George Town shophouses, trishaws, and Komtar at golden hour](assets/cities/penang.jpg)

**Penang** — UNESCO-listed George Town, Gurney Drive's hawker coast, and the country's most-documented tourist-hypnosis robbery environment. The George Town tourist-hypnosis (pukau) robbery ring (August 2025 NST #1254749 Iranian arrests; November 2025 Simpang Ampat raid documenting fourteen Japanese and four Chinese targets); the PEN airport Grab cancellation and teksi sapu tout combination; Gurney Drive and New Lane hawker tourist-price overcharge; the George Town street-art and costumed-character photo shakedown on Armenian Street and Upper Penang Road; George Town trishaw-tour bait-and-switch with forced commission stops; and tourist-minimart unlabelled-price overcharge across the Komtar / Chulia Street tourist strip.

![Stylized illustration of Langkawi — Eagle Square statue and Pantai Cenang turquoise sea](assets/cities/langkawi.jpg)

**Langkawi** — The duty-free archipelago off the northern Kedah coast, with Pantai Cenang as the scam epicenter. The Pantai Cenang jet-ski damage-compensation shakedown (one PRC tourist paid RM 1,000 in the viral case); the Langkawi International Airport (LGK) coupon-taxi overcharge combined with LIMA 25 fake-deals listings; duty-free fake alcohol and counterfeit RM 100 notes (August 2025 arrests of seven); the Oriental Village SkyCab and SkyBridge ticket tout plus already-scanned QR resales; Pantai Cenang beach massage, henna, and hair-braid pressure upsell; and island-hopping boat bait-and-switch operators (linked to both September and October 2025 drownings).

![Stylized illustration of Kuching — the Sarawak River waterfront and Astana palace at golden hour](assets/cities/kuching.jpg)

**Kuching** — Sarawak's capital, the Main Bazaar riverfront, and the gateway to Bako, Semenggoh, and Mulu. The Kuching International Airport (KCH) unlicensed taxi and Grab cancellation pattern; Bako National Park boat-fee and unofficial "guide" overcharge (SFC sets the foreigner fee at RM 20); orangutan and wildlife-tour bait-and-switch across Semenggoh and Mulu multi-day packages; Iban longhouse cultural-tour hidden-cost and fake-donation pressure; Sarawak Cultural Village fake-bundle packaging and unofficial RWMF ticket reselling; and money-changer shortchanging on Main Bazaar and Carpenter Street.

![Stylized illustration of Kota Kinabalu — Mount Kinabalu silhouette above the South China Sea](assets/cities/kota-kinabalu.jpg)

**Kota Kinabalu** — Sabah's capital, the Mt Kinabalu gateway, and a city that opened 2026 with an RM 11.1 million quarterly online-fraud tally. The Mt Kinabalu fake climbing-package pattern (one documented RM 18,000 loss — Sabah Parks is the only legitimate booking channel); the Kota Kinabalu Airport (KKIA / BKI) taxi-coupon and Grab cancellation combination; Jesselton Point illegal island-hopping touts (November 2025 Borneo Post arrests); the Semporna lobster-switch and seafood-swap scam (October 2025 viral Chinese-couple video); the KK-wide online-fraud environment (RM 11.1 million in losses January–March 2026 per NST #1400388); and Sabah interior day-trip markup on Sepilok, Kundasang, and Poring packages.
