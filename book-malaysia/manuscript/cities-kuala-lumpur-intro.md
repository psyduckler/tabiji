Kuala Lumpur is Malaysia's capital and the single densest tourist-scam ecosystem in the country — a city of roughly eight million across the Klang Valley, funnelling arrivals through KLIA and KLIA2 before depositing them at the foot of the Petronas Twin Towers. The scams cluster at four venues: the airport arrivals curb, the Bukit Bintang / KLCC / Pavilion retail triangle, the Chinatown Petaling Street heritage strip, and the phone + web channel that intercepts travelers before they even land. The KLIA and KLIA2 *teksi sapu* airport overcharge is the flagship — unlicensed drivers approach inside the terminal offering "fixed RM 200" fares into the city while the Kaunter Teksi Rasmi coupon runs RM 75–85 and Grab runs RM 60–70, often paired with a fake-Grab-cancellation gambit once the bag is in the boot. At the Petronas Towers skybridge, touts work the queue outside the Concourse ticket counter telling day-trippers the timed slots are "sold out" and steering them toward inflated third-party packages. The Central Market and Chinatown gem-investment confidence con recruits solo travelers over a friendly coffee; fake plainclothes police demand passport and wallet "inspections" near Masjid Jamek LRT; Batu Caves runs garland, photographer, and temple-donation markups on tourists climbing the 272 steps; and a fake MDAC (Malaysia Digital Arrival Card) website is charging RM 145 for the form that is genuinely free at imigresen-online.imi.gov.my.
