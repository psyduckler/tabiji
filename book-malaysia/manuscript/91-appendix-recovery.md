# The Recovery Playbook {-}

If something in this book has happened to you, this appendix is where you start. The actions below are ordered by urgency, not by severity. Even if you are not sure yet how serious the scam was, working through the first two timelines will put you in the best possible position to recover your money, your evidence, and your peace of mind.

## The first fifteen minutes

Move to a safe, well-lit location with CCTV and a witness or two. In Malaysia's urban cores, your best nearby refuges are the security desk of the nearest shopping mall (Pavilion KL, Suria KLCC, Gurney Plaza, KSL City, Cenang Mall, Imago KK, Vivacity Megamall — every mall has a visible security counter), a branded hotel lobby, a mamak restaurant, or a 24-hour 7-Eleven or KK Mart. These locations have cameras, staff, and a phone. Do not argue at the scene, do not pay a "second" or "damage" charge, and do not sign a new receipt.

If you feel physically threatened or coerced, dial **999** from any phone. 999 is Malaysia's nationwide emergency number and routes to police (PDRM), ambulance, fire, and the Civil Defence Force. English-speaking operators are available 24/7 in the major cities.

Cancel the card that was used before you do anything else. The fastest line is the fraud number on the back of your card — Malaysian card terminals are online, and issuers can freeze an account in under sixty seconds. Have the last four digits and the approximate transaction amount ready before you dial.

Screenshot everything you can: the bill, the business's signboard, the street outside, the payment terminal, the QR-code menu if one was involved, and any WhatsApp, DuitNow, or Touch 'n Go transaction confirmations. Save your Grab or Maxim trip history — both apps retain the driver's plate, the exact route, and a timestamped map. Evidence captured in the first fifteen minutes is evidence you will use for weeks afterward.

## The first hour

File a **laporan polis** (police report) at the nearest **balai polis** (police station), or ask your hotel's front desk to direct you to the one closest to your scam. A laporan polis costs nothing, is filed in English or Bahasa Malaysia, and generates a printed report with a reference number that you will need for chargebacks, travel insurance, and embassy follow-up. Say, in plain English: "I am a tourist and I need to file a scam report." If you are in Kuala Lumpur, the dedicated **Tourist Police unit at INFOTUR KL** can be reached on **+60 3-2149 6590** — they keep English-speaking officers on duty.

If the scam was financial (a fraudulent transfer, a phishing SMS you clicked, a romance or investment scam, a QR-code payment you did not authorize), call the **National Scam Response Centre (NSRC) on 997** before anything else. NSRC is Malaysia's 24/7 anti-scam coordination hotline, operated jointly by Bank Negara Malaysia and PDRM; since its full launch in September 2025 it can freeze suspect beneficiary accounts in minutes if you call within the first hour.

Email your card issuer's fraud line from the address on your account statement, forwarding the screenshots you captured. A written paper trail strengthens the chargeback file even when you have already spoken to the issuer by phone.

Contact your embassy or high commission in Kuala Lumpur if a passport was photographed, held, or taken, or if you need consular help coordinating medical care or translation. KL-based contact numbers are listed in the next appendix.

## The first day

For vendor scams (unmarked prices, restaurant overcharges, tour bait-and-switch, surcharge disputes), file a **KPDN consumer-protection complaint** at **eaduan.kpdn.gov.my** or through the ez-ADU mobile app. KPDN (Kementerian Perdagangan Dalam Negeri dan Kos Sara Hidup) is the federal consumer-affairs ministry; complaints are forwarded to the state KPDN office nearest to the business, and enforcement officers have real authority over price-display and misleading-trade violations.

For financial fraud (bank transfers, card charges, scam-linked e-wallet debits), your parallel channel is **Bank Negara Malaysia's BNMTELELINK on 1-300-88-5465** during business hours, with NSRC 997 covering the 24/7 window. BNMLINK will log the incident against the beneficiary bank and can escalate with the issuer on your behalf.

Email your travel-insurance provider with a dated summary, the laporan polis reference number, and all photographs. Even if you are not sure the loss is covered, opening the claim early protects your position and starts the clock on the insurer's own investigation.

Preserve every piece of digital evidence: screenshots in a dedicated folder, the full Grab or Maxim trip history exported from the app, DuitNow and Touch 'n Go transaction records, WhatsApp threads with the scammer (export the chat as .txt with media), and the original QR codes if any were scanned. Back it all up to cloud storage before you leave Malaysia.

## The first week

File the formal **chargeback** with your card issuer inside the network window. Visa and Mastercard typically allow chargebacks within sixty days of the statement date; American Express allows up to one hundred and twenty days. Submit the laporan polis reference, the KPDN complaint number, and your photographic evidence as the core file — Malaysian merchants lose a significant share of chargebacks when the cardholder has filed a matching police report within forty-eight hours.

Report the scam to your home country's equivalent fraud-intake service from home: the US **IC3** at ic3.gov, the UK's **Action Fraud** at actionfraud.police.uk, Canada's **CAFC** (Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre), or Australia's **Scamwatch** at scamwatch.gov.au. These reports do not recover individual losses, but they feed cross-border intelligence that links Malaysian scam networks to the same operators targeting travellers in other markets.

Follow up on the KPDN complaint. For unresolved vendor disputes under RM 50,000, KPDN may refer your file to the **Tribunal Tuntutan Pengguna Malaysia (TTPM, Consumer Claims Tribunal)** — sometimes called *Saman Rahsia* — which adjudicates tourist consumer claims without requiring a Malaysian lawyer or a return visit.

Consider posting a neutral, factual review on Google Maps or Tripadvisor, naming the business by its official name and location, the amount involved, and the date. Avoid emotional language, personal accusations, or staff names. Future travellers read recent reviews more than any official enforcement database.
