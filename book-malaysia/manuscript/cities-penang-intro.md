Penang is the UNESCO heritage island off Malaysia's northwest coast, Penang International Airport (PEN) feeding the southern end of the island and the Penang Bridge carrying the land traffic over from Butterworth. George Town holds the UNESCO core — shophouse rows along Armenian Street, Love Lane, and Chulia Street, the KOMTAR tower looming over the old commercial district, Ernest Zacharevic's street art threading the back alleys. Gurney Drive runs the hawker-food strip along the north coast; Batu Ferringhi spreads the resort-beach traffic; Kek Lok Si temple cascades down the hillside above Air Itam; and the Penang Hill funicular climbs to the colonial hill-station cafes. The scam ecosystem tracks the international arrivals. A tourist-hypnosis (*pukau*) robbery ring saw Iranian suspects arrested in August 2025; in November 2025 Japanese and Chinese tourists were the latest victims in Simpang Ampat, walking away with wallets drained under trance. PEN airport runs the standard Grab-cancellation and *teksi sapu* overcharge on arrivals. Gurney Drive and New Lane hawker stalls quote tourist-price premiums of 50–100% off the Malay-language menu; George Town street-art and costumed-character photographers demand RM 30–50 after the shutter; trishaw operators pull the heritage-route bait-and-switch with forced commission stops at batik "workshops"; and tourist minimarts along Chulia Street sell water and snacks without posted prices, reading the receipt off the till-screen at checkout.
