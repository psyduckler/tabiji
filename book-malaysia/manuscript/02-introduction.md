# How to Use This Book {-}

Malaysia is extraordinary. It is also, contrary to the warm welcome at every arrival hall, home to a specific and repeatable set of tourist scams that PDRM (Polis Diraja Malaysia), KPDN (the Ministry of Domestic Trade), MOTAC (Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture), and JPJ (Jabatan Pengangkutan Jalan) log in steadily rising volumes. KLIA and KLIA2 run an active *teksi sapu* overcharge circuit that *New Straits Times* and *The Star* have covered for more than a decade. Melaka's QR-code "fake payment" scam along Jonker Street produced a public arrest wave in April 2026 that *Bernama* and *Malay Mail* both ran on the front page. Genting's Gohtong Jaya strip has a patin-fish bill-shock pattern so well-documented that a single RM 902 receipt went viral across r/malaysia in 2025 and triggered a KPDN inspection. Cameron Highlands tour-van operators have been caught writing RM 1,470 day-trip invoices that JPJ seized vehicles over in December 2025. And Sabah's interior online-fraud environment hit RM 11.1 million in reported losses between January and March 2026 alone, per *Daily Express* and *Borneo Post* — a figure the NSRC 997 hotline (24/7 since September 2025) is still working through.

We want you to come home with stories about the Petronas Towers at blue hour, the laksa on Jalan Alor, the orangutans at Semenggoh, the SkyBridge over Machinchang, and the Sabah sunsets at Tanjung Aru — not about an RM 18,000 fake Mt Kinabalu climbing package or an RM 145 "MDAC arrival card" that was always free.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents sixty specific scams that trip up first-time visitors across ten Malaysian cities, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been reported in the Malaysian press — *NST*, *The Star*, *Malay Mail*, *Free Malaysia Today*, *Bernama*, *Rakyat Post*, *Borneo Post*, *Daily Express* — cross-referenced against PDRM, KPDN, JPJ, or LPKP Sarawak records, and confirmed by recent traveler accounts on r/malaysia, r/KualaLumpur, r/penang, r/Sabah, r/Sarawak, and r/Malacca.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First, the name of the scam, the category it falls into (transport, overcharging, fraud, counterfeit, pickpocket, impersonation), and how serious and how common it is. Then two short sections: **How this scam works** — a plain narrative of how it unfolds in real time, with real ringgit amounts where we have them (USD only for packages quoted in USD, like Mt Kinabalu climbing permits) — and **How to avoid it**, the specific moves that shut it down. Each entry ends with where it happens and a four-panel comic in the Yusof Gajah folk-naïf style — hot-pink, marigold, turquoise, and emerald, with elephant and lotus motifs — showing the exchange so you can spot the pattern visually, not just in words.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. Most scams in Malaysia are variations on six patterns — the unlicensed-transport bait, the QR-code swap, the bill-shock markup, the impersonation (fake PDRM, fake hotel staff, fake parking officer), the distraction lift, and the pressure upsell. Learn the patterns and you will spot a new scam the moment it starts — even one that is not in this book. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will then read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters, you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **Malaysian-traveler quick-reference card**. Short scripts you can say, word for word, at the moments when your politeness would normally get you into trouble — the key phrases that end *teksi sapu* attempts, fake-police shakedowns, QR-swap receipt demands, and jet-ski damage-compensation confrontations. Take a screenshot of it on your phone. Because Bahasa Malaysia is the national language and Mandarin, Tamil, and various Chinese dialects are widely spoken, we include short Bahasa equivalents for the phrases that land hardest in the local tongue.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A step-by-step flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam — including how to file with NSRC 997, MOTAC's 1300-88-5050 tourist line, and the nearest *balai polis*. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens, you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. 999 for emergencies, 997 for scam response, PDRM non-emergency lines, tourist police desks, embassies, and the nearest English-speaking medical care for every major Malaysian city, organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and skim the three appendices. Read the chapters for the cities you will be visiting. Take a screenshot of the quick-reference card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book turns out to apply during your trip, open it again in the Grab or in your hotel room and check what to do.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.

*Welcome to Malaysia, and safe travels. Selamat jalan.*
