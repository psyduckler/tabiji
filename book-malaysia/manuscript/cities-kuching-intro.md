Kuching is Sarawak's state capital and the cultural gateway to Malaysian Borneo, Kuching International Airport (KCH) sitting eleven kilometers south of a riverside old town that still feels closer to a Brooke-era trading post than to Peninsular Malaysia. The Kuching Waterfront runs the Sarawak River's south bank opposite the golden-roofed Astana and the star-shaped Fort Margherita; the Main Bazaar and Carpenter Street hold the Chinatown shophouse grid; Bako National Park juts out on a peninsula an hour north; Semenggoh Wildlife Centre runs the morning and afternoon orangutan feedings; the Sarawak Cultural Village in Santubong stages longhouse life for day-trippers; and the Rainforest World Music Festival packs the Santubong grounds every July. The scam profile follows the nature-tour economy. KCH airport arrivals get the unlicensed-taxi and Grab-cancellation routine. Bako National Park boat touts at Bako Bazaar quote RM 80 per head for a trip the Sarawak Forestry Corporation sets at RM 20, padded with unofficial "guide" fees. Orangutan and wildlife-tour packages — particularly Semenggoh-plus-Mulu combinations — bait-and-switch the itinerary and substitute cheaper lodges. Iban longhouse cultural tours build in hidden costs and fake-donation requests; Sarawak Cultural Village fake bundles include unofficial RWMF ticket resellers; and Main Bazaar money-changers shortchange first-time visitors by 50–100 ringgit per transaction with quick-count handoffs.
