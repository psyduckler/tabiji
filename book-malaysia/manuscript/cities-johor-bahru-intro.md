Johor Bahru is Malaysia's southern gateway and the busiest land border in the world — Sultan Iskandar CIQ clears over 350,000 crossings on peak weekends as Singaporean day-trippers pour north for cheaper groceries, salons, and buffets. The city wraps around the Causeway: City Square and KSL City anchor the downtown retail core, R&F Mall sits literally at the CIQ exit, Johor Premium Outlets draws the luxury-brand coach crowd, and Legoland Malaysia pulls the family traffic out to Iskandar Puteri. The scam profile is border-specific. The illegal cross-border SG↔JB taxi racket — drivers without the red cross-border plates flipping passengers between Woodlands and JB for double the licensed fare — drew a wave of September 2025 JPJ seizures with vehicles impounded at the CIQ approach. Pasar Karat and Holiday Plaza run counterfeit luxury goods at convincing markups; motorbike snatch-theft works Jalan Wong Ah Fook and the Taman Pelangi backstreets, targeting tourists with phones in back pockets and bags on the road-side shoulder; fake parking-fine QR codes are plastered on windshields at Danga Bay and City Square car parks, linking to phishing pages that drain Maybank and CIMB accounts; pasar malam vendors pass fake RM 50 and RM 100 notes in change to travelers whose eyes are on the satay; and budget massage parlors along Jalan Tebrau push a cupping upsell that ends with blistered shoulders and a RM 400 bill.
