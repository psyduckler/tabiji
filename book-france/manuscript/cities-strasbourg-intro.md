Strasbourg’s scam profile has one feature that dominates everything else: the *Christkindelsmärik*, France’s oldest Christmas market, which draws over two million visitors each year between late November and December and concentrates them into the narrow streets of Grande Île around the Cathedral, Place Broglie, Place Kléber and the Petite France quarter. *Dernières Nouvelles d’Alsace* and the Strasbourg *Police nationale* flag the market weeks as the highest-risk window of the year for pickpocketing in the *Bas-Rhin* — professional teams work the crowds with hands full of *vin chaud* and food, bags open, attention on the lights.

Outside market season, the pattern reverts to a normal mid-size French city: the petition-clipboard operation at the Cathedral steps and Place Kléber, friendship-bracelet teams working the Petite France bridges, tram-line B and C pickpockets during rush hour, and the fake-police wallet-check scam exploiting the German-border proximity with claims of “cross-border checks” — the *Police nationale* never conducts those on the street, only at fixed crossings. Private Euronet and Travelex ATMs around the cathedral and train station charge excessive fees and push Dynamic Currency Conversion; bank-branded ATMs inside BNP Paribas, Crédit Mutuel or Société Générale branches are the correct default. A safe default: visit the Christmas market on a weekday morning before 11am rather than Saturday afternoon, wear a zipped cross-body bag across the front of your body in every market lane, validate your tram ticket on boarding, and if anyone flashes a badge claiming a border-check, ask to walk together to the nearest *commissariat*.
