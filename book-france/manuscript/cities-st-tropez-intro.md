Saint-Tropez has a scam profile unlike anywhere else in France because a meaningful share of the overcharging is built into the venue itself. *Fortune* and French media have documented beach clubs and port-front restaurants operating internal diner databases — if you do not spend enough or tip enough on one visit, your reservation for the next one quietly disappears. Clubs such as Nikki Beach impose weekend minimum spends of €1,500 per head or €5,000 per table, and one widely reported incident involved a waiter following an Italian customer into the parking lot to demand an additional €500 on an already €500 tip. The mayor of Saint-Tropez has publicly called this pattern “racketeering” in comments to the local press. Service is legally included in every French menu price under the *service compris* rule, but that does not stop staff at several Port and Pampelonne venues from steering American and Middle Eastern tourists toward a second tip on the card terminal.

Layered over the restaurant pattern is the same Riviera luxury-watch operation that works Cannes — three separate Saint-Tropez incidents in a single 2024 summer weekend produced a combined roughly €400,000 in stolen timepieces — plus a villa-burglary pattern that operates at an unusual scale. A widely reported 2023 incident saw two British tourists lose close to €1 million in handbags, watches, and jewelry from a five-star hotel suite ransacked within hours of check-in; local police questioned a hotel employee but filed the case without arrest. A safe default: ask for minimum-spend policies in writing before booking any beach club, never wire deposits for villas outside an established agency with a verifiable *RCS* (French company registry) number, request upper-floor rooms with no terrace access, and leave any watch over €5,000 at home — the Pampelonne beach clubs are under continuous surveillance by the same spotters who work Cannes.
