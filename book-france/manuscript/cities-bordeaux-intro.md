Bordeaux’s scam risk is moderate by French standards — the city is less tourist-saturated than Paris or Nice, but the pedestrian geometry concentrates the risk sharply. Rue Sainte-Catherine is one of the longest pedestrian shopping streets in Europe at 1.2 kilometers, and it runs directly between Place de la Victoire and Place de la Comédie, threading past the tram lines and funneling tourists past the Miroir d’Eau and Place de la Bourse. That corridor is where the pickpocket teams, bracelet operators, and clipboard-petition crews work — the same organized groups documented by UK FCDO guidance and *Rick Steves* trip reports, using the standard distraction playbook while an accomplice lifts a phone or wallet.

The Gare de Bordeaux Saint-Jean is the second concentration point. Thieves boarding trains just before doors close — a group of four to six crowding confused travelers, then exiting as the doors lock — is a documented pattern on the Paris–Bordeaux TGV and on regional lines. The airport taxi economy at Bordeaux-Mérignac runs the usual unlicensed-solicitation grift inside the terminal; the official taxi rank outside Arrivals is the correct default, or Uber/Bolt for price transparency. A safe default: keep bags zipped and in front of your body on Rue Sainte-Catherine and through Saint-Jean’s concourse, use only official taxis at the airport, never pay for a holiday rental outside Airbnb or Vrbo’s platform, and verify any Bordeaux address on Google Street View before wiring deposits.
