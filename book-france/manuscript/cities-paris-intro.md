Paris sits at the top of every European pickpocket chart Euronews publishes. The city generates 16.5 percent of all theft-focused travel reviews on the continent — more than Rome, Barcelona, or Prague. The signature operation is the Hamidovic gang, a Bosnian-led organization that *Connexion France* documented as employing roughly one hundred young girls recruited from Eastern Europe, with daily theft quotas of €300 to €1,000 and responsibility for two-thirds of all Métro thefts before ringleaders were arrested after a three-year investigation. France24 covered the same group’s trial, noting members “all give the same surname and claim they are 12 years old” to avoid custody. A *Le Parisien* sting in July 2025 sent two journalists — one dressed as a Parisian local, one in an Eiffel Tower T-shirt — through the same tourist-zone cafés and documented the American being charged roughly 50 percent more across every restaurant tested.

The risk is concentrated in four corridors: Châtelet–Les Halles through Gare du Nord along the RER B (the single highest-risk transit route, fed by CDG airport arrivals); the Louvre–Tuileries–Champs-Élysées axis where the gold-ring and clumsy-jogger crews work; the Sacré-Cœur steps in Montmartre, where bracelet scammers surrounded the Prefect of Police, Bernard Boucault, himself in 2014; and the Trocadéro–Eiffel Tower plaza, where a three-card monte crew became the subject of an eleven-million-view TikTok in August 2025 after an American tourist filmed the sequence (“imagine being scammed by three cups in 2025”) and the clip circulated through every Paris-travel feed for a month. A safe default: take the RER B from CDG only with your bag on your lap, book hotels in the Marais or Saint-Germain rather than around Gare du Nord, treat every clipboard and “found” gold ring as the opening move of a team operation, and keep your phone inside a zipped pocket whenever you step within arm’s reach of a Métro door.
