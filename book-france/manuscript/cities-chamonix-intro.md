Chamonix’s scam economy has almost nothing in common with the rest of France — the risk is not pickpockets on a Métro, it is fake chalet listings and unlicensed mountain operators whose failure modes are significantly more serious than a lost wallet. The dominant scam is winter-season chalet-rental fraud: operators screenshot luxury listings, repost them at suspiciously low peak-season rates on Facebook Marketplace and independent sites, and demand wire-transfer deposits. Victims arrive to find the property occupied by its actual owner, or not existing at all. *Euronews* and local booking agencies flag this as the single most widespread scam affecting Chamonix visitors.

The second layer is impersonation of certified mountain professionals. Unlicensed “guides” advertising Mont Blanc summit attempts at €1,500 and up have turned back or abandoned exhausted clients near high-altitude refuges on the Goûter Route, with no insurance coverage if anything goes wrong — the only legitimate route is a UIAGM/IFMGA-certified guide booked through the Compagnie des Guides de Chamonix. Fake ski instructors undercut the École du Ski Français on rate, and French courts have fined operators over €26,000 for employing uncertified instructors. On-slope, snatch-and-grab theft of €2,000 skis from restaurant racks at Planpraz, La Flégère, and Les Grands Montets is routine during peak weeks. The Geneva-to-Chamonix airport transfer is the first pressure point most travelers hit: unlicensed drivers quote per-person when they mean per-vehicle, or refuse to cross the French border mid-route without extra cash. A safe default: book only through the Compagnie des Guides or IFMGA-registered guides, verify the *carte professionnelle* before any summit attempt, pre-book Geneva transfers through an operator listed on the Chamonix-Mont-Blanc tourist-office directory (chamonix.com) or one carrying a valid *Licence LOTI* French passenger-transport license — named examples include Alpine Fleet and Chamonix Cabs, both of which publish fixed per-vehicle rates and appear on the tourist-office list at time of writing — and never wire money for a chalet you have not seen verified on Google Street View.
