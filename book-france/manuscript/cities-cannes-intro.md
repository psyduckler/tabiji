Cannes has the same Riviera pickpocket baseline as Nice, plus a luxury-crime layer that operates on a different order of magnitude. In 2024, the Alpes-Maritimes *département* recorded 301 luxury watches stolen worth roughly €7.7 million — organized crews with spotters identifying Rolex and Patek Philippe targets on La Croisette, signaling to moped riders with tampered plates who snatch and flee. Attacks have involved tear gas. The crews work restaurant terraces along the Boulevard de la Croisette and Port Canto hotel lobbies, and during the Festival de Cannes in May the operation compresses into roughly two weeks of maximum density around the Palais des Festivals.

Outside the watch trade, the dominant scam during festival season is accommodation fraud — *Travel and Tour World* has documented fake villa and apartment listings spiking sharply when the festival compresses demand, with scammers pulling photos from legitimate listings and insisting on wire-transfer payment. Film Festival itself is a small cottage industry of counterfeits: fake screening tickets, fake party invitations, and bogus “festival internship programs” charging thousands of euros. The standard taxi scam works the Nice Airport–Cannes run (€85 official fixed rate) with drivers letting the meter continue past the fixed fare. A safe default: book any accommodation for the May festival window on a credit card through an established platform and never by wire transfer, verify all Festival passes through festival-cannes.com only, photograph the taxi meter at pickup, and if you are wearing anything worth over €5,000 on your wrist, assume you have been spotted before you sit down.
