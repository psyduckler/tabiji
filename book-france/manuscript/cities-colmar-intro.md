Colmar is a small-town Alsace scam profile driven by two seasonal spikes and one year-round *Alsace Wine Route* pattern. The biggest risk window is the Colmar Christmas markets from late November through December, when professional pickpockets work the dense crowds moving between the stalls at Place des Dominicains, Place de l’Ancienne Douane, and Petite Venise — the medieval lanes are narrow enough that a crush at any stall creates cover. Restaurant overcharging in the same zone is common, with tourists reporting things like a €7-per-person “sharing charge” for splitting a menu.

The Gare de Colmar is the second concentration point — organized teams pose as helpful strangers offering luggage assistance on the platform stairs, one distracting while another goes through bags. The Wine Route pressure-sales pattern is more of a dignity tax than a scam: free tastings at uncertified *caves* from Eguisheim through Riquewihr rely on generous pours and social pressure to push bottle purchases. Rental-car break-ins at Wine Route parking lots follow the same pattern as Alpine trailheads — crews watching tourists open trunks, then smashing windows during the tasting. QR-code parking-meter “quishing” — fake stickers over legitimate codes, redirecting to phishing sites that capture card details — has been documented by *Connexion France* throughout France, including in Colmar. A safe default: visit the Christmas markets Monday–Thursday before noon, decline luggage help at the station, use only *Vignobles et Découvertes*-certified wineries, and pay parking at the meter with a chip-card rather than scanning any sticker-mounted QR code.
