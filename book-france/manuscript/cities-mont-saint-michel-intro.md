Mont-Saint-Michel is not a pickpocket town, it is a concentrated tourist-trap ecosystem where the island’s single narrow street — Grande Rue — functions as a kilometer-long retail funnel with no repeat customers and therefore no incentive for any operator to provide fair value. The most famous example is La Mère Poulard, which charges €40–80 for a soufflé omelet and €19–26 for a bottle of cider (under €3 at supermarkets), with 30-to-40-minute waits just to have an order taken. Tripadvisor reviews over a decade are consistent: the food is mediocre, the reputation is the product. Small private museums on Grande Rue charge €9 each, €18 as a package, for dusty exhibits that multiple reviewers have called “a disgrace.” The parking lot is its own price trap at €14–25 per visit with opaque signage, and the hotel inventory on the island is controlled largely by one family (the Poulard group), with documented last-minute “overbooking” relocations to inferior mainland properties at the same premium rate.

The real safety concern is not the shops but the bay itself. The *Préfecture de la Manche* authorizes only 67 certified guides to lead bay crossings, because the mudflats contain quicksand pockets and the incoming tide advances faster than a person can run — up to six kilometers per hour — and unaccompanied tourists have required helicopter rescue or died. A safe default: do not eat on the island, skip every private museum, visit the Abbey (€13), which is the only genuine paid attraction worth the fee, check *ot-montsaintmichel.com* tide schedules before you park, and never step onto the mudflats without a *Préfecture*-certified guide.
