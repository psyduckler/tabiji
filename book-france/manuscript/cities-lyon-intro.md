Lyon’s scam density is lower than Paris or Marseille but the geometry is different — the city runs two parallel tourist axes (Vieux Lyon on the Fourvière side and the Presqu’île between Place Bellecour and Hôtel de Ville) joined by heavily used Métro and tram crossings, and pickpocket teams move along the transit seams. Métro Line D and the T2 tram are the main pressure points, along with the Confluence tram stop where groups of women with oversized bags have been observed surrounding tourists to gain backpack access. Gare Part-Dieu and Gare Perrache are the two high-volume arrival points and both carry the standard French-rail pattern: clipboard petition teams, fake-ticket touts, and RER-style door-close snatches during rush hour.

The restaurant-overcharging layer is worth a separate note because Lyon trades heavily on its reputation as France’s gastronomic capital, and tourist-zone *bouchons* in Vieux Lyon and on Rue Mercière have been flagged repeatedly — by *Rick Steves* forums and *The Local France* — for inflated bills, “tourist menu” swaps, and automatic expensive-bottled-water service. Private Euronet ATMs around Part-Dieu and the tourist zones charge excessive fees and push Dynamic Currency Conversion at exchange rates up to four times worse than French bank ATMs. A safe default: keep your bag in front on Métro D and the T2, use ATMs inside BNP Paribas, Société Générale, or LCL branches only, decline DCC at every prompt, eat in a *bouchon* recommended by a local rather than one with a chalkboard facing Vieux Lyon’s tourist flow, and buy tram tickets only from TCL machines.
