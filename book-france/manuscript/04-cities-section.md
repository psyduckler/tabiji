# The Scams: City by City {-}

The chapters that follow document every scam reported in sixteen French cities. Cities are ordered by visitor volume and reading utility — starting with Paris, then down to the Riviera (Nice, Cannes, Saint-Tropez), across Provence and the Languedoc (Marseille, Avignon, Montpellier, Toulouse), up through Lyon to the Alps (Chamonix, Annecy), over to the Atlantic (Bordeaux, Biarritz), and finishing in Alsace and Normandy (Strasbourg, Colmar, Mont-Saint-Michel).

If you are visiting only a few of these cities, skip directly to the relevant chapters. If you are doing a longer Grand Tour, skim them all — the same core scams reappear with local variations. The Sacré-Cœur friendship-bracelet trap that ambushes visitors in Montmartre runs identically on La Croisette in Cannes, at the Basilique Saint-Sernin in Toulouse, and at Strasbourg Cathedral. The *rabatteur*-style tourist-menu overcharge — one posted price outside, a higher English menu inside — is documented in every city in this book. Métro and tram pickpocket clusters track French transit maps, from the Paris Line 1 and RER B to Lyon’s T2, Montpellier’s Comédie stop, and Strasbourg’s Lines B and C. And in the Alps, the chalet-rental fraud around Chamonix is a winter-season cousin of the fake Airbnb listings that plague Paris, Marseille, and Bordeaux year-round. Knowing the pattern in one city makes you faster to spot it in the next.

## The Sixteen Cities at a Glance {-}

![Paris — Eiffel Tower and Haussmann rooftops at dusk](assets/cities/paris.jpg)

**Paris** — The Louvre, the Eiffel Tower, and one of the densest tourist-scam ecosystems in Europe. The Sacré-Cœur friendship-bracelet ambush on the Montmartre steps that once caught the Paris Prefect of Police himself; a Châtelet–Les Halles door-close pickpocket gang that a *Connexion France* investigation tied to two-thirds of all Métro thefts; the CDG unlicensed-taxi circuit quoting €200 for the official €56 fixed-rate Right Bank run; and a *Le Parisien* sting that caught Eiffel Tower–area cafés charging American tourists roughly 50 percent more than locals.

![Nice — Promenade des Anglais curve and Baie des Anges](assets/cities/nice.jpg)

**Nice** — The Riviera’s anchor city, with scams stacked along the Promenade des Anglais. Beach grab-and-run on the pebble shore while you swim; a Nice Airport tram-ticket trap that sells a €10 “airport ticket” for a ride that costs €1.70 one stop away at Grand Arenas; *vol à la portière* car-door grabs at traffic lights targeting rental plates; and a Riviera-wide luxury-watch snatch pattern working the seafront hotels.

![Cannes — Croisette palms and Palais des Festivals](assets/cities/cannes.jpg)

**Cannes** — The Croisette, the Palais des Festivals, and a 2024 Alpes-Maritimes tally of 301 watches stolen worth roughly €7.7 million. Moped-borne Rolex and Patek snatches on restaurant terraces; taxi drivers who start the Nice-Airport fixed rate (€85) with the meter already running; a Film Festival season that doubles as prime hunting ground for Airbnb phantom-listing scams; and the classic petition-clipboard pickpocket team at the Palais des Festivals.

![Saint-Tropez — Vieux Port yachts at golden hour](assets/cities/st-tropez.jpg)

**Saint-Tropez** — Beach clubs, superyachts, and a scam ecosystem the mayor himself has publicly called “racketeering.” Restaurants reportedly maintaining internal databases that bump diners who don’t tip American-style; Pampelonne Beach clubs with weekend minimum-spend policies (Nikki Beach at €1,500 per head); a single weekend of three separate watch thefts totaling roughly €400,000; and villa-rental fraud built on stolen photos and wire-transfer deposits.

![Marseille — Vieux-Port and Notre-Dame de la Garde](assets/cities/marseille.jpg)

**Marseille** — The Vieux-Port, the Calanques, and a pickpocket density around Gare Saint-Charles that ranks with any Paris hub. Team lifts on Métro Line 1 and La Canebière; the Provence-wide smash-and-grab at Calanques trailhead parking lots; airport-taxi overcharges quoting double the legitimate €50 day / €60 night run; and tourist-menu stings at Vieux-Port terraces where a €28 menu hides 9 of 13 dishes behind €5–10 supplements.

![Avignon — Palais des Papes and Pont Saint-Bénézet](assets/cities/avignon.jpg)

**Avignon** — The Palais des Papes, the Pont d’Avignon, and the July Festival d’Avignon that floods the Intra-Muros with hundreds of thousands of distracted theatergoers. Festival-week pickpocket surges on Place de l’Horloge; TGV station luggage theft on trains stopping briefly at Avignon TGV; Les Halles market upselling at 3–4x comparable Provence markets; and the petition-clipboard team stationed outside the Palace.

![Montpellier — Place de la Comédie and Three Graces fountain](assets/cities/montpellier.jpg)

**Montpellier** — A student city with a tram system designed for pickpockets. Organized teams working Tram Lines 1 and 2 at the Comédie and Gare Saint-Roch stops; Three Graces fountain distraction lifts at Place de la Comédie café terraces; TGV luggage thefts that ring station-stop snatches across the French rail network; and A9 rest-stop car break-ins on the approach from Nîmes.

![Toulouse — Place du Capitole at blue hour](assets/cities/toulouse.jpg)

**Toulouse** — La Ville Rose, the Canal du Midi, and Gare Matabiau — the most theft-prone stretch between Rue Bayard and the Allées Jean-Jaurès. Bracelet ambushes on the steps of the Basilique Saint-Sernin; canal-side bicycle grab-and-ride phone snatches from outdoor terraces; the petition-clipboard deaf-mute script on Place du Capitole; and the broken-meter taxi gambit at Toulouse-Blagnac Airport.

![Lyon — Vieux Lyon and Fourvière Basilica above the Saône](assets/cities/lyon.jpg)

**Lyon** — Gastronomy capital, finance hub, and a Confluence tram stop where women with oversized bags surround tourists to reach their backpacks. Métro Line D and T2 tram pickpocket teams; Part-Dieu and Perrache fake Métro-ticket touts selling expired passes; tourist-menu overcharges on Rue Mercière and in Vieux Lyon; and Euronet private-ATM fee stacks charging up to four times the Société Générale or BNP rate.

![Chamonix — Mont Blanc and Aiguille du Midi](assets/cities/chamonix.jpg)

**Chamonix** — Mont Blanc, Aiguille du Midi, and the biggest scam pattern in the French Alps: fake chalet rentals with screenshotted photos, wire-transfer deposits, and listings that evaporate on arrival. Unlicensed “mountain guides” charging €1,500 for Mont Blanc summit attempts and abandoning clients near the Refuge de Tête Rousse; counterfeit Compagnie du Mont-Blanc ski passes that trigger €999 gate fines; and Geneva Airport transfer drivers who refuse to cross into France without extra cash.

![Annecy — turquoise lake and Palais de l’Ile](assets/cities/annecy.jpg)

**Annecy** — The Venice of the Alps, with a turquoise lake and a clipboard-petition team working the Old Town pedestrian zones. Summer beach-belongings theft at Plage d’Albigny and Plage des Marquisats; rental-car break-ins at trailhead parking around the lake; dual-menu restaurant overcharges on the Old Town lakefront terraces; and a 2025 ATM-skimming ring that the *gendarmerie* broke after €580,000 in losses across Western Europe.

![Bordeaux — Place de la Bourse and Miroir d’Eau](assets/cities/bordeaux.jpg)

**Bordeaux** — Wine capital, Place de la Bourse, and Europe’s longest pedestrian shopping street at Rue Sainte-Catherine. Team pickpocket lifts at the Miroir d’Eau; Gare Saint-Jean door-close gangs of four to six that board TGVs at the last second and exit before the doors finish closing; tram pickpocket clusters at tourist-serving stops; and Bordeaux-Mérignac Airport unlicensed taxi quotes of 2–3x the normal fare.

![Biarritz — Grande Plage and Rocher de la Vierge](assets/cities/biarritz.jpg)

**Biarritz** — Basque surf town on the Atlantic. The bird-droppings pickpocket setup on the Promenade du Casino; beach-blanket grabs at Grande Plage and Côte des Basques during July and August; remote-beach rental-car break-ins at Plage de la Milady and cliffside parking; and the “broken camera” guilt-trip scam at the Rocher de la Vierge viewpoint.

![Strasbourg — Cathedral and Petite France half-timbered houses](assets/cities/strasbourg.jpg)

**Strasbourg** — The Cathedral, Petite France, and a Christkindelsmärik that draws over 2 million visitors into ideal pickpocket conditions every December. Christmas-market professional teams working Place Broglie and Place Kléber; petition-clipboard crews on the Cathedral steps; Tram Lines B and C lifts between the Gare Centrale and the Old Town; and a German-border fake-police wallet-check variant claiming “cross-border” authority.

![Colmar — Petite Venise canal and half-timbered houses](assets/cities/colmar.jpg)

**Colmar** — Alsace Wine Route hub, Petite Venise, and a Gare de Colmar luggage-helper team that works the platform stairs. Christmas-market pickpockets in the narrow medieval lanes; a France-wide QR-code parking-meter *quishing* scam with fake stickers stuck over legitimate codes; tourist-menu traps charging €7 “sharing supplements” near Maison Pfister; and the rental-car break-in pattern at Wine Route village parking lots from Eguisheim to Riquewihr.

![Mont-Saint-Michel — tidal causeway and Abbey silhouette](assets/cities/mont-saint-michel.jpg)

**Mont-Saint-Michel** — UNESCO monastery, 14-meter tides, and a single narrow Grande Rue where every restaurant is a tourist trap. La Mère Poulard omelets at €40–80 each, cider at €19–26 per bottle; a parking lot charging €17 for 30 minutes with no alternative; dusty private museums at €9 each or €18 bundled, described by reviewers as “a disgrace”; and uncertified bay-crossing “guides” leading tourists into the quicksand pockets that have triggered multiple helicopter rescues — only 67 Préfecture-de-la-Manche-authorized guides are legally qualified to cross.
