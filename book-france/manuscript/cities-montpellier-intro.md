Montpellier’s scam profile is heavily transit-weighted. The TaM tram network — particularly Lines 1 and 2, and the stops at Gare Saint-Roch, Place de l’Europe, and Comédie — is where most tourist theft happens, worked by organized teams who exploit the boarding-and-exiting crush to lift phones and wallets from backpacks and back pockets. Gare Saint-Roch itself and the blocks immediately around it have a rougher reputation after dark, and the station forecourt is where unofficial taxi drivers solicit arriving travelers with inflated flat rates (the legitimate airport-to-center fare is roughly €35 daytime and €42 at night from Montpellier Méditerranée Airport).

The TGV line into Saint-Roch is the second pressure point. *Connexion France* has documented the broader French pattern of up to 400 monthly luggage thefts across the TGV network, with crews boarding at scheduled stops and grabbing bags from overhead racks just before doors close — Montpellier’s position on the Paris–Barcelona corridor keeps it squarely inside that pattern. Outside transit, Place de la Comédie is the tourist-trap center of gravity: the Three Graces fountain, the outdoor café terraces, and the pedestrian flows around the Opéra Comédie draw the petition-clipboard and gold-ring teams. A safe default: validate your tram ticket on boarding, keep phones out of back pockets and bags zipped and in front of you between Comédie and Saint-Roch, sit where you can see your luggage on TGVs, and use the official airport taxi rank or TaM shuttle rather than any driver soliciting inside the terminal.
