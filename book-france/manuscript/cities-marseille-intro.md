Marseille’s scam economy runs harder than most French cities because the *Vieux-Port*–Gare Saint-Charles corridor concentrates tourists, commuters, and organized crews into roughly half a square kilometer, and the *Police nationale* and *BAC* (Brigade anti-criminalité) spend most of their tourist-zone effort trying to keep the pickpocket turnover moving. Métro Line 1 between Saint-Charles and Vieux-Port is the spine — doors-closing snatches, shoulder-press distractions, and children-swarm pickpocketing during boarding. The fake-police wallet-check operation is active enough that the standard warning applies: real *Police nationale* officers do not inspect your wallet or count your cash on the street, only your ID.

The second layer is car-based. Marseille and the surrounding Provence countryside run one of the highest rental-car break-in rates in Western Europe, with the Calanques trailhead parking lots notorious for smash-and-grabs where thieves watch tourists park, track typical hike durations, and empty the car in under a minute. A9 motorway rest stops approaching the city are targeted for the same reason — rental plates are visible at 50 meters. The airport-taxi economy mirrors Nice’s: roughly €50 daytime and €60 at night is the real Marseille Provence Airport to city-center fare, and anything significantly above that coming from a driver soliciting inside the terminal is an unlicensed operator. A safe default: take the official taxi rank outside Arrivals (never the terminal-interior approach), stay on Métro Line 1 only with bag in front of you and hands on it, book Calanques hikes via shuttle bus rather than rental car, and treat the pedestrian streets off Canebière after dark as a different city from the restored Vieux-Port frontage.
