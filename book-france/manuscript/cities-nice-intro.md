Nice runs two parallel scam ecosystems: the daytime Promenade des Anglais pickpocket circuit aimed at mass tourists, and a high-end luxury-watch robbery operation that has turned the Côte d’Azur into one of Europe’s densest Rolex-hunting grounds. *Nice-Matin* and regional Police nationale reports have tracked a surge in moped-based watch thefts along the Promenade and around the Negresco-era hotels, with organized teams on scooters pulling up to diners on restaurant terraces. On the other end of the spectrum, the airport-to-town corridor runs a steady overcharging operation — the official fixed taxi rate from Aéroport Nice Côte d’Azur to the city center is €32, but unlicensed drivers at the arrivals hall routinely quote €60 or more, and the airport tram ticket machine itself sells a tourist-priced €10 “airport ticket” when the regular Lignes d’Azur fare is €1.70 from the Grand Arenas stop one station away.

The risk zone is compact: the Promenade des Anglais beach frontage, Vieux Nice between Place Masséna and the Cours Saleya, the Gare de Nice-Ville forecourt, and the Grand Arenas tram stop where luggage-laden travelers figuring out ticket machines are targeted by “helpful” teens. Rental-car drivers get a third pattern — the *vol à la portière* car-door grab at traffic lights and rental-car break-ins at Mercantour-adjacent trailhead parking in the Alpes-Maritimes hinterland, both of which the département has among the highest rates in Western Europe. A safe default: take the tram from Grand Arenas (walk one stop) rather than the airport platform, buy only Lignes d’Azur–branded tickets at the machine and validate immediately on boarding or eat a €40 fine from the inspector, leave any watch above €5,000 in the hotel safe, and eat at least two blocks back from the Promenade.
