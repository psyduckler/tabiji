Annecy is one of the lower-risk cities in this book — a lakefront Alpine town where the scam volume is a fraction of Paris or Marseille — but the patterns that do exist are concentrated tightly around two zones: the Vieille Ville pedestrian streets running past the Palais de l’Île, and the beach frontage on Lake Annecy during July and August. Clipboard-petition teams and distraction-theft groups work the Old Town and lakefront promenade in peak summer, targeting visitors taking photos at the canals or queuing for boat tours.

Beach theft at Plage d’Albigny, Plage des Marquisats, and the Talloires beaches is the most frequent incident type during warm months — towel-and-bag snatches while owners are swimming. The rental-car pattern that affects all of Alpine France applies here too: thieves identify rentals in trailhead and viewpoint parking lots around the lake, then smash windows to get at luggage and electronics. An international ATM skimming ring was broken up in 2025 after stealing more than €580,000 across Western Europe, and standalone ATMs in Annecy’s tourist zone fit the same profile. A safe default: leave passports and excess cash at the hotel before going to the beach, never open the trunk of a rental car in the lot where you park, use ATMs inside bank branches, and hold your bag in front of you in the crush around the Palais de l’Île between 11am and 4pm.
