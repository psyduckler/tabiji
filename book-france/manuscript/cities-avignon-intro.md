Avignon is a calm Provençal town for most of the year and a scam-density spike for three weeks each July during the *Festival d’Avignon*, when hundreds of thousands of visitors pour into the walled historic center. The festival compresses theater crowds into the Palais des Papes courtyard, Place de l’Horloge, and every café terrace inside the ramparts, and professional pickpocket teams follow the ticket queues. *Le Dauphiné Libéré* and regional Police nationale briefings consistently flag the festival window as the highest-risk period in Vaucluse for tourist theft.

Outside festival season, the picture is smaller but predictable: the petition-clipboard teams work Place de l’Horloge and the Palais approaches, the gold-ring opener appears along the Rhône quays, and the Avignon TGV station and Avignon-Centre station both have taxi overcharge problems with unofficial drivers targeting arriving visitors. SNCF and *Sud Ouest* have documented the broader TGV-luggage-theft pattern on French long-distance rail — crews boarding at a stop, waiting for the doors-closing chime, then grabbing luggage from the overhead rack as they exit — and Avignon’s two stations sit on the Paris–Marseille mainline where this operation runs regularly. A safe default: if you visit during festival dates, wear a zipped cross-body bag across the front of your body at every performance queue, buy festival tickets only through festival-avignon.com, keep rolling luggage on your lap (not the overhead rack) on TGV trains into Avignon TGV, and use the official taxi rank at either station rather than anyone soliciting fares.
