Biarritz’s scam profile is shaped by its coastline. The dominant risk is beach-belongings theft at Grande Plage, Plage Miramar, and Côte des Basques during July and August — the classic Atlantic-resort pattern of a towel unattended for fifteen minutes while the owner swims, and a thief who has been watching from the sand scoops the phone, wallet, and keys in one pass and disappears into the promenade crowd. Remote surf-parking lots (Côte des Basques, Plage de la Milady, the cliffside access roads south of town) see high rates of rental-car break-ins by crews who stake out the lots for the afternoon surf window.

The town itself runs the standard French pedestrian-zone operation at much lower density than Paris: bird-droppings distraction thefts along the Promenade du Casino and Avenue Édouard-VII, clipboard-petition teams on the Grande Plage promenade, shell-game and three-card monte setups near the casino during high season. Les Halles de Biarritz on Saturday mornings is the one predictable indoor pickpocket zone — locals and tourists jostling over *piment d’Espelette* and seafood, with the cash changing hands providing the distraction. The Biarritz-Pays Basque Airport (BIQ) to city-center taxi is roughly €25 from the official rank; unlicensed drivers inside the terminal quote €40 or more. A safe default: bring only a waterproof pouch to the beach, leave valuables in the hotel, never open the trunk in a surf-beach parking lot once you have arrived, hold your bag in front of you at Les Halles, and use only the official taxi rank or a ride-hailing app at the airport.
