# How to Use This Book {-}

France is extraordinary. It is also, statistically, one of the most scam-prone countries in Europe for first-time visitors. Paris alone registers tens of thousands of tourist pickpocketings a year, most of them concentrated on half a dozen Métro lines and four or five square miles of central arrondissements. The French Riviera works a cash-tourist circuit from Cannes to Monaco every summer. The Alps have their own chalet-rental fraud season every winter. French news pages publish a fresh Police nationale sweep from a different city almost every week.

We want you to come home with stories about the cafés, the cathedrals, the mountain villages, and the long lunches — not about a €400 taxi from Charles de Gaulle or a wallet lifted on Line 1 before you even reached your hotel.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents one hundred and ninety-one specific scams that trip up first-time visitors, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been reported in the French press, cross-referenced against Police nationale or Gendarmerie arrest records, and confirmed by recent traveler accounts on r/ParisTravelGuide, r/france, r/francetravel, and the city-specific subreddits.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First, the name of the scam, the category it falls into (pickpocket, transport, overcharging, tourist trap, and so on), and how serious and how common it is. Then two short sections: **How this scam works** — a plain narrative of how it unfolds in real time, with real euro amounts where we have them — and **How to avoid it**, the specific moves that shut it down. Each entry ends with where it happens. If you can read one entry, you can read them all.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. Most scams in France are variations on six patterns. Learn the patterns and you will spot a new scam the moment it starts — even one that is not in this book. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will then read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters, you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **French exit-phrase card**. Short phrases you can say, word for word, at the moments when your politeness would normally get you into trouble. Take a screenshot of it on your phone.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A step-by-step flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens, you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. Police, ambulance, tourist lines, embassies, consulates, and the nearest English-speaking medical care, organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and skim the three appendices. Read the chapters for the cities you will be visiting. Take a screenshot of the phrase card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book turns out to apply during your trip, open it again in the taxi or in your hotel room and check what to do.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.

Bon voyage.
