Toulouse’s risk map has two sharply defined hotspots: the Place du Capitole–Basilique Saint-Sernin pedestrian zone, where the bracelet and petition teams work the UNESCO-listed pilgrimage-route crowd, and the Gare Matabiau train station with the blocks between Rue Bayard and Allées Jean-Jaurès, which local guides and American-expat forums consistently flag as the riskier end of the city for theft and muggings. Saint-Sernin draws a steady flow of tourists up from Capitole, and the friendship-bracelet operators have adapted to the route — a string on the wrist at Saint-Sernin’s porch is the signature move.

The Canal du Midi and Garonne quays add a second pattern: bicycle-grab snatches where thieves on bikes rip phones and bags from outdoor café tables along the water. The shell-game and three-card monte crews appear occasionally on the busier pedestrian stretches, always rigged, always with planted shills in the crowd. Métro pickpocketing concentrates on Line A rush-hour boardings at Capitole, Jean-Jaurès, and Matabiau. The airport taxi economy at Toulouse-Blagnac has the same pattern as other French cities — unofficial solicitation inside the terminal, drivers using night-tariff rates during the day — and the official taxi rank outside Arrivals is the correct default. A safe default: keep your hands in your pockets walking Place du Capitole and the approach to Saint-Sernin, never leave your phone at the edge of a Canal du Midi café table, use only the official Blagnac taxi rank or the Tisséo tram (€1.70), and treat the Matabiau forecourt at night as a different environment from the daytime station.
