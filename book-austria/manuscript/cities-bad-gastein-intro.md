Bad Gastein is a dramatic Belle Époque spa town stacked around a waterfall in a narrow alpine valley — half faded grand-hotel grandeur, half ski-and-wellness resort enjoying a stylish revival. It is safe and quiet, and its scams reflect a place that lives on accommodation, thermal spas, and mountain excursions. The two that matter most are the fake holiday-apartment listing — a too-cheap belle-époque studio whose "owner" wants a deposit wired to an email — and the stack of small overcharges around spas, restaurants, and the long transfer from Salzburg Airport.

The risk sits online (rental listings around Straubinger Platz and the Kaiser-Wilhelm-Promenade), at the Felsentherme spa, on the mountain-restaurant terraces, and on the roughly 100-kilometer airport run. Defenses: book lodging only through a platform with pay-through protection and a chargeable card — never wire a deposit to an individual, and be deeply suspicious of any apartment priced well below the rest of the market. Pre-book the Salzburg transfer with a named company at a written price rather than taking a curbside offer. At the Felsentherme, check exactly what your entry ticket includes before you add extras. Decline the mountain-restaurant bread basket if you don't want it, and read the bill. The Gasteiner Heilstollen radon gallery is a genuine medical facility — book it directly, not through a concierge package. And at the Euronet machine by the station, choose euros.
