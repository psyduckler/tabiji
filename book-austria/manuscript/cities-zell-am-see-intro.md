Zell am See is a lake-and-glacier resort beneath the Kitzsteinhorn — a summer-hiking and winter-ski hub that sits roughly 90 kilometers from its nearest airport at Salzburg. It is a safe, family-oriented town, and its scams are almost entirely transport-and-excursion overcharges aimed at arriving visitors who just want to get to the lake. The standout is the taxi problem: unlicensed "black taxis" that greet you warmly at the Bahnhof or at Salzburg Airport, fixed-price transfer shakedowns over that long mountain route, and no-meter quotes for short in-town hops and day trips.

The risk lives at the Zell am See station and Schüttdorf, at distant Salzburg Airport, on the Esplanade lakefront, and at the excursion desks selling glacier and waterfall tours. Defenses: ignore any driver who approaches you claiming to be "with the hotel taxis" — use a marked, metered car or a pre-booked named transfer with a written price. Confirm the meter is running for in-town rides. Before you pay for a cable car or an excursion, ask your hotel about the Zell am See–Kaprun Summer Card, which is often handed to guests free and can replace a 61-euro Kitzsteinhorn ticket. Compare any "private" Grossglockner or Krimml Waterfalls tour against doing it yourself. At a lakeside café, ask whether the water is tap (free) or bottled, and read the bill before you pay.
