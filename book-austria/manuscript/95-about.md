# About Tabiji {-}

Tabiji is a travel-safety research organization. We document scams, health risks, and local advisories for travelers across more than fifty cities worldwide, and we publish everything as a free public resource at **[tabiji.ai](https://tabiji.ai)**.

We started this series after noticing that most scam roundups online skip the one thing travelers actually need in the moment: the exact phrase, the exact move, that ends the interaction. Remembering a list of fifty-six scams is hard when you are tired and under pressure. Remembering six patterns is much easier. That is the shape we have tried to give this book.

Every scam documented here was drawn from Austrian-press coverage — *Der Standard*, *Die Presse*, the *Kronen Zeitung*, the *Kurier*, *Falter*, the *Salzburger Nachrichten*, the *Oberösterreichische Nachrichten*, the *Tiroler Tageszeitung*, and the *Kleine Zeitung* — cross-referenced against police and Federal Criminal Police (Bundeskriminalamt) warnings, consumer-protection notices from the VKI and the Arbeiterkammer, and firsthand traveler accounts on Reddit's r/Austria, r/Vienna, and the destination-specific forums. Where we have named operators, documented case counts, or euro amounts, we cite them. Where a pattern has been reported but not formally investigated, we say so.

For new scams and live updates to the cases in this book, scan the QR code on the back cover or visit **[tabiji.ai/scams](https://tabiji.ai/scams)**. We maintain the database quarterly, and most corrections reach the site within a week of a credible report.

We hope you come home with stories about the coffee houses and the mountains.

— Tabiji
