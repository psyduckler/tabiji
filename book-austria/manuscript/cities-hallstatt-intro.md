Hallstatt is the most-photographed village in Austria and, per resident, the most overtouristed place in this book — a UNESCO lakeside hamlet of roughly eight hundred people that can absorb ten thousand day-trippers in a single day. There is essentially no crime here; the "scams" are the frictions of a tiny, car-hostile, view-obsessed village monetizing a flood of visitors. The recurring traps are all logistical: getting fined for driving where you shouldn't, overpaying to park, getting upsold on the ferry, and absorbing a lakeview bill that "lands like a punch."

The danger zones are the village access barriers, the P1–P4 lots on the southern edge, the Markt boat dock, and the restaurant terraces on and around the Marktplatz. The defenses: do not drive into the core — when a man waves you past the barrier, he is waving you into a no-car-zone fine; park in the official lot outside and walk or take the ferry. Read the parking machine (the lots are pay-by-the-hour and a lost ticket is penalized). Carry a little cash for the ferry but confirm prices before any "private boat" upsell. At a lakefront table, read the menu, check the bill line by line, and choose euros if the card reader offers a conversion. And before you fly a drone for the postcard shot at the Haus Cian viewpoint, check that it's permitted — fines apply.
