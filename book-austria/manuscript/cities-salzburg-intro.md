Salzburg is a small, safe, intensely touristed baroque city — Mozart's birthplace, the *Sound of Music* backdrop, a UNESCO old town that fills every summer and every Advent. Its scams are built around that fame and that turnover: a restaurant host on the side streets off the Getreidegasse waving you toward an inflated menu, Sound of Music tours sold at a markup or rebadged as something they're not, counterfeit Mozart memorabilia, and the orange Euronet ATMs tuned to overcharge. There is also a long-running sympathy approach at the Hauptbahnhof and a private-parking extortion play near the riverbank — both low-grade, both avoidable.

The risk concentrates in the old town around the Getreidegasse, Alter Markt, and Mozart's birthplace, plus the station and the park-and-walk lots used by drivers dodging the steep city garages. Safe defaults: eat one street back from the Getreidegasse, and never follow a host who is luring tourists from the doorway — read the printed menu first. Book Sound of Music and Mozart tours from an established operator's own website, not a curbside or hotel-desk pitch. Park only in official, clearly-signed garages, not an "open area" someone is gesturing you toward. And at any ATM — especially a Euronet on Linzergasse or Getreidegasse — decline the conversion and choose euros.
