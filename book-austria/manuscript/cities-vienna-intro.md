Vienna is overwhelmingly safe — and that is precisely what makes its scams effective. The capital records around 5,300 pickpocket cases a year and accounts for more than half of all pickpocketing in Austria, but the violence rate is so low that visitors relax completely, which is the opening every scam here is built around. The threats are quiet and legal-looking: a card terminal pre-set to dollars, a "fixed" airport fare, a costumed Mozart selling a concert that isn't, a plainclothes "officer" who wants to see your wallet and your PIN. Der Standard has documented the fake-police ID-check scheme against tourists in the Innere Stadt, and the Federal Criminal Police put Vienna's apartment-rental fraud at roughly 30 cases a week.

The risk is concentrated in a compact zone: the 1st-district tourist triangle of Stephansplatz, Graben, and Kärntner Strasse, plus the U1 line, the escalators rising into Stephansplatz station, and the forecourts of Hauptbahnhof and Westbahnhof. Step a few blocks off that axis — into the Beisl where locals actually eat — and both the prices and the risk drop sharply. Safe defaults for first-timers: take the CAT or the ÖBB train from the airport (never a curbside "taxi"), buy concert tickets only from the venue's own website, always choose euros at any ATM or card reader, and keep your bag in front of you with both hands on it on a crowded U-Bahn.
