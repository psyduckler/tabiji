Graz is Austria's second city — a relaxed university town with a UNESCO-listed old town of red rooftops crowned by the Schlossberg clock tower. It sees far fewer foreign tourists than Vienna or Salzburg, and it is correspondingly safe, but it still carries the country's standard kit of money-only scams plus one genuine pickpocket hotspot. The one to know is Jakominiplatz, the central tram hub, where the brief crush as doors open on lines 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7 is exactly the window a pickpocket needs.

The risk sits at Jakominiplatz, on the Schlossberg terrace, along the Herrengasse shopping promenade between the Landhaus and Hauptplatz, at Graz Airport and the late-night taxi ranks, and across the Innenstadt's Blue Zone parking. Defenses: in the tram crush at Jakominiplatz, put your bag in front of you with both hands on it and keep your wallet out of back pockets. On the Schlossberg, expect a view-tax markup and read the menu before you sit. Decline the conversion at the Euronet ATMs on Herrengasse and Hauptplatz and choose euros. Insist on the meter for airport and late-night taxis. And take Graz's Blue Zone parking seriously — it is stricter than it looks, and a short overstay earns a 24-euro slip. Don't sign the Herrengasse clipboard.
