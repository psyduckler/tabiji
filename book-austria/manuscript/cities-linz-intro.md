Linz is an easy, low-crime city — the Ars Electronica town, a working Danube port, and an increasingly popular river-cruise stop with a vast baroque Hauptplatz at its heart. The scams here are the mild, money-only kind that follow cruise passengers and first-time arrivals: an off-meter taxi, a shore excursion sold at a markup, a terrace upsell, a currency-conversion ATM, and the occasional clipboard team. You are not going to be robbed in Linz; you may be quietly overcharged if you take the first option that presents itself.

The risk clusters around three nodes: the cruise dock at Donaupark (between the Brucknerhaus and the Lentos), the Hauptplatz and Altstadt restaurant strip, and the taxi rank at the Hauptbahnhof. The defenses are the same ones that work everywhere in this book. From the dock, book the Salzburg day trip yourself by train (the ÖBB Linz–Salzburg run is fast and cheap) rather than off the ship's flyer. Insist the taxi meter runs, and remember it stops protecting you once the cab crosses the city line toward Hörsching airport — agree the airport total first. On the Hauptplatz, read the menu before you sit and skip any place with a host waving you in. Use a bank ATM rather than the Euronet box on Landstraße, and always choose euros. Buy the Pöstlingbergbahn and attraction tickets at the official counter.
