The Wachau is the loveliest stretch of the Austrian Danube — a UNESCO valley of terraced vineyards, apricot orchards, and the blue-towered abbey of Dürnstein — and its scams are almost entirely a function of the cruise-and-coach tide. On a busy day, a riverboat or a fleet of buses empties hundreds of day-trippers into villages built for a few hundred residents, and the local economy briefly reorganizes itself around moving that crowd through a meal, a ticket, and a souvenir as fast as possible. Nothing here is violent or even particularly aggressive; it is turnover pricing, reseller markups, and the small confusions of a place not designed for cars.

The pinch points are predictable: Dürnstein's single narrow Hauptstraße, the ship docks at Krems and Melk, the parking ring around Dürnstein's no-car core, and the bright Euronet ATMs near both docks. The defenses are simple. Buy boat tickets at the official company counter and confirm the operator and the ship before you pay — Krems-to-Melk runs two competing lines. Park in the signed lots (P1–P9 at Dürnstein are app-only and pay-by-the-hour) and read the machine before you wander off. Buy Melk Abbey tickets at the abbey, not a third-party "priority" site. Treat roadside "hausgemacht" jam and "echter Wachauer" Schnaps as souvenirs, not guarantees. And at any ATM, choose euros.
