Innsbruck is the Tyrolean capital, wedged spectacularly between the Nordkette range and the Inn river — a compact, walkable old town crowned by the Golden Roof and ringed by ski slopes. It is a safe alpine city, and its scams are a small, mostly low-grade set aimed at tourists moving through the Altstadt and the station: an off-meter airport taxi, a currency-conversion ATM, a few reseller and middleman markups, and a couple of contact-based plays in the old town and the nightlife strip.

The risk concentrates on Maria-Theresien-Strasse and around the Goldenes Dachl, in the Altstadt bars near the Innstrasse, at the Hauptbahnhof and Kranebitten airport, and at the old-town tour desks. Defenses: at the airport rank, insist on the meter rather than accepting a flat "airport price" of 30 to 35 euros. Do not hand your camera to a stranger who offers to take your photo — the "broken camera" blame is a setup. Be wary of new "friends" in a bar who want to move you to a "much better place," which tends to end in a padded bill. Buy your Nordkette lift ticket and any ski pass at the official counter, not from a "discount" middleman near the station. Reach Swarovski Crystal Worlds in Wattens on the cheap public shuttle rather than a 38-to-41-euro tour-desk package. And at any ATM, decline the conversion and choose euros.
