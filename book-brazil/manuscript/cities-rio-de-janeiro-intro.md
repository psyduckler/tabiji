Rio is the flagship — the Cidade Maravilhosa, Brazil's postcard city, and the country's densest tourist-scam ecosystem. For the traveler doing a standard Rio week, the scam surface area is narrow and predictable: the two airports (Galeão GIG for international arrivals 20 km north, and Santos Dumont SDU for domestic short-hops downtown); the beach corridor from Leblon through Ipanema to Copacabana; the nightlife triangle of Lapa → Santa Teresa → Botafogo; the icon day-trips to Cristo Redentor on Corcovado and the Sugarloaf cable car; the favela-tour operators in Rocinha, Vidigal, and Santa Marta; and the two mega-event windows of Carnaval (late February / early March) and Réveillon (New Year's Eve on Copacabana).

Rio's scam economy follows a characteristic rhythm. Airport arrivals see the "Special Taxi" kiosk network target jet-lagged tourists in the first ten minutes. The beach shifts move through the day — morning pickpocket teams work Ipanema Arpoador; afternoon *arrastão* (sweep-theft) waves hit Copacabana; sunset caipirinha-bar "my friend, best place in Rio" touts work the beachfront kiosks. The nightlife shift takes over after dark in Lapa, and the R\$ 10,000 bar-bill trap is the single most expensive thing that can happen to a Rio visitor who follows a stranger into an unmarked door. The monument scams run on the fringes of the official Cristo and Sugarloaf queues. And the seasonal Carnaval and Réveillon booking-fraud cycles kick in six to nine months before each event.

The official Rio figures: about 6 million visitors per year; DEATUR (the state tourist police) logs roughly 3,000 foreign-visitor incident reports annually, a minority of the real volume. The defenses in this chapter are the ones DEATUR officers and the Rio tourism board publish on their own channels — plus the specific scripts that do not appear on official pages but recur across r/Rio, r/RiodeJaneiro, and r/Brazil threads filed by real visitors in 2025 and 2026.
