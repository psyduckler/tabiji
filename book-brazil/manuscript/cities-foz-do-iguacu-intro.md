Foz do Iguaçu — the Brazilian tripoint city between Paraná state, Argentina's Misiones province, and Paraguay's Alto Paraná department — is a single-purpose tourism destination: the Iguaçu Falls, which straddle the Brazil–Argentina border and which the World Wonders vote placed on every serious traveler's bucket list. Most itineraries here run two to three nights, with the goal of seeing both the Brazilian side (panoramic "postcard" views) and the Argentine side (immersive up-close trails, the Garganta del Diablo viewpoint, 80% of the trail length), plus optional visits to the Itaipu Dam and — for duty-free shoppers — Ciudad del Este, Paraguay.

The scam surface area in Foz is bounded but sharp, shaped by the tripoint border logistics that every visitor must navigate.

First, the airport: Cataratas (IGU) sits 13 km SE of Centro and 10 km from the Parque Nacional do Iguaçu entrance, and the "Special Taxi" / "Taxi Cataratas" kiosks quote R\$ 150–R\$ 300 to Centro when Uber is R\$ 45–R\$ 75. Late-night arrivals (Foz has many international Paraguay and Argentina connections landing after midnight) see Uber wait times spike to 20–40 minutes. Second, the Parque Nacional do Iguaçu itself: the legitimate entry is R\$ 110 foreigners / R\$ 87 Brazilians, bookable online at the official ICMBio concessionaire site *cataratasdoiguacu.com.br*. The "skip-the-line VIP R\$ 550" offers from hotel concierges and airport tour-desks are pure commission-markup — the only legitimate skip-the-line is buying the R\$ 110 ticket ONLINE one day ahead, which saves the 30-minute counter queue.

Third, **Ciudad del Este day-trips** are the sharpest financial-risk component. The duty-free shopping strip across the Ponte da Amizade in Paraguay is legendary, but 70%+ of "brand-name" goods on Av. Monseñor Rodríguez are counterfeit. The Ponte da Amizade pedestrian crossing has documented pickpocket + motoboy snatch-and-grab; Uber crossings are R\$ 50–R\$ 80 one-way (most Brazilian drivers do this route). If you go: day-trip 9 AM–3 PM only, be back on the Brazilian side by 4 PM, shop only at official malls (Shopping del Este, Shopping China, Shopping Mariscal López), stay under the USD 500 Brazilian personal-import limit, and — critically — reject the "mandatory Paraguay tourist tax R\$ 50 at border" which does not exist for day-trippers.

Fourth, Itaipu Binacional — the world's second-largest hydroelectric dam — runs two legitimate tour products directly at *turismoitaipu.com.br*: Panorâmica (R\$ 108 foreigners, 2 hours, accessible) and Especial (R\$ 246, internal turbines, physical). Airport tour-desks and hotel concierges sell both at 2–3× markup with fake "exclusive access" branding. Fifth, the Foz restaurant scene is dominated by churrascaria rodízio (all-you-can-eat Brazilian barbecue): reputable chains Rafain Churrascaria, Cabeça de Boi, Bufalo Branco, Chama Gaúcha at R\$ 140–R\$ 180 "preço adulto" versus tourist-zone churrascarias at R\$ 250–R\$ 320 for the same menu. And sixth, the Argentine-side crossing to Puerto Iguazú has its own set of border-transfer scams, including the occasional "mandatory Argentine reciprocity fee R\$ 350 per US citizen" demand (this fee was abolished in 2016 for US / EU / Canadian citizens).

Foz violent-crime incidence is very low for visitors in the Centro / hotel-zone / Cataratas corridor; the border areas (Ponte da Amizade, Av. San Blas in Ciudad del Este) have the tripoint region's elevated risk profile. DEATUR Foz is at **+55 45 3523 3322**.
