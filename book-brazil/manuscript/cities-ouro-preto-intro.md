Ouro Preto — "Black Gold," Brazil's first UNESCO World Heritage site inscribed in 1980 — is a compact baroque colonial town in the mountains of Minas Gerais, 100 km SE of Belo Horizonte. Founded in 1698 at the height of the Brazilian gold rush, Ouro Preto is the pre-eminent architectural ensemble of 18th-century Brazilian baroque: 23 churches (including Aleijadinho's masterpieces at São Francisco de Assis and Nossa Senhora do Pilar), the Museu da Inconfidência on Praça Tiradentes, the Mina da Passagem historic gold mine, and an eight-by-six walking-block Centro Histórico of cobblestone streets at gradients of 15–25%.

For international visitors, Ouro Preto is typically a three-day stop within a broader Minas Gerais circuit that includes Mariana (15 km east, Brazil's first vila), Congonhas (100 km southwest, Aleijadinho's 12 Prophets at Basílica do Bom Jesus), and Tiradentes (160 km west, a twin colonial town with the São João del-Rei heritage steam-train connection). Ouro Preto has no commercial airport — visitors fly to Belo Horizonte Confins (CNF, 130 km NW) and transfer.

The scam surface area is characteristic of a UNESCO colonial-town tourism economy with one extraordinary peak-demand window: Ouro Preto Carnaval, organized around the UFOP (Universidade Federal de Ouro Preto) *repúblicas estudantis* (student-houses with names and histories going back to the 18th century), drawing 80,000+ attendees to a town of 75,000 residents across five nights in February or March.

First, the Confins (CNF) airport-to-Ouro Preto transfer: reputable direct shuttles (Ouropreto Turismo, Ferrari Transportes) run R\$ 180–R\$ 280 per person for the 2-hour BR-040 drive; airport touts quote R\$ 900+ private. Second, the Centro Histórico runs a "mandatory guide R\$ 200" pressure scam on Praça Tiradentes (legitimate Cadastur-badged guides book through the Centro de Informações Turísticas at Praça Tiradentes 41 for R\$ 150–R\$ 250 per 3–4 hour circuit) plus "fake skip-the-line church tickets" at R\$ 60 each (the real churches are R\$ 20 each at the door; there is no skip-the-line queue) plus a fake "IPHAN preservation tax R\$ 80" demand at Matriz Pilar entrance (IPHAN does not collect tourist fees — it is a scam).

Third, Ouro Preto's signature souvenirs are carved pedra-sabão (soapstone) and mined Minas Gerais gemstones (emerald, topaz, aquamarine), and both have documented 2025 tourist-shop overcharges. Legitimate soapstone at the Feira de Artesanato Largo do Rosário runs R\$ 15–R\$ 60 for figurines that Centro Histórico tourist shops price at R\$ 80–R\$ 250. Gemstones require IBGM, GIA, or IGI laboratory certificates — "shop-issued certificates" are counterfeit paperwork. Fourth, Ouro Preto Carnaval runs an aggressive *repúblicas* + pousada cancel-and-relist booking-fraud cycle and a Galo da Madrugada counterfeit-wristband economy.

Fifth, Ouro Preto dining runs the standard couvert + serviço padding with a specific "*comida mineira*" buffet weigh trap (legitimate Chafariz, Bené da Flauta, O Passo Pizza Jazz at R\$ 70–R\$ 100 per kg; tourist-zone buffets at R\$ 110–R\$ 160 per kg). And sixth, the Minas Gerais colonial-circuit (Mariana + Congonhas + Tiradentes) day-trip market runs a commission-stop pattern with "all-three-in-one-day R\$ 800" offers that are geographically impossible (Congonhas and Tiradentes are in opposite directions from Ouro Preto; Tiradentes alone requires an overnight).

Ouro Preto has effectively zero violent-crime risk for visitors. The Centro Histórico is cobblestone + steep hills (gradient 15–25% on some streets); the Trenzinho de Ouro Preto tourist mini-tram at R\$ 45 per person (30-min hop-on circuit) is genuinely useful for mobility.
