# Appendix A: Brazilian Portuguese Exit-Phrase Card {-}

These are the phrases most likely to end a scam in Brazil. They are arranged by situation. For each phrase you will see the **English translation**, the **Brazilian Portuguese** (with accent marks), and an approximate pronunciation guide in italics. Brazilian Portuguese is softer and more musical than European Portuguese; the guides below reflect the Brazilian register specifically.

A practical suggestion: before your trip, take a screenshot of this appendix on your phone, and bookmark any phrase you think you are most likely to need. Say the first three or four out loud a few times. You will feel much less awkward using them when the moment comes.

## A note on pronunciation

Brazilian Portuguese has a few sounds that English speakers find tricky, but defensive phrases do not require perfect pronunciation. The key words carry the meaning from context alone. If you say "*não, obrigado*" with any approximation, any competent vendor will understand "no, thank you."

A few pointers that will get you 80 percent of the way:

- *ã* and *ão* are nasalized vowels — say "ow" through your nose for *ão* (as in *não*, pronounced *nown* with nasal *n*).
- *nh* is a palatal *ny* sound, as in canyon — *senhor* is *sen-yor*.
- *lh* is like *ly* — *milho* is *mee-lyo*.
- The letter *r* at the start of a word or doubled (*rr*) is pronounced like English "h" — *Rio* is *HEE-oh*, not *REE-oh*; *carro* is *KAH-hoo*.
- *s* at the end of a syllable is usually "sh" in Rio and "s" in São Paulo — both work.
- *t* and *d* before an *i* or final *e* become "ch" and "j" in Rio — *tia* is *CHEE-ah*; *vinte* is *VEEN-chee*. In São Paulo they are the English *t* and *d*.

## A note on politeness

Brazilian Portuguese uses **obrigado** (if you are male) or **obrigada** (if you are female) for "thank you." Use the form that matches your gender regardless of whom you are speaking to. Adding *por favor* ("please") or *com licença* ("excuse me") to the start of any request softens it considerably.

Throughout this appendix we use *você* for "you" — the universal informal form in Brazil, appropriate for any adult.

## Asking before you commit

**How much is it?**  
Quanto custa?  
*KWAN-too KOOS-tah?*

**Please show me the menu first.**  
Pode me mostrar o cardápio primeiro?  
*POH-jee mee mos-TRAR oo kar-DAH-pyo pree-MAY-roh?*

**Does this have a couvert?**  
Tem couvert?  
*Tain koo-VEHR?*

**What is the couvert price per person?**  
Qual é o valor do couvert por pessoa?  
*Kwahl eh oo vah-LOR doo koo-VEHR por peh-SOH-ah?*

**The service charge is optional, correct?**  
O serviço é opcional, certo?  
*Oo ser-VEE-soo eh op-syon-OW, SEHR-too?*

**Law 13.419 of 2017 makes the serviço optional. I'd like to refuse it.**  
A Lei 13.419 de 2017 torna o serviço opcional. Eu gostaria de recusar.  
*Ah lay TREH-zee KWAH-troo dezh-NOH-vee jee doysh meel dezh-seh-CHEH TOR-nah oo ser-VEE-soo op-syon-OW. Eh-oo gos-tah-REE-ah jee heh-koo-ZAR.*

**What is the price per kilogram?**  
Qual é o preço por quilo?  
*Kwahl eh oo PREH-soo por KEE-loo?*

**I'd like it weighed before cooking, please.**  
Pode pesar antes de cozinhar, por favor?  
*POH-jee peh-ZAR AHN-chees jee koh-zee-NYAR, por fah-VOR?*

## Declining — polite but firm

**No, thank you.**  
Não, obrigado / obrigada.  
*Now, oh-bree-GAH-doo / oh-bree-GAH-dah.*

**I'm not interested, thank you.**  
Não tenho interesse, obrigado.  
*Now TEN-yoo in-teh-RES-see, oh-bree-GAH-doo.*

**I already have a guide.**  
Eu já tenho um guia.  
*Eh-oo jah TEN-yoo oom GHEE-ah.*

**Please don't touch me.**  
Por favor, não me toque.  
*Por fah-VOR, now mee TOH-kee.*

**Please take this ribbon off my wrist.**  
Por favor, tire esta fita do meu pulso.  
*Por fah-VOR, CHEE-hee ES-tah FEE-tah doo meh-oo POOL-soo.*

## Rideshare and transport

**I've already ordered an Uber / 99.**  
Eu já pedi um Uber / 99.  
*Eh-oo jah peh-JEE oom OO-bair / no-VEN-tah ee NOH-vee.*

**Where is the Aplicativos pickup zone?**  
Onde fica a zona de aplicativos?  
*OHN-jee FEE-kah ah ZOH-nah jee ah-plee-kah-CHEE-voos?*

**I won't pay more than the in-app price.**  
Não vou pagar mais do que o valor do aplicativo.  
*Now voh pah-GAR mah-eesh doo kee oo vah-LOR doo ah-plee-kah-CHEE-voo.*

**Please follow the route on my map.**  
Por favor, siga a rota no meu mapa.  
*Por fah-VOR, SEE-gah ah HOH-tah noo meh-oo MAH-pah.*

**Stop here please — I want to get out.**  
Pare aqui, por favor — eu quero descer.  
*PAH-ree ah-KEE, por fah-VOR — eh-oo KEH-roo des-SEHR.*

## Payment refusals

**I will pay by credit card only — not PIX.**  
Vou pagar apenas com cartão de crédito — não com PIX.  
*Voh pah-GAR ah-PEH-nahs kohn kar-TOWN jee KREH-jee-too — now kohn peesh.*

**I don't have a CPF.**  
Não tenho CPF.  
*Now TEN-yoo seh-peh-EH-fee.*

**Please use the chip — not manual entry.**  
Por favor, use o chip — não digite o número.  
*Por fah-VOR, OO-zee oo sheep — now jee-GHEE-chee oo NOO-meh-roo.*

**I will not sign this receipt.**  
Eu não vou assinar este recibo.  
*Eh-oo now voh ah-see-NAR EHS-chee heh-SEE-boo.*

## Summoning help

**I will call the police — 190.**  
Eu vou chamar a Polícia Militar — cento-e-noventa.  
*Eh-oo voh shah-MAR ah poh-LEE-sya mee-lee-TAR — SEN-too ee noh-VEN-tah.*

**I will call SAMU — 192.**  
Eu vou chamar o SAMU — cento-noventa-e-dois.  
*Eh-oo voh shah-MAR oo SAH-moo — SEN-too noh-VEN-tah ee doysh.*

**Where is the DEATUR office?**  
Onde fica a DEATUR?  
*OHN-jee FEE-kah ah day-ah-TOOR?*

**I need to file a police report.**  
Preciso registrar um Boletim de Ocorrência.  
*Preh-SEE-zoo heh-jees-TRAR oom boh-leh-CHEEM jee oh-koh-HEN-sya.*

**I'd like to speak to the manager.**  
Eu gostaria de falar com o gerente.  
*Eh-oo gos-tah-REE-ah jee fah-LAR kohn oo jeh-REN-chee.*

## The universal fallback

If you forget every other phrase, remember these two. Said calmly, they end most encounters:

**"Não, obrigado"** (*Now, oh-bree-GAH-doo*) — "No, thank you." Use this for every street approach. Repeat and keep walking.

**"Chamo a polícia"** (*SHAH-moo ah poh-LEE-sya*) — "I'm calling the police." Use this when "no, thank you" does not work. Pull out your phone. Dial **190** or **192**. Say "*English, por favor*." The dispatcher will locate an English speaker for you.
