Salvador — Salvador da Bahia, Brazil's first colonial capital from 1549 to 1763 — is the cultural heart of Afro-Brazilian Bahia: home of capoeira, acarajé, axé music, Candomblé religion, and the UNESCO-inscribed Pelourinho historic district. For international visitors, Salvador is the essential Northeast anchor, often paired with Morro de São Paulo (a car-free island 60 km south via fast-boat) or Praia do Forte up the coast. Most visitors spend three to five nights here.

The scam surface area is characteristic of a dense historic-district tourism economy. First, the airport: Deputado Luís Eduardo Magalhães (SSA) sits 28 km from Pelourinho, and the "Special Taxi" kiosks at Terminals 1 and 2 quote R\$ 200–R\$ 400 to Barra / Pelourinho / Rio Vermelho / Itapuã when Uber is R\$ 60–R\$ 130. Late-night Uber supply thins materially after 22:00 — documented in r/Brazil "Finding ubers/99s at night in Salvador, Bahia" (2025) — which unlicensed drivers exploit. Second, Pelourinho runs the signature **fita do Senhor do Bonfim ribbon-tying forced-tip scam**: a local in traditional Bahiana white dress offers to tie a colorful blessing ribbon around your wrist "for three wishes — a gift from Bahia" and then demands R\$ 50–R\$ 200 after the knots are tied. The real fita — sold at the official Igreja do Senhor do Bonfim gift shop — costs R\$ 2. Third, Pelourinho and the Centro Histórico (Largo do Pelourinho, Terreiro de Jesus, Cruzeiro de São Francisco) are tourist-police-patrolled 9 AM–5 PM but phone-snatch + pickpocket risk rises sharply after sunset.

Fourth, the Morro de São Paulo day-trip is Bahia's flagship beach excursion, accessible by catamarã (R\$ 130–R\$ 220 round trip via Biotur, Ilha Bela, or Cassi Tour from the Mercado Modelo / Comércio pier), with tourist-strip quotes running as high as R\$ 800 per person "private speedboat." Fifth, the Pelourinho + Rio Vermelho restaurant scene runs the standard Brazilian couvert artístico + serviço padding with a specific Pelourinho-strip twist: the "cortesia capoeira roda" (free capoeira demonstration) which ends with a R\$ 50 donation demand to a hat. Sixth, Carnaval — the world's largest street party, 2.5 million daily participants, late February or early March — drives accommodation and ticket prices 5–10× normal and a counterfeit-ticket ecosystem for camarote (grandstand) and trio elétrico (parade-truck) tickets.

Salvador's violent-crime incidence is higher than the Southeast cities (Rio, São Paulo, Belo Horizonte), but for visitors who stay in the Barra / Pelourinho / Rio Vermelho / Itapuã corridor and use Uber after dark, street safety during tourist hours is manageable. DEATUR Bahia at Praça Anchieta (Pelourinho) is staffed 24/7 with English-speaking officers.
