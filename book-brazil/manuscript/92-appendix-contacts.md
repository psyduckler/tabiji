# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication, but phone numbers do change, consulates move, and hospitals occasionally renumber their switchboards. If a number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus *Brasil* and you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results.

**Before you travel**, we strongly recommend you double-check your own embassy or consulate's current number directly on its official website — the US State Department's *travel.state.gov*, the UK's *gov.uk*, Canada's *travel.gc.ca*, Ireland's *dfa.ie*, Australia's *smartraveller.gov.au*. Save the main switchboard and the after-hours duty-officer line on your phone before departure, and screenshot this appendix so you can read it on a Rio Uber or an Amazon boat ride where your signal cuts out. Even a verified number can become a wrong number between editions.

## The numbers that work everywhere in Brazil

These are the lines to dial from any phone — mobile or landline, Brazilian SIM or roaming — anywhere in the country. All of them are free.

- **Polícia Militar (emergency):** **190**. National police emergency line. The first operator may speak Portuguese only; say *"English, please"* slowly and the dispatcher will locate an English speaker, particularly in Rio, São Paulo, Salvador, and other major tourist cities. Use this for theft, assault, harassment, and any incident that is unfolding in real time.
- **SAMU — Ambulance and medical emergency:** **192**. National ambulance dispatch, free. English is available at the major dispatch centers in Rio, São Paulo, Brasília, Salvador, Recife, Manaus, and Florianópolis; elsewhere the first operator may be Portuguese-only and will locate an English speaker. For any serious injury, calling the nearest large private hospital directly (numbers below) is often faster than 192 in remote areas.
- **Corpo de Bombeiros (fire and rescue):** **193**. National fire-and-rescue dispatch.
- **PROCON — Consumer protection:** **151**. Routes you to your state's PROCON based on your current location. Complaints against restaurants, hotels, tour operators, rental agencies, retail shops, and general consumer fraud. Portuguese-language first line; English via callback in most cases. State portals: São Paulo *procon.sp.gov.br*; Rio *procon.rj.gov.br*; Bahia *procon.ba.gov.br*; Pernambuco *procon.pe.gov.br*; Ceará *procon.ce.gov.br*; Amazonas *procon.am.gov.br*; Paraná *procon.pr.gov.br*; Distrito Federal *procon.df.gov.br*; Santa Catarina *procon.sc.gov.br*; Minas Gerais *procon.mg.gov.br*.
- **Disque 100 — Human-rights hotline:** **100**. For trafficking, sexual exploitation, and human-rights violations (including the "Mega Model" beach-recruit pattern documented in Florianópolis and other coastal cities). Free, 24/7, English available on request.
- **Disque Denúncia — Anonymous crime tip line:** **181**. For reporting crimes anonymously when you prefer not to file a BO in your own name. Portuguese-first, callback available in English.
- **Polícia Federal — for border, immigration, and federal crimes:** **194**. At Ciudad del Este / Ponte da Amizade border (Foz do Iguaçu) the direct line is **+55 45 3576 5200**.
- **Banco Central do Brasil Ouvidoria:** **0800 646 2492**. For PIX disputes, MED escalations, and any bank-related complaint that the bank's own fraud desk has not resolved. Portuguese first, English on callback.
- **CADASTUR — National tourism operator registry:** *cadastur.turismo.gov.br*. For verifying any Brazilian tour guide or operator's license before booking, and for filing complaints against registered operators.

## Card issuer emergency lines (callable from Brazil)

When you call, have your card number (or the last four digits), the approximate transaction amount, and the time of the fraudulent charge ready.

- **Visa Global Customer Assistance (collect from Brazil):** +1 303 967 1096. 24/7, English.
- **Mastercard Global Service (collect from Brazil):** +1 636 722 7111. 24/7.
- **American Express International Service Center (collect):** +1 336 393 1111. 24/7.

If your card was issued by a bank (not directly by Visa, Mastercard, or Amex), the fastest route is the number printed on the back of the card. That line routes you to your own issuer's fraud desk and they can freeze the card in under a minute. Save this number in your phone under a searchable contact name before you travel — hunting for a number on a stolen card is impossible.

## The Banco Central PIX emergency quick-card

If you have just been defrauded via PIX, the recovery mechanism is **MED** (*Mecanismo Especial de Devolução*). To open:

1. Call your bank's fraud line (see card-issuer section above).
2. Say: *"Preciso abrir um MED por fraude no PIX"* (I need to open an MED for PIX fraud).
3. Have ready: the date and time of the transaction, the amount, the receiving account's Chave PIX (CPF, email, phone, or random key), and any screenshots showing the scam context (WhatsApp messages, Facebook post, fake booking page).
4. Also file a *Boletim de Ocorrência* at a Polícia Civil *Delegacia de Crimes Cibernéticos* (Cybercrime Unit) or online at *delegaciaeletronica.gov.br*. Include the transaction reference number (*ID da transação*) and any screenshots.
5. Open a parallel complaint with the Banco Central Ouvidoria at **0800 646 2492** or *bcb.gov.br*.

MED cases typically resolve in 7–10 business days if approved. The Banco Central publishes MED statistics quarterly — approval rates for legitimate fraud cases are above 60%, and the Banco Central's willingness to intervene has increased materially since 2024.

**Important:** MED has an 80-day filing window from the date of the transaction. Beyond 80 days, the only recovery mechanism is a civil claim (*Ação de Cobrança*) in Brazilian small-claims court (*Juizado Especial Cível*), which is rarely practical for foreign travelers.

**Prevention is the strategy.** Before PIX'ing anyone in Brazil who you do not know personally, run the receiving CPF or CNPJ through *gov.br* and *serasa.com.br* — the Serasa and Serpro consumer-credit databases publish risk scores and fraud flags that catch the majority of known-scam accounts.
