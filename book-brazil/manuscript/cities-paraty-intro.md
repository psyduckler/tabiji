Paraty — the colonial Portuguese port UNESCO-inscribed in 2019, 250 km southwest of Rio on the Costa Verde — is the antithesis of Búzios. Where Búzios is pastel-painted beach-resort, Paraty is whitewashed 18th-century cobblestone, imperial-era sugar-and-gold-shipment architecture, a car-free Centro Histórico of eight-by-six walking blocks, and a bay of some sixty islands. Most visitors spend one to three nights here, often as part of a Rio–São Paulo circuit on the BR-101 Costa Verde highway.

The scam surface area in Paraty is characteristic of colonial-town tourism. First, getting here: the Rio / São Paulo-to-Paraty bus + private-transfer market mirrors Búzios — licensed Costa Verde Transportes bus R\$ 95–R\$ 140 from Novo Rio rodoviária, 4 hours; tourist-desk quotes at R\$ 600–R\$ 900 private. The BR-101 road has 250+ curves between Angra and Paraty, which is why we recommend Dramamine 30 minutes before departure. Second, the Paraty Bay schooner boat tour is the dominant day-trip product — 5–6 hours, Lagoa Azul, Praia Vermelha, Ilha Comprida, Praia da Lula, snorkeling + buffet. Reputable operators at R\$ 70–R\$ 120 per person; unlicensed operators at R\$ 500+ private. Third, the Centro Histórico restaurant strip sells the standard Brazilian couvert artístico + serviço padding alongside seafood "*preço de mercado*" traps, with the Paraty-specific wrinkle of live-music couvert at R\$ 180 per person for a single guitarist.

Fourth, Paraty is Brazil's historical center of artisanal cachaça production with multiple working alambiques (distilleries) going back to the Portuguese colonial era — and the "premium cachaça tasting" commission-scam runs on this heritage, with tourist-facing bodegas pricing 500ml bottles of "master cachaça" at R\$ 1,500+ that the distillery itself sells for R\$ 60–R\$ 180. Fifth, the Paraty day-trips to Trindade (a beach village 35 km south) and Angra dos Reis / Ilha Grande are where commission-stop tour operators rotate through coconut-candy shops, artisanal markets, and cachaça bodegas on the way. And sixth, the pousada cancel-and-relist fraud peaks around Carnaval and FLIP (*Festa Literária Internacional de Paraty*), Brazil's premier literary festival, held in late July and drawing 30,000+ attendees to a town of 45,000 residents.

Paraty has effectively zero violent-crime risk for visitors. The scams here are all financial and all predictable — but they add up fast if you don't know the patterns.
