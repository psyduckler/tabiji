Armação dos Búzios — Rio state's premier weekend beach peninsula, 175 km east of the capital along the Região dos Lagos — sits on a strip of twelve named beaches and a single cobblestoned main street (Rua das Pedras). For most international visitors, Búzios is a two-to-three-night extension of a Rio week, or a standalone long-weekend from São Paulo. It is small enough (25,000 permanent residents swelling to 150,000 in Carnaval and Réveillon) that every vendor knows the arrival rhythm of every hotel and pousada, which is both its charm and the origin of its characteristic scams.

The scam surface area in Búzios is narrower than Rio and sharper. First, the ride to get here: the Rio-to-Búzios private-transfer market is dominated by a few licensed operators and a larger population of freelance drivers who target foreign arrivals at Galeão (GIG) with R\$ 1,500+ quotes for a trip the 1001 Auto Viação bus does for R\$ 95 one-way in comfortable air-conditioned coaches. Second, the flagship beach-hopping schooner tour — 5 hours, 8–12 beach stops, snorkeling, lunch on the boat — is the object of an overcharge + unlicensed-operator scam that runs at peak season. Third, the Rua das Pedras dining strip sells a specific Búzios version of the restaurant couvert + serviço + seafood "*per quilo*" triple trap. Fourth, the upscale beach clubs at João Fernandes, Geribá, and Ferradura sell "VIP couples beds" at R\$ 1,500+ on beaches that are 100% free public federal property. Fifth, the pousada + Airbnb peak-date market for Carnaval and NYE is a reliable cancel-and-relist fraud cycle. And sixth, the combined Búzios + Arraial do Cabo + Cabo Frio day-trip tours run a commission-stop pattern at cachaça bodegas and artisanal shops.

Búzios is safer than Rio in violent-crime terms — a small upscale resort town with low street-crime incidence — but the financial-scam density is if anything higher, because everything is tourism-dependent and the peak-season spike is extreme.
