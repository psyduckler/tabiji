Our story

The problem with travel planning.

You know the drill. You decide to go to Tokyo. You Google "Tokyo itinerary." You get 50 blog posts that all recommend the same things: Shibuya Crossing, Tsukiji Market, a day trip to Hakone. Generic. Recycled.

Written for search engines, not for you.

So you go to Reddit. You spend six hours reading r/JapanTravel threads. You find incredible tips — the ramen shop under the train tracks in Shinjuku, the free observation deck most tourists miss, the neighborhood where locals actually eat. That's the good stuff.

But now you have 40 browser tabs, a scattered Google Doc, and no idea how to turn it into a day-by-day plan that makes geographic sense.

That's the gap tabiji fills.

The method

How it actually works.

We don't ask an AI to "plan a Tokyo trip." That gives you the same generic output everyone else gets. Our process looks like this:

  1. Step 01

    Mine real traveler intel

    We scour thousands of Reddit posts, travel forums, and local recommendation threads for your specific destination. Not blog spam — actual travelers sharing what worked and what didn't.

  2. Step 02

    Filter signal from noise

    Not every Reddit comment is gold. We cross-reference recommendations, check recency, and prioritize tips that multiple travelers independently confirm. The hole-in-the-wall ramen spot that keeps getting mentioned? That makes the cut.

  3. Step 03

    Build a logistics-optimized plan

    Great recommendations are useless if you're zigzagging across the city. We cluster activities by neighborhood, optimize for realistic travel times, and build a day-by-day flow that doesn't have you on the subway for three hours between meals.

  4. Step 04

    Personalize to your trip

    Your dates, group size, travel style, budget, and special requests all shape the final itinerary. A solo foodie backpacker gets a very different plan than a family of four on a luxury trip — even for the same city.

The result: a detailed, day-by-day itinerary with specific restaurant names, addresses, timing, transit directions, and insider tips — all for free.

The team

Who runs this.

Editor

Bernard Huang

Bernard founded Clearscope in 2016 — a content-research platform used by editorial teams at the New York Times, HubSpot, Shopify, and Adobe (acquired by Mutiny in 2023). He started tabiji.ai to apply the same research-first approach to a problem that had been bugging him for a decade: tourist-scam information lives scattered across police PDFs, foreign-language press, and decade-old Reddit threads. tabiji is the index that didn't exist.

He edits the safety series, runs the research pipeline, and signs every byline you see on a scam page.

Editorial standards

Rebecca Leung

Rebecca sets the editorial bar for what a real travel recommendation looks like — would a careful traveler actually use this? Would she send her parents here? Her judgment is the reason a tabiji entry reads like a sharp friend wrote it, not an algorithm. She reviews the editorial standards every entry has to clear before it ships.

Tools & automation. The research pipeline is heavily automated. We use large-language-model tools to surface candidate scams across thousands of Reddit threads, foreign-language press, and police records — and to draft initial entries. Every published entry is reviewed by an editor before it ships. AI-assisted pages are labeled as such; we don't pass machine-written content off as a human-reported article.

Production runs from a small server farm in Austin, Texas.

What we believe

And what we don't.

✓
Real data over AI guessing

We research what travelers actually recommend right now — not what a model memorized in 2023.

✓
Free, always

Human travel agents cost $200–500. We charge nothing. Great trips shouldn't sit behind a paywall.

✓
Show, don't gatekeep

Every destination guide, every tip, every itinerary — all free. No upsell, no premium tier.

And the opposite — what we won't do.

✕
No affiliate kickbacks that shift our recommendations

If we link to a tour, it's because we think it's worth your money — not because it pays us a commission to say so.

✕
No selling your trip data

Your itinerary, your trip details, your email — none of it gets sold to advertisers.

✕
No pretending an AI is a human

Psy is an AI agent. We label AI-written content as AI-written. Bernard and Rebecca are humans. The distinction matters.

✕
No recycled blog spam

Every scam in our books is verified against police records and local press. Every Popular Picks city is cross-checked across ten-plus Reddit threads. If we can't substantiate it, it doesn't ship.
