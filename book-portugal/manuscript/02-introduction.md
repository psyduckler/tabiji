# How to Use This Book {-}

Portugal is extraordinary. It is also, by Portuguese consumer-protection agency ASAE's own admission, one of the Western European countries where scam complaints filed by foreign visitors have grown fastest in the 2024–2026 window. Lisbon's Tram 28 is the single most pickpocket-heavy tourist route in the country — the r/LisbonPortugalTravel community has documented the team-based lift pattern in thread after thread. Porto's Vila Nova de Gaia port-wine cellar district runs a commission-bundling upsell that packages €15–€25 direct tastings into €60–€150 "combined cellar + cruise + fado" tour-office offers. The Algarve's Klass Wagen rental-car depots at Faro Airport run what r/Algarve calls a "well-rehearsed scam" with duct-taped cars, rigged fuel gauges, and fabricated post-rental damage claims. And Portugal's modern accommodation-fraud pattern has a specific and dangerous shape — scammers compromise a hotel's Booking.com property-inbox, then message guests off-platform to "re-verify" their credit card, harvesting credentials that enable fraudulent charges weeks later.

We want you to come home with stories about the azulejo-tiled Lisbon neighborhoods, the Douro valley vineyards, the sea caves at Ponta da Piedade, the 80-foot waves at Praia do Norte, the levada hikes through Madeira's laurel forests, and the pastel de nata fresh from the Belém ovens — not about a €800 "damage claim" on a duct-taped rental car or a €300 Oura Strip bar tab you never ordered.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents sixty-five specific scams that trip up first-time visitors to Portugal, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been reported in the Portuguese press, cross-referenced against PSP (Polícia de Segurança Pública), GNR (Guarda Nacional Republicana), ASAE (consumer protection), Turismo de Portugal, or Decreto-Lei 24/96 consumer-protection records, and confirmed by recent traveler accounts on r/portugal, r/lisboa, r/Lisbon, r/LisbonPortugalTravel, r/Coimbra, r/Madeira, r/Algarve, r/PortugalExpats, r/TravelPortugal, and r/Bookingcom.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First, the name of the scam, the category it falls into (pickpocket, transport, tourist trap, nightlife), and how serious and how common it is. Then two short sections: **How this scam works** — a plain narrative of how it unfolds in real time, with real euro (€) amounts where we have them — and **How to avoid it**, the specific moves that shut it down. Each entry ends with where it happens and a four-panel folk-pop comic (drawn in the José de Guimarães style that anchors contemporary Portuguese modernism) showing the exchange so you can spot the pattern visually, not just in words.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. Most scams in Portugal are variations on six patterns. Learn the patterns and you will spot a new scam the moment it starts — even one that is not in this book. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will then read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters, you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **European Portuguese exit-phrase card**. Short phrases you can say, word for word, at the moments when your politeness would normally get you into trouble. The pronunciation guides are approximate, but the key words will carry the meaning even with a foreign accent. Take a screenshot of it on your phone.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A step-by-step flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam — including Portugal-specific instructions on the Multibanco / SIBS fraud-freeze procedure, Visa and Mastercard chargeback mechanics, denuncia filing at your nearest PSP or GNR station, and the English-speaking Lisbon PSP Turismo tourist-help line. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens, you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. The pan-European 112 emergency number, PSP Turismo tourist-police phone lines in every major city, ASAE consumer-protection hotlines, the Banco de Portugal fraud desk, embassy numbers for the UK, US, Canada, Australia, and Ireland, and the nearest English-speaking hospitals, organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and skim the three appendices. Read the chapters for the cities and regions you will be visiting. Take a screenshot of the phrase card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book turns out to apply during your trip, open it again in the Uber or in your hotel room and check what to do.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.

*Olá, boa viagem* — hello and safe travels.
