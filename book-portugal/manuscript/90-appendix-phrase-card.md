# Appendix A: European Portuguese Exit-Phrase Card {-}

These are the phrases most likely to end a scam in Portugal. They are arranged by situation. For each phrase you will see the **English translation**, the **European Portuguese** (with accent marks), and an approximate pronunciation guide in italics. European Portuguese is crisper and more closed-vowel than Brazilian Portuguese; the guides below reflect the Lisbon / Porto register.

A practical suggestion: before your trip, take a screenshot of this appendix on your phone, and bookmark any phrase you think you are most likely to need. Say the first three or four out loud a few times. You will feel much less awkward using them when the moment comes.

## A note on pronunciation

European Portuguese has a few sounds that English speakers find tricky, but defensive phrases do not require perfect pronunciation. The key words carry the meaning from context alone. If you say "*obrigado, não*" with any approximation, any competent vendor will understand "thank you, no."

A few pointers that will get you 80 percent of the way:

- *ã* and *ão* are nasalized vowels — say "ow" through your nose for *ão* (as in *não*, pronounced *now* with nasal *n*).
- *nh* is a palatal *ny* sound, as in canyon — *senhor* is *sen-yor*.
- *lh* is like *ly* — *milho* is *mee-lyo*.
- The letter *r* at the start of a word or doubled (*rr*) is a guttural, uvular sound closer to French *r* — *Rua* is *HOO-ah*, *carro* is *KAH-roo*.
- Final *s* is "sh" across most of Portugal — *Lisboa* sounds like *leezh-BOH-ah*.
- Unstressed *e* and *o* at word endings are very short and almost swallowed — *obrigado* sounds more like *oh-bree-GAH-doo* (with a tiny final *oo*) or even *oh-bree-GAH-d*.

## A note on politeness

European Portuguese uses **obrigado** (if you are male) or **obrigada** (if you are female) for "thank you." Use the form that matches your gender regardless of whom you are speaking to. Adding *se faz favor* or *por favor* ("please") softens any request considerably.

Portuguese uses a formal "you" (*o senhor* / *a senhora*) with strangers and staff. If you are ever uncertain, default to the formal register — it is always polite and never causes offense.

## Asking before you commit

**How much is it?**  
Quanto custa?  
*KWAN-too KOOSH-tah?*

**Please show me the menu first.**  
Pode mostrar-me a ementa primeiro, se faz favor?  
*POH-d moosh-TRAR-m ah eh-MEN-tah pree-MAY-roo, s'fahsh fah-VOR?*

**Does this have a couvert?**  
Tem couvert?  
*Taym koo-VEHR?*

**What is the couvert price per person?**  
Qual é o preço do couvert por pessoa?  
*Kwahl eh oo PREH-soo doo koo-VEHR por p'SOH-ah?*

**I didn't order it, I don't pay for it.**  
Não pedi, não pago.  
*Now p'DEE, now PAH-goo.*

**Please take the couvert back — I didn't order it.**  
Leve o couvert, por favor — eu não pedi.  
*LEH-v oo koo-VEHR, por fah-VOR — ew now p'DEE.*

**What is the price per kilogram?**  
Qual é o preço por quilo?  
*Kwahl eh oo PREH-soo por KEE-loo?*

**I'd like it weighed before cooking, please.**  
Pode pesar antes de cozinhar, por favor?  
*POH-d p'ZAR AHN-tsh d'koo-zee-NYAR, por fah-VOR?*

## Declining — polite but firm

**Thank you, no.**  
Obrigado, não. / Obrigada, não.  
*Oh-bree-GAH-doo, now. / Oh-bree-GAH-dah, now.*

**I'm not interested, thank you.**  
Não tenho interesse, obrigado.  
*Now TAYN-yoo een-t'REH-s, oh-bree-GAH-doo.*

**I already have a guide.**  
Já tenho um guia.  
*Zhah TAYN-yoo oom GEE-ah.*

**Please don't touch me.**  
Por favor, não me toque.  
*Por fah-VOR, now m TOH-k.*

## Rideshare and transport

**I've already ordered a Bolt / Uber.**  
Já pedi um Bolt / Uber.  
*Zhah p'DEE oom Bolt / OO-ber.*

**Please turn on the meter.**  
Ligue o taxímetro, por favor.  
*LEE-g oo tak-SEE-meh-troo, por fah-VOR.*

**Where is the Bolt / Uber pickup zone?**  
Onde fica a zona de Bolt / Uber?  
*ON-d FEE-kah ah ZOH-nah d'Bolt / OO-ber?*

**I won't pay more than the in-app price.**  
Não pago mais do que o preço da aplicação.  
*Now PAH-goo mahsh doo k'oo PREH-soo dah ah-plee-kah-SOWN.*

**Stop here, please — I want to get out.**  
Pare aqui, por favor — eu quero sair.  
*PAH-r ah-KEE, por fah-VOR — ew KEH-roo sah-EER.*

## Payment refusals

**I will pay by credit card only — not bank transfer.**  
Pago apenas com cartão de crédito — não com transferência.  
*PAH-goo ah-PEH-nahsh kohm kar-TOWN d'KREH-dee-too — now kohm trahnsh-feh-RAYN-sya.*

**Please use the chip — not manual entry.**  
Use o chip, por favor — não introduza o número manualmente.  
*OO-z oo sheep, por fah-VOR — now een-troo-DOO-zah oo NOO-meh-roo mah-noo-ahl-MEN-t.*

**I will not sign this receipt.**  
Não vou assinar este recibo.  
*Now voh ah-see-NAR EHSH-t reh-SEE-boo.*

**Please choose 'NO CONVERSION' / 'EUR' on the ATM screen.**  
(Button labels are in English on Portuguese ATMs — press **EUR** or **No Conversion**, never accept the home-currency conversion option.)

## Summoning help

**I will call the police — 112.**  
Vou chamar a polícia — cento-e-doze.  
*Voh shah-MAR ah poh-LEE-sya — SEN-too ee DOH-z.*

**I will call PSP Turismo — the tourist police.**  
Vou chamar a PSP Turismo — a polícia de turismo.  
*Voh shah-MAR ah PEH-SEH-PEH too-REEZH-moo — ah poh-LEE-sya d'too-REEZH-moo.*

**I need to file a denuncia.**  
Preciso de registar uma denúncia.  
*Preh-SEE-zoo d'reh-zheesh-TAR OO-mah d'NOON-sya.*

**I'd like to speak to the manager.**  
Gostaria de falar com o gerente.  
*Goosh-tah-REE-ah d'fah-LAR kohm oo zh'REN-t.*

**Show me your PSP identification — I want to go to the police station.**  
Mostre-me a sua identificação da PSP — eu quero ir à esquadra.  
*MOHSH-tr-m ah SOO-ah ee-den-tee-fee-kah-SOWN dah PEH-SEH-PEH — ew KEH-roo eer ah shkwah-drah.*

## The universal fallback

If you forget every other phrase, remember these two. Said calmly, they end most encounters:

**"Obrigado, não"** (*Oh-bree-GAH-doo, now*) — "Thank you, no." Use this for every street approach. Repeat and keep walking.

**"Chamo a polícia"** (*SHAH-moo ah poh-LEE-sya*) — "I'm calling the police." Use this when "thank you, no" does not work. Pull out your phone. Dial **112**. Say "*English, please*." The dispatcher will locate an English speaker for you.
