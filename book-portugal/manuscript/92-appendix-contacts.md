# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication, but phone numbers do change, consulates move, and hospitals occasionally renumber their switchboards. If a number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus *Portugal* and you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results.

**Before you travel**, we strongly recommend you double-check your own embassy or consulate's current number directly on its official website — the US State Department's *travel.state.gov*, the UK's *gov.uk*, Canada's *travel.gc.ca*, Ireland's *dfa.ie*, Australia's *smartraveller.gov.au*. Save the main switchboard and the after-hours duty-officer line on your phone before departure, and screenshot this appendix so you can read it on a Lisbon Bolt or a Madeira bus where your signal cuts out. Even a verified number can become a wrong number between editions.

## The numbers that work everywhere in Portugal

These are the lines to dial from any phone — mobile or landline, Portuguese SIM or roaming — anywhere in the country. All of them are free.

- **112 — European emergency number:** **112**. Routes to police (PSP or GNR), ambulance (INEM), or fire (bombeiros) depending on your situation. Works from any EU mobile, including eSIMs and home-country SIMs on roaming. Portuguese-language first line; English is standard in all major cities and at every airport. Use this for theft, assault, harassment, medical emergencies, or any incident unfolding in real time.
- **PSP Turismo Lisboa (tourist police, English-speaking):** **+351 21 342 1634**. 24/7. ## The Portuguese "não pedi, não pago" quick-card

If you were charged for anything that arrived at your table unordered — a couvert plate, a bread basket, olives, pâté, a tapas-style portion, a glass of water, or any "complimentary" item — Portuguese consumer law is on your side. To refuse the charge:

1. At the table, calmly say: *"Não pedi, não pago"* (*Now p'DEE, now PAH-goo* — "I didn't order it, I don't pay for it").
2. Hand the item back. If it has been opened, leave it; you are not required to consume it.
3. If the manager insists on charging, request: *"Quero falar com o gerente, por favor"* (I want to speak to the manager, please). Refuse to sign any receipt with the disputed charge.
4. If still unresolved, photograph the bill, the posted menu (or the absence of one), and the business license. Pay only the items you ordered, and inform the manager that you will file an **ASAE** complaint the same day.
5. File online at **asae.gov.pt/denuncias** within 24 hours. Include photos of the bill, the menu, and the business's visible ASAE registration. ASAE's regulatory response includes fines, license review, and for repeat offenders, suspension.

The "não pedi, não pago" rule is one of the few consumer protections that works *at the table*, in the moment. It is widely known to Portuguese waitstaff and managers, and its citation almost always ends the conversation.

## The Multibanco + card-fraud quick-card

If you have just been defrauded at an ATM or had your card skimmed:

1. Call your card-issuing bank's fraud line (number on the back of the card). Ask to freeze the card immediately.
2. For Portuguese Multibanco cards, also call **SIBS** at **+351 21 791 8200** to flag the transaction on the network.
3. File a denuncia at the nearest PSP or GNR station (or online at **psp.pt/Pages/reportarcrime.aspx**) within 24 hours. The denuncia reference number is required for your credit-card chargeback claim.
4. Open the Visa or Mastercard chargeback dispute with your card issuer within 48 hours. Include: the denuncia reference, screenshots of the transaction, a timeline, and a brief statement of what happened.
5. For SEPA bank transfers (where you wired a deposit to a fake IBAN), contact your bank's fraud department and request a **SEPA recall** within 10 business days.

**Prevention is the strategy.** Use Multibanco ATMs inside bank branches only. Avoid standalone Euronet / Travelex machines entirely. On any ATM that offers "Dynamic Currency Conversion," always choose **NO CONVERSION** or **EUR** — the DCC option adds 8–15% to every withdrawal.
