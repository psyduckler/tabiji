# Appendix B: Post-Scam Recovery Playbook {-}

If something in this book has happened to you, this appendix is where you start. The actions below are organized by urgency, not by severity. Even if you are not sure yet how serious the scam was, working through the first two timelines will put you in the best possible position to recover your money, your evidence, and your peace of mind.

## In the first fifteen minutes

Leave the venue. If you are physically inside a place where you were scammed — a restaurant with a suddenly inflated bill, a rental-car depot demanding a "damage" deposit, a "fixed-price" taxi that has driven you somewhere unexpected, an Oura Strip bar escalating with a €400 tab for a single round of drinks — do not argue, do not try to negotiate, and do not pay with a second form of payment. The single most important thing is to get yourself out of the environment and somewhere quiet.

Do not sign anything, including receipts. A signed credit-card slip is much harder to dispute than one you refused to sign. If staff are blocking your exit, dial **112** — the pan-European emergency number that routes to PSP or GNR depending on where you are. For tourist-specific help, the PSP Turismo Lisbon line is **+351 21 342 1634**, 24/7, English-speaking.

Screenshot everything you can, as discreetly as possible: the bill, the sign outside the venue, the street intersection (Google Maps dropped-pin is ideal), the business license (in Portugal, the *Alvará Sanitário* or ASAE registration is usually displayed near the entrance), the card terminal, and the face of the person who served you if you can do it without escalating. The information you capture in these fifteen minutes is the evidence you will use for weeks afterward. If a pickpocket team is still in the area, walk 100 meters in any direction before you pull out your phone to screenshot.

If your bag or wallet was just stolen on the street, shout — Portuguese shopkeepers, café staff, and pedestrians will often help, and a visible commotion is the fastest way to draw attention. In Lisbon's Alfama and Baixa, the nearest PSP station is always within ten blocks. In Porto's Ribeira and Baixa, PSP Porto maintains stations at Rua do Heroísmo and São Bento. In Faro and the Algarve, GNR operates the rural coverage and PSP the urban nodes at Faro, Albufeira, Portimão, and Lagos. Portuguese police officers routinely handle foreign-visitor reports, and English-speaking staff are standard at PSP Turismo posts.

## Within the first hour

Walk to the nearest PSP or GNR station if one is within ten minutes. In Lisbon, PSP Turismo maintains a dedicated tourist-support office at Palácio Foz (Praça dos Restauradores) with English-speaking officers. In Porto, the PSP Turismo office is at the São Bento train station level. In Faro, the PSP station is at Rua do Prior. Say *"Quero registar uma denúncia"* (*KEH-roo reh-zheesh-TAR OO-mah d'NOON-sya* — "I want to file a denuncia"). You will walk out with an official *denúncia* report stamped with a case number. This is the document your credit-card issuer and travel insurer will demand.

Portugal also runs an **online pre-filing system** for non-violent incidents: the **PSP online crime-reporting portal** at **psp.pt/Pages/reportarcrime.aspx**. Online filing works for stolen-object reports, credit-card fraud, and minor incidents. Serious incidents (assault, robbery with violence, major fraud) must be filed in person at a physical esquadra — and almost always on the same day as the incident; delayed reports raise suspicion and slow the paperwork.

Freeze the card that was used. International emergency lines for the major card issuers are listed in Appendix C. These numbers work from Portugal and route you to an English-speaking representative. Have your card number (or at least the last four digits) and the approximate transaction amount ready before you call. If the charge was a restaurant overcharge, a rental "damage deposit," or an inflated bar bill, also call your bank's fraud line directly — the number on the back of your card — because fraud desks sometimes freeze faster than the Visa or Mastercard emergency lines. If the card is Portuguese-issued, the major banks (Caixa Geral de Depósitos, Millennium BCP, Santander, Novo Banco, BPI, ActivoBank) all run 24/7 card-loss lines.

**If the fraud involved Multibanco or an off-platform bank transfer**, the mechanics are different from a credit-card dispute. See the dedicated Multibanco and bank-transfer section below.

## Within the first twenty-four hours

File a chargeback with your card issuer. Use the PSP denuncia reference number as documentation. Chargebacks initiated within twenty-four hours of the transaction have a significantly higher success rate than those filed a week later. Visa and Mastercard both offer strong chargeback rights for unauthorized or materially misrepresented charges — one reason we recommend credit over debit and over bank transfer for every Portugal trip.

Call the **ASAE** (Autoridade de Segurança Alimentar e Económica) consumer-protection hotline. ASAE is Portugal's food-safety and economic-activity regulator; it handles restaurant overcharging, unlisted couvert, misrepresented pricing, and tour-operator fraud. Its Linha Verde (toll-free consumer line) is **+351 21 798 7160**, and its online complaint portal is **asae.gov.pt/denuncias**. Filing an ASAE complaint in Portugal has genuine teeth — repeat-offender businesses face fines, licence suspensions, and in serious cases, closure orders.

If the scam involved timeshare pressure, a "holiday club" contract, or a "prize" presentation, the Portuguese consumer protection rules under **Decreto-Lei 37/2011** give you a **14-day cooling-off period** to rescind any timeshare contract without penalty. Exercise it *immediately* via registered letter (*carta registada com aviso de receção*) to the operator. Keep the posting receipt and the return receipt — those are your proof of timely withdrawal.

If your passport was photographed, held, or taken by anyone involved, notify your embassy or consulate immediately. Passport data tied to a scam can show up in fraud attempts weeks later, and your embassy will advise on whether a replacement is needed. US citizens should contact the US Embassy in Lisbon. UK citizens can reach the British Embassy in Lisbon or the Honorary Consulate in Portimão. Canadian citizens have an embassy in Lisbon and a consular office. Australian and Irish citizens have consular representation in Lisbon.

Email your travel-insurance provider with a summary of what happened, timestamps, the denuncia reference number, and photographs of the bill. Even if you are not sure whether the loss is covered, opening the claim early protects your position. The major dispatchers serving the European market — Allianz Partners, AXA Partners, World Nomads, Europ Assistance, Chubb — all operate 24/7 English-language lines for travelers and have Portuguese medical-provider networks. Portuguese private hospitals will typically bill your insurer directly once a *Guarantee of Payment* is in place; public hospitals (SNS) require you to attend with a European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) or a Portuguese ID, and may require you to pay up front and reclaim later if your case is not an emergency.

If the scam involved an off-platform rental or a hotel-booking phishing attack, open the dispute on the booking platform (Booking.com, Airbnb, Expedia, Hotels.com, VRBO) as well as with your card issuer. Platforms can sometimes recover deposits the card company cannot, and Booking.com in particular runs a dedicated fraud-investigation team for property-inbox compromise incidents — you can reach their support at **+44 20 3320 2609** or through the in-app Contact Us flow.

## If the fraud involved Multibanco ATMs or bank transfer

**Bank-transfer recovery is narrower than credit-card chargeback.** Portugal's Multibanco network is operated by **SIBS**, the national inter-bank switch. If your card was skimmed at a Multibanco ATM or a standalone Euronet / Travelex machine, or if you wired a SEPA deposit to an off-platform "landlord," the path forward is different.

Within the first 48 hours, call your card-issuing bank's fraud line and report the transaction as unauthorized. For Portuguese-issued Multibanco cards, also call **SIBS** at **+351 21 791 8200** to flag the transaction on the network. If the card is non-Portuguese, your home bank's Visa / Mastercard chargeback rights apply — file the chargeback claim with screenshots of the transaction, your denuncia, and your travel dates as proof you were in Portugal.

If the fraud was a **SEPA bank transfer** (for example, you wired a €500 Sintra apartment deposit to an IBAN that turned out to be fake), contact your bank's fraud department and request a **SEPA recall** under the SEPA Credit Transfer Recall procedure. Recalls must be initiated within 10 business days of the transfer. Success depends on whether the receiving bank has already allowed the beneficiary to withdraw the funds — the earlier you initiate, the better.

For SIM-swap fraud (criminals transferring your phone number to their SIM to intercept bank codes), immediately call your Portuguese carrier (MEO, NOS, Vodafone, or Nowo) and request SIM suspension plus a port-out block. Then call your bank's fraud line before anything else — SIM-swap fraud moves faster than theft recovery, and the first hour is decisive.

## Within the first week

File a formal consumer-protection complaint with **ASAE** via **asae.gov.pt/denuncias**. ASAE is Portugal's primary consumer-protection regulator; it does not recover individual losses automatically, but its regulatory actions against repeat-offender businesses are one of the few effective pressures on the scam economy. Tourist-facing businesses know which complaints reach ASAE; a filed complaint frequently produces a restitution offer that the shop refused to make on the day of the incident.

For tour-operator and travel-agency disputes, file a parallel complaint with **Turismo de Portugal** at **turismodeportugal.pt** — the national tourism authority. Turismo de Portugal keeps the RNAVT (Registo Nacional dos Agentes de Viagens e Turismo) license records of every registered travel agency and tour operator; with the RNAVT number or company name (always photograph a tour-office RNAVT license before booking), Turismo de Portugal can flag the operator, order compensation, and — for repeat offenders — revoke the license. For rental-car disputes specifically, also file with **IMT** (Instituto da Mobilidade e dos Transportes) at **imt-ip.pt**.

For banking and Multibanco disputes — a charge your bank refuses to reverse, a SEPA recall that failed, an ATM skim you consider the bank should cover — escalate to the **Banco de Portugal Serviço de Cliente** at **bancoportugal.pt/cliente** or by calling **+351 21 313 0000**. The Banco de Portugal is the financial-sector regulator and its consumer division has a public complaint database; a recorded Banco de Portugal complaint motivates banks to resolve where a direct-to-bank complaint would stall.

For aviation disputes, **ANAC** (Autoridade Nacional da Aviação Civil) runs a passenger complaint portal at **anac.pt**. For broader tourism disputes, file with **DECO** (Associação Portuguesa para a Defesa do Consumidor) at **deco.pt** — Portugal's largest independent consumer-rights organization; DECO can mediate complaints and, for members, pursue legal action on your behalf.

Submit the full travel-insurance claim with all evidence gathered so far. Include the business's name, the address, the exact transaction details, the denuncia reference number, the ASAE reference number, and the chargeback reference.

Consider leaving a neutral, factual review on Google Maps, TripAdvisor, or Portal da Queixa (**portaldaqueixa.com** — Portugal's largest consumer-complaint platform). Describe what happened, the amounts involved, and the date, without emotional language or accusations. Reviews from recent victims are the single most reliable way future travelers avoid the same scam. Do not name individual staff members; describe the business by its official name and location only. Portuguese defamation law (both civil and criminal) can be strict — stick to verifiable facts and receipts.

## If your phone or SIM was stolen

Call your carrier immediately to suspend the line. The four major Portuguese carriers — **MEO** (dial **16200**), **NOS** (dial **16990**), **Vodafone Portugal** (dial **16912**), and **Nowo** (dial **+351 21 019 9999**) — all operate 24/7 stolen-device lines. Suspending the line prevents the thief from using your number to receive SMS two-factor-authentication codes, from initiating bank transfers on your phone, and from running up international-roaming charges. If you are on a tourist eSIM (Airalo, Saily, Nomad), the app will let you suspend the line in a few taps; do that before the denuncia.

File a denuncia at the nearest esquadra (or online at **psp.pt/Pages/reportarcrime.aspx**) and include your phone's **IMEI** number (the fifteen-digit serial; find it on the original box, in your Apple or Google account, or by dialing \*#06# *before* the theft). The IMEI lets police flag the device on the Portuguese stolen-phone registry and is required for any insurance claim.

If your SIM was swapped — criminals transferring your number to intercept bank codes — this is time-critical. Call the bank's fraud desk before anything else. Then file both the carrier-suspension request and a denuncia as cybercrime. Portuguese SIM-swap fraud rose sharply in 2024–2025; carriers added ID-verification requirements but the attack vector remains active.

## If the scam involved drink-spiking, drug-planting, or physical harm

Go to a hospital before you go to the police. Toxicology tests have a short window and cannot be run retroactively. A list of 24/7 hospitals with English-speaking staff in each city is in Appendix C. At the hospital, ask for a toxicology screen (*"exame toxicológico, por favor"*); Portuguese private hospitals such as Hospital da Luz (Lisbon / Porto / Faro), CUF Descobertas (Lisbon), Hospital Lusíadas (Lisbon / Porto / Albufeira / Funchal), and Hospital Particular do Algarve (Faro / Albufeira / Alvor) all run these routinely and will print a report you can use as evidence.

For drug-planting scams — where a stranger or someone posing as a "plainclothes police" demands cash to "make a possession charge go away" — stay calm, do not resist, and call **112** before any money changes hands. Real Portuguese narcotics cases go through official processes with written documentation, photographed evidence, and a mandatory visit to the esquadra. Demands for cash to "make it go away" are the signature of a scam, not of legitimate enforcement. Portugal decriminalized small-quantity drug possession in 2001, so even a genuine possession encounter does not proceed through on-the-spot fines.

In Portugal, the pan-European medical-emergency and police number is **112**. For tourist-specific help, PSP Turismo Lisbon runs **+351 21 342 1634**, 24/7, with English support. For any incident involving a foreign tourist, direct-dial phones for every city in this book are listed in Appendix C.

If you are inside a venue and drink-spiking is suspected, have a friend call **112** *before* you leave, not after — sometimes the aggressive bill-pressure escalates once the scammer realizes you are leaving.
