# About the Tabiji Travel Safety Series {-}

**Tabiji** publishes practical, specific, evidence-based scam-safety guides for international travelers. We believe the best travel-safety book is one you reread on the flight, then never need again — and that a ten-minute read before a trip is worth more than a hundred-page encyclopedia you never open.

Every volume in the Tabiji Travel Safety Series follows the same structure:

- A short introduction and a chapter on six universal red-flag patterns — the grammar of scams in that country, so you recognize a new one the moment it starts.
- Per-city chapters covering every scam reported in that destination, with real currency amounts, real street names, real neighborhoods, and real operators named in the local-language press.
- Three appendices: an exit-phrase card in the local language; a post-scam recovery playbook with the specific banking, consumer-protection, and tourist-police escalation paths for that country; and a verified emergency-contacts appendix with tourist-police phones, embassy addresses, English-speaking hospitals, and card-issuer emergency lines.

Our sources include local-language news reports, consumer-protection agency filings (ASAE in Portugal, PROCON in Brazil, DGCCRF in France, etc.), tourist-police records (PSP Turismo in Portugal, DEATUR in Brazil, Tourist Police 1155 in Thailand), and direct traveler reports on country-specific and city-specific Reddit communities. Every scam in every Tabiji volume cites at least three independent sources.

## Corrections and updates

We read every correction. If you encountered a scam in Portugal that is not in this book, or you found a detail in this book that has changed, please email **corrections@tabiji.ai**. Include the city, the specific venue or intersection, the scam pattern, and — if possible — a screenshot of the bill, the receipt, or the denuncia reference number. Verified corrections are credited in the next edition.
