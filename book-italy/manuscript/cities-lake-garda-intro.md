Lake Garda is Italy's largest lake and sits across three regions (Lombardy, Veneto, Trentino-Alto Adige), with a tourist footprint concentrated in Sirmione, Peschiera del Garda, Bardolino, Malcesine, and Limone sul Garda. The defining 2025 coverage is Corriere Brescia's "Sirmione salassi" feature (literally *bloodletting*) — a day in Sirmione documented €75 lunches and €8 single-scoop gelato along the peninsula, with a viral Instagram reel showing the escalation.

The signature Garda street scam is the *finti sordomuti* petition ring working Bardolino's waterfront — Polizia Locale di Bardolino's sindaco-sceriffo Nicotra personally blocked two members, and L'Arena covered a coordinated sweep of four fake deaf-mutes with an officer injured during the pickup. The ticketing traps cluster around Grotte di Catullo (real fee €6 / €3 ridotto per Polo Museale, but sold by bundles at €20) and the Malcesine cable car (Funivia Monte Baldo, real price €22 round-trip). Navigazione Laghi operates the only official ferry; Como-to-Bellagio is €11 standard and €16 hydrofoil. A safe default: buy every ticket from the official operator (NLC for ferries, Polo Museale for Grotte, Funivia Monte Baldo direct), park outside Sirmione's castle bridge and walk in (no unofficial "attendants"), and eat one street back from the promenade in every lakefront town.
