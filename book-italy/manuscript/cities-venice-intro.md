Venice is the most tightly policed pickpocket environment in Italy, and paradoxically one of the most aggressively targeted. The historic centre is physically an island of narrow calli and bottlenecked bridges, which means the pickpocket teams know exactly where you will be at each hour of the day. Corriere del Veneto's October 2025 investigation named a single ring responsible for much of the San Marco and Rialto activity, with a 20-year-old Bosnian leader and a documented €2,500-per-day take per member. More than two hundred pickpockets were detained in 2025; most were released within a night.

The risk zone is the "vaporetto triangle" — Piazzale Roma to Ferrovia, Ferrovia to San Marco, and the perimeter of Piazza San Marco itself. Water-taxi scams start the moment you land at Marco Polo (abusivi outnumber licensed operators roughly two-to-one per il Gazzettino). Gondolas off the posted €90 daytime rate are overcharging by definition. A safe default: take the Alilaguna blue line from Marco Polo (€15), buy only ACTV-badged vaporetto tickets at the machines with the blue logo, and eat at least four hundred metres off the San Marco–Rialto axis.
