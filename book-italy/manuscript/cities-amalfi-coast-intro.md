The Amalfi Coast covers about fifty kilometres of SS163 from Sorrento to Salerno — Amalfi, Ravello, Praiano, Atrani, Minori, Maiori — plus the sea lanes that connect them. Positano and Sorrento have their own chapters; this one covers the rest. The scam profile is dominated by transit: Fanpage documented a Carabinieri sweep of six unlicensed NCC drivers migrating from Naples on cruise days to work Amalfi's Molo Pennello. The legitimate taxi fare from Positano to Amalfi (eight kilometres) has been documented at €50; from Sorrento to Amalfi at €200; from Capodichino to Amalfi at €180 — the SITA Sud bus does the whole coast for €2.10 per leg.

The tourism tax rose to €4 per night for 2026, which is now the most common "city fee in cash" cover for fake rentals. The parking at Amalfi town (Piazza Flavio Gioia, €5/hr official) attracts parcheggiatori abusivi who redirect drivers to unofficial lots. The Villa Rufolo and Villa Cimbrone entry fees (€7–€8) are frequently repackaged by third-party tour sellers as €25–€40 bundles. A safe default: take SITA Sud for every SS163 leg (no parking exposure), buy ferry tickets only at Travelmar, NLG, or Alicost booths on the piers, pay accommodation tax only by card through the platform, and eat one block off the waterfront rather than on it.
