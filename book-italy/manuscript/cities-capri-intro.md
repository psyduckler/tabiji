Capri is a monopoly economy, and its scams are the scams monopolies produce. The Blue Grotto is not a single ticket — it is a four-fee stack: roughly €18 for the transfer boat from Marina Grande, €18 for the mandatory Cooperativa Battellieri rowboat and cave entry, a customary €2–€5 tip that the rower states loudly while you are already inside the grotto, and a €2 Comune tax. Families arrive thinking it is a €15 visit and leave having paid €35–€45 a head. When the sea is too rough and the grotto is closed, many "Blue Grotto included" boat tours still charge the full fare. Tripadvisor's recurring label for this is *"Truffa al 100%"* and *"La Truffa Azzurra."*

The Faraglioni beach clubs publish their own minimum-spend figures — La Fontelina charges €200 per person in high season, Da Luigi €115 plus €10 each way for the tender — but neither is obvious from a distance. The April 2026 Comune di Capri ordinance fining aggressive touts up to €500 is the clearest signal that the Piazzetta's scam density had finally become a political problem. A safe default: take the ATC #3 bus from Anacapri to the Grotta Azzurra stop and skip the transfer boat entirely; order espresso at the bar (standing) in any Piazzetta café, which is one-third the seated rate; and verify any private-boat-tour operator's refund terms in writing before paying.
