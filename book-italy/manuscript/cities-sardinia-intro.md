Sardinia's single most important warning is not a scam — it is the law. Taking sand, shells, or pebbles off a Sardinian beach is a criminal offense under Sardinia's regional Law n. 16/2017. The Agenzia delle Dogane has seized more than 900 kg in Olbia alone between 2020 and 2025, and fined eleven tourists €3,000 each in a single 2023 sweep at Cagliari-Elmas airport. A souvenir jar of sand bought from a Porto Cervo gift shop does not exempt you; the Guardia di Finanza x-ray inspects checked and carry-on baggage equally.

The scams proper cluster around three failure points. La Pelosa beach at Stintino has a €3.50 per-adult reservation sold via the Comune's apl.stintinospiagge.it portal; third-party resellers charge €15–€25 for the same booking. Olbia, Cagliari-Elmas, and Alghero-Fertilia airport rental-car counters have a well-documented phantom-damage pattern — Cittadinanzattiva has published warnings, and r/Sardinia firsthand threads repeat the same €300–€1,000 damage-deposit charges across years. The Porto Istana *peso d'oro* restaurant bill (€1,155 for cheese and sausage al etto, La Nuova Sardegna, July 2023) is the island's most-cited coperto trap. A safe default: buy La Pelosa tickets only at the official Stintino portal; film your rental car's condition from every angle at pickup and return; and confirm restaurant minimum-spend rules in writing before sitting on Costa Smeralda.
