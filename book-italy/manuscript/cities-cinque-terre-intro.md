The Cinque Terre — Monterosso, Vernazza, Corniglia, Manarola, Riomaggiore — looks like five villages on a UNESCO-listed coast and operates like a single high-friction ticketing puzzle. The Parco Nazionale publishes the Cinque Terre Treno MS card at a three-tier seasonal price: €17.30 in the low-attendance band (2 Nov–31 Dec 2026), climbing to €32.50 at Band C summer peak. Resellers at La Spezia Centrale and in the villages sell look-alike passes at €35–€50 year-round, regardless of band.

The most-documented scam is not a local operator at all: it is the Trenitalia digital-ticket check-in fine. Multiple r/ItalyTravel threads ("Fined on Trenitalia first time in Italy," "Legal Scam on Trainitalia") document €60 on-board fines for travellers who showed an email QR without pressing the in-app "check in" button before boarding. The ferry network runs separately — Navigazione Golfo dei Poeti, €30 one-way Monterosso to Riomaggiore — and quietly skips Corniglia because there is no dock there (cliff geography); Rick Steves' own guide documents this. A safe default: buy the Cinque Terre Card only at the Parco Nazionale office in any station or the official site **[parconazionale5terre.it](https://parconazionale5terre.it)**; press the "check in" button on every Trenitalia ticket before boarding; and budget for a separate €3 regional-train ticket to and from Corniglia if you are ferry-hopping the rest.
