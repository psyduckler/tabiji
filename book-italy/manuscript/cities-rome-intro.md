Rome holds the top spot in almost every European pickpocket ranking, and it earns the position by sheer volume: Fiumicino and Ciampino deliver roughly 50 million visitors a year into a historic centre only three kilometres across, and the scam operators have had two thousand years to perfect the routes. The daily Romatoday crime blotter reads like a list of the scams in this chapter: a Polizia Locale sting in Piazza del Duomo, a tre-campanelle crew arrested near Trevi, a POS-pirata contactless theft on Line B of the metro, another counterfeit-gladiator photographer fined outside the Colosseum.

The risk is concentrated in a compact zone: the axis from Termini through Via Nazionale to the Colosseum, the Centro Storico between Piazza Venezia and Piazza Navona, the Trevi-Spanish Steps corridor, the Vatican approach along Via della Conciliazione, and the A and B metro lines during commuter hours. Step two blocks off that axis and the risk drops by more than half. A safe default for first-timers: book hotels in Trastevere or Monti rather than around Termini, walk rather than take the metro during the day, and treat every "free" rose, "free" bracelet, and "free" photo as a loaded invoice.
