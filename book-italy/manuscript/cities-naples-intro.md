Naples has a reputation for chaos that is partly deserved and partly a story the city has stopped fighting. The sharp edge is the *scippo* — a moped slowing beside you just long enough to cut a bag strap — and a ring of Napoletani-connected Rolex thieves that operates as far afield as Marbella and Puerto Banús (a Nov 2024 joint Italian–Spanish arrest netted five suspects including one woman spotter). The older edge is the Piazza Garibaldi tre campanelle, which Fanpage headlined *"dopo 50 anni possibile che ci caschino ancora?"* — fifty years on, tourists still fall for it. The softer edge is the Capodichino airport transfer overcharge: the real Comune fare to Piazza Garibaldi is €21 and to Molo Beverello €24; abusivi SUV drivers were fined €73,000 in a single 2024 Guardia di Finanza sweep for working the airport without a taxi licence.

The risk zone is Piazza Garibaldi and the blocks immediately around Napoli Centrale, the pedestrian streets off Via Toledo after dark, Spaccanapoli restaurants at tourist prices, and the Circumvesuviana line outbound to Pompeii and Sorrento. A safe default: take the Alibus from Capodichino (€5) rather than any ground transfer, keep a phone and wallet only in a zipped crossbody bag, and eat in the neighbourhoods Neapolitans eat in — Rione Sanità, Chiaia, Vomero — rather than along the tourist-menu strip.
