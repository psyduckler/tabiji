Positano is the most-photographed cliff in Italy and its scams have become Italian-MP-level political theatre. In October 2025, deputy Francesco Emilio Borrelli posted a viral Facebook complaint headlined *"Quando il parcheggio a Positano costa 14€ l'ora incluse le guardie del corpo…"* (When parking in Positano costs €14 an hour, bodyguards included). Il Mattino has separately documented a Neapolitan woman who ran a full clone website of a real Positano hotel, defrauding more than ten wire-deposit victims. The town's local entrepreneur Ivan Mastro has publicly called the private-transfer market *"sciacallaggio"* — jackal behaviour against tourists.

The risk zone is the SS163 approaches, the few paid parking lots (Mandara, Marasca, and the unofficial pullouts), the Spiaggia Grande waterfront restaurants where *pesce al etto* can turn a €40 meal into a €150 bill, and the Spiaggia Grande private-lido row where minimum-spend rules are rarely posted. Parking disputes have occasionally escalated to physical altercations — Corriere del Mezzogiorno documented a July 2022 assault case — so never escalate with a parcheggiatore in person. A safe default: do not drive to Positano. Take the Alilauro ferry from Sorrento, Naples, or Amalfi, or the SITA Sud bus (€2.10). If you must arrive by car, park at Parcheggio Pasitea or Parcheggio Mandara, get a printed ticket, and photograph the posted rate card before you leave the lot.
