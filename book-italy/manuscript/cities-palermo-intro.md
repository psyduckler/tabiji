Palermo has the hardest scam profile in this book, which is not the same as saying it is unsafe: most travelers have an uneventful trip. But when things go wrong in Palermo, they go wrong faster and with less back-and-forth than in Rome or Bologna. The signature Sicilian scam is the *truffa dello specchietto* (mirror-crash scam) — a driver pulls alongside your rental, something cracks that sounds like you clipping his mirror, and he demands €100–€300 in cash. A March 2026 Carabinieri arrest in Carini pinned a 20-year-old Catanese on exactly this script, placed under *obbligo di dimora*. The moped scippo on Via Maqueda and Via Roma is the more common mechanism; the Mondello beach lido concession (held almost monopoly by Mondello Italo Belga since 1909) layers on a different kind of extraction.

The risk zone covers Via Maqueda and Via Roma between Quattro Canti and Piazza San Domenico, the Ballarò, Vucciria, and Capo markets at tourist rates, the Punta Raisi airport taxi queue (legitimate zone caps: €39 city / €44 extended / €50 airport-to-periphery), and the SS113 / SS121 highway approach where specchietto crews work. A safe default: take the Prestia e Comandè bus from Punta Raisi (€6.90) rather than a taxi, never stop your car on the SS roads if a fellow driver flags you over, and walk the markets on a Sunday when the tourist-tier pricing recedes.
