Pisa is a two-to-four-hour day trip for most visitors, which compresses its scam density into the tightest window in the book. You arrive at Pisa Centrale, you walk the 1.5 kilometers through Piazza dei Miracoli, you take the Leaning Tower photo, and you leave — and in that loop, you are statistically more likely to encounter a scam attempt than on a full day in Florence. The city acknowledged this explicitly in 2026, launching a dedicated Polizia Turistica on Piazza del Duomo whose entire justification was "borseggi e abusivi" at the Campo dei Miracoli.

The two most reliable scams are the Via Santa Maria bracelet and rose ambush (the 500-meter approach street from the Tower to the Arno is saturated with aggressive vendors), and the LAM Rossa bus fare-checker shakedown — where two "inspectors" block the ticket validator on boarding and then fine you one stop later for the ticket you couldn't validate. Pisa Today has a clean arrest record for a bracelet pair working this exact loop. The third risk is Pisa Centrale itself, where La Nazione reports *"lo sciame di borseggiatrici"* — a swarm of pickpocketers — the Polizia deploys plainclothes officers to suppress. A safe default: walk the 25 minutes from Centrale to the Tower rather than taking LAM Rossa, buy your Tower ticket only from the official Opera della Primaziale Pisana site (€20), and refuse every vendor on Via Santa Maria without stopping your walk.
