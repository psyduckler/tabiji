Taormina has become, since *The White Lotus* Season 2, one of the most-visited cliff towns in the Mediterranean — and the scam density has scaled with the traffic. The Teatro Antico (the town's signature Greek-Roman amphitheatre) has been under active restoration since early 2025; a May 2025 Gazzetta del Sud story documented tourists arriving with pre-paid "skip-the-line" tour tickets to find the theatre blocked by scaffolding and tarps, with no refund offered. Isola Bella — the offshore nature reserve reached by cable car — has a real WWF-managed entry fee of €5 (€2.50 ridotto); a Facebook Sicilia Vacanze post documents a tourist paying €1,000 to a scammer selling fake "resident" tickets.

The Etna tour market is the most important risk because it is also criminal: only guides registered with the Collegio Guide Alpine Vulcanologiche Etna can legally take clients above 2,500 metres, and unlicensed guides carry *esercizio abusivo della professione* criminal exposure. A November 2017 Guardia di Finanza operation denounced an unlicensed guide leading fifteen tourists to the Procura di Catania. The Catania Fontanarossa airport taxi fare to Taormina is roughly €70–€90 for the 55-kilometre trip; the ATM bus runs to Taormina for €6.50. A safe default: buy Teatro Antico tickets only at **[parconaxostaormina.com](https://parconaxostaormina.com)**, confirm scaffolding status before a tour company takes your money; book Etna summit hikes only through Alpine-Guide-of-Etna members; take the ATM bus from Catania rather than a taxi.
