## PART I — BEFORE YOU BEGIN (~10,000 words)

### Chapter 1 — Two Million Decisions (~2,800 words) ★ NEW HOOK

**Opening scene** (~600 words). Marcus, 58, project manager in Denver. 11 p.m. Tuesday. Kitchen table. The orthopedic appointment confirmed bilateral knee replacement. The US estimate is $22,000 out of pocket after insurance. Marcus has been on Reddit for two hours. He has bookmarked three clinics in Costa Rica, India, and Mexico. His wife is asleep upstairs. He has not told his brother. He drinks the last of the coffee. He bookmarks three websites. He closes the laptop without booking anything. The scene establishes voice, stakes, and reader identification.

**The data anchor** (~700 words):
- Global medical tourism economy estimated at $74–$92B (Patients Beyond Borders).
- 14–16M international medical travelers annually globally.
- US outbound: 1.4–2.0M Americans per year (CDC Yellow Book).
- The unprepared fraction: documented in patterns of harm — BBL mortality (CDC MMWR 73(3): 93 US-citizen deaths in DR 2009–2022, 92% involving BBL), the M. abscessus outbreaks documented by CDC, transplant tourism documented by State Department.
- The patterns matter; the framework that prevents them is what this book teaches.

**What this book is and isn't** (~700 words):
- A field guide for the buyer's seat — not a destination directory.
- Buyer-protection — not promotional.
- A framework for the decision — not a recommendation of where to go.
- The seven leverage points — what you preserve, what you give up.

**What the book will help you do, and what it cannot** (~500 words):
- Help you slow down before deposit; verify providers; read quotes; protect records; manage the relationship across borders.
- Cannot do: tell you which procedure to have; rank clinics; substitute for in-person clinical evaluation.

**Closing** (~300 words):
- Marcus's question — "should I even consider this?" — is the question the book exists to help answer.
- Path forward: Ch 2 explores why medical tourism exists; Ch 10 has the self-test; Ch 11 is the 60-day preparation plan; Ch 32 is the recommend-against editorial position.

### Chapter 2 — The Four Kinds of Medical Tourism (~1,800 words)

- **Cost-driven** (Marcus's category). The largest segment by volume. Why prices differ.
- **Access-driven.** Insurance denial, deductible cliff, US capacity constraints, US wait lists for some procedures.
- **Expertise-driven.** Specific surgical techniques or specialists concentrated abroad (Brazilian BBL technique, Indian cardiac corridor, Belgian hip resurfacing).
- **Niche-driven.** Procedures not available in the US (some IVF configurations, certain experimental treatments under expanded access).
- The framework adapts to each but is identical at the core.
- Most readers are cost-driven; the book is written to all four.

### Chapter 3 — Why Prices Differ Across Borders (~1,800 words)

- Labor cost differential (sourced; see Research Notes §2).
- Regulatory and liability cost differential.
- Currency and purchasing power.
- The bundled vs. unbundled pricing pattern.
- The "international patient department" pricing pattern — destination hospitals charge international patients 20–60% above local rates.
- Why the headline differential is real but smaller than headline figures suggest (carry the full-cost-calculator framing forward).

### Chapter 4 — The Accreditation Landscape (~2,000 words)

- JCI: standards, evaluation, public directory.
- AAAASF, ISO 9001, ISQua meta-accreditation, country-level bodies (NABH, ACHSI, HAI, Trent).
- What accreditation does NOT evaluate (surgeon outcomes, individual case quality).
- The accreditation theater problem.
- The verification path: search the public directory; do not rely on facility marketing.
