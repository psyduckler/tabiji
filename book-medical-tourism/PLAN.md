## 1. PURPOSE AND STATUS

This plan is the comprehensive execution document for *The Medical Tourism Field Guide* — Volume 3 of the Tabiji Field Guides series. It is the second pass after a structured three-audit review of v1; the changes from v1 are summarized in §23 and threaded throughout the relevant sections.

Approval of this plan triggers Phase 1 work and the start of reviewer outreach. Both run in parallel.

The book's central commitment: a **foundation book** about medical tourism that teaches the decision framework applicable to any procedure, surveys the major procedure categories at appropriate depth (not specialist depth), and takes editorial positions on a small number of patterns the evidence demands. It is not a survey book replacing V1 and V2; it is the front door of the series funnel.

## 2. STRATEGIC POSITIONING

The book is a **Foundation Book**, not a Survey Book or an Industry Exposé. It serves the reader one step earlier in the funnel than V1 or V2 — the reader who has not yet picked a procedure, who is asking *should I even consider care abroad, and if so, how do I think about it.*

This is the strongest commercial position for three reasons:

1. **Largest addressable audience.** V3's audience is anyone considering medical care abroad — 15–25M Americans annually who research it seriously. V1 (~5M) and V2 (~3M) are subsets.
2. **Highest-volume search keyword.** "Medical tourism guide" is materially higher search volume than any procedure-specific keyword. A calm, sourced, buyer-protection book wins this keyword against a market saturated with destination-promotional content.
3. **Cross-volume amplifier.** Procedure surveys in V3 point readers to V1 and V2 rather than competing with them. V3 is the feeder volume that compounds the series.

The path to a best-seller here is durability, not virality. The metrics that matter are sustained monthly sales over 24 months, cross-volume sell-through, and companion-site organic traffic. This is the foundation volume that establishes Tabiji as *the* buyer-protection brand for medical tourism — a position that compounds across volumes.

## 4. AUDIENCE SEGMENTS — FIVE READER PROFILES

Five primary segments. Each gets explicit chapter mapping in §10 below.

### Segment 1 — The Cost-Crisis Reader (anchor: Marcus)

- US adult, 45–70, facing a major medical decision they cannot afford at US prices. Often quoted $20K+ for dental, $30K+ for joint replacement, $50K+ for cardiac, $80K+ for cancer. Insurance has denied, run out, or applies an unmeetable deductible. Researches at night, under pressure.
- **What this book solves:** turns a panic decision into a structured decision. The reader leaves with a self-test, a complexity ladder, and a 60-day pathway.
- **Chapters that primarily serve this segment:** 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 18, 20, 21, 22, 38.
- **Anchor composites:** Marcus (Ch 1, 11, 38).

### Segment 2 — The Chronic-Condition Portfolio Reader (anchor: Eleanor)

- US adult, 55–75, with a chronic condition that may require multiple interventions over the next several years (e.g., bilateral knee replacement + extensive dental + cataract surgery). Recently retired or near retirement; has time; cares about quality and sequence.
- **What this book solves:** gives a framework for sequencing procedures across time, destinations, and recovery windows.
- **Chapters that primarily serve this segment:** 8, 9, 10, 11, 14, 17, 18, 21, 24, 25, 28, 35.
- **Anchor composites:** Eleanor (Ch 11, 35).

### Segment 3 — The Adult-Child Researcher (anchor: Daniel)

- 35–55, often a working professional, researching options for an aging parent quoted high or denied at home. Strong emotional load; often in family conflict over the decision.
- **What this book solves:** a defensible decision framework they can share with parent, siblings, and other family.
- **Chapters that primarily serve this segment:** 1, 10, 11, 14, 23, 35, 36.
- **Anchor composites:** Daniel (Ch 23, 36).

### Segment 4 — The Diaspora Reader (anchor: Priya)

- First- or second-generation immigrant considering care in country of origin. Mexico, India, Turkey, the Philippines, Vietnam, Eastern Europe, Korea, etc. Often family-connected in destination; speaks the language; knows the local medical system at some level. Medical tourism is a *different decision* for this reader.
- **What this book solves:** validates the diaspora frame, warns about specific patterns where family pressure or "the doctor we always go to" leads to a worse decision, and provides verification tools that work in the destination country.
- **Chapters that primarily serve this segment:** 1, 12, 14, 16, 17, 21, 24, 35.
- **Anchor composites:** Priya (Ch 16, 17).

### Segment 5 — The Exploratory Reader (anchor: Karen)

- 30–70, curious, not under pressure. Often referred by a friend or scrolling out of curiosity. Future cost-crisis reader.
- **What this book solves:** the calm orientation. The book reads as a serious adult resource rather than a destination ad.
- **Chapters that primarily serve this segment:** 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 30, 33.
- **Anchor composite:** Karen (Ch 31).

### The cross-segment unifier

All five segments share the same need: a *framework* before a destination. The book's positioning ("How to think about healthcare abroad before you pick a procedure") speaks to all five segments without compromising any of them.

## 5. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The market consists of older books (Patients Beyond Borders is the most-cited; 2015 era), procedure-specific self-published guides (often by patients or facilitators with commercial relationships), and a large ecosystem of destination-promotional content (Medical Tourism Magazine, hospital marketing sites, facilitator websites).

**What's missing:**

1. A modern, sourced, buyer-protection foundation book. Every comprehensive medical tourism book in the top 50 Amazon results is at least 8 years old, destination-directory framing, or both. None teaches the decision framework with citations to CDC, FDA, peer-reviewed literature, and named accreditation bodies.
2. A book that takes editorial positions on documented harm patterns.
3. A book that names the facilitator industry's incentive structures to patients.
4. A book that addresses the diaspora reader directly.
5. A book that survives 5+ years without going stale.

**Tabiji's moat:** calm-protective voice (V1/V2 established), sourced citations, editorial positions on documented patterns, no commercial relationships with industry actors, a series brand that compounds, a companion site for evergreen content.

**What we should not try to be:** a destination directory (Patients Beyond Borders covers that adequately), a hospital ranking (data too thin), a facilitator review (no infrastructure for ongoing monitoring), or an industry exposé (different genre, different voice).

## 6. EDITORIAL VOICE AND "BERNARD'S NOTES" FEATURE

### Voice (same as V1/V2)

The five voice commandments, written into every chapter draft:

1. **Specific over general.** Cite the source, name the body, give the figure.
2. **Verify over assume.** Every clinical claim has a source. Every regulatory claim has a body. Every dollar figure has a currency and a year.
3. **Pause over urgency.** The book never rushes the reader.
4. **Framework over destination.** The book never recommends a country or clinic. It recommends a *process*.
5. **Reader's seat over expert's seat.** Every chapter is from the consumer's vantage.

### "Bernard's Notes" feature (NEW)

Five to seven short editorial inserts (300–500 words each) at key inflection points where the editor's voice breaks through systematically. These are the moments where editorial conviction shows — what V1 and V2 had as occasional emergent moments are now structured features.

## 7. EDITORIAL POSITIONS — PATTERNS WE RECOMMEND AGAINST

The book takes explicit editorial positions on a small number of patterns where the evidence is strong enough that "ask better questions" framing is insufficient.

The candidate list (subject to reviewer sign-off):

1. **Transplant tourism in documented organ-trafficking corridors** — China, Pakistan, Egypt, the Philippines (pre-2008), Bangladesh. Primary sources: CMA Journal, The Lancet organ-trafficking commentary, China Tribunal Final Report (2020), U.S. State Department TIP reports, The Transplantation Society position statements. The book's position is the strongest possible.
2. **Stem-cell, regenerative, exosome, peptide, and longevity tourism.** FDA repeated warnings, ISSCR consumer guide. Entire category framed as warning, not buyer's guide.
3. **Unapproved alternative cancer therapies abroad** — laetrile, German biological cancer clinics, Mexican alternative cancer clinics, similar. NCI factsheets, peer-reviewed harm case series in JCO and JAMA Oncology, investigative journalism on documented patient harms.
4. **Surgery in non-accredited "boutique" or "luxury" facilities regardless of country**, where accreditation is implied via vague language without verifiable accreditation by a recognized body.
5. **Any procedure where the surgeon's board certification, registration number, and country of practice cannot be verified in a public registry before deposit.**
6. **Any procedure where the anesthesia provider's credentials cannot be verified in the destination country's official register.**
7. **BBL with intramuscular fat injection in non-accredited facilities** — carried from V2; the evidence base demands consistency.
8. **Surgery during the first 12 months after major adverse life events** (death of close relative, divorce, job loss, major diagnosis). Not categorical prohibition — the "timing question."

### Editorial threading (NEW vs v1)

The recommend-against position is the editorial spine of the book. In v1 it was buried as Part IX. In this revision:

- **Front matter disclaimer** explicitly cites that the book takes editorial positions on documented harm patterns.
- **Ch 1 hook** references the existence of such patterns.
- **Several procedure-survey chapters** reference the relevant entry in the recommend-against chapter (e.g., the bariatric survey references the unapproved-surgery-in-unaccredited-facilities entry; the cosmetic survey references the BBL entry).
- **The full chapter** (Ch 32) is the culmination, not the surprise.
- **Bernard's Note #6** sits after Ch 32 and contextualizes the editorial position.

The reader who only reads the first half of the book still encounters the editorial spine.

### Legal risk mitigation

- Per-claim sourcing.
- Malpractice attorney reviews the recommend-against chapter specifically.
- Fallback path: publish as standalone editorial appendix if reviewer demands softening unsupported by the data.

## 8. READING PATHS — NAVIGATION FOR FIVE READER SEGMENTS (NEW)

A "Reading Paths" page in the front matter (after the Scenario Index, before the Introduction) helps readers locate the essential chapters for their situation. Six paths.

### Path A — "I am in cost crisis and need to act in 60 days"

Read in order: Ch 1 → Ch 7 → Ch 8 → Ch 10 (self-test) → Ch 11 (60-day plan) → Ch 14 (second opinions) → Ch 18 (true cost) → Ch 20 (deposits) → Ch 32 (recommend-against) → relevant procedure survey → Ch 38 (when things go wrong).

### Path B — "I have time and want to plan well"

Read entire book in order. The full framework is for you.

### Path C — "I'm helping a parent or family member decide"

Read: Ch 1 → Ch 7 → Ch 10 → Ch 14 → Ch 16 → Ch 23 → relevant procedure survey → Ch 35 (companion role) → Ch 36 (follow-up at home) → Composite Scenario closing.

### Path D — "I'm considering care in my country of origin"

Read: Ch 1 → Ch 16 → Ch 17 (destination evaluation, including the diaspora-specific section) → Ch 24 or relevant procedure survey → Ch 14 → Ch 35 → Composite Scenario B (Priya).

### Path E — "I'm exploring; not committed to any procedure"

Read: Ch 1 → Ch 2 → Ch 3 → Ch 4 → Ch 7 → Ch 8 → Ch 10 → scan procedure surveys → Ch 32 → Final Note.

### Path F — "I had a bad outcome and am considering revision"

Read: Ch 1 → Ch 32 → Ch 38 → the relevant procedure survey → Ch 14 → Worksheet 20 (Cross-Border Claim).

The Reading Paths page is a 500-word section. It is positioned to help readers navigate; it explicitly says "you can also just read in order."
