# Appendix E: By severity

Not every scam in this book costs the same. The Friendship Bracelet
Trap costs ten euros and twenty seconds. An express kidnapping in
the back of a rigged taxi costs every dollar in your bank account
and several hours of fear. Both are scams. They deserve different
attention.

This appendix sorts the thirty chapters into three severity tiers.
Read the catastrophic-tier chapters in full, regardless of where
you are traveling. Read the frequent-tier chapters in full if your
destination is named in the chapter. Skim the minor-tier chapters
once and rely on the patterns from Part I to recognize them in the
field.

---

## Catastrophic tier (read in full, every reader)

These six scams carry physical-safety risk, coercion, drugging,
forced cash extraction, or document/identity loss, and the most
extreme cases extract more than a thousand dollars in under an
hour. They are concentrated in specific cities and specific
scenarios, not universally distributed. The severity here is
defined by the kind of harm, not the dollar range alone.

1. **Express Kidnapping Taxi (Chapter E).** A rigged ride forces
   ATM stops and drains accounts. Mexico City, São Paulo, Caracas,
   Buenos Aires, Bogotá. Up to three thousand US dollars per
   incident.

2. **Fake Drug-Search Police Sting (Chapter F).** Planted
   contraband, cash demand against threat of arrest. Bali Kuta,
   Hanoi Old Quarter, Sihanoukville, Bangkok Khao San, Sharm
   el-Sheikh. Two hundred to three thousand US dollars per
   incident.

3. **Drink Spiking & Bar Bill Trap (Chapter D).** Drugged drinks,
   four-figure bar tabs, card runs en route home. Bangkok Sukhumvit,
   Tokyo Kabukicho, Hanoi Bui Vien, Bogotá Zona Rosa, Medellín
   Provenza. Three hundred to five thousand US dollars per
   incident.

4. **Gem & Jewelry Shop Pressure (Chapter G).** Closing-room sales
   of glass and synthetic stones at ten to fifty times retail.
   Bangkok tuk-tuk gem loop, Marrakech medina, Jaipur Pink City,
   Khan el-Khalili. Five hundred to fifty thousand US dollars per
   incident.

5. **Phone Snatch Motorcycle (Chapter P).** Drive-by hand-grab
   from a motorbike; geometric capture, no recovery. São Paulo
   Avenida Paulista, Saigon Bui Vien, Bogotá Carrera 7, Naples
   Spaccanapoli. Four hundred to fifteen hundred US dollars per
   device, plus account-recovery hours.

6. **Tea House Invitation (Chapter T).** Friendly approach,
   side-street second-floor tea ceremony, four-figure bill. Beijing
   Wangfujing, Shanghai Nanjing Road, Xi'an Muslim Quarter,
   Hangzhou West Lake. One to five thousand US dollars per
   ceremony.

These six share a pattern: they exploit a captive position (in a
car, in a bar, in a back-room teahouse, in the geometry of a
sidewalk). Pattern 1 (the Captive-Position Lever) and Pattern 7
(Manufactured Reciprocity) in Part I cover the underlying mechanics.

If you read nothing else, read these six. The recovery is hardest,
the harm is worst, and the defenses are the most counter-intuitive.

## Frequent tier (read for your destination)

These seventeen scams are commonly encountered in tourist zones,
typically extract fifty to several thousand US dollars per
incident, and rarely involve physical threat. They run on
travelers who have not read about them in advance. Reading the
chapter in full before the relevant trip almost always neutralizes
them. (A few of the larger frequent-tier extractions, such as the
Carpet Shop Pressure Sale, can run higher than the usual range
when a closing-room sale lands; the harm class still places them
here rather than in the catastrophic tier.)

7. **Airbnb Off-Platform Fraud (Chapter A).** Phantom listing,
   off-platform deposit, bait-and-switch, double booking. Buenos
   Aires Palermo, Rome Trastevere, Lisbon Alfama, Marrakech medina,
   Bali Canggu. Two hundred eighty to four thousand USD per
   incident.

8. **Airport Arrival Scams (Chapter A).** Unlicensed taxi, fake
   "Tourist Information," meet-and-greet hustles, currency-counter
   spread, SIM-kiosk markup. Bangkok BKK, Mexico City MEX, Cancún
   CUN, Bali DPS, Cairo CAI. Twenty to two hundred USD per
   incident.

9. **ATM Currency Conversion Trap (Chapter A).** Dynamic currency
   conversion default at POS, ATM, hotel checkout, and online
   booking. Italy, Spain, Czech Republic, France, UK. Five to
   fifteen percent silent markup per transaction.

10. **ATM Skimming (Chapter A).** Overlay skimmer, shoulder-surf
    PIN capture, private-ATM cassette skim, Lebanese-loop trap.
    Paris Champs-Élysées, Barcelona Las Ramblas, Rome Termini,
    Bangkok Sukhumvit, Bali Kuta. Two hundred to four thousand EUR
    per cloned card.

11. **Bus and Train Station Scams (Chapter B).** Fake fare checker,
    ticket-machine helper, luggage-helper theft, wrong-platform
    redirect. Rome Termini, Naples Centrale, Paris Gare du Nord,
    Madrid Atocha. Twenty-five to one hundred fifty EUR per
    encounter.

12. **Carpet Shop Pressure Sale (Chapter C).** Hotel-referral
    funnel, captive-position showroom, FedEx under-declaration.
    Istanbul Grand Bazaar, Marrakech medina, Cairo Khan el-Khalili,
    Cappadocia. Fifteen hundred to twelve thousand USD per closing.

13. **Closed-Attraction Redirect (Chapter C).** Fake closure claim,
    redirect to commission shop, gem-store or carpet-shop kickback.
    Delhi Lal Qila, Bangkok Grand Palace, Agra Taj Mahal Friday,
    Angkor Wat morning, Cusco Cathedral Sunday. One hundred to
    fifteen hundred USD per redirect.

14. **Counterfeit Currency Returns (Chapter C).** Taxi bill swap,
    small-shop change padding, cambio counterfeit-mix, private-ATM
    cassette load. Buenos Aires, Mexico City, Cairo, Marrakech.
    Fifty to seven hundred USD per swap.

15. **Currency Exchange Cambio (Chapter C).** Sub-market street
    rate, back-room rate switch, airport-window markup, small-bill
    short-count. Buenos Aires Calle Florida, Tangier ferry exit,
    Casablanca CMN, Cancún Hotel Zone. Five to twenty percent
    silent loss per transaction.

16. **Distraction-Theft Pickpocket Team (Chapter D).** Bird-poop /
    mustard splat, spilled-coffee helper, dropped-camera pickup,
    map-help confusion. Madrid Sol, Rome Termini, Barcelona Plaza
    Catalunya, Buenos Aires Microcentro. Two hundred to two
    thousand USD per wallet.

17. **Fake Antique Souvenir Markup (Chapter F).** Fake Aboriginal
    art, fake Damascene metalwork, fake Dead Sea cosmetics, antico
    branding bait, counterfeit luxury bags. Sydney The Rocks,
    Istanbul Grand Bazaar, Cairo Khan el-Khalili, Rome Porta
    Portese, Bangkok MBK. One hundred to two thousand USD per item.

18. **Fake Booking Website (Chapter F).** Cloned Airbnb listing,
    Booking.com phishing email, typo-domain hotel site, WhatsApp
    fixer deposit, fake skip-the-line ticket reseller. Global, no
    geographic concentration. One hundred to forty-eight hundred
    USD per incident.

19. **Fake Government Tourist Office (Chapter F).** Airport
    arrivals booth, taxi-driver redirect, closed-attraction
    redirect, brochure-display shop. Bangkok Suvarnabhumi, Cairo
    CAI, Hanoi Noi Bai, Marrakech RAK. Thirty to two hundred USD
    per incident.

20. **Fake Skip-the-Line Tickets (Chapter F).** Street-scalper cash
    sale, online checkout clone, third-party reseller markup,
    hotel-concierge bundle. Rome Vatican, Barcelona Sagrada
    Familia, Paris Louvre, Istanbul Hagia Sophia. Twenty-five to
    two hundred EUR per ticket.

21. **Fake Tour Guide (Chapter F).** Closed-attraction redirect,
    fake museum guide, mountain-trail mid-extortion, horseback /
    camel mid-trail extortion, fake park ranger. Cairo Giza
    Pyramids, Petra, Marrakech medina, Cappadocia, Costa Rica
    national parks. Thirty to five hundred USD per incident.

22. **QR Code Quishing (Chapter Q).** Parking-meter overlay,
    transit-kiosk spoof, restaurant menu QR, fake-fine windshield
    slip. Paris Avenue Montaigne, Berlin Alexanderplatz, London
    West End, San Francisco Union Square. Three hundred to four
    thousand USD per harvest.

23. **Restaurant Bill Padding (Chapter R).** Items not ordered,
    swapped menu prices, card-retry triple charges, rounding-up
    cash, forced-tip pressure, service-charge stacking. Rome,
    Athens Plaka, Barcelona Las Ramblas, Venice San Marco. Ten to
    eighty USD per meal.

These seventeen chapters cover the bulk of what most travelers
encounter. The reading strategy is by destination, not exhaustive.

## Minor tier (skim once, recognize on sight)

These seven scams are low-complexity, walk-past-defense scams
that typically resolve quickly when recognized. Per-incident
dollar losses are usually small, though a few outliers (a long
bracelet session, a beach-club minimum-spend across a couple, a
multi-round monte session) push higher. They are still worth
knowing because the crews who run them often coordinate with crews
who run more serious scams. A minor-tier encounter is sometimes
the screen for a frequent-tier follow-up.

24. **Aggressive Street Vendor (Chapter A).** Beaded bracelet, rose
    at the table, selfie-stick upsell, trinket placement. Rome
    Spanish Steps, Paris Sacré-Cœur, Barcelona Las Ramblas. Twenty
    to two hundred EUR per demand.

25. **Beach Chair Lounger Hustle (Chapter B).** Italian *concessione*,
    Greek beach-club bottle service, Turkish beach club, Spanish
    *chiringuito*, boundary ambiguity. Mykonos Paradise, Italian
    Adriatic, Costa del Sol, Bodrum. Fifty to five hundred EUR per
    couple.

26. **Cover Charge (Coperto / Otoshi / Couvert) (Chapter C).**
    Italian *coperto*, Japanese *otoshi*, French *couvert*, German
    *Gedeck*, Iberian / Greek pre-set platter. Rome Trastevere,
    Tokyo Kabukicho, Athens Plaka, Munich Marienplatz. Five to
    fifty USD per meal.

27. **Friendship Bracelet Trap (Chapter F).** Tie-first wrap,
    coin-trick lure, friendly-greeting slip, group-surround. Paris
    Sacré-Cœur, Rome Spanish Steps, Barcelona Las Ramblas, Florence
    Piazza della Signoria. Five to fifty USD per wrist.

28. **The Gold Ring Trick (Chapter G).** Single mechanic, two
    closing pivots. Paris Pont des Arts, Pont Neuf, Champ de Mars,
    Tuileries, Quai du Louvre, plus eleven copy cities across
    France. Ten to one hundred twenty EUR per incident.

29. **Henna Tattoo Ambush (Chapter H).** Marrakech grab-and-paint,
    Cairo PPD black-henna, Istanbul Sultanahmet, Jaipur Pink City.
    Ten to fifty USD per wrist plus chemical-allergy risk on PPD
    variants.

30. **Three Card Monte (Chapter T).** Cards (find-the-queen),
    three-shells (cup-and-ball), crowd-shill lure, lookout-and-
    runner crew. London Oxford Street, NYC Times Square, Paris
    Champs-Élysées, Barcelona Las Ramblas. Twenty to five hundred
    USD per round, plus pickpocket exposure.

The reading strategy for these seven is to recognize the play in
the first three seconds and walk on. You do not need to know every
detail. You need to know it is a play.

## How to use the severity tiers

The catastrophic tier is the cover-to-cover read for every
traveler, because the exposure is geographically narrower than the
impact. A two-week European vacation does not include any
catastrophic-tier scams; a two-week trip with a stop in Mexico City
and a side trip to Bangkok might include three.

The frequent tier is the destination-specific read. Match the
chapter list above against your itinerary. Most readers will get
more value from reading two or three frequent-tier chapters at full
depth than skimming all seventeen.

The minor tier is the recognition-only read. Skim each chapter
once. The first three seconds at the bracelet stall, the gold-ring
bend, and the monte table is the only window the variants need;
recognition is the entire defense.

The web atlas at *tabiji.ai/scams/atlas* covers an additional set
of scams beyond the thirty in this book (rental-car phantom damage,
luggage theft on trains, taxi meter manipulation, ZTL fine cascade,
and others). The web atlas is updated continuously; this book is
the cover-to-cover annual edition.
