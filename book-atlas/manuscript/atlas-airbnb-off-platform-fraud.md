## The portero

Marcus arrived in Buenos Aires on a Wednesday night at nine, off a
twelve-hour flight from JFK, with his thirty-five-liter backpack and an Airbnb
confirmation that read "Calle Honduras 4500, apartamento 4B, código
de portero 1452" (building intercom code 1452).

Honduras was the way every Palermo Soho article had described it.
A 1920s low-rise. Street-art murals on the ground floor. A
bookshop on the corner. He found number 4500 on the second
walk-by. He pressed 1452 on the *portero*, the building intercom.

Nothing happened.

He tried again. Nothing.

He messaged the host on Airbnb in-app. *"Hola Carlos, estoy en la
puerta, el código no funciona, podés ayudarme?"* (*Hi Carlos, I'm
at the door, the code is not working, can you help?*) Delivered.
No reply. He waited ten minutes. Tried again. Still nothing.

He walked around to the side entrance. There was a small directory
of names next to a row of mailboxes. Six apartments listed: 1A, 1B,
2A, 2B, 3A, 3B. There was no 4B. He walked back to the street and
looked up. The building was three stories. There was no fourth
floor.

The Airbnb listing had shown a fourth-floor apartment with a
balcony overlooking a quiet street, original tile floors, a vintage
clawfoot bathtub. The photos were beautiful. The deposit was 280
USD. The apartment did not exist.

Marcus stood on the sidewalk and started the recovery sequence in
his head.

## The trick

The pattern is Brand-Mimicry Storefront: the listing, the host
profile, and the platform-styled message thread look real, while
the payment routing or the apartment itself sits outside the
platform's protections. Airbnb off-platform fraud depends on you
accepting a payment route or an apartment that those protections
do not cover. The platform's payment system gives you cancellation
rights, refund rights, dispute resolution, and chargeback access.
Once any of those protections drop, the scammer extracts and
disappears.

The on-platform-only rule defeats all four mechanics. Pay only
through the platform. Refuse every off-platform payment request
categorically. The protection layer is what you are paying the
platform for.

## The four mechanics

**The phantom listing.** The most-documented variant globally. A
scammer scrapes high-quality photos from a real listing somewhere
else (often a high-end hotel, a real-estate brokerage page, a
Pinterest-shared apartment), creates a fake host profile, posts the
listing at below-market rate. They accept the deposit and either
disappear or direct guests to an address that does not exist. The
average phantom listing lives twenty-one days before Airbnb removes
it. New clones appear continuously. Most reported in: Buenos Aires
(Palermo and Recoleta), Rome (Trastevere and Centro Storico),
Lisbon (Bairro Alto and Alfama), Mexico City (Roma Norte and
Condesa), Marrakech medina, Athens Plaka.

**The off-platform deposit.** The host accepts your booking on
Airbnb, then immediately messages: *"let's continue communication
via WhatsApp to save platform fees"* or *"I can give you a discount
if we handle payment via wire transfer."* Once you pay outside the
platform, you lose all refund rights, cancellation protection, and
chargeback access. The host then either disappears or check-in
proceeds but the deposit cannot be recovered when complications
arise. Most reported in: Marrakech, Bali (Canggu, Ubud), Buenos
Aires (Palermo, San Telmo), Cairo (Zamalek), Casablanca.

**The bait-and-switch.** The listing photos show a beautiful
apartment in a desirable neighborhood. On arrival, the host meets
you and explains: *"the apartment is being painted today, my
brother has a similar one in [different neighborhood], let me take
you there."* The substitute is smaller, in a worse area, often in a
different city quadrant. Most reported in: Venice (Cannaregio and
Castello), Bali (Canggu and Seminyak), Marrakech medina, Croatia
(Hvar, Korčula), Mexico City peripheral neighborhoods.

**The double-booking bait.** The host accepts two simultaneous
bookings for the same dates, knowing they can only deliver one
apartment. They wait until the day of arrival and offer the
apartment to whichever guest is paying more (longer stay, premium
add-ons, off-platform supplements). The displaced guest gets a
last-minute cancellation message claiming illness or "unforeseen
maintenance." Airbnb refunds the original booking but does not
compensate for last-minute hotel pricing differences. Most reported
in: Croatia (Hvar, Korčula, Dubrovnik) in peak July–August, Greece
(Mykonos, Santorini) in July–September, Bali (Canggu, Uluwatu),
Buenos Aires during peak event windows.

## Where it runs (11 countries, Argentina dominant)

Airbnb fraud concentrates in markets with high tourist Airbnb
density, peak-season inventory crunch, and weak local
consumer-protection enforcement. Argentina alone accounts for
a substantial share of global exposure, driven by Buenos Aires
phantom-listing density and post-pandemic Airbnb growth. Italy,
Morocco, and Mexico round out the top four.

The seasonal concentration matters. Croatian and Greek coastal
inventory crunches between mid-July and late August. Buenos Aires
peaks during major events (the World Cup window, the Pope Francis
funeral in 2025). Bali and Marrakech run continuously. Phantom
listings cluster around weekends in any market.

## The five red flags

If two or more fire while you are evaluating a listing or
arriving at one, route around the booking.

- **Photos that look stock-perfect.** Reverse-image-search any
  listing photo before you book. If it appears on a hotel website
  or a Pinterest board from 2019, the listing is a phantom.
- **A new host with under ten reviews.** New hosts can be
  legitimate, but the combination of "new host" plus "below-market
  price" plus "high-demand neighborhood" is the phantom-listing
  signature. Wait for reviews.
- **Any request to communicate or pay outside Airbnb.** This is
  the off-platform deposit pattern. The phrase "to save platform
  fees" is the canonical opening line.
- **An on-arrival switch to a different apartment.** The bait-and-
  switch only succeeds when the guest agrees to relocate. The
  Airbnb listing is contractual.
- **A last-minute cancellation in peak season.** This is the
  double-booking bait. Have a refundable backup hotel for any
  high-demand date.

## The messages that shut it down

Airbnb fraud plays out on messaging apps, not face-to-face, so the
defense is in writing rather than spoken. Always send these on the
in-app message thread, never on WhatsApp or email. The in-app
thread is your evidence for any later dispute.

When the host suggests moving to WhatsApp:

> *Thanks, but I prefer to keep all communication on Airbnb so we
> have a record. Happy to discuss anything here.*

When the host requests a wire transfer to "save platform fees":

> *Airbnb's payment system is the only one I use for bookings.
> Please send any deposit requests through the platform.*

When the host suggests a different apartment on arrival:

> *I booked the listing at [address] on Airbnb. I'd like to access
> that specific apartment as advertised, or I'll need to request
> emergency rebooking through Airbnb support.*

When the host cancels last-minute claiming an emergency:

> *Understood. Please confirm the cancellation in the Airbnb app so
> the platform processes the refund.*

The phrasing matters less than the channel. The in-app thread is
the channel. Anything off-platform is the scammer's territory.

## If this happens to you

You arrived at the apartment and the address does not match, the
host has stopped responding, or the actual apartment is nothing
like the listing. Airbnb fraud losses are highly recoverable
through the platform's dispute resolution, less recoverable
through credit-card chargeback, and rarely worth pursuing through
local police. The first hour matters because the in-app evidence
(messages, photos, timestamped status) is the only thing the
platform support team will look at.

Within thirty minutes of arrival or check-in failure: open the
Airbnb app, navigate to *Help → Trip issues → I cannot reach my
host* or *I cannot get into the listing*. Submit dated photos of
the building exterior, the door or *portero* or keypad, and the
messaging thread showing host non-response. Request emergency
rebooking. The platform offers replacement apartments at Airbnb
cost (you do not pay the markup) within thirty to sixty minutes
for any host-misrepresentation case.

Within sixty minutes: walk to a hotel within six blocks. Book
something refundable. Do not search Airbnb again that night. The
emergency rebooking takes the platform a few hours; you need a
bed sooner than that.

Within twenty-four hours: file a credit-card chargeback if the
deposit is significant and the platform has not yet refunded.
Cite the Airbnb support ticket number. Most card issuers reverse
within thirty days.

Marcus walked six blocks to a hotel that had availability and
booked a room for two nights while he sorted out the rest. His
deposit came back from Airbnb seven days later under the
host-misrepresentation policy. By day three of the trip he had a
re-booked apartment in Recoleta, and the only artifact of the first
night was the screenshot of the empty *portero* directory in his
camera roll.
