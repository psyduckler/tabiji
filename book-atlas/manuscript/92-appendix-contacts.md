# Appendix C: Emergency contacts by country

The country book in the Travel Safety Series for your specific
destination contains complete embassy, hospital, and tourist-police
contacts for every city in that volume. This appendix is the master
index across the twenty-four countries currently covered, organized
for quick lookup.

For each country, four numbers:

- **The general emergency number** (police, fire, medical) for the
  country.
- **The tourist-police hotline** if it exists.
- **The cyber-fraud reporting number** if it exists.
- **The number for your embassy** in the country's capital. This
  appendix lists the United States, United Kingdom, and Canadian
  embassy numbers, which between them cover most English-speaking
  travelers. For other nationalities, the country book has the full
  matrix.

A note before the lists: most catastrophic scams (express kidnapping,
fake police shakedowns, drink spiking) require the general emergency
number and your embassy. Most non-catastrophic scams (overcharges,
commission detours, redirect attempts) require the tourist police if
the country has them, the regular police if not, and a follow-up call
to your bank.

The numbers below are accurate as of publication. We re-verify every
year. If you find a number that has changed, write to us at
editorial@tabiji.ai and we will update the next edition.

## How to use this appendix

Save the four numbers for your destination country to your phone
contacts before you fly. The list above is for reference; the saved
contacts are what you actually use. Tag the contacts with the country
name so they sort correctly when you have entries for multiple
destinations.

If you are visiting a country not on this list, your country's
foreign-affairs ministry maintains an emergency-contacts list for
every country it has consular relations with. Look it up before you
fly. Save the same four kinds of numbers.

The single most useful number, in any country, is the general
emergency number. Memorize it. The next is your embassy's after-hours
line, which is the same as the main number for most embassies but
varies. Memorize that too.
