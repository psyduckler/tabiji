# A · ATM Skimming

## The twenty euro

You walk up to a standalone ATM half a block off the Champs-Élysées
at six on a Saturday evening because the BNP Paribas line is
fifteen people deep. The standalone says INTERNATIONAL CARDS WELCOME
in five languages. The slot looks normal. You insert your card and
start the withdrawal.

Behind you, a man in a windbreaker approaches with a smile and a
twenty-euro note in his hand. *"Excusez-moi, monsieur, vous avez
fait tomber ça."* (*Excuse me, sir, you dropped this.*) You half-
turn. Your PIN goes into the keypad while your eyes are on him. His
partner across the plaza, phone raised like a tourist taking a
photo, has just captured your four digits.

The twenty he is holding was always his. While you thank him and
turn back, your card has already been swapped for a near-identical
decoy by a third person who came up on your other side. You finish
the withdrawal, take the cash and the wrong card, and walk away.

By the time your bank flags the cloned-card transactions in
Marseille that night, you are 4,000 euros in.

That is the universal mechanic, executed at the Paris archetype: a
standalone ATM with skimming hardware, a three-person team
running shoulder-surf PIN capture, and a card swap. *Le Parisien*
publishes annual coverage of the Champs-Élysées operation under
headlines like *"L'arnaque au distributeur"* (*the ATM scam*).
The same crews work the Marais, Montmartre, and the perimeter of
Sacré-Cœur.

## The trick

The pattern is Brand-Mimicry Storefront: an overlay faceplate, a
"Use This ATM" handwritten sign, or a private-ATM cabinet that
mimics a real bank machine. ATM skimming depends on you not
noticing for the seconds it takes to capture your card data and
your PIN. The capture itself is
fast, under three seconds. Unwinding it after a clone is in the
wild requires hours of bank disputes and possibly an embassy
visit if your access to cash is gone. The defensive routine is a
sixty-second sequence that runs once per ATM, in any country.

The wiggle-cover-stay routine is the universal defense. Sixty
seconds, three steps, applied at every ATM in every country.

**Wiggle.** Before inserting your card, push the card slot from
side to side. A genuine slot has zero play. An overlay rocks. If
it rocks, walk to the next ATM.

**Cover.** Cup your free hand over the keypad while you type your
PIN. Block any line of sight from above (camera) and from behind
(human shoulder-surfer).

**Stay.** Do not leave the machine until your card and your
receipt have returned. If your card does not return within
thirty seconds, call your card issuer's international fraud line
from where you are standing.

## The four mechanics

**Overlay skimmers.** A fake card-slot faceplate fits over the
genuine ATM slot. As your card passes through, a battery-powered
reader records the magnetic-stripe data. The overlay is colored
and shaped to match the machine but is loosely attached, designed
for fast install and removal between police patrols. Crews install
in early morning and harvest at end of shift. **Defense:
wiggle the slot.** Genuine slots are firm. Most reported at: Paris
Champs-Élysées, Barcelona Las Ramblas, Rome Termini, Bangkok
Sukhumvit.

**Shoulder-surf PIN capture.** An observer (human or pinhole
camera) records your PIN as you type. The human variant runs as a
"helpful stranger" approach combined with two- or three-person
card-swap teams. The camera variant is a tiny lens hidden in a
plastic strip above the keypad, often near a handwritten sign
saying *USE THIS ATM*. Both feed cloned cards. **Defense: cup your
free hand over the keypad.** Most reported in: Paris Métro and
Champs-Élysées, Barcelona Plaça Catalunya, Madrid Sol.

**Private-ATM machines.** ATMs owned and stocked by a private
company (not a bank) that have skimming hardware permanently
installed in the cassette and reader path. The owner gets a
small per-transaction fee from the network plus all the skimmed
card data. Common branding: *Euronet*, *Cardtronics*, generic
*ATM* signage. Bank-issued chargeback claims are harder against
private-ATM transactions because the company name does not match
a bank. **Defense: avoid entirely.** Use only major-bank-branded
ATMs. Most reported in: Bangkok Sukhumvit and Khao San, Bali Kuta
and Seminyak, Cancún Hotel Zone, Prague Wenceslas.

**Lebanese-loop card traps.** A thin plastic loop is inserted into
the card slot before your transaction. The loop grips your card so
it cannot return. You attempt several PIN entries. The machine
reports an error. You eventually walk away assuming the bank kept
your card. A waiting scammer, who has watched your PIN entries
via shoulder-surf or camera, extracts the trapped card and uses it
before you can call your issuer. **Defense: if your card does not
return within thirty seconds, do not walk away.** Stand at the
machine and call your issuer. Most reported in: Prague Wenceslas,
Bucharest Old Town, Sofia city center.

## Where it runs (19 countries, Western Europe and Southeast Asia dominant)

ATM skimming concentrates where two conditions overlap: dense
tourist foot traffic and prevalence of non-bank ATMs. Western
Europe and Southeast Asia account for the bulk of
documented variants.

France (Paris dominant), Spain (Barcelona, Madrid), Italy (Rome
Termini), Czech Republic (Prague), Romania (Bucharest), Bulgaria
(Sofia), Thailand (Bangkok Sukhumvit, Khao San; Phuket), Indonesia
(Bali Kuta, Seminyak), Mexico (Cancún Hotel Zone), Vietnam (Hanoi
Old Quarter, Ho Chi Minh City). Heavy concentration also in major
European train stations and at airport-arrivals-hall standalones
labeled *"International Cards Welcome."*

A 2025 *Daily Mail* investigation estimated 212,000 tourists to
France had been scammed across five years, with ATM fraud a
significant contributor.

## The five red flags

Two or more in the first ten seconds: walk to the next ATM.

- **The card slot rocks when you push it sideways.** Overlay
  skimmer. Confirmed.
- **The faceplate is a slightly different color than the rest of
  the machine.** Some overlays are ABS plastic at a slightly
  different shade than the brushed metal of the original.
- **A handwritten sign points to this ATM specifically.** Real
  banks do not handwrite signs to redirect customers.
- **A "helpful stranger" approaches as you start the
  transaction.** The shoulder-surf team has selected you. Do not
  break eye contact with the keypad until the receipt prints.
- **The ATM is freestanding inside a shop, mini-market, or
  pharmacy without bank branding.** Private-ATM. The skimmer is
  in the cassette path. Walk to the bank branch.

## The phrases that shut it down

ATM skimming refusal is mostly non-verbal. Walk away from
suspicious machines. Cup your free hand over the keypad. Do not
engage with anyone who approaches. A phrase ends the rare
verbal-distraction approach immediately.

| Language | Phrase | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| French | *Laissez-moi tranquille.* | Leave me alone. |
| Spanish | *Déjame en paz.* | Leave me alone. |
| Italian | *Mi lasci stare.* | Leave me alone. |
| Czech | *Nechte mě být.* | Leave me alone. |
| Thai | *Yah maa yung kap/ka.* | Don't bother me. |
| English | "Step back. Now." | (firm, eye on the keypad) |

If a stranger holds up cash and claims you dropped it, do not
turn around. Keep your hand cupped over the keypad. If the
distraction continues, cancel the transaction (red button), wait
for your card to return, and walk to a bank-branch ATM.

## If this happens to you

Your bank flagged a transaction you did not make, or your card
was retained at an ATM and the wallet shows charges already
posting. The first hour matters most. The cloned card will be
used in cycles until you freeze it, and Visa or Mastercard
zero-liability policies require you to report the unauthorized
transactions promptly.

Within five minutes: call your card issuer's twenty-four-hour
international fraud line. The number is printed on the back of
every card. Save photos of every card back separately on your
phone for exactly this moment. Freeze the card. The issuer will
block all future transactions and reverse fraudulent ones.

Within thirty minutes: file a police report at the local
tourist-police office or the nearest *commissariat*. The report
number is what most card issuers' fraud-investigation teams
require for full reimbursement above a small threshold.

Within twenty-four hours: visit the embassy or consulate if your
card was your only access to cash. Most embassies issue an
emergency travel document and arrange a wire transfer of
emergency funds within twenty-four to seventy-two hours.

For private-ATM transactions: the chargeback is harder because
the merchant name on the dispute is generic *("ATM withdrawal")*.
Cite the police report and the wiggle-test result if the slot
rocked. Card issuers reverse with patience and good documentation.
