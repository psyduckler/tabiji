# Appendix D: By traveler type

The thirty scams in Part II do not target every traveler equally.
Some scams optimize for older travelers. Some optimize for solo
female travelers. Some optimize for the digital nomad rushing
through an airport. This appendix is for readers who want a
shorter list calibrated to who they are.

Pick the profile closest to yours. Read the chapters listed for
that profile in full. Skim the rest.

A traveler often fits more than one profile. A 64-year-old man
traveling alone for the first time abroad reads both the
over-sixty list and the first-time-international list. A
28-year-old woman working remotely from Bali reads both the solo-
female-traveler list and the digital-nomad list. The lists overlap
on purpose. The scams that target multiple profiles appear in
multiple lists.

---

## For the solo female traveler

You travel alone. Some scams in this book optimize for women
traveling without a male companion specifically because the
scammer assumes social pressure will close the deal faster.

The ten chapters most relevant:

1. **Friendship Bracelet Trap (Chapter F).** Bracelets tied to
   wrists in tourist plazas. Paris, Rome, Barcelona, Florence.
   Hands in pockets near monuments.
2. **Henna Tattoo Ambush (Chapter H).** "Free" henna applied then
   charged. Marrakech Jemaa el-Fnaa, Cairo Khan el-Khalili,
   Istanbul Sultanahmet, Jaipur.
3. **Tea House Invitation (Chapter T).** Friendly art-student
   invites you to a side-street tea ceremony with no posted prices.
   Beijing, Shanghai, Xi'an, Hangzhou.
4. **Drink Spiking & Bar Bill Trap (Chapter D).** Drugged drink
   leads to four-figure bill or compliance. Bangkok Sukhumvit,
   Bogotá Zona Rosa, Tokyo Kabukicho, Hanoi Bui Vien.
5. **Aggressive Street Vendor (Chapter A).** Trinkets pressed into
   hands. Anywhere with foot-traffic tourist density.
6. **Carpet Shop Pressure Sale (Chapter C).** Door closes, tea
   ceremony begins. Istanbul, Marrakech, Cappadocia.
7. **Express Kidnapping Taxi (Chapter E).** A hailed cab and a
   forced ATM run. Mexico City Roma Norte, Bogotá, Buenos Aires,
   São Paulo. Critical for solo female travelers in Latin America
   after dark.
8. **Distraction-Theft Pickpocket Team (Chapter D).** The
   bird-poop or mustard splat that disguises a wallet lift. Madrid
   Sol, Rome Termini, Barcelona Plaza Catalunya.
9. **Counterfeit Currency Returns (Chapter C).** Fake bills
   substituted during change, particularly relevant when paying
   cash. Buenos Aires, Mexico City.
10. **Phone Snatch Motorcycle (Chapter P).** Drive-by hand-grab
    from a passing motorbike. São Paulo, Saigon, Naples,
    Cartagena. Sidewalk-side rule and front-pocket phone storage.

The pattern: most of these run on social-engineering plus speed.
The defense is consistent across all ten: do not extend your hand
to a stranger; do not enter a venue without posted prices; trust
the front-desk safe over the in-room safe; phone in deep front
pocket on busy streets after dark.

A separate note: the express-kidnapping chapter is the single
highest-financial-impact scam targeting solo female travelers
documented in the 2026 archive. Read that chapter twice if your
itinerary includes a Latin American city after dark.

## For the over-sixty traveler

You have time and budget that you did not have when you were
younger, which is exactly the demographic several of the scams in
this book optimize for. The nine chapters:

1. **The Gold Ring Trick (Chapter G).** The "found" gold ring
   becomes a forced gift. Paris dominant. Twelve cities.
2. **Closed-Attraction Redirect (Chapter C).** "The site is closed
   today, but I know a better one." Delhi, Bangkok, Cairo, Cusco.
3. **Carpet Shop Pressure Sale (Chapter C).** Closed-door carpet
   ceremony with hotel-referral commission. Istanbul, Marrakech,
   Cappadocia.
4. **Cover Charge (Coperto / Otoshi / Couvert) (Chapter C).** The
   small line at the bottom of the bill that was not on the menu.
   Rome Trastevere, Tokyo Kabukicho, Athens Plaka.
5. **Restaurant Bill Padding (Chapter R).** Items not ordered,
   inflated *coperto*, service-charge stacking. Italy, Greece,
   Spain, Portugal.
6. **Aggressive Street Vendor (Chapter A).** Trinkets, roses, and
   bracelets pressed on the politeness reflex.
7. **Tea House Invitation (Chapter T).** Friendly stranger,
   too-warm welcome, side-street tea house. Beijing, Shanghai,
   Xi'an, Hangzhou.
8. **ATM Currency Conversion Trap (Chapter A).** Dynamic currency
   conversion at POS, ATM, hotel checkout. Italy, Spain, Czech
   Republic, France.
9. **Beach Chair Lounger Hustle (Chapter B).** The minimum-spend
   surprise on the Mediterranean lounger. Mykonos, Italian Adriatic,
   Costa del Sol.

The pattern: most run on social grace, the politeness reflex of a
generation that does not want to be rude. The defense is the same
across all nine: walking away is not rude. Saying no is not rude.

A separate note: the express-kidnapping variant in Latin American
cities (Chapter E) and the fake drug-search police sting in
Southeast Asia (Chapter F) both target older travelers
specifically. Read those chapters in full if your itinerary
includes Mexico City, Bogotá, Bali, or Bangkok.

## For the parent traveling with kids

You travel with one or more children, which slows your reaction
time and changes the geometry of every encounter. The seven
chapters:

1. **Aggressive Street Vendor (Chapter A).** Trinkets and toys
   pressed on the children specifically.
2. **Distraction-Theft Pickpocket Team (Chapter D).** Distraction-
   team variants that exploit a parent's attention to their child.
   The mustard splat, the dropped camera, the unfolded map.
3. **Beach Chair Lounger Hustle (Chapter B).** The
   Mediterranean-beach minimum-spend that compounds across a
   family of four.
4. **Restaurant Bill Padding (Chapter R).** Items not ordered,
   forced tip, service-charge stacking. Higher per-meal capture on
   family-sized tables.
5. **Friendship Bracelet Trap (Chapter F).** Bracelets tied to
   children specifically; the parent pays to end the encounter.
6. **Three Card Monte (Chapter T).** A street game that doubles
   as a working pickpocket team. NYC Times Square, London Oxford
   Street, Paris Champs-Élysées.
7. **Closed-Attraction Redirect (Chapter C).** "The site is closed
   today, but I will take the kids to a better attraction." The
   redirect ends at a commission shop, not an attraction.

The pattern: these scams optimize for the cognitive load of
attending to children. The defense is one parent attentive to the
children at all times, the other parent attentive to the
surroundings. Switch every twenty minutes.

A separate note: in-room hotel safe theft is more acute when
traveling with children because of the additional identity
documents (children's passports) at risk. The web atlas at
tabiji.ai/scams/atlas covers hotel-room theft in detail; for the
purposes of this book, prefer the front-desk safe for documents.

## For the digital nomad

You travel for months at a time, work from cafés and coworking
spaces, book everything on your phone, and do most of your
financial life over Wi-Fi. The nine chapters:

1. **QR Code Quishing (Chapter Q).** Codes on parking meters,
   menus, restaurant table-tents leading to phishing sites.
2. **Airbnb Off-Platform Fraud (Chapter A).** Host moves payment
   to wire transfer; apartment does not exist or is bait-and-
   switched.
3. **Fake Booking Website (Chapter F).** Clone sites, phishing
   emails, WhatsApp fixers, fake skip-the-line ticket sellers.
4. **ATM Skimming (Chapter A).** Card details captured at
   tourist-zone ATMs.
5. **ATM Currency Conversion Trap (Chapter A).** Dynamic currency
   conversion at every POS, ATM, hotel checkout, and online
   booking.
6. **Counterfeit Currency Returns (Chapter C).** Fake bills
   substituted during change, particularly relevant when paying
   cash for cafés and short-term rentals.
7. **Currency Exchange Cambio (Chapter C).** Sub-market rates on
   street-corner cambios, including the back-room rate switch.
8. **Phone Snatch Motorcycle (Chapter P).** Drive-by hand-grab
   from a motorbike. The single highest-impact loss for the
   nomad: phone, two-factor codes, hotel reservation, boarding
   pass, all in one device.
9. **Fake Drug-Search Police Sting (Chapter F).** Backpacker
   nightlife strips in Southeast Asia where corrupt or
   counterfeit-uniformed officers run shakedowns.

The pattern: these scams optimize for the rushed, the Wi-Fi-only,
and the I-can-figure-this-out-on-the-go reader. The defense is
slowing down once on initial setup. Most of these scams have a
preventable signature in their first thirty seconds.

A separate note: most of the dangerous nomad scams now run on
messaging platforms (WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram DMs) rather
than physical encounters. The defense is the same as it was in
2010: never wire money to someone you have not met.

## For the budget backpacker

You travel cheap, on public transit, with a hostel network as
your information layer. The nine chapters:

1. **Bus and Train Station Scams (Chapter B).** "Platform changed,"
   "let me help with your bag," fake fare-checker fines. Rome
   Termini, Naples Centrale, Paris Gare du Nord.
2. **Airport Arrival Scams (Chapter A).** Unlicensed-taxi curb
   intercept, fake-tourist-information booth, meet-and-greet
   markup. Bangkok BKK, Mexico City MEX, Cancún, Bali DPS.
3. **Fake Skip-the-Line Tickets (Chapter F).** "Express" tickets
   near monuments are no faster than the official ones, and many
   are blank PDFs. Vatican, Sagrada Familia, Louvre, Hagia Sophia.
4. **Closed-Attraction Redirect (Chapter C).** "The site is closed
   today" leads to a commission shop on the way to a fictional
   alternative. Delhi, Bangkok, Agra, Angkor.
5. **Fake Drug-Search Police Sting (Chapter F).** Real or
   counterfeit-uniformed officers run a shakedown. Bali Kuta,
   Hanoi, Bangkok Khao San, Sharm el-Sheikh.
6. **Drink Spiking & Bar Bill Trap (Chapter D).** Cheap-entry bar
   bait, no posted menu, four-figure exit bill. Khao San,
   Kabukicho, Hanoi Bui Vien.
7. **Restaurant Bill Padding (Chapter R).** Items not ordered,
   swapped menu prices, service-charge stacking. Khao San, Las
   Ramblas, Sicilian seasides.
8. **Three Card Monte (Chapter T).** Street game with a working
   pickpocket team in the surrounding crowd. Times Square, Oxford
   Street, Las Ramblas.
9. **Phone Snatch Motorcycle (Chapter P).** The drive-by
   hand-grab. Naples Spaccanapoli, Saigon Bui Vien, Bogotá Carrera 7.

The pattern: these scams optimize for low-budget travelers who
cannot afford to lose what is taken but who travel through the
specific transit zones (bus stations, scooter rental shops, beach
hostels) where these scams concentrate. The defense is procedural,
slow, and unfortunately requires deviating from the speed-and-
vibes ethos that backpacking optimizes for.

A separate note: scooter-rental phantom-damage charges, hotel-room
theft, and luggage theft on long-haul trains are documented in
the broader web atlas at tabiji.ai/scams/atlas; they are not in
this book but are worth knowing if Bali, Phuket, Cusco, La Paz,
or Hoi An is on your itinerary.

## How to use this appendix

Find the profile closest to yours. Read those chapters in full.
The chapters not on your list are still worth skimming, because
no profile fits perfectly and adjacent profiles overlap. But the
chapters on your list are the ones to know cold before you fly.

If your trip is to a single country, layer this appendix with the
country book for that destination. The country book lists which
specific chapters apply to each city you'll visit. The appendix
here narrows by who you are. The intersection is the shortest
practical reading list.

The web atlas at tabiji.ai/scams/atlas covers additional scam
types beyond the thirty in this book (rental-car phantom damage,
luggage theft on trains, taxi meter manipulation, photo-fee
shakedowns, free walking tour tip extortion, and others) and is
updated continuously. If a scam appears in your traveler-type
list above but not in the table of contents, that is because the
book covers thirty high-impact scams; the broader catalog lives
on the web.
