## The lobby

Just past ten on a Thursday night, Harry and his wife walked back
to their Naama Bay hotel in Sharm el-Sheikh from a beach
restaurant on the Red Sea. Dinner had been at a small Italian
place run by a couple from Bologna: two glasses of Beaujolais
each, home by ten.

As they crossed the hotel lobby toward the elevator, two Egyptian
men in dark blue uniforms with TOURIST POLICE insignia stepped
forward. The taller one held up a credential card: a counterfeit-
looking plastic ID with a Cairo address. No Ministry of Interior
seal that Harry recognized.

He spoke in clipped English.

*"Sir, ma'am, hotel guest reported noise complaint about you.
Please come with us to outside area, bag check, very fast."*

The shorter "officer" stood two meters back with his hand on a
holstered baton. The lobby attendant at the desk did not look up.

Harry's wife reached for the cross-body bag she had been carrying
all evening. Harry stopped her.

*"I'm going to phone our embassy first. We're not consenting to a
search without consular access."*

He took out his phone. He dialed the US Embassy Cairo emergency
line, which he had saved in his contacts before the trip:
+20-2-2797-3300. The duty officer picked up after thirty-five
seconds.

The taller "officer" said tightly, *"Sir, embassy not necessary,
just bag check, very simple."*

Harry kept the phone to his ear.

## The trick

The fake drug-search police sting is the catastrophic-tier end of
the Authority Costume family. It runs on two patterns at once. The
uniform (real or counterfeit) creates Authority Costume. The demand
to move to a private location creates the Captive-Position Lever.
Once the traveler is in a side alley, a hotel back office, or a
parked vehicle with the "officer" and an accomplice, the encounter
moves outside the witness window where most documented shakedowns
extract cash.

The mechanic varies between countries. Some documented variants
involve corrupt officers in real uniforms operating shakedown
sidelines; others involve fake-uniformed impersonators. Both
versions converge on a similar play: bag search, planted substance
or contraband-redefinition (a claim that legal items such as
vitamins or prescription medication are controlled), and a cash
demand framed as a way to avoid laboratory testing or arrest.

The defense is built around requesting consular access and staying
visible. Each rule below reduces the surface area of the encounter
without requiring you to physically resist anyone in uniform.

The embassy-call-first rule: at the first accusation, calmly
request to phone your home-country embassy or consulate before
consenting to a search or signing anything. Do not pay cash. Do
not sign any document in a language you cannot read; ask for a
translator or written translation. Real police processes vary by
country, but consular contact is your right under the Vienna
Convention on Consular Relations and is the universal de-escalator
that most documented variants disengage from. Embassy emergency
lines are listed in your country's official traveler resources;
save them in your phone before you fly.

The public-view rule: do not move to a side location, alley,
private office, or parked vehicle at an officer's instruction. Ask
that any search happen in public view, in lit space, with
bystanders or CCTV present. Real police investigations are usually
conducted in public at the point of contact or at official
stations through formal process. Shakedown attempts depend on a
private location because the cash extraction cannot happen on
camera.

If a search is unavoidable: do not physically resist a uniformed
officer. Comply with the bag search itself. Do not pay cash. Keep
asking for consular access and a translator. Document the
officer's name, badge number, vehicle, and uniform details in your
phone the moment you are released. Cash demands paid on the spot
to anyone in uniform are illegal in both the destination country
and home-country jurisdictions; pursuit through embassy and police
channels after the fact is the right recovery path.

## The five mechanics

**Bali Kuta corrupt-cop.** The most-dangerous documented variant.
Some Indonesian POLRI officers in real uniforms have been reported
operating shakedown sidelines on Kuta, Seminyak, and Canggu
nightlife strips. The pattern: approach tourists exiting nightclubs
late at night, demand a bag search, plant a small amount of
cannabis or MDMA, demand cash (typically 500–3,000 USD) framed as
a way to avoid arrest. Indonesian narcotics law carries
multi-year prison sentences for any quantity of controlled
substance, which is the leverage. The US, UK, Canadian, and
Australian embassies in Jakarta have all issued advisories about
shakedown attempts in Bali nightlife zones. Defense: embassy-call-
first rule. US Embassy Jakarta +62-21-5083-1000.

**Hanoi Old Quarter bag search.** Vietnamese variant. Cong An
officers in green uniforms approach tourists carrying backpacks
near Hoan Kiem Lake and HCMC Pham Ngu Lao backpacker district.
Demand bag search for narcotics; plant a small substance or claim
a legal item (vitamin pills, prescription medication) is
contraband. Cash demand 200–1,500 USD. Defense: phone US Embassy
Hanoi +84-24-3850-5000. Always carry prescription medication in
labeled bottles with a copy of the prescription.

**Sihanoukville fake-uniform.** Cambodian variant, primarily
fake-uniformed crews. Sihanoukville Serendipity and Otres Beach
strips host crews in counterfeit Cambodian National Police
uniforms (slightly wrong color, "Tourist Police Sihanoukville"
insignia which is not a real Cambodian agency). Approach tourists
at beach bars; demand bag search; plant marijuana or MDMA. Cash
demand 200–1,000 USD. Real Cambodia National Police uniforms have
khaki-and-blue insignia with a Ministry of Interior seal. Defense:
phone US Embassy Phnom Penh +855-23-728-000.

**Bangkok Khao San urine test.** Thai variant. Crews in fake or
real Royal Thai Police uniforms approach tourists exiting clubs on
Khao San Road or Phuket Patong. Demand a urine drug test for
marijuana or methamphetamine; produce a counterfeit positive-test
strip; demand 300–2,000 USD cash bribe. Thailand decriminalized
marijuana in 2022 but methamphetamine remains a Class 1 controlled
substance; the crew leverages the regulatory confusion. Real Thai
police drug-testing procedure runs at police stations through
formal process, not via sidewalk strips. Defense: phone US Embassy
Bangkok +66-2-205-4000.

**Sharm el-Sheikh Tourist Police hotel approach.** Egyptian
variant. Counterfeit-uniformed crews approach tourists at hotel
lobbies or pool areas in Sharm el-Sheikh and Hurghada. Claim a
noise complaint or other minor infraction; produce a counterfeit
Tourist Police badge; demand a bag search; plant a small substance
or claim a legal item is contraband. Cash demand 200–1,500 USD.
Real Egyptian Tourist Police carry credential cards with the
Ministry of Interior seal. Defense: phone US Embassy Cairo
+20-2-2797-3300.

## Where it runs (5 countries, Indonesia dominant for severity)

The fake drug-search police sting concentrates in Southeast Asian
tourist destinations and Egyptian Red Sea resort strips. Indonesia
runs the highest-stakes variant because of Article 112's prison-
sentence risk. Vietnam, Cambodia, Thailand, and Egypt run lower-
sentence variants but with similar architecture.

The pattern is regional. African and Middle Eastern variants
outside Egypt are uncommon. Latin American police shakedowns follow
a different mechanic (express kidnapping, not drug planting).
European police operate under stronger consular-oversight regimes,
which suppresses the variant.

Within the five countries, the pattern concentrates at nightlife
strips, backpacker districts, and resort hotel lobbies where
tourist-target demographics intersect with weak enforcement of
bribe-extraction inside the police force itself.

## The five red flags

Any one of these in a police-uniform encounter abroad: phone the
embassy before consenting to anything.

- **A request to move to a side alley, private office, hotel back
  room, or parked vehicle for the search.** Real police searches
  happen in public view at the point of contact, or at official
  stations through official process.
- **A demand for cash payment to "avoid arrest" or "avoid
  laboratory testing."** Real police investigations go through
  procedure. Cash demands at the point of accusation are
  shakedowns by definition.
- **A counterfeit-looking plastic credential card with no national
  ministry seal, no logo, or a generic "Tourist Police" insignia
  that does not match the country's real agency structure.**
- **A refusal to allow phone contact with your embassy.** Real
  police accept embassy calls. Fake police retreat at the request.
- **A "drug test" administered on the street with a foil-packaged
  test strip.** Real drug tests happen at police stations or
  hospitals, never on a sidewalk.

## The phrases that shut it down

Stated calmly, with the phone visible, before any bag is opened:

| Language | Phrase | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| English | "I am phoning my embassy now. I do not consent to a search without consular access." |  |
| Indonesian | *Saya akan menelepon kedutaan saya dulu.* | I will phone my embassy first. |
| Vietnamese | *Tôi sẽ gọi cho đại sứ quán của tôi trước.* | I will phone my embassy first. |
| Khmer | *Khnhom nung tourasap stantout muny.* | I will phone my embassy first. |
| Thai | *Phŏm/dichăn jà thoh sàthăn-thût gòn.* | I will phone my embassy first. |
| Arabic (Egypt) | *Hattasel bil-safara bita'i awwilan.* | I will phone my embassy first. |

Keep the call audible (speakerphone is one option) so the officer
can hear that consular contact is engaged. Surrender the device
only if it is safe to do so; do not give up your only
communication line while under threat. Most documented
impersonator variants disengage within a minute of the embassy
call connecting; corrupt-real-officer variants move to formal
process, which is the right outcome.

## If this happens to you

You consented to the bag search. The "officer" produced a small
clear baggie and said it was yours. He demanded the equivalent of
fifteen hundred US dollars to "avoid the lab test and arrest."

Even at this point, do not pay. The cash payment is the variant's
goal; refusal extends the timeline and brings consular oversight.

Within five minutes: phone your embassy on the visible phone
with the officer present. Keep the call audible — speakerphone
is one option — so the officer can hear that consular contact is
engaged; pass the device over only if it is safe to do so. The
duty officer will either confirm the variant (counterfeit
uniform, no real agency) and instruct the impersonator to leave,
or instruct you on the
next step for a real-corrupt-officer encounter.

Within thirty minutes of release: photograph the location, the
officer's face if you can do so safely, the vehicle license plate,
the uniform details, and any business card or "ticket" left
behind. Save to cloud immediately so the photos persist if the
phone is later seized.

Within one hour: file a report at the official local tourist police
station. Real Tourist Police stations exist in every country in the
family: Indonesia POLRI district stations, Vietnam Cong An district
stations, Cambodia National Police, Royal Thai Tourist Police 1155,
Egyptian Tourism Police 126. The official station report is
admissible to your home embassy and to your travel insurance.

Within twenty-four hours: contact your embassy's consular office to
file a citizens-services report. The US, UK, Canadian, and
Australian consular offices in Jakarta, Hanoi, Phnom Penh, Bangkok,
and Cairo all process the report and forward to home-country
authorities.

For cash already paid: cash is rarely recoverable. Some
premium-tier travel insurance policies cover documented shakedown
losses with a tourist-police report number; check your policy's
"emergency cash" or "criminal-incident cash-loss" clause before
the trip. Processing typically takes thirty to ninety days when
covered.

Harry's call to US Embassy Cairo lasted forty seconds. The duty
officer asked for the officer's badge number, then asked Harry to
hand over the phone. The taller "officer" listened for ten seconds.
He said something in Arabic to his partner. Both walked briskly out
of the lobby and disappeared into the Naama Bay night. Harry and
his wife went up to their room. The next morning he filed a report
at the Egyptian Tourism Police office on the Sharm corniche, and he
kept the photo of the counterfeit badge on his phone. He did not
see the men again. He did not change hotels.
