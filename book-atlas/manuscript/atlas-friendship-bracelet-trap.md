## The string

You walk up the Rue Foyatier stairs from Abbesses metro toward the
Sacré-Cœur basilica at six on a Saturday evening. The basilica's
white dome glows in the early evening light above you. The stairs
are crowded with tourists and locals returning from Pigalle.

Halfway up, two men in their twenties step out from a small
landing, smiling, holding what look like colored strings braided
into thin bracelets.

The first one calls out.

*"Madame, un cadeau pour la dame, juste un petit bracelet, attends
une seconde, un cadeau, c'est gratuit, vraiment, viens."* (*Ma'am,
a gift for the lady, just a little bracelet, wait a second, a gift,
it's free, really, come.*)

He is already extending a string toward your wife's hand. She pulls
back; he steps closer. The second man has come up behind you,
casually blocking the path back down the stairs.

The first man's hand has the string in a loop. He moves toward your
wife's wrist with practiced speed. She raises both hands to chest
height. He changes target, reaches for yours. Your hands are still
at your sides. The string loops over your wrist and his hand pulls,
knotting it tight in two seconds.

*"Voilà, c'est pour toi mon ami, c'est un bracelet d'amitié, juste
vingt euros pour aider, oui?"* (*There, it's for you my friend,
it's a friendship bracelet, just twenty euros to help, yes?*)

You shake your head. You step around the second man and walk up
the stairs without stopping. The first calls after you. *"Vingt
euros, c'est rien, pour les enfants, pour la famille."* You keep
walking. He does not follow. At the top of the stairs you stop;
the string can be cut off later at the hotel or with help from a
café staffer. It is woven cotton thread, the kind that costs
about thirty cents per ten meters at a wholesale shop.

## The trick

The friendship bracelet trap runs on two patterns at once.
Manufactured Reciprocity does the opening: the string is offered as
a gift, *un cadeau, gratuit*, no money, no obligation. Captive-
Position Lever does the closing: once the string is on your wrist,
the request becomes a refusal of generosity. The second crew member
blocking the path back creates the physical lever. The whole
sequence runs in under a minute.

The mechanic depends on access to your wrist or fingers for the
two seconds it takes to wrap. The defense is correspondingly
physical.

The hand-in-pocket reflex: the moment any stranger approaches with
something in their hand (a string, a coin, a flower, a "free
gift"), both your hands go into your front pockets or onto your
bag straps. Pocketed hands cannot be wrapped. The crew has
practiced the wrist-grab on hundreds of tourists; you do not need
a counter-grab, you need to remove the wrist from access.

The defense in depth is the no-extension rule. Do not extend your
hand for street magic, palmistry demonstrations, "lucky symbols,"
or any "free gift" offered by a stranger in a tourist plaza or on
a tourist staircase. The extension is the access. Refuse the
extension and the variant cannot start.

If the string is already on: do not pay. The string is woven cotton
worth about thirty cents. Walk through the request, around any
person blocking your path, and cut it off when you are clear of
the encounter. Twenty euros is the demand. Zero is the value of
the string. You owe nothing.

## The four mechanics

**Tie-first wrap.** The most common variant. The "gift" is offered
verbally as the crew member's hand simultaneously moves to your
wrist with a pre-tied loop. The string is on before the verbal
exchange completes. Most reported at Paris Sacré-Cœur stairs and
Champs-Élysées, Rome Spanish Steps and Trevi approaches, Barcelona
Plaza Catalunya and Las Ramblas, Florence Piazza della Signoria.

**Coin-trick lure.** A "street magician" approaches with a coin
trick or a sleight-of-hand demonstration. The trick requires you
to extend your hand to "hold" the coin. Once the hand is extended,
the string goes on instead of the coin. Most reported at Marrakech
Jemaa el-Fnaa, Cairo Khan el-Khalili, Istanbul Sultanahmet.

**Friendly-greeting slip.** A man approaches with a friendly *hello,
my friend, where are you from* and extends a hand for a handshake.
The handshake covers the wrist long enough to slip a string on.
Most reported at Barcelona Las Ramblas, Lisbon Alfama, Athens
Plaka.

**Group-surround.** Three to four crew members converge on a solo
target or a couple on a narrow stair, alley, or plaza corner. Two
block the exit while one wraps. Most reported at Paris Sacré-Cœur
Rue Foyatier stairs, Rome Spanish Steps, Florence Ponte Vecchio
approaches.

## Where it runs (14 countries, France dominant)

The friendship bracelet trap concentrates in Western European and
North African tourist plazas with high foot traffic and narrow
pedestrian geometry (stairs, alleys, plaza corners). France
accounts for the highest documented volume, driven by Sacré-Cœur
and the Champ de Mars below the Eiffel Tower. Italy, Spain,
Morocco, Egypt, Greece, and Portugal carry the bulk of the
remainder. Smaller-volume variants run in the USA (Times Square),
Mexico (Mexico City Centro Histórico), Argentina (Buenos Aires
Microcentro), Brazil (Rio Copacabana), and a few Asian capitals
(Bangkok Khao San).

The pattern travels with foot-traffic geometry. Cities with broad
pedestrian boulevards (most of Northern Europe, Tokyo, Singapore)
produce far less of it. Cities with narrow stairs, sloping plazas,
and choke points (Paris Sacré-Cœur, Rome Spanish Steps, Lisbon
Alfama) produce the most.

## The five red flags

Any one of these in the first ten seconds of a stair, alley, or
plaza approach: hands in pockets, walk through the encounter
without breaking stride.

- **A stranger holding a string, bracelet, ribbon, or "lucky charm"
  toward you.** The hand with the object is the access.
- **A *cadeau, gratuit, free gift* opening line.** Real gifts in a
  tourist plaza do not exist. The framing is the bait.
- **A second person standing behind you or to the side at the
  exact moment of the approach.** This is the group-surround
  signature.
- **A street-magic, palmistry, or coin demonstration that requires
  you to extend your hand.** The extension is the wrap window.
- **A friendly *hello, my friend, where are you from* that ends
  with an extended handshake.** The handshake is the access.

If a string is already on your wrist when you read this: do not
pay. Walk away. Cut it off when you are clear. The string is
worthless thread.

## If this happens to you

You paid five, ten, or twenty euros to make the encounter end. The
crew has moved on to the next tourist on the next stair. Your wrist
has a thirty-cent piece of cotton thread on it.

The cash is not recoverable. The bracelet hustle is high-volume,
low-stakes, and the cash is fungible. The actionable response is
preventive for the next encounter on the same trip and the next
trip.

For pickpocket loss alongside the bracelet: roughly half of
documented cases include a wallet-lift accomplice working your back
pocket while your hands and eyes are on the bracelet. Check your
front pocket and daypack zipper as soon as you are clear. If
something is missing, follow the standard pickpocket-recovery
sequence: card freeze within five minutes, police report within an
hour, embassy contact for any passport loss within twenty-four
hours.

For pressure that escalated into physical contact (grabbing,
blocking exit, threatening tone): file a report with the local
police line. Paris Police 17, Rome Polizia 113, Barcelona Mossos
112, Marrakech Tourist Police 19, Istanbul Tourist Police 155. The
report goes into the city register; the bracelet crews work known
beats and periodic enforcement sweeps follow accumulated complaints.

For travel-insurance claims: bracelet-only losses are below the
deductible threshold of most policies. Bundled losses (bracelet
plus pickpocket) qualify under the standard cash-loss category with
a police report number.
