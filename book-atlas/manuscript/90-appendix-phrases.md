# Appendix A: The eleven-language exit-phrase index

Take a screenshot of the language pages relevant to your trip. The
phrases that follow have been chosen because they shut down the
specific patterns documented in Part I. They are not introductions to
the language. They are surgical tools.

For each language, three categories:

- **Refusal:** the polite no, said calmly while walking away.
- **Escalation:** the firmer no, said when the polite no does not
  work.
- **Distress:** the call for help, including the universal de-
  escalator (the word for *police* in any language).

A pronunciation guide follows each phrase. We use approximate phonetic
spellings for English speakers, not the IPA. The goal is to be
understood, not to be linguistically correct.

The phrase index is also available as a screenshot-friendly card in
the inside-cover material of the paperback edition, and as a free
printable PDF on the book's webpage at tabiji.ai/scams/atlas.

## Japanese

For: Japan only. Different scams from most of the world (Roppongi
bar traps, Kabukicho touts, fake monks at temples) but the same
defensive phrases work.

Japanese has three scripts. We use the most accessible (Romaji,
phonetic Latin) plus the polite forms appropriate for a tourist.

| Situation | Phrase | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polite refusal | *Iie, kekkō desu.* | ee-eh, KEK-koh dess | No, that's fine / No thank you. |
| I don't want | *Irimasen.* | ee-ree-mah-SEN | I don't need it. |
| I want a receipt | *Ryōshūsho o kudasai.* | ryoh-SHOO-shoh oh koo-dah-SAI | A receipt please. |
| Where is the police box? | *Koban wa doko desu ka?* | KOH-ban wah DOH-koh dess kah | Where is the koban? |
| Help! | *Tasukete!* | tah-SOO-keh-teh | Help! |
| Police | *Keisatsu* | KAY-saht-soo | Police |

A note on Japanese: every neighborhood has a *koban* (small police
box) with at least one officer on duty, often English-speaking in
tourist zones. Walking to one is the de-escalation move for almost
any situation in Japan.

| Situation | Phrase (Simplified) | Pinyin | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polite refusal | 不用，谢谢 | bù yòng, xièxie | No need, thank you. |
| I don't want it | 我不要 | wǒ bù yào | I don't want. |

the amount to tax authorities at the price they charge you.

## How to use these phrases

The phrase that ends the most encounters is the polite refusal said
once, calmly, while continuing to walk. Do not stop walking. Do not
make eye contact. Do not apologize.

If the polite refusal does not work, the escalation phrase said firmly
shifts the dynamic. Do not raise your voice. Firmness is signaled by
slowing the cadence, not by volume.

If the escalation phrase does not work, the word *police* in any
language is the universal de-escalator. Said at conversational volume.
The scammer disengages within two seconds in almost every case
documented in this book. Even saying it in English in a non-English-
speaking country tends to work, because the word for police is similar
across most European languages and is widely recognized in travel zones
worldwide.

In the rare case that *police* does not work, walk to the nearest
uniformed officer, hotel front desk, or restaurant with staff. Do not
go to a quieter location. Public spaces are your friend.

The catastrophic cases (express kidnapping, drink spiking, fake
drug-search police sting) are not addressed by phrases. Refer to
Appendix B, *The post-scam recovery playbook*, for those.
