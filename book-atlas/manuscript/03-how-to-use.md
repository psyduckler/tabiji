# How to use this book

This book has two readers in mind. The first is the reader who
opens it the week before a trip, takes screenshots of the phrase
index and the emergency contacts, lands without incident, and never
looks at the book again. The second is the reader who opens it on
the third night of the trip, in a hotel room, wallet gone, and
reads the recovery playbook before placing a single phone call.
Both reads are good outcomes. The first read is the prep. The
second read is the rescue. The book is written for both at the
same time.

Most reference books are read by jumping in at the index. This
one rewards reading in order, at least the first time.

## Read Part I first, all the way through

The Seven Universal Patterns. They are short. The whole part runs
to about twenty-five pages. If you read nothing else in this book
before your trip, read this. Every chapter that follows is a worked
example of one of those seven patterns. You will recognize the
pattern in real life before you recognize the specific scam you are
reading about, and that is more useful than memorizing thirty
scripts.

## Then skim the atlas

Part II is thirty chapters in alphabetical order. The first read
is a skim. Look at the chapter headers, scan the scene-opens, glance at
the red flags. Two hours, cover to cover, gives you a working map of
the scam landscape worldwide.

The deep read comes later. Pick the chapters that match where you
are going (the country emergency-contacts list in Appendix C will
help you match destinations against local police, embassy, and
tourist-line numbers) or the kind of traveler you are (the
by-traveler-type index in Appendix D). Those are the chapters worth
your full attention before the trip.

If your trip is tomorrow and you have time for a smaller subset,
read the catastrophic-tier chapters first. Appendix E sorts the
thirty chapters by severity (catastrophic six, frequent seventeen,
worth-knowing seven) and gives you a reading order calibrated to
impact rather than alphabet.

## Take three screenshots before you fly

The phrase index in Appendix A is what you actually want on your phone
in-country. Take a screenshot. Same for the emergency contacts in
Appendix C and the recovery playbook in Appendix B. If you have a
problem in the moment, you do not want to be flipping pages in a
paperback in the back of a taxi in a language you do not speak. You want
the screenshot on your home screen, ready in two thumb taps.

## Read your destination volume next

This book is the field guide. The country books in the Travel Safety
Series are the destination guides. They go deeper on the local
neighborhoods, the local addresses, the local language phrases, the
local police hotlines. Each chapter in this book ends with the country
volumes that cover its scam. After you read this book, pick up the one
that matches your trip.

## Re-read after each trip

Reading this book before a trip teaches you the patterns. Reading it
after a trip teaches you which patterns you almost fell for and which
ones you still have not seen. The second read is shorter, more
practical, and tends to convert "interesting" into "memorized."

If a scam in this book happened to you, write to us:
editorial@tabiji.ai. Your report goes into next year's edition.

## A note on chapter ordering

Thirty chapters in alphabetical order is a deliberate choice that
will sometimes feel arbitrary. Aggressive Street Vendor sits next to
Airbnb Off-Platform Fraud, which has nothing to do with it. Tea House
Invitation sits next to Three Card Monte, which has everything to do
with it. We considered ordering by category (transport, distraction,
overcharging) and we considered ordering by severity (catastrophic to
minor). Both create the same problem: a reader looking up "carpet
shop pressure sale" wants to find it under C, not under "vendor" or
"moderate severity." Alphabetical wins on lookup speed. The thematic
threads run through Part I, and the severity index in Appendix E
gives a calibrated reading order if your trip is tomorrow. The
by-traveler-type index in Appendix D groups the chapters into the
reading lists for the solo female traveler, the over-sixty
traveler, the digital-nomad traveler, and the family traveler.

## You will not need most of it

Most of the readers of this book will return home from their trips
having met none of the thirty scams in here. That is the point. The
five minutes you spend on the chapter that turns out to apply is the
five minutes that pays for the book. The twenty-eight chapters that
did not apply are the proof that you traveled smart, and that the
scammers
who did try to pick you out of the crowd looked at you and decided you
were going to be too much work.
