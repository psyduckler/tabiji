The Whitsundays are famous for Whitehaven Beach and the outer Great Barrier Reef — and infamous for a cluster of overcrowded day-cruise operators with long TripAdvisor "scam alert" review histories. Whitehaven Xpress, Cruise Whitsundays, and Spirit of Whitsundays have all accumulated reviews using the word "scam" (e.g. "Whitehaven Xpress — Airlie Beach: 'If you like to be scammed, this is the cruise to do. Firstly, the description says 30 pax max, but there were 60–70 pax'"). The second exposure is the bareboat sailing charter damage deposit — at Abel Point Marina and Hamilton Island Marina, unscrupulous operators claim hull or engine damage at return that was never the renter's responsibility, pocketing $1,500–$2,500 deposits via credit-card pre-authorizations.

The risk zones are the day-cruise booking booths at Airlie Beach and Hamilton Island, the bareboat charter pickup desks at Abel Point Marina, the Facebook "sailing package + hostel" WHV market, and the Daydream Island resort aggregator-booking pipeline. The safe default: book smaller sailing boats (Derwent Hunter, Adventurer, Siska) with capacity caps of 15–30 guests, verified by TripAdvisor reviews from the past six months; film any bareboat charter inside and out at pickup and return; pay deposits only on credit cards (not debit); and book hostels and resorts only via HostelWorld, Booking.com, or direct with the resort.
