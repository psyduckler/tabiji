# Appendix B: Post-Scam Recovery Playbook {-}

If something in this book has happened to you, this appendix is where you start. The actions below are ordered by urgency, not by severity. Even if you are not sure yet how serious the scam was, working through the first two timelines will put you in the best possible position to recover your money, your evidence, and your peace of mind.

## In the first fifteen minutes

Leave the venue. If you are physically inside a place where you were scammed, do not argue, do not try to negotiate, and do not pay with a second form of payment. The single most important thing is to get yourself out of the environment and somewhere quiet.

Do not sign anything, including receipts. A signed credit-card slip is much harder to dispute than one you refused to sign. If staff are blocking your exit, this is a crime in Australia (coercion or unlawful detention), and the correct response is to dial **000** and stay on the line. Australian emergency operators respond in English everywhere in the country and will dispatch police to the venue if needed.

Screenshot everything you can, as discreetly as possible: the bill, the venue's ABN number (often on the receipt or displayed at the register), the street outside, the business licence, the card terminal, and the face of the person who served you if you can do it without escalating. The information you capture in these fifteen minutes is the evidence you will use for weeks afterward.

If your bag or wallet was just stolen at a beach or in a CBD crowd, walk directly to the nearest police station or Surf Life Saving Club (at every major beach) and ask them to call police. Activate Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) on a companion's phone if you were separated from yours. Do not confront suspected thieves yourself — Australian police prefer to intercept them with the device location rather than risk a street altercation.

## Within the first hour

Walk or drive to the nearest police station, or file online. Most Australian states offer online crime reporting for low-severity property theft and fraud — usually faster than waiting in person:

- **NSW:** Police Assistance Line on 131 444 or file online at [police.nsw.gov.au/contact_us/report_a_crime](https://www.police.nsw.gov.au/contact_us/report_a_crime)
- **Victoria:** file online at [police.vic.gov.au/report-a-crime](https://www.police.vic.gov.au/report-a-crime)
- **Queensland:** online reporting at [police.qld.gov.au/online-reporting](https://www.police.qld.gov.au/online-reporting)
- **WA:** PoliceAssistanceLine 131 444 or file online at [police.wa.gov.au/Contact-Us](https://www.police.wa.gov.au/Contact-Us)
- **SA:** 131 444 or online at [police.sa.gov.au/your-safety/report-a-crime](https://www.police.sa.gov.au/your-safety/report-a-crime)
- **Tasmania:** 131 444 or [police.tas.gov.au/reports/](https://www.police.tas.gov.au/reports/)
- **NT:** [pfes.nt.gov.au/police/online-reporting-services](https://pfes.nt.gov.au/police/online-reporting-services)
- **ACT:** [police.act.gov.au/crime-and-safety/report-crime](https://www.police.act.gov.au/crime-and-safety/report-crime)

Say, in plain English: "I was scammed — I'd like to file a report." Ask for the incident reference number — you will need it for the card chargeback, the travel-insurance claim, and the ScamWatch report. You do not need perfect or formal language; plain English is fine.

Freeze the card that was used. International emergency lines for the major card issuers are listed in Appendix C. These numbers work from anywhere in Australia and route you to an English-speaking representative within one or two minutes. Have your card number (or at least the last four digits) and the approximate transaction amount ready before you call. If the scam was a restaurant overcharge, a taxi rip-off, or a bogus tour charge, also call your bank's fraud line directly — the number on the back of your card — because fraud desks sometimes freeze faster than the Visa or Mastercard emergency lines.

## Within the first twenty-four hours

File a chargeback with your card issuer. Use the police incident reference number as documentation. Chargebacks initiated within twenty-four hours of the transaction have a significantly higher success rate than those filed a week later. Australian consumer law and the Visa/Mastercard/Amex card-network rules give strong chargeback rights for unauthorized or materially misrepresented charges — one reason we recommend credit over debit cards for every Australian trip.

Report to ScamWatch at **[scamwatch.gov.au/report-a-scam](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/report-a-scam)**. ScamWatch is the Australian government's central scam-reporting service operated by the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC). It does not recover individual losses, but reports are aggregated into enforcement priorities that feed into ACCC investigations. For SIXT rental phantom damage, Gold Coast timeshare traps, and fake booking-site fraud specifically, a ScamWatch report feeds a live caseload.

If your passport was photographed, held, or taken by anyone involved, notify your embassy or consulate immediately. Passport data tied to a scam can show up in fraud attempts weeks later. US citizens can start a replacement report through the US Consulate nearest to your location (Sydney, Melbourne, or Perth; Canberra coordinates some services). Embassy contact details for the major source countries are in Appendix C.

Email your travel-insurance provider with a summary of what happened, timestamps, the police reference number, and photographs of the bill. Even if you are not sure whether the loss is covered, opening the claim early protects your position.

If the scam was an off-platform rental or a wire-fraud hotel booking, open the dispute on the booking platform (Airbnb, Booking.com, Stayz) as well as with your card issuer. Platforms can sometimes recover deposits the card company cannot.

## Within the first week

File a formal complaint with your state's consumer affairs body:

- **NSW Fair Trading:** [fairtrading.nsw.gov.au](https://www.fairtrading.nsw.gov.au)
- **Consumer Affairs Victoria:** [consumer.vic.gov.au](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au)
- **Queensland Office of Fair Trading:** [qld.gov.au/law/fair-trading](https://www.qld.gov.au/law/fair-trading)
- **Consumer Protection WA:** [commerce.wa.gov.au/consumer-protection](https://www.commerce.wa.gov.au/consumer-protection)
- **Consumer and Business Services SA:** [cbs.sa.gov.au](https://www.cbs.sa.gov.au)
- **Consumer Affairs and Fair Trading Tasmania:** [cbos.tas.gov.au](https://www.cbos.tas.gov.au)
- **Consumer Affairs NT:** [consumeraffairs.nt.gov.au](https://consumeraffairs.nt.gov.au)
- **Access Canberra (ACT):** [accesscanberra.act.gov.au](https://www.accesscanberra.act.gov.au)

These bodies do not typically recover individual losses directly, but they aggregate complaints that drive regulatory action. For restaurant surcharge disputes, timeshare contract issues, and rental-car damage claims, the state consumer affairs body is the correct escalation path.

For taxi overcharging specifically, file a parallel complaint with the state transport regulator (Transport NSW, Transport for Victoria, Transport and Main Roads QLD, etc.). Include the taxi licence number, the driver's ID where visible, the route taken, and a comparison to the expected metered fare (Google Maps or the Uber in-app estimate).

Submit the full travel-insurance claim with all evidence gathered so far. Include the business's name, the address, the exact transaction details, the police reference number, the ScamWatch report ID, and the chargeback reference.

Consider leaving a neutral, factual review on Google Maps or TripAdvisor. Describe what happened, the amounts involved, and the date, without emotional language or accusations. Reviews from recent victims are the single most reliable way future travelers avoid the same scam. Do not name individual staff members; describe the business by its official name and location only.

## If the scam involved drink-spiking or physical harm

Go to a hospital before you go to the police. Toxicology tests have a short window and cannot be run retroactively. At the hospital, ask specifically for a drug screen or toxicology panel. Keep the hospital report; it is evidence.

In Australia, the ambulance, fire, and police emergency number is **000** from any phone. English operators are available 24/7 everywhere in Australia. If you are inside a venue and drink-spiking is suspected, have a friend call 000 *before* you leave — sometimes aggressive bill pressure escalates once the scammer realizes you are leaving.

Your embassy can help coordinate English-speaking medical care and, if your passport was involved, coordinate a replacement. Consular registration before travel is worth doing — the US STEP program, the UK Foreign Office travel advisory, the Canadian Registration of Canadians Abroad, and Australia's own Smartraveller program all allow pre-registration that shortens the response time in an emergency.
