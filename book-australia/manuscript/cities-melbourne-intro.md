Melbourne's scam mix is dominated by financial rather than physical risk. Tullamarine's in-terminal "fake taxi" bait is sophisticated enough that the airport itself has installed warning signs — touts in high-vis vests intercept arrivals at baggage claim and walk them to unmarked sedans in the short-term carpark for rides that balloon from a $55 Uber estimate to $185 at drop-off. The city's second major exposure is the SIXT rental-car phantom damage scam, with documented r/australia invoices ranging from $1,040 to $6,500 for "damage" that was never real. A third exposure, fresh for 2024–2026, is the fake QR-code parking ticket: a yellow sticker on your windshield with a prominent QR code demanding payment within 24 hours, where scanning the code sends your card details to a phishing site.

The risk zones are Melbourne Airport T2 and T4 arrivals halls, CBD street-parking zones where QR stickers are planted, Flinders Street Station at rush hour (tram pickpocketing cluster), and the Great Ocean Road online booking pipeline where cloned sites run Google Ads above the legitimate operators. The safe default: take SkyBus ($22) or pre-booked rideshare from Tullamarine; film every panel of any rental car at pickup and drop-off; pay parking only through the PayStay or CellOPark app; and book Great Ocean Road tours through Gray Line, Go West, or Autopia Tours directly.
