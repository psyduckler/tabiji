Alice Springs is the geographic center of Australia's most persistent cultural fraud: fake Aboriginal art. r/sydney's "Indigenous Art" thread states the scale — "heaps and heaps of fake Indigenous art dealers/sellers" — and the Alice galleries on Todd Mall run the densest concentration of mislabeled pieces. A "Papunya-style" dot painting labeled as by a traditional artist from Papunya Community, priced at $680, may be a mass-produced overseas print with no connection to any Indigenous artist. The second major exposure is Uluru accommodation and experience fraud — cloned "Sounds of Silence" and "Field of Light" booking sites run Google Ads above the real Ayers Rock Resort site, and Facebook posts offer "cheap Yulara hostel alternatives" that simply do not exist (Yulara is effectively a single-operator resort village).

The risk zones are Todd Mall galleries for unprovenanced Aboriginal art, Google Ads searches for "Uluru sunset dinner" or "Field of Light booking," and Facebook backpacker groups offering Uluru accommodation. The safe default: buy Aboriginal art only from Indigenous Art Code signatories (indigenousartcode.org) — Papunya Tula Artists, Mbantua Gallery, Araluen Arts Centre, Keringke Arts; book every Uluru experience through ayersrockresort.com.au and the National Park admission through parksaustralia.gov.au; and if driving a rental onto the Mereenie Loop or Finke Desert roads, buy the supplementary unsealed-road insurance — every standard rental contract voids insurance on those surfaces.
