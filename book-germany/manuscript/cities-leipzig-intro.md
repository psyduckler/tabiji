Leipzig is Saxony's trade-fair city and one of Germany's fastest-growing tourist destinations — Bach's St. Thomas, the Gewandhaus, the Augustusplatz opera house, and the Völkerschlachtdenkmal. The scam ecosystem is smaller than Dresden's but concentrates on three corridors: the **Marktplatz and Augustusplatz** Altstadt axis, the **Leipzig Hauptbahnhof** (the largest railway terminus in Europe by floor area), and the **Nikolaikirchhof / Barfußgäßchen** restaurant and bar quarter. The Saxon Landespolizei's Leipzig district logs a seasonal spike during the Leipzig Book Fair (*Leipziger Buchmesse*, March) and the Messe-week surges.

The **Leipzig Zentrum "handshake scam"** is the signature Leipzig approach. A man in the Marktplatz or Augustusplatz strides up, shakes your hand with genuine warmth, asks where you're from, and in the handshake moment a second team member eases your wallet or phone from your pocket. The script is a variant of the Berlin clipboard pattern but uses physical contact instead of a petition. The Leipzig Polizei and *Leipziger Volkszeitung* have covered it every spring since 2021.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Leipzig Hauptbahnhof "Saxony tourist card" street sale** at the station's east exit sells laminated "Leipzig Welcome Cards" for €25 that are counterfeits of the real €14 card sold at the DB Reisezentrum — the QR code fails at the first tram reader. The **Barfußgäßchen bill-padding** at the bar strip running between Markt and Nikolaikirchhof adds "table service fees" and "German beer selection" line items that weren't announced. The **Leipziger Messe-week accommodation fraud** is a nationwide problem concentrated on Leipzig during Buchmesse (March) and Auto Mobil International (April) — fake Airbnb listings and off-platform "private apartments" that don't exist; lose €400–€1,200 per booking.

A safe default: keep handshakes brief and your other hand on your pocket; buy the Leipzig Welcome Card only at the DB Reisezentrum inside Hauptbahnhof or at **leipzig.travel**; eat on the Katharinenstraße or Reichsstraße side streets rather than the Barfußgäßchen choke point; book accommodation only through Booking.com or Expedia, especially during Messe weeks; take the S-Bahn S1 or the tram 15 from Hauptbahnhof to Augustusplatz for €2.90; and call **110** or visit the Polizeirevier Leipzig-Zentrum at Ritterstraße 26 — five minutes from Markt.
