Baden-Baden is Germany's grand-hotel spa town — the Trinkhalle, the Friedrichsbad and Caracalla-Therme thermal baths, the Lichtentaler Allee park promenade, and the Kurhaus casino (inspiration for Dostoyevsky's *The Gambler*). The scam ecosystem is small but upscale-priced, concentrated on three corridors: the **Kurhaus and Römerplatz** central plaza, the **Friedrichsbad / Caracalla-Therme** thermal-bath quarter, and the **Baden-Baden train station** at Oos (three kilometers from the city center). *Badisches Tagblatt* covers the seasonal patterns.

The **Baden-Baden casino "roulette system" gambling scam** is the signature Baden-Baden pitch. Foreign visitors at the Kurhaus are approached by English-speaking "regulars" pitching a roulette or blackjack betting system that requires an initial €500–€2,000 "cooperation buy-in" and a "split the winnings" arrangement. The "regular" disappears after the first losing session with the buy-in. The Kurhaus's own security knows the pattern and flags it on-site; the Polizei files dozens of incident reports every year from mostly-male business-traveler victims.
