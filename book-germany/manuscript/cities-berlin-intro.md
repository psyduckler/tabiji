Berlin runs Germany's oldest and most rehearsed approach-scam ecosystem, and almost all of it converges on three corridors: **Pariser Platz at the Brandenburg Gate**, **Alexanderplatz** (the Fernsehturm plaza and the World Clock), and the **Berlin Hauptbahnhof** main concourse. The *Bundespolizei* has been posting English-language pickpocket warnings at Hauptbahnhof since 2019, and the Berlin Polizei's annual *Kriminalstatistik* consistently ranks the Brandenburg Gate zone as one of Germany's top pickpocket-density squares. *Der Spiegel*, *Tagesspiegel*, *Berliner Morgenpost*, and *Deutsche Welle* cover new variants of these scripts every tourist season.

The **Brandenburger Tor clipboard-and-bracelet pickpocket** is the signature Berlin approach. A young woman with a laminated "petition for deaf children" (or a pre-tied friendship bracelet) steps into your path on Pariser Platz; you stop to listen, and a second accomplice eases your phone or wallet from your bag while you read the clipboard. Losses are typically €500–€1,500 in phone + card exposure, plus whatever cash is in the wallet. The script has been documented in *Tagesspiegel* and on r/berlin in continuous variants since at least 2014.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Berlin Hauptbahnhof "ticket-machine helper"** is a man in a fluorescent yellow vest — not a real DB employee — who offers to help you buy a regional or S-Bahn ticket; you hand him €20–€40 in cash, he taps the screen a few times, and walks away before the transaction completes. The **fake U-Bahn Kontrolleur** in a navy polo demands a €60 on-the-spot cash fine for a ticket he says is invalid; real BVG inspectors always travel in pairs, carry a visible BVG ID, and issue the fine by invoice — never cash. The **BER Airport flat-rate taxi tout** inside Terminal 1 arrivals quotes €80–€120 for a ride into Berlin-Mitte that meters out at €50–€60 (and the FEX airport train is €4.40 to Hauptbahnhof in 30 minutes, scam-immune).

A safe default: take the **FEX** or **S9** from BER into the city; use **FREE NOW** or **Uber** for city rides with the fare quoted up front; keep your phone in a front pocket and your wallet in a crossbody bag on Pariser Platz, Alexanderplatz, and inside Hauptbahnhof; never pay any "fine" in cash and always ask to see a full BVG/DB ID before engaging with any inspector; and call **110** (Polizei) or walk into the Polizei Hauptwache in Keibelstraße 35, Mitte, for any escalation. The central Berlin Bundespolizei desk is inside Berlin Hauptbahnhof — a three-minute walk from the main concourse. The Polizei Berlin English-language service line is **+49 30 4664-4664**.
