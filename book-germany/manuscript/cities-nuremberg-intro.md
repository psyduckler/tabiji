Nuremberg is Franconian old town at its most photogenic — the Kaiserburg on its sandstone crag, the Frauenkirche facade on the Marktplatz, and the **Christkindlesmarkt**, the world's most-visited German Christmas market. The scam ecosystem follows the calendar: light most of the year, dense for the four weeks of Christkindlesmarkt in late November and December. Risk concentrates on three corridors: the **Marktplatz and the Christkindlesmarkt stalls**, the **Nuremberg Hauptbahnhof** arrivals concourse, and the **Hauptmarkt / Lorenzer Altstadt** restaurant quarter. *Nürnberger Nachrichten* and *Bayerischer Rundfunk* cover new variants every December.

The **Christkindlesmarkt pickpocket ring** is the signature Nuremberg scam. The market's 180 stalls pack into a single narrow square for five weeks, drawing two million visitors. Teams of three or four work the choke points — the Lebkuchen (gingerbread) stalls, the Nürnberger Würstchen (finger-sized sausage) counters, the "Christkind" mulled-wine tent — one spills Glühwein, another apologizes and dabs at your coat, a third eases the wallet from your pocket. Losses run €300–€900 per incident. The Nuremberg Polizei's Marktwache station adjacent to the Hauptmarkt stays open through the market run specifically for this pattern.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Christkindlesmarkt "artisan Glühwein mug"** stalls take a €15–€18 deposit on mugs that are €5 at standard stalls — the mugs often turn out to be machine-pressed imports marked up past fair retail on the mug-collector niche. The **Nuremberg Hauptbahnhof ticket-machine helper** runs Berlin's yellow-vest cash-disappears script. The **"British credit card" sob-story** circulating in Nuremberg's Altstadt targets arriving tourists with a scripted "my card was just frozen, I need €80 for a train home" request — the Polizei logs the pattern specifically among Hauptbahnhof and Königstor arrivals.

A safe default: through the Christkindlesmarkt run, crossbody bags zipped and front-pocketed phones only; for mugs, ask the per-deposit and per-Glühwein prices separately before committing (the posted festival standard is €5 deposit, €4.50 Glühwein — anything dramatically higher is the collector-niche markup); for Lebkuchen and Nürnberger sausage-gifts, buy from stalls with posted per-kilo signs; take the U2 or U3 from Hauptbahnhof to Hauptmarkt for €3.60 (three minutes); and call **110** or visit the **Polizeiinspektion Nürnberg-Mitte** at Am Ochsenzwinger 1 — eight minutes from Hauptmarkt. Service line: **+49 911 2112-3311**. During Christkindlesmarkt, the on-site Marktwache is staffed daily 10:00–22:00.
