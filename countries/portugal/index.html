🏥 Healthcare Summary

Good quality

💊 Medication Restrictions

Portugal follows EU regulations on medication imports. Carry prescription medications in original packaging with a doctor's letter or valid prescription. For narcotics and psychotropic substances, a Schengen certificate is recommended. Common medications are widely available at pharmacies (farmácias), which are well-stocked and staffed by knowledgeable pharmacists. Portugal has decriminalized personal possession of small quantities of drugs, but importing controlled substances without documentation remains illegal.

  • Benzodiazepines (Xanax, Valium) — RESTRICTED: Legal with prescription. Portugal has high benzodiazepine prescription rates. Available from pharmacies with valid medical prescription (receita médica).
  • Adderall / amphetamines — BANNED: Amphetamines are controlled narcotics. Adderall is not approved in Portugal (or the EU generally). Possession for personal use is decriminalized (treated as administrative offense) but importing/selling remains criminal.
  • Cannabis / CBD — RESTRICTED: Personal use decriminalized since 2001 (up to 25g / 5g resin). Medical cannabis legal since 2018 (Law 33/2018) with prescription. CBD products legal if THC <0.2%. Portugal pioneered decriminalization but recreational sales remain illegal.
  • Methylphenidate (Ritalin, Concerta) — RESTRICTED: Available by prescription for ADHD. Controlled psychotropic substance under INFARMED regulation. Carry original packaging and prescription.
  • Codeine / opioids — RESTRICTED: Codeine combinations available by prescription. Stronger opioids require special prescriptions. Personal use possession decriminalized under 2001 law but supply remains criminal.

🙏 Cultural Tips

Tipping: Not obligatory but appreciated. Leave 5-10% at restaurants for good service (service is not automatically included). Cafes: leave small coins. Taxi drivers: round up. Portugal has lower tipping expectations than Spain or France.

Cultural taboos to avoid:

  • Confusing Portugal with Spain (or assuming it's similar) — culturally distinct
  • Assuming everyone speaks Spanish (Portuguese is different — they speak Portuguese)
  • Not acknowledging saudade — the Portuguese are proud of this cultural concept
  • Speaking too loudly (Portuguese culture values modesty and discretion)
  • Rushing through a pastel de nata — these must be savored
