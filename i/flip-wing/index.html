⚡ Before You Go — Essentials

🚅 Japan Rail Pass

A 14 or 21-day JR Pass is essential for this trip. It covers bullet trains (Shinkansen) between Tokyo, Nagoya, Osaka, Kyoto, and Hiroshima, plus many local JR lines. Activate it on Day 8 when you leave Tokyo — use IC cards (Suica/Pasmo) for Tokyo metro.

🌧️ Late May Weather

Expect 20-28°C across Japan. Late May is the very start of tsuyu (rainy season) in western Japan — Osaka and Kyoto may see afternoon showers. Pack a compact umbrella and light rain jacket. Tokyo will be warm and pleasant.

💴 Budget Tips

Konbini (convenience stores) like 7-Eleven and Lawson have incredible cheap meals — onigiri, bento, sandwiches. Lunch sets at restaurants are 30-50% cheaper than dinner. Many temples and shrines are free. Group izakaya courses offer great value for 5+.

📱 Staying Connected

Rent a pocket WiFi at the airport or get eSIMs for everyone. Coverage is excellent nationwide. Download the Navitime or Google Maps transit app — Japan's train system is complex but these make it easy.

🎫 Book Ahead

Ghibli Park and PokéPark KANTO require advance tickets (often months ahead). Book Ghibli Park via Boo-Woo Ticket or Lawson. PokéPark tickets via the official app. Some popular restaurants need reservations too.

Day 1 Shibuya · Harajuku · Omotesando

Welcome to Tokyo — Shibuya & Harajuku

Touch down in Tokyo and dive straight into its most iconic neighborhoods. Cross the world-famous Shibuya Crossing, explore Harajuku's wild fashion scene, and kick off the trip with an evening izakaya feast.

Afternoon

Shibuya Crossing & Shibuya Sky

After checking in, head to the world's busiest pedestrian crossing. Watch the organized chaos from the Starbucks above, then ascend Shibuya Sky — the open-air observation deck at Shibuya Scramble Square (230m) for 360° Tokyo panoramas.

📸 Shibuya Sky is best near sunset — book a timed slot online
🐕 Visit the Hachiko statue outside Shibuya Station for the group photo
🏬 Shibuya 109 is right here for Japanese street fashion shopping
Evening

Harajuku & Takeshita Street

Walk from Shibuya to Harajuku (15 min) and stroll down Takeshita Street — Tokyo's wildest shopping lane packed with rainbow cotton candy, crepe stands, and kawaii fashion boutiques.

🍦 Try a rainbow cotton candy or Japanese crepe from Marion Crêpes
🛍️ Omotesando is Tokyo's Champs-Élysées — luxury brands in stunning architecture
⛩️ Meiji Jingu entrance is right here — save the full visit for another day
🍺 Dinner
Uobei Shibuya (Genki Sushi)
High-speed conveyor belt sushi where you order on a touchscreen and plates zoom to your seat on a mini bullet train. Fun, casual, and incredibly affordable — perfect for a group.
💰 ¥1,000-2,000pp · 📍 Dogenzaka, Shibuya · No reservations, short wait
Jet-lagged? Shibuya's neon energy will wake you right up. Konbini (7-Eleven, Lawson) are open 24/7 for late-night snacks — their egg sandwiches are legendary.
Day 2 Asakusa · Central Tokyo · Ueno

Ancient Asakusa & Imperial Tokyo

Step back in time at Senso-ji, Tokyo's oldest temple, then contrast with the wide-open spaces around the Imperial Palace. End the day with a yakitori alley crawl.

Morning

Senso-ji Temple & Nakamise Street

Arrive early at Senso-ji — Tokyo's oldest and most visited Buddhist temple (founded 645 AD). Walk through the massive Kaminarimon (Thunder Gate) and browse Nakamise-dori's 200+ stalls.

⛩️ Arrive before 9am to beat crowds — the temple is open 24/7
🍡 Try ningyo-yaki (custard-filled cakes) and age-manju (fried sweet buns)
📸 The five-story pagoda is stunning against blue sky
☕ Breakfast
Pelican Café
Legendary Tokyo bakery since 1942, famous for their thick-cut milk bread toast. Small and always busy — arrive early.
💰 ¥500-800pp · 📍 Kotobuki, Taito · Closes when bread sells out
Afternoon

Imperial Palace East Gardens

Explore the Imperial Palace East Gardens — free, peaceful, and beautifully landscaped on the grounds of the former Edo Castle. The stone walls and moats are impressive remnants of the shogunate era.

🏯 Free admission · Closed Mon & Fri
🌳 The ninomaru garden is stunning — traditional Japanese landscaping
📸 Nijubashi Bridge with the palace in the background is the classic Tokyo shot
Evening

Ameyoko Market & Ueno

Head to Ameyoko — the bustling market street under the Yamanote Line tracks near Ueno. Street vendors sell everything from fresh seafood to sneakers.

🦑 Fresh seafood stalls selling uni, crab legs, and grilled squid on sticks
🍺 Grab tall boys from the vendors and street-drink like a local
🍺 Dinner
Hoppy Street (Hoppy-dori)
A rowdy, festive alley of outdoor izakayas where locals gather for cheap yakitori, fried gyoza, and hoppy beer. Perfect for a group — just pick a stall and sit down.
💰 ¥1,500-3,000pp · 📍 Near Senso-ji, Asakusa · No reservations needed
Day 3 Akihabara · Ginza · Tsukiji Outer Market

Otaku Paradise & Glamorous Ginza

Morning in Akihabara's electric wonderland of anime, gaming, and gadgets. Afternoon, shift gears to the sophistication of Ginza. End with fresh sushi near the old Tsukiji market.

Morning

Akihabara Electric Town

Dive into the sensory overload of Akihabara — Tokyo's otaku capital. Multi-story arcades, retro game shops, anime mega-stores, and themed cafés.

🎮 Super Potato — legendary retro game shop across 5 floors
🕹️ SEGA and Taito Station arcades — UFO catchers, rhythm games, purikura
📚 Mandarake — massive secondhand anime/manga store
☕ Lunch
Kanda Matsuya
Historic soba noodle shop serving handmade buckwheat noodles since 1884. The cold soba with dipping sauce is sublime — a true Tokyo institution.
💰 ¥800-1,200pp · 📍 Kanda Sudacho, Chiyoda · Lunch rush 11:30-1pm
Afternoon

Ginza District

Tokyo's most glamorous neighborhood — broad boulevards lined with flagship stores, art galleries, and department stores. Architecture from the futuristic Mikimoto building to the classic Wako clock tower.

🏬 Ginza Six — luxury mall with rooftop garden and Tsutaya bookshop
🎨 Ginza galleries are free — check Shiseido Gallery or Pola Museum Annex
📸 On weekends, Chuo-dori becomes a pedestrian-only promenade
Evening

Tsukiji Outer Market Area

The outer market still thrives with dozens of stalls and small restaurants serving the freshest sushi, tamagoyaki, and wagyu skewers. Visit for the atmosphere and late-afternoon bites.

🥚 Yamacho — famous tamagoyaki (sweet egg omelet on a stick)
🐟 Sushi Zanmai for great quality without insane waits
🍷 Dinner
Uoshin Nihonbashi
Fresh-from-market seafood izakaya with excellent sashimi platters perfect for sharing. Cold sake and a lively group atmosphere.
💰 ¥3,000-5,000pp · 📍 Nihonbashi area · Reservations recommended for 5+
Ginza can be pricey, but window shopping is free and the architecture is incredible. Save your yen for the food — that's where it really shines.
Day 4 Shinjuku · Kabukicho · Golden Gai

Shinjuku — Gardens, Neon & Legendary Nightlife

Explore Tokyo's vibrant Shinjuku — morning in the serene Gyoen gardens, afternoon skyscraper views, then the main event: Golden Gai and Kabukicho after dark.

Morning

Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden

One of Tokyo's most beautiful parks — 144 acres of French formal, English landscape, and Japanese traditional gardens. In late May, the iris and rose gardens are in full bloom.

🌹 Rose garden peaks mid-May — over 100 varieties
🍱 No alcohol allowed, but picnics welcome — grab bento from a konbini
💰 ¥500 admission · Open 9am-6pm
Afternoon

Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building

Free 202m observation decks with panoramic city views. On clear days you can see Mt Fuji to the west.

🏙️ North & South observation decks — both free, North less crowded
🛍️ Don Quijote Shinjuku nearby — chaotic discount shopping for souvenirs
☕ Lunch
Fuunji Ramen
Legendary tsukemen (dipping ramen) shop — rich, thick, intensely flavored fish and pork broth with springy noodles. Always a line, always worth it.
💰 ¥900-1,200pp · 📍 Near Shinjuku South Exit · Expect 20-40 min wait
Night

Golden Gai Bar Hopping

Tokyo's legendary nightlife district — six narrow alleys packed with over 200 tiny bars, each seating just 5-10 people. Every bar has its own theme, music, and personality.

🍸 Most bars charge a ¥500-1,000 seating charge — totally normal
🎵 Try Albatross (chandelier bar), Deathmatch in Hell (horror), or Champion
👥 Group of 5+ will need to split up — bars are TINY. Part of the charm.

Kabukicho & Godzilla Head

Walk through the neon-drenched streets of Kabukicho. The giant Godzilla head atop Shinjuku Toho Building is an iconic photo op.

🦎 Godzilla head best seen from below at night
⚠️ Area is safe but avoid touts offering "free drinks"
🍺 Dinner
Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane)
Smoky alley of tiny yakitori joints feeding Shinjuku since the 1940s. Squeeze onto a stool, order chicken skewers and beer, and soak up old Tokyo.
💰 ¥1,500-3,000pp · 📍 West side of Shinjuku Station · Best after 6pm
Day 5 Gotokuji · Kichijoji · Shimokitazawa

Local Tokyo — Lucky Cats, Parks & Vintage Vibes

Escape the tourist trail into Tokyo's beloved local neighborhoods. Morning at the charming cat temple of Gotokuji, afternoon in Kichijoji's parks and markets, evening in bohemian Shimokitazawa.

Morning

Gotokuji Temple — The Lucky Cat Temple

This serene Buddhist temple is the birthplace of the maneki-neko (beckoning cat). Hundreds of white lucky cat figurines line shelves and altars, left by visitors whose wishes came true.

🐱 Buy a small maneki-neko (from ¥300) and make a wish
📸 The shelves overflowing with cat figurines are incredibly photogenic
🚃 Take Odakyu Line to Gotokuji Station — 5 min walk
⏰ Free admission · Best visited morning for quiet
Afternoon

Kichijoji & Inokashira Park

Consistently voted Tokyo's most desirable neighborhood. Stroll through Inokashira Park, rent swan boats on the lake, and explore the Harmonica Yokocho alley of tiny bars and eateries.

🦢 Swan boat rentals on Inokashira Pond — ¥700/30 min
🛍️ Harmonica Yokocho — maze of yakitori bars, craft shops, and vintage stores
🍖 Satou — famous butcher with legendary ¥240 menchi katsu (meat croquettes)
☕ Lunch
Satou Menchi Katsu + Harmonica Yokocho
Grab the famous ¥240 menchi katsu from the legendary Satou butcher, then find a Harmonica Yokocho spot for cold beer and yakitori.
💰 ¥1,000-2,000pp · 📍 Kichijoji · Satou line moves fast
Evening

Shimokitazawa — Vintage, Cafés & Live Music

Tokyo's bohemian heart. Narrow streets packed with vintage clothing stores, record shops, indie cafés, and live music venues. Like Williamsburg meets Harajuku.

👗 New York Joe Exchange and Flamingo are top vintage shops
🎵 Check live music at Shelter or THREE
🍺 Shimokita Taproom for excellent craft beer
🍺 Dinner
Shirube Shimokitazawa
Cozy izakaya serving creative Japanese small plates and excellent sake. Warm and inviting — perfect for a group dinner after vintage shopping.
💰 ¥2,500-4,000pp · 📍 Shimokitazawa · Reservations recommended
Day 6 Meiji Jingu · Yoyogi Park · Roppongi

Meiji Shrine, Yoyogi Park & Roppongi Night Out

Start with a peaceful morning at Tokyo's grandest Shinto shrine, spend the afternoon people-watching in Yoyogi Park, then hit Roppongi for rooftop views and the city's most international nightlife scene.

Morning

Meiji Jingu Shrine

Tokyo's most important Shinto shrine, set in 170 acres of forested parkland. The towering torii gate and gravel path through ancient trees feels like entering another world — right in the middle of the city.

⛩️ The massive cypress wood torii gate at the entrance is 12m tall
🌳 The forest was planted in 1920 with 100,000 donated trees from across Japan
🍶 Write a wish on an ema (wooden plaque) and hang it at the shrine
⏰ Free admission · Opens at sunrise, closes at sunset
Afternoon

Yoyogi Park

Tokyo's Central Park. On weekends, the park comes alive with street performers, rockabilly dancers, cosplayers, and picnicking families. Rent a blanket and people-watch.

🎸 The rockabilly dancers at Harajuku entrance are a Tokyo institution
🌸 Beautiful in any season — green and lush in May
🍺 Pick up drinks and snacks from the konbini for a park session
☕ Lunch
Afuri Ramen (Harajuku)
Famous yuzu shio (citrus salt) ramen — lighter and more refreshing than typical heavy tonkotsu. Beautiful modern space with an open kitchen.
💰 ¥1,000-1,500pp · 📍 Harajuku/Omotesando area · Quick-casual
Night

Roppongi Hills & Mori Art Museum

Head to Roppongi Hills for the Mori Art Museum (52nd floor) and the Sky Deck rooftop — an open-air helipad turned observation platform. The night views are breathtaking.

🎨 Mori Art Museum stays open until 10pm — contemporary art with views
🌃 Sky Deck (rooftop) costs extra but has unobstructed 360° night views
📸 Tokyo Tower lit up against the cityscape is magical from here

Roppongi Nightlife

Roppongi is Tokyo's most international party district. From upscale cocktail bars to massive clubs, this is where the night never ends.

🍸 Try Two Rooms — sophisticated rooftop bar with skyline views
🎶 If you want to dance, V2 Tokyo or 1OAK are the big clubs
🍻 For casual vibes, the alleys behind Roppongi Crossing have small bars
🍷 Dinner
Gonpachi Nishi-Azabu
The restaurant that inspired the iconic fight scene in Kill Bill. Multi-level wooden interior, excellent yakitori, soba, and tempura. A fun group dining experience with theatrical atmosphere.
💰 ¥3,000-5,000pp · 📍 Nishi-Azabu · Reservations recommended for 5+
Day 7 Yomiuriland · PokéPark KANTO

PokéPark KANTO — Gotta Catch 'Em All!

Dedicate a full day to PokéPark KANTO at Yomiuriland — Tokyo's massive Pokémon theme park. Ride the gondola up, explore themed zones, watch live shows, and collect exclusive Pokémon merch. A must for any fan.

Morning

Travel to PokéPark KANTO

Take the Keio Line to Keio-Yomiuriland Station, then ride the Sky Shuttle gondola up to the park — the aerial views of the surrounding hills are beautiful. Download the official PokéPark app before you go!

📱 DOWNLOAD THE APP BEFOREHAND — you need it for show lotteries and reservations
🚡 The gondola ride is part of the experience — great views
⏰ Arrive by 9:30am — gates open at 10am and popular attractions fill up fast
🎫 Book tickets well in advance online — they sell out
Afternoon

PokéPark KANTO Exploration

Explore the themed zones featuring life-size Pokémon encounters, interactive experiences, and photo ops galore. The Kanto region comes alive with themed rides, shows, and activities.

📸 Life-size Pokémon statues and encounter zones throughout
🎭 Enter the show lottery via the app for live performances
🛍️ The gift shop has park-exclusive merch you can't get anywhere else
🍦 Pokémon-themed food stalls — Pikachu curry, Pokéball onigiri, character drinks
☕ Lunch
PokéPark Food Court
Character-themed meals inside the park — Pikachu curry, Eevee parfaits, and Pokéball-shaped rice balls. Half the fun is eating food that looks like Pokémon.
💰 ¥1,000-2,000pp · 📍 Inside PokéPark · Multiple stalls
Evening

Yomiuriland Evening & Return

If you still have energy, explore the rest of Yomiuriland amusement park (separate tickets). Take the gondola back down as the sun sets — the views of Tokyo's skyline are gorgeous.

🎢 Yomiuriland has roller coasters and rides if the group wants more thrills
🌅 Evening gondola ride back is beautiful at golden hour
🍺 Dinner
Ichiran Ramen (Shinjuku)
End the day with Japan's most famous solo-dining ramen chain. Individual booths with customizable broth — choose richness, spice level, garlic, and noodle firmness.
💰 ¥1,000-1,500pp · 📍 Shinjuku · Open late, no reservations
PokéPark can easily fill a full day. Battery packs are essential — the app drains your phone. Bring rain gear just in case (late May can be unpredictable).
Day 8 Izu Peninsula · Atami · Jogasaki Coast

Izu Peninsula — Coastal Cliffs & Hot Springs

Leave Tokyo behind and head south to the Izu Peninsula. Take the bullet train to Atami, explore dramatic coastal cliffs on the Jogasaki Coast trail, and soak in natural onsen overlooking the Pacific Ocean. Adventure and relaxation in one day.

Morning

Shinkansen to Atami & Izu Coast

Take the Tokaido Shinkansen from Tokyo to Atami (45 minutes), then connect to local trains along the eastern Izu coast. The scenery shifts from cityscape to ocean views and lush green mountains.

🚅 Shinkansen Kodama to Atami — about 45 min from Tokyo Station
🌊 Sit on the left side for ocean views along the coast
🏨 Consider staying overnight in a ryokan for the full experience
Afternoon

Jogasaki Coast Hiking Trail

One of Japan's most spectacular coastal walks. Follow clifftop paths past dramatic rock formations, cross a suspension bridge 48m above the ocean, and pass a lighthouse with panoramic views. The adventure highlight of the trip.

🌉 The Kadowaki Suspension Bridge (48m high, 60m long) is thrilling
🥾 Full trail is 9km but the best section (bridge to lighthouse) is about 3km
🌊 Waves crash against volcanic rock formations below — incredible sounds
📸 Jogasaki Lighthouse has 360° views — don't miss the stairs up
☕ Lunch
Local Seafood Shokudo
Stop at a local shokudo (cafeteria) near Izu-Kogen Station for fresh-caught kinmedai (golden-eye snapper) — the regional specialty. Simple, fresh, incredible.
💰 ¥1,200-2,000pp · 📍 Near Izu-Kogen Station · Cash preferred
Evening

Onsen & Relaxation

Check into a ryokan (traditional Japanese inn) with natural hot spring baths. Soak in an outdoor rotenburo overlooking the ocean as the sun sets. This is the relaxation the trip was built for.

♨️ Most ryokans include kaiseki dinner and breakfast in the rate
🧖 Onsen etiquette: wash thoroughly before entering, no swimwear, towels stay outside the water
👘 Wear the provided yukata (light kimono) around the inn — it's expected and comfortable
🌅 Request an ocean-view room for sunset from the bath
🍷 Dinner
Ryokan Kaiseki Dinner
Multi-course traditional Japanese dinner served in your room — local seafood, seasonal vegetables, delicate preparations. Usually 8-12 courses. An unforgettable culinary experience.
💰 Included with ryokan stay · 📍 In-room dining
Day 9 Mt Fuji · Kawaguchiko · Fuji Five Lakes

Mt Fuji — Japan's Sacred Peak

Travel from Izu to the Fuji Five Lakes region for jaw-dropping views of Japan's most iconic mountain. Cycle around Lake Kawaguchi, visit the Chureito Pagoda, and feel the spiritual power of Fuji-san up close.

Morning

Travel to Kawaguchiko

Head north from Izu through scenic mountain roads to the Fuji Five Lakes region. The first glimpse of Mt Fuji emerging above the treeline is unforgettable — especially with the snow-capped peak against clear May skies.

🚌 Bus from Mishima or train connections via Kofu — about 2-3 hours from Izu
🗻 Mt Fuji still has snow on the summit in May — perfect for photos
📸 Clear mornings offer the best views — cloud cover often builds by afternoon

Chureito Pagoda

Climb 398 steps to this five-story pagoda for the most famous view in Japan — the bright red pagoda framing Mt Fuji with cherry trees in the foreground (though by May, it's green and lush instead of pink).

📸 THE classic Japan postcard shot — red pagoda + Fuji
🥾 398 steps up — moderate effort, huge reward
⏰ Go early morning for fewest crowds and best light
Afternoon

Lake Kawaguchiko Cycling

Rent bikes and cycle the scenic path around Lake Kawaguchi — the reflections of Mt Fuji in the calm lake water are mesmerizing. Stop at lakeside cafés, art museums, and photo spots along the 20km circuit.

🚲 Bike rentals available at Kawaguchiko Station — about ¥1,000/day
📸 Ubuyagasaki cape has the best Fuji reflection photo spot
☕ Kachi Kachi Ropeway takes you to Mt Tenjo for aerial views
🌊 Lake is calm in the morning — best for mirror-like Fuji reflections
☕ Lunch
Houtou Fudou
Kawaguchiko's most famous restaurant serving houtou — flat udon noodles in a thick miso-based soup with pumpkin and vegetables. The regional specialty, perfect fuel for cycling.
💰 ¥1,200-1,800pp · 📍 Kawaguchiko · Expect a wait on weekends
Evening

Fujisan Onsen & Sunset Views

Soak in a hot spring with Mt Fuji views as the sun sets behind the mountain. Several public onsen and hotel baths in the area offer stunning Fuji panoramas.

♨️ Fujiyama Onsen is a large public facility near Kawaguchiko Station
🌅 Golden hour on Fuji is spectacular — the alpenglow effect turns the peak pink
🏨 Many lakeside hotels have Fuji-view baths
🍷 Dinner
Local Izakaya near Kawaguchiko
Casual izakaya dinner featuring local specialties — venison, horse sashimi (basashi), and fresh river fish. The Fuji area has a distinctive mountain cuisine quite different from coastal cities.
💰 ¥2,500-4,000pp · 📍 Kawaguchiko town
Day 10 Nagoya · Ghibli Park · Aichi

Ghibli Park — Walking Through Miyazaki's Dreams

Travel to Nagoya and spend the day at Ghibli Park — Studio Ghibli's magical theme park set within the Aichi Commemorative Park. Walk through recreations of scenes from Totoro, Spirited Away, Howl's Moving Castle, and more.

Morning

Travel to Nagoya & Ghibli Park

Take the Shinkansen from Mishima (near Fuji) to Nagoya (about 1.5 hours), then the Linimo monorail to Ai-chikyuhaku-kinen-koen Station. Ghibli Park is spread across five areas within the larger commemorative park.

🚅 Tokaido Shinkansen to Nagoya — about 1.5 hours
🚝 Linimo monorail from Fujigaoka to the park entrance
🎫 TICKETS MUST BE PRE-BOOKED — they sell out months ahead. Use Boo-Woo Ticket or Lawson
📱 The O-Sanpo Day Pass covers all 5 areas (morning or afternoon slot)

Ghibli's Grand Warehouse

An indoor area recreating scenes from across Ghibli's filmography. Walk through the fantastical food stalls from Spirited Away, sit in Catbus, explore a 1:1 recreation of the Boiler Room, and discover hidden Ghibli characters throughout.

📸 No-Face train from Spirited Away — sit next to the spirit for photos
🐱 The life-size Catbus is a highlight — kids and adults love it equally
🎬 Mini-theater shows exclusive Ghibli short films (included with ticket)
Afternoon

Hill of Youth & Dondoko Forest

Explore the outdoor areas — the Hill of Youth features a recreation of the antique shop from Whisper of the Heart, while Dondoko Forest has Satsuki and Mei's house from My Neighbor Totoro in a beautiful woodland setting.

🏡 Satsuki and Mei's House — meticulously recreated with period-accurate furnishings
🌳 Dondoko Forest walk — giant Totoro statue at the top of the trail
🏰 Valley of Witches features Howl's Moving Castle and Witch's House
🚶 The park is spread out — wear comfortable shoes, expect lots of walking
☕ Lunch
Ghibli Park Café / Lawn Picnic
The park has food stalls and the surrounding Aichi Park has picnic areas. Alternatively, grab bento from Nagoya Station konbini before arriving.
💰 ¥800-1,500pp · 📍 Inside park or surrounding green areas
Evening

Nagoya Evening — Osu & Sakae

Head back to central Nagoya. Explore the Osu shopping arcade — a vibrant covered market street with vintage shops, electronics, and street food. Then head to Sakae for Nagoya's nightlife scene.

🛍️ Osu Kannon shopping district — 1,200 shops in covered arcades
🏯 Nagoya Castle is nearby if you want a quick evening photo of the illuminated castle
🍺 Sakae district has Nagoya's best bars and clubs
🍺 Dinner
Yamamotoya Honten — Miso Nikomi Udon
Nagoya's signature dish: thick udon noodles simmered in rich red miso broth in a clay pot. Yamamotoya is the most famous purveyor — hearty, warming, and uniquely Nagoya.
💰 ¥1,000-1,800pp · 📍 Multiple locations in Nagoya · No reservations needed
Ghibli Park tickets sell out months in advance! Book as early as possible. The park is not an amusement park — it is a walking experience. There are no rides, just beautifully crafted worlds to explore.
Day 11 Ise · Toba · Mie Prefecture

Ise Grand Shrine & Toba Pearl Divers

Journey to Mie Prefecture for Japan's most sacred Shinto shrine — Ise Jingu — then to the coastal town of Toba to meet ama pearl divers and feast on the freshest seafood in the country.

Morning

Ise Grand Shrine (Ise Jingu)

Japan's holiest shrine, rebuilt every 20 years for over 1,300 years. The Inner Shrine (Naiku) is set deep within an ancient cypress forest — the spiritual atmosphere is overwhelming. This is the heart of Japanese Shinto.

⛩️ Visit the Inner Shrine (Naiku) — the most important of the 125 shrines
🌲 Walk across the Uji Bridge into the sacred forest — the air changes
🙏 Photography is restricted near the main hall — respect the sacred space
📿 The shrine has been rebuilt every 20 years since 690 AD — next rebuild: 2033
☕ Lunch
Okage Yokocho
Traditional shopping street near the Inner Shrine recreating Edo-period architecture. Famous for Ise udon (thick, soft noodles in dark sauce) and akafuku mochi (sweet rice cakes). A foodie paradise.
💰 ¥800-1,500pp · 📍 Adjacent to Naiku shrine · Many stalls and restaurants
Afternoon

Toba — Mikimoto Pearl Island & Ama Divers

Head to Toba to visit Mikimoto Pearl Island — where cultured pearl farming was invented. Watch ama (women free divers) demonstrate their ancient diving technique, and learn about Japan's fascinating pearl industry.

🦪 Mikimoto Pearl Island — museum + live ama diving demonstrations
🤿 Ama divers have been harvesting seafood for 2,000+ years
💎 The pearl museum shows how cultured pearls are made — fascinating process

Meoto Iwa (Married Couple Rocks)

Visit the sacred Meoto Iwa — two rocks in the ocean connected by a shimenawa rope, representing the union of the creator gods. One of Japan's most iconic spiritual sights.

📸 Best photographed at sunrise, but impressive any time
⛩️ Futami Okitama Shrine nearby has adorable frog statues everywhere
Evening

Toba Seafood Feast

Toba is one of Japan's premier seafood destinations. The waters here produce incredible Ise-ebi (spiny lobster), oysters, abalone, and turban shells. Treat the group to an unforgettable seafood dinner.

🦞 Ise-ebi season peaks in fall, but the local seafood is outstanding year-round
🦪 Toba oysters are farmed in the nutrient-rich bay — plump and sweet
🏨 Many ryokans in Toba include spectacular seafood kaiseki dinners
🍷 Dinner
Ama Hut Seafood BBQ (Osatsu Kamado)
Eat grilled seafood over an open fire, served in a seaside hut by women dressed as traditional ama divers. Abalone, turban shells, oysters, and clams cooked right in front of you — absolutely unique.
💰 ¥3,000-5,000pp · 📍 Osatsu area, Toba · Reservations required
Day 12 Osaka · Dotonbori · Shinsekai · Namba

Osaka — The Kitchen of Japan

Welcome to Osaka — Japan's street food capital, comedy hub, and nightlife powerhouse. Spend the day eating your way through Dotonbori, explore the retro charm of Shinsekai, and end with Osaka's legendary nightlife.

Morning

Travel to Osaka & Osaka Castle

Take the train from Toba/Ise to Osaka (about 2 hours). Drop your bags and head to Osaka Castle — the dramatic five-story tower sits atop a stone-walled hilltop surrounded by moats and parkland.

🏯 Osaka Castle is a reconstruction but the museum inside is worthwhile
🌳 The surrounding Nishinomaru Garden is beautiful in late spring
📸 Best photo angles from across the moat on the south side
Afternoon

Dotonbori Street Food Crawl

Osaka's motto is kuidaore — 'eat till you drop.' Dotonbori is ground zero for this philosophy. Walk the canal-side street and eat everything: takoyaki, okonomiyaki, gyoza, kushikatsu, and more.

🐙 Takoyaki (octopus balls) — try Creo-Ru or Kukuru for the best
🥞 Okonomiyaki (savory pancake) — Mizuno is legendary, expect a line
🍢 Kushikatsu (deep-fried skewers) — Daruma is the iconic spot
📸 The Glico Running Man sign is Osaka's most famous photo spot

Shinsekai District

Walk south to Shinsekai — Osaka's retro entertainment district. Built in 1912 to blend Paris and New York, it now has a wonderfully faded charm. The Tsutenkaku Tower overlooks streets lined with kushikatsu shops and game parlors.

🗼 Tsutenkaku Tower — modest views but great retro vibes
🍢 Shinsekai is THE kushikatsu district — try Jan Jan Yokocho alley
🎰 Retro game centers and pachinko parlors everywhere
☕ Lunch
Dotonbori Street Food
Skip the sit-down and graze! Takoyaki from Creo-Ru, gyoza from Chao Chao, and kushikatsu from Daruma. Budget about ¥2,000-3,000 total for a full street food lunch.
💰 ¥2,000-3,000pp · 📍 Dotonbori · Walk and eat
Night

Namba & Osaka Nightlife

Osaka's nightlife rivals Tokyo but with a friendlier, more outgoing energy. Start with drinks on the Dotonbori canal, then head to the bars around Namba and Shinsaibashi. Osakans are famous for their warmth and humor.

🍸 Bar Nayuta — intimate cocktail bar with legendary bartender
🎤 Karaoke is a MUST in Osaka — try Jankara for cheap group rooms
🎶 AMMONA in Shinsaibashi for clubbing till dawn
🍺 Ura-Namba (back streets of Namba) has excellent standing bars and izakayas
🍺 Dinner
Toyo (Standing Sashimi Bar)
Legendary standing sushi/sashimi bar in Kuromon Market area. The tuna is butchered right in front of you and served on the spot. Casual, chaotic, and absolutely delicious. Cash only.
💰 ¥2,000-4,000pp · 📍 Near Kuromon Market, Namba · No reservations, stand and eat
Day 13 Kyoto · Fushimi Inari · Arashiyama · Gion

Kyoto — Temples, Bamboo & Geisha District

Day trip to Kyoto — just 15 minutes from Osaka by Shinkansen. Experience the jaw-dropping 10,000 vermillion torii gates of Fushimi Inari, the ethereal bamboo groves of Arashiyama, and the mysterious geisha district of Gion.

Morning

Fushimi Inari Shrine

Arrive at dawn for the most magical experience in all of Japan — thousands of bright orange torii gates snaking up a mountainside through dense forest. The full hike to the summit takes 2 hours, but even 30 minutes is unforgettable.

⛩️ Start EARLY (before 7am) to have the gates almost to yourself
🥾 Full summit hike: 4km, 2 hours. The mini summit at 30 min is also rewarding.
🦊 Fox statues throughout — the fox is Inari's messenger
📸 The dense tunnel sections are the most photogenic — fewer people = better shots
☕ Breakfast
Vermillion Café
Charming café right at the base of Fushimi Inari, perfect for post-hike coffee and pastries with a view of the shrine entrance.
💰 ¥500-1,000pp · 📍 Next to Fushimi Inari station · Opens early
Afternoon

Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

Walk through the towering bamboo forest of Arashiyama — the light filtering through thousands of bamboo stalks creates an otherworldly atmosphere. Continue to the scenic Togetsukyo Bridge spanning the Katsura River.

🎋 Arrive before 9am or after 4pm for fewer crowds
🐒 Iwatayama Monkey Park is a 20-min climb — wild macaques with city views
🌉 Togetsukyo Bridge is especially beautiful with mountains in the background

Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)

One of Kyoto's most iconic sights — a three-story pavilion covered in gold leaf reflecting perfectly in the mirror pond. The surrounding strolling garden is immaculate.

✨ The reflection in the pond is the money shot — arrive when it's calm
💰 ¥500 admission · Your ticket is a beautiful calligraphic charm
🍵 Grab matcha and wagashi at the tea garden inside the grounds
☕ Lunch
Arashiyama Yoshimura Soba
Handmade soba noodles with a view of the Togetsukyo Bridge and Katsura River. The tempura soba set is excellent. Tatami seating with river views.
💰 ¥1,200-1,800pp · 📍 Arashiyama · River-view seating
Evening

Gion District — Geisha Quarter

Wander the atmospheric streets of Gion as dusk falls. The wooden machiya townhouses, stone-paved lanes, and soft lantern light create an enchanting atmosphere. You might spot a maiko (apprentice geisha) hurrying to an engagement.

👘 Hanami-koji is the main geisha street — respect their privacy, no chasing for photos
🏮 Shirakawa canal with its weeping willows and stone bridges is magical at dusk
⛩️ Yasaka Shrine at the eastern end is beautiful lit up at night
🍵 Consider a traditional tea ceremony experience in Gion — about ¥2,000-4,000pp
🍷 Dinner
Pontocho Alley Dining
Narrow alley running along the Kamogawa River, packed with restaurants from high-end kaiseki to casual izakayas. In summer, restaurants extend seating onto kawadoko (riverside platforms). Choose any spot — they're all atmospheric.
💰 ¥2,500-5,000pp · 📍 Pontocho, Kyoto · Riverfront spots book up fast
Kyoto is a day trip from Osaka — just 15 min by Shinkansen or 30 min by regular train. Return to your Osaka hotel for the night.
Day 14 Nara · Southern Kyoto Temples

Nara's Ancient Deer & Kyoto's Hidden Temples

Morning with Nara's famously friendly deer and the colossal Great Buddha, then afternoon exploring Kyoto's lesser-known southern temples — Tofuku-ji's zen gardens and the stunning Byodo-in (the temple on the ¥10 coin).

Morning

Nara Park & Todai-ji Temple

Train to Nara (45 min from Osaka) and enter Nara Park — home to 1,200 free-roaming deer considered sacred messengers. They'll bow to you for deer crackers! Then visit Todai-ji — the enormous wooden temple housing a 15-meter bronze Buddha.

🦌 Buy shika senbei (deer crackers, ¥200) and the deer will bow to you
🙏 Todai-ji's Great Buddha Hall is the world's largest wooden building
📸 The deer are adorable but can be pushy — hide your crackers!
⏰ Arrive early to beat tour groups — the deer are friendliest in the morning

Kasuga Taisha Shrine

Walk through Nara Park's ancient forest to Kasuga Grand Shrine — famous for its 3,000 stone and bronze lanterns. The approach through the primeval forest with deer grazing alongside is enchanting.

🏮 3,000 lanterns line the paths and hang in the corridors
🌳 The surrounding Kasugayama Primeval Forest is a UNESCO site
⛩️ ¥500 for the inner sanctuary — the lantern-filled corridors are worth it
☕ Lunch
Kakinoha Sushi (Persimmon Leaf Sushi)
Nara's famous local specialty — pressed sushi wrapped in fragrant persimmon leaves. Try Tanaka at Kintetsu Nara Station or any shop along the approach to Todai-ji.
💰 ¥800-1,200pp · 📍 Near Todai-ji or Kintetsu Nara Station
Afternoon

Tofuku-ji Temple

Head to Kyoto's Tofuku-ji — one of the great Zen temples, famous for its stunning gardens. The Hojo garden (designed in 1939) is a masterpiece of modern zen garden design with moss, stone, and raked gravel.

🌿 The moss and checkered stone garden is unlike anything else in Kyoto
🍁 Famous for autumn leaves, but the gardens are beautiful year-round
📸 The Tsutenkyo (bridge) corridor offers unique elevated garden views
Evening

Return to Osaka & Ura-Namba

Head back to Osaka for your last night in the city. Explore Ura-Namba (back streets of Namba) — a maze of tiny standing bars, yakitori joints, and local izakayas where Osakans actually hang out.

🍺 Ura-Namba standing bars — ¥300-500 beers and cheap snacks
🎤 One last karaoke session with the group — Jankara or Big Echo
🌙 Take a final walk along the Dotonbori canal at night for the neon reflections
🍺 Dinner
Ajinoya Okonomiyaki
One of Osaka's best okonomiyaki restaurants — watch them make your savory pancake on the teppan right in front of you. The pork and squid mix is incredible. Perfect casual group dinner.
💰 ¥1,200-2,000pp · 📍 Namba · May have a queue but moves fast
Day 15 Hiroshima · Miyajima Island

Hiroshima — Peace, History & Miyajima's Floating Torii

A powerful and moving day trip from Osaka. Visit the Hiroshima Peace Memorial and Museum, then ferry to Miyajima Island to see the iconic floating torii gate of Itsukushima Shrine — one of Japan's most beautiful sights.

Morning

Shinkansen to Hiroshima & Peace Memorial Park

Take the early Shinkansen from Shin-Osaka to Hiroshima (80 minutes). Walk to the Peace Memorial Park — a solemn, beautifully designed park centered on the A-Bomb Dome, the only structure left standing near the bomb's hypocenter.

🕊️ The A-Bomb Dome is a UNESCO World Heritage Site — preserved as it stood after the blast
🕯️ The Children's Peace Monument with its paper cranes is deeply moving
🏛️ The Peace Memorial Museum was renovated in 2019 — deeply impactful exhibits
⏰ Museum opens at 8:30am — arrive early, allow 1-1.5 hours
Afternoon

Miyajima Island & Itsukushima Shrine

Ferry from Hiroshima to Miyajima Island (about 1 hour total including tram to port). The iconic floating torii gate of Itsukushima Shrine appears to hover over the water at high tide — one of Japan's Three Most Scenic Views.

⛩️ The vermillion torii gate "floats" at high tide and is walkable at low tide — check tide times!
🦌 Wild deer roam Miyajima too — smaller and more relaxed than Nara's
🏯 Explore Senjokaku (Hall of 1,000 Tatami Mats) and the five-story pagoda
🚡 Miyajima Ropeway to Mt Misen summit for panoramic inland sea views (if time allows)
☕ Lunch
Hiroshima Okonomiyaki
Hiroshima-style okonomiyaki is layered (not mixed like Osaka's) with noodles, cabbage, egg, and toppings stacked on the griddle. Try Nagata-ya or Hassei near the Peace Park for the best.
💰 ¥900-1,500pp · 📍 Near Peace Park, Hiroshima · Nagata-ya has a line — worth it
🍺 Snack
Miyajima Grilled Oysters & Momiji Manju
Miyajima is famous for two things: massive grilled oysters from the Seto Inland Sea (¥200-400 each) and momiji manju — maple-leaf-shaped cakes filled with sweet bean paste, custard, or chocolate.
💰 ¥500-1,000pp · 📍 Miyajima shopping street
Evening

Return to Osaka

Take the Shinkansen back to Shin-Osaka (80 min). Reflect on a powerful, moving day. If you have energy, grab a nightcap in Namba or near your hotel.

🚅 Last Shinkansen back is around 9pm — don't miss it!
🍺 Grab ekiben (station bento) and a beer for the ride back — a Japanese train tradition
🍷 Dinner
Ekiben on the Shinkansen
Buy a Hiroshima-specialty ekiben (station bento) at Hiroshima Station for the ride back. The anagomeshi (grilled conger eel on rice) bento is a Hiroshima classic.
💰 ¥1,000-1,500pp · 📍 Hiroshima Station shops
This is a long day but absolutely doable as a day trip. Leave Osaka by 7am Shinkansen, spend morning in Hiroshima, afternoon on Miyajima, return by 9pm. JR Pass covers everything.
Day 16 Osaka · Kuromon Market · Umeda · Departure

Final Day — Osaka's Last Bites & Sayonara

Your final day in Japan. Make the most of the morning with Osaka's incredible Kuromon Market, explore the skyline from Umeda Sky Building, pick up last-minute souvenirs, and say sayonara to an unforgettable 16-day adventure.

Morning

Kuromon Market — Osaka's Kitchen

Osaka's 190-year-old "Kitchen" — a covered market stretching 580 meters with 150+ stalls selling the freshest seafood, fruit, and street food. This is your breakfast and lunch in one glorious grazing session.

🐟 Giant grilled scallops, king crab legs, uni (sea urchin), and fatty tuna sashimi — all eaten standing
🍓 Japanese strawberries and perfectly cubed fruit — worth the splurge
🐡 Some stalls serve fugu (pufferfish) if you're feeling adventurous
⏰ Stalls open around 9am — busiest 10am-noon
☕ Breakfast/Brunch
Kuromon Market Grazing
This IS your meal — graze through the market eating fresh sashimi, grilled seafood, tamagoyaki, and seasonal fruit. Budget ¥3,000-5,000 for a full market breakfast.
💰 ¥3,000-5,000pp · 📍 Kuromon Market, Namba · Best 9am-noon
Afternoon

Umeda Sky Building & Souvenir Shopping

Head to Umeda for the futuristic Sky Building — two towers connected by a floating garden observatory at 173m. The escalator through the open air between towers is thrilling. Then hit the underground shopping streets for last-minute souvenirs.

🏙️ The Floating Garden Observatory has 360° open-air views
🛍️ Buy Japanese snack boxes, Kit Kat flavors, and matcha sweets at Don Quijote or department store basements (depachika)
📦 Osaka Station has excellent souvenir shops — 551 Horai's pork buns are the classic Osaka omiyage
🧳 Pack or ship — Japan Post and Yamato offer affordable international shipping
Evening

Final Walk & Departure

Take one last stroll through your favorite Osaka neighborhood. If departing from Kansai International Airport (KIX), take the Nankai Rapit express train from Namba (38 min) or JR Haruka from Tennoji/Shin-Osaka.

✈️ KIX: Nankai Rapit from Namba (38 min) or JR Haruka from Shin-Osaka (50 min)
✈️ ITM (Itami/Osaka domestic): Airport bus from major stations
🎌 Sayonara, Japan! You've covered 16 incredible days across the entire country
🍺 Lunch/Farewell
551 Horai Butaman (Pork Buns)
Osaka's most beloved takeaway — juicy steamed pork buns from 551 Horai. Every Osakan knows the smell. Grab a box at any station shop for the journey to the airport.
💰 ¥500-800 for a box · 📍 Every major station · THE Osaka souvenir
Tax-free shopping: spend ¥5,000+ at participating stores and show your passport for 10% tax refund. Department stores have dedicated tax-free counters.

✈️ Getting There

  • Fly into Tokyo Narita (NRT) or Haneda (HND) — Haneda is closer to the city
  • Depart from Osaka Kansai (KIX) for a one-way route — no backtracking
  • Book open-jaw flights (into Tokyo, out of Osaka) to save time and money

🏨 Where to Stay

  • Tokyo: Shinjuku or Shibuya for nightlife access and train connections
  • Izu: Book a ryokan with onsen for the full experience
  • Kawaguchiko: Lakeside hotel with Fuji views
  • Nagoya: Near Nagoya Station for easy Shinkansen access
  • Osaka: Namba or Shinsaibashi for food and nightlife

🌡️ Weather

  • Mid-May: 20-26°C, mostly sunny with occasional rain
  • Late May: 22-28°C, tsuyu (rainy season) may begin in western Japan
  • Humidity increases throughout May — carry a handkerchief and stay hydrated
  • UV is strong — sunscreen and hats recommended for outdoor days

💳 Money & Tipping

  • Japan is increasingly cashless but carry some yen — small shops and shrines are cash-only
  • NO TIPPING in Japan — it can actually be considered rude
  • 7-Eleven and Japan Post ATMs accept international cards
  • Tax-free shopping at ¥5,000+ — show passport at participating stores

📱 Etiquette Tips

  • Remove shoes when entering temples, ryokans, and many restaurants (look for the raised floor)
  • No eating or drinking while walking (except festival areas)
  • Phones on silent (manner mode) on public transport — no calls
  • Bow slightly when greeting — it goes a long way
  • Queue patiently — Japan has amazing queue culture
