⚡ Before You Go — Essentials

🎓 Graduation Schedule

April 29 and May 1 — Northeastern University ceremonies in Boston. May 8 — graduation ceremony in New Haven. Plan outfits and arrive early to secure good seats.

🚗 Road Trip Segments

NYC to New Haven is ~90 min by car. Vegas to Grand Canyon South Rim is ~4.5 hours. Grand Canyon to SLC is ~6.5 hours. SLC to Yellowstone (West Entrance) is ~5 hours. Budget for gas, snacks, and scenic stops.

🏔️ Yellowstone in May

Mid-May in Yellowstone means some roads may still be opening for the season. Expect cool temps (30-55°F), possible snow, and fewer crowds. Pack layers, waterproof jackets, and sturdy hiking boots.

💰 Budget Tips

Book flights early (Boston→Vegas, SLC→Chicago). Share Airbnbs for the group. Casual dining keeps costs down — this itinerary focuses on local gems over fine dining. National park passes are $35/vehicle.

Day 1 Back Bay · Beacon Hill · Boston Common

Arrival in Boston — Lobster & the Common

Touch down in Boston and get your bearings in one of America's most walkable cities. Stroll through the Public Garden, explore Beacon Hill's gas-lit streets, and kick off the trip with New England's finest lobster roll.

Afternoon

Boston Common & Public Garden

America's oldest public park is the perfect first stop. Walk through the Public Garden, see the famous Swan Boats (if running), and cross into Beacon Hill — one of the most photogenic neighborhoods in the country with its brick row houses and gas lanterns.

🌳 Boston Common dates to 1634 — oldest city park in the US
🦢 Swan Boats in the Public Garden run late April through September
📸 Acorn Street in Beacon Hill — the most photographed street in America
🦞 Late Lunch
Neptune Oyster
Tiny North End spot famous for the best lobster roll in Boston — warm, buttered, overflowing. Worth the wait.
💰 $$ · 📍 63 Salem St, North End · Cash only, expect a line
Evening

North End Food Walk

Boston's Little Italy is packed with incredible Italian restaurants, bakeries, and cafés. Walk Hanover Street, grab cannoli from Mike's Pastry or Modern Pastry, and soak in the old-world atmosphere.

🍝 Carmelina's for hearty Italian — no reservations, casual vibe
🍰 Mike's Pastry vs. Modern Pastry — the eternal Boston debate
🍷 Stroll Hanover Street and people-watch
🍕 Dinner
Carmelina's
Beloved North End Italian spot — massive portions, lively atmosphere, and the garlic bread is legendary. Perfect for a big group.
💰 $$ · 📍 307 Hanover St, North End
Day 2 Freedom Trail · Faneuil Hall · Seaport

Freedom Trail & Faneuil Hall

Walk the 2.5-mile Freedom Trail through 16 historic sites spanning the American Revolution, feast at Faneuil Hall Marketplace, and explore Boston's buzzy Seaport District.

Morning

Freedom Trail Walk

Follow the red-brick line from Boston Common through 16 of America's most important Revolutionary War sites. Pass the Massachusetts State House, Park Street Church, the Old North Church, and Paul Revere's House.

🧱 2.5 miles, about 2-3 hours at a leisurely pace
⛪ Old North Church — where the "one if by land, two if by sea" lanterns hung
🏠 Paul Revere House — oldest remaining structure in downtown Boston (1680)
Afternoon

Faneuil Hall & Quincy Market

Historic marketplace turned food hall. Grab lunch from the dozens of food stalls inside Quincy Market — clam chowder in a bread bowl is the move. Street performers and local vendors make it a lively scene.

🥣 Boston Chowda Co. inside Quincy Market — award-winning chowder
🎭 Street performers on the cobblestone plaza
🛍️ Browse local artisan shops in the surrounding buildings
🥣 Lunch
Boston Chowda Co.
Award-winning New England clam chowder in a bread bowl at Quincy Market. Thick, creamy, and full of clams.
💰 $ · 📍 Quincy Market, Faneuil Hall
Evening

Seaport District

Boston's trendiest neighborhood — waterfront restaurants, the ICA museum, and great cocktail bars. Walk along the Harborwalk as the sun sets over the harbor.

🎨 Institute of Contemporary Art — stunning waterfront architecture
🍸 Envoy Hotel rooftop bar — harbor views and cocktails
🚶 Harborwalk for a scenic evening stroll
🍽️ Dinner
Row 34
Stylish Seaport oyster bar and seafood restaurant. Excellent raw bar, craft beers, and a fun, lively atmosphere perfect for groups.
💰 $$$ · 📍 383 Congress St, Seaport
Day 3 Northeastern University · Fenway · South End

🎓 Graduation Day #1 — Northeastern University

The first big celebration! Attend your cousin's graduation ceremony at Northeastern, then explore the Fenway neighborhood and celebrate with a special dinner in the South End.

Morning

🎓 Northeastern University Graduation Ceremony

Arrive early to secure good seats for the commencement ceremony. Northeastern's campus is beautiful in late April with spring blooms. Bring tissues — it's going to be emotional!

🎓 Arrive at least 1 hour early for seating
📸 Photo ops around campus — Centennial Common is great
👔 Dress semi-formal — spring weather can be unpredictable in Boston
📍 Check Northeastern website for exact ceremony location and time
Afternoon

Post-Ceremony Celebration

After the ceremony, take a walk through the Fens — a beautiful Frederick Law Olmsted-designed green space adjacent to campus. The Rose Garden is beginning to bloom in late April.

🌹 Kelleher Rose Garden in the Back Bay Fens
🏟️ Walk past Fenway Park — even from outside, it's iconic
📸 Group photos at the Northeastern sign and around campus
🥪 Lunch
El Jefe's Taqueria
Casual, delicious burritos and tacos right near Northeastern's campus. Perfect post-ceremony fuel — big portions, affordable, and fun.
💰 $ · 📍 83 Gainsborough St, Fenway
Evening
🍽️ Celebration Dinner
The Salty Pig
Charcuterie boards, artisan pizzas, and an incredible craft beer list. Casual-upscale vibe perfect for a graduation celebration with a big group.
💰 $$ · 📍 130 Dartmouth St, Back Bay
Make a reservation for dinner — graduation weekends are busy across the city. Book at least a week in advance.
Day 4 Cambridge · Harvard · MIT

Cambridge Day — Harvard, MIT & Brunch

Cross the Charles River to Cambridge and explore two of the world's most famous universities. Wander Harvard Yard, geek out at MIT, and discover the incredible food scene along Massachusetts Avenue.

Morning

Harvard Yard & Harvard Square

Walk through the hallowed gates of Harvard Yard, rub the foot of the John Harvard statue for luck, and explore Harvard Square's bookshops, buskers, and cafés.

📸 Touch the John Harvard statue's foot — it's tradition (and always shiny)
📚 Harvard Book Store — incredible indie bookshop
🏛️ Harvard Art Museums — world-class collection, free with student ID
☕ Brunch
The Friendly Toast
Funky, eclectic brunch spot with creative dishes, big portions, and a fun vibe. Think pumpkin pancakes, breakfast burritos, and craft cocktails.
💰 $$ · 📍 1 Kendall Square, Cambridge
Afternoon

MIT Campus & The Infinite Corridor

Walk through MIT's campus along the Charles River. See the iconic Great Dome, the Stata Center (Frank Gehry's wildest building), and the Media Lab. The campus is an architectural playground.

🏗️ Stata Center — looks like it's melting, Frank Gehry masterpiece
🔬 MIT Museum — quirky exhibits on robots, holograms, and kinetic art
🌊 Memorial Drive along the Charles — great for a group walk
Evening

Charles River Esplanade

Walk along the Boston side of the Charles River as the sun sets. The Esplanade is beautiful in spring — sailboats, joggers, and the Boston skyline reflecting on the water.

🌅 Best sunset views from the Longfellow Bridge
⛵ Community Boating offers sunset sailing (seasonal)
🍽️ Dinner
Shy Bird
Farm-to-table casual dining in Cambridge with an incredible rotisserie chicken and seasonal menu. Warm, welcoming atmosphere great for groups.
💰 $$ · 📍 1 Kendall Square, Cambridge
Day 5 Northeastern University · Fenway · Newbury Street

🎓 Graduation Day #2 — Northeastern University

Round two! Attend the second graduation ceremony at Northeastern, then celebrate on iconic Newbury Street with shopping and a festive group dinner.

Morning

🎓 Northeastern University Graduation Ceremony #2

Second cousin's big day! Same drill — arrive early, dress well, bring the energy. You know the campus now, so navigate like a pro.

🎓 Same tips as Day 3 — arrive early, check the website for exact time/location
📸 Get the family group photo you missed last time
🎉 This calls for an extra-special celebration tonight
Afternoon

Newbury Street Shopping & Strolling

Boston's premier shopping street — eight blocks of boutiques, galleries, cafés, and brownstones. Great for a post-ceremony wind-down. The street gets more eclectic (and affordable) as you walk from Arlington toward Mass Ave.

🛍️ Boutiques, vintage shops, and galleries line both sides
☕ Trident Booksellers & Café — beloved bookshop-café combo
🎨 Galleries on the upper blocks toward Mass Ave
☕ Lunch
Trident Booksellers & Café
Iconic Newbury Street bookshop and café. Browse books while waiting for your avocado toast and house-roasted coffee.
💰 $$ · 📍 338 Newbury St, Back Bay
Evening
🍽️ Celebration Dinner
Buttermilk & Bourbon
Southern-inspired comfort food in Back Bay — fried chicken, biscuits, shrimp & grits, and a killer bourbon cocktail list. Fun, festive, and perfect for celebrating.
💰 $$ · 📍 160 Commonwealth Ave, Back Bay
Last night in Boston! Pack tonight so you can leave for NYC first thing tomorrow morning.
Day 6 Lower Manhattan · SoHo · Chinatown

NYC Arrival — Downtown Manhattan

Take the train or drive from Boston to NYC (~4 hours). Dive straight into Lower Manhattan's incredible energy — walk the Brooklyn Bridge, explore SoHo, and feast in Chinatown.

Morning

Travel: Boston → New York City

Drive or take Amtrak from Boston to NYC. The Amtrak Acela takes ~3.5 hours and drops you at Penn Station in Midtown. If driving, budget 4-5 hours with traffic.

🚂 Amtrak Acela: Boston South Station → NYC Penn Station (~3.5 hrs)
🚗 Driving: I-90 W to I-84 to I-95 S, about 4-5 hours
💡 Book Amtrak early for best prices — group of 5+ should compare cost vs. rental car
Afternoon

Brooklyn Bridge Walk

Walk across the Brooklyn Bridge from the Manhattan side for stunning views of the skyline and the Statue of Liberty in the distance. End in DUMBO for the famous Manhattan Bridge photo from Washington Street.

🌉 Walk takes about 30 minutes — go slowly and enjoy the views
📸 DUMBO — Washington Street for the iconic Manhattan Bridge frame photo
🍦 Brooklyn Ice Cream Factory on the waterfront
Evening

Chinatown Food Crawl

NYC's Chinatown is one of the best food neighborhoods in the world. Walk Mott Street and Canal Street, popping into noodle shops, dumpling houses, and bakeries.

🥟 Joe's Shanghai — legendary soup dumplings (xiao long bao)
🍜 Xi'an Famous Foods — hand-pulled noodles and cumin lamb
🧋 Bubble tea from Tiger Sugar or Boba Guys
🥟 Dinner
Joe's Shanghai
The soup dumpling institution. Order the crab & pork xiao long bao and prepare for flavor explosion. Cash only, no frills, incredible food.
💰 $$ · 📍 46 Bowery, Chinatown
Day 7 Midtown · Times Square · Central Park

Iconic Manhattan — Central Park to Times Square

Hit the big-ticket Manhattan landmarks: Central Park in the morning, the Met or MoMA in the afternoon, and the sensory overload of Times Square at night.

Morning

Central Park

Enter from the south and walk through the Mall, past Bethesda Fountain, and up to the Bow Bridge. In early May, the cherry blossoms may still be lingering and the park is lush and green.

🌸 Bethesda Fountain — the heart of the park
🌉 Bow Bridge — most romantic spot in Central Park
🚣 Rent a rowboat on the Lake (seasonal, weather permitting)
🏰 Belvedere Castle for panoramic park views
☕ Brunch
Sarabeth's Central Park South
Classic NYC brunch institution. Famous for their lemon ricotta pancakes, jams, and eggs benedict. Right at the park entrance.
💰 $$$ · 📍 40 Central Park S
Afternoon

The Metropolitan Museum of Art

One of the world's greatest museums. You could spend days here — focus on the Egyptian Temple of Dendur, the American Wing, and the rooftop garden (open May-October) with stunning Central Park views.

🏛️ Suggested donation for NY residents, $30 for visitors
🌿 Rooftop Garden opens in May — cocktails with skyline views
⏱️ Budget 2-3 hours minimum
Evening

Times Square & Broadway

Love it or hate it, Times Square is a must-see. Walk through the neon canyon, then catch a Broadway show if you're up for it. Even just people-watching is entertainment.

🎭 TKTS booth in Times Square — discounted same-day Broadway tickets
💡 Most spectacular after dark — the lights are overwhelming
📸 Red Steps viewing platform at the TKTS booth
🍕 Dinner
Joe's Pizza
The quintessential NYC slice. No-frills, cash-only, perfect thin-crust pizza. A New York rite of passage.
💰 $ · 📍 7 Carmine St, Greenwich Village (original location)
Day 8 Chelsea · Meatpacking · West Village

The High Line, Chelsea Market & West Village

Explore NYC's trendiest neighborhoods. Walk the High Line elevated park, eat through Chelsea Market, and get lost in the charming streets of the West Village.

Morning

The High Line

Walk this elevated park built on a former railway line from the Meatpacking District up through Chelsea. Art installations, gardens, and incredible views of the Hudson River and the city. Best experienced before the crowds.

🌿 1.45 miles, about 45 min to walk the full length
🎨 Rotating art installations along the way
📸 Great views of the Hudson and the Whitney Museum
Afternoon

Chelsea Market

Incredible food hall in a former Nabisco factory. Browse artisan vendors, grab tacos from Los Tacos No. 1, fresh lobster from The Lobster Place, or dumplings from Very Fresh Noodles.

🌮 Los Tacos No. 1 — arguably the best tacos in NYC
🦞 The Lobster Place — fresh seafood market and restaurant
🍩 Doughnuttery — mini doughnuts, freshly made
🌮 Lunch
Los Tacos No. 1
Small stand inside Chelsea Market with incredible al pastor, carne asada, and chicken tacos. Always a line, always worth it.
💰 $ · 📍 Chelsea Market, 75 9th Ave
Evening

West Village Stroll

The West Village is NYC at its most charming — tree-lined streets, brownstones, jazz clubs, and some of the city's best restaurants. Wander Bleecker Street and Perry Street for the quintessential New York vibe.

🎵 Village Vanguard — legendary jazz club (if you can get tickets)
🏡 Perry Street — the Carrie Bradshaw stoop from Sex and the City
📚 Three Lives & Company — beautiful independent bookshop
🍝 Dinner
L'Artusi
Upscale-casual Italian in the West Village. Seasonal pasta, incredible wine list, and a sophisticated but relaxed atmosphere. Great for a group dinner.
💰 $$$ · 📍 228 W 10th St, West Village · Reserve ahead
Day 9 Williamsburg · Bushwick · East Village

Brooklyn Cool — Williamsburg & the East Village

Cross into Brooklyn for vintage shopping, street art, and incredible food in Williamsburg and Bushwick. Return to the East Village for one of NYC's best dinner neighborhoods.

Morning

Williamsburg, Brooklyn

Brooklyn's creative epicenter — vintage shops, street art, waterfront parks with Manhattan skyline views, and some of the best brunch spots in the city. Start at the Bedford Avenue L stop and wander.

🎨 Street art murals throughout Williamsburg and neighboring Bushwick
🛍️ Vintage shops on Bedford Avenue — Buffalo Exchange, Beacon's Closet
🌊 Domino Park — waterfront views of the Manhattan skyline
☕ Brunch
Sunday in Brooklyn
Famous for their malted pancakes with hazelnut maple praline. One of NYC's best brunch spots with a beautiful open kitchen.
💰 $$$ · 📍 348 Wythe Ave, Williamsburg
Afternoon

Smorgasburg or Brooklyn Brewery

If it's a Saturday, hit Smorgasburg — the famous outdoor food market with 100+ vendors. Otherwise, tour the Brooklyn Brewery, browse the Artists & Fleas market, or walk to Bushwick for street art.

🍔 Smorgasburg — Saturdays at Williamsburg waterfront (April-October)
🍺 Brooklyn Brewery — tours and tastings
🖌️ Bushwick Collective — outdoor street art gallery
Evening

East Village Bar Hop & Dinner

Head back to Manhattan's East Village — NYC's most eclectic nightlife neighborhood. Dive bars, izakayas, and incredible cheap eats.

🍶 Decibel — underground sake bar
🍛 Curry Row (E 6th St) for cheap, fun Indian food
🍺 McSorley's Old Ale House — NYC's oldest bar (1854)
🍜 Dinner
Veselka
Iconic 24-hour Ukrainian diner in the East Village. Pierogies, borscht, and kielbasa in a bustling, colorful setting. A New York institution since 1954.
💰 $$ · 📍 144 2nd Ave, East Village
Day 10 Statue of Liberty · Ellis Island · Financial District

Lady Liberty, Wall Street & Little Italy

Visit the Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island — essential Americana. Then walk through the Financial District, past the 9/11 Memorial, and end with dinner in Little Italy.

Morning

Statue of Liberty & Ellis Island

Take the first ferry from Battery Park to Liberty Island. Walk around the base of the statue (pedestal access requires advance booking), then hop to Ellis Island to explore the Immigration Museum — incredibly moving if your family has an immigration story.

🗽 Book ferry tickets in advance at statuecruises.com
🏛️ Ellis Island Immigration Museum — free with ferry ticket
⏰ First ferry is 8:30am — go early to avoid crowds
⏱️ Budget 3-4 hours for both islands
Afternoon

9/11 Memorial & Museum

The reflecting pools where the Twin Towers stood are deeply moving. The memorial is free and open-air. The museum beneath tells the full story — allow 2 hours and bring tissues.

💧 The reflecting pools are free to visit anytime
🏛️ Museum tickets: ~$26/adult, book online
🌳 The Survivor Tree — a pear tree that survived the attacks
🥯 Lunch
Russ & Daughters Cafe
Classic NYC Jewish deli — bagels, lox, smoked fish, and egg creams. An institution since 1914, now with a sit-down café on the Lower East Side.
💰 $$ · 📍 127 Orchard St, Lower East Side
Evening
🍝 Dinner
Rubirosa
Thin-crust pizza and homemade pasta on Mulberry Street. The tie-dye vodka pizza is legendary. Casual, fun, and always packed — a true NYC Italian gem.
💰 $$ · 📍 235 Mulberry St, Nolita
Day 11 Upper West Side · Harlem · Washington Heights

Uptown Culture — Harlem, Gospel & Tacos

Explore upper Manhattan — the cultural richness of Harlem, soul food, and if it's Sunday, a gospel brunch. End the NYC chapter with an incredible meal before heading to New Haven tomorrow.

Morning

Harlem Cultural Walk

Harlem is the heart of African American culture. Walk along 125th Street past the Apollo Theater, stop by the Studio Museum, and soak in the neighborhood's vibrant energy and history.

🎤 Apollo Theater — where legends from Ella Fitzgerald to Lauryn Hill were discovered
🎨 Studio Museum in Harlem — Black art and culture
📸 Sylvia's Restaurant — Harlem institution since 1962
🍳 Brunch
Red Rooster Harlem
Chef Marcus Samuelsson's acclaimed restaurant. Southern comfort food meets global flavors — fried chicken, cornbread, and a lively brunch scene with live music.
💰 $$$ · 📍 310 Lenox Ave, Harlem
Afternoon

American Museum of Natural History

One of the world's great museums — the dinosaur halls, the whale room, and the Rose Center for Earth and Space are awe-inspiring. Great for the whole group.

🦕 4th floor dinosaur halls are the highlights
🐋 Blue whale room is breathtaking
🌌 Rose Center Planetarium — Hayden Sphere show
Evening

Last Night in NYC

Make your last NYC dinner count. Head to a neighborhood you haven't explored yet, or revisit a favorite. Tomorrow you're New Haven-bound.

📦 Pack up tonight — New Haven is a quick trip tomorrow
🗽 One last walk through your favorite NYC neighborhood
🍽️ Dinner
Ippudo
World-famous Japanese ramen chain — the Shiromaru Classic (tonkotsu) is silky, rich, and perfect. Always busy, always worth it.
💰 $$ · 📍 65 4th Ave, East Village
Day 12 New Haven Green · Yale · Wooster Square

🎓 New Haven — Brother's Graduation & Legendary Pizza

Drive to New Haven for your brother's graduation, explore the beautiful Yale campus, and eat what many consider the best pizza in America.

Morning

Drive NYC → New Haven

Quick 90-minute drive from NYC to New Haven via I-95. Arrive in time to settle in and prep for the ceremony.

🚗 ~90 minutes via I-95 N
💡 Park near the university — check ceremony parking instructions
👔 Dress semi-formal for the graduation

🎓 Brother's Graduation Ceremony

The big one! Your brother's graduation day. Arrive early, find great seats, and celebrate this incredible milestone.

🎓 Check the university website for exact ceremony time and location
📸 Plan group photos at iconic campus spots afterwards
🎉 This deserves a major celebration dinner
Afternoon

Yale University Campus Walk

After the ceremony, explore Yale's stunning Gothic campus. The Beinecke Rare Book Library (with its translucent marble walls) and the Yale University Art Gallery are both free and extraordinary.

📚 Beinecke Library — the marble walls glow with natural light
🎨 Yale University Art Gallery — free, world-class collection
🏛️ Gothic architecture everywhere — feels like Hogwarts
Evening

New Haven Pizza Pilgrimage

New Haven-style pizza (apizza) is coal-fired, thin, charred, and legendary. Frank Pepe's and Sally's Apizza have been battling for the crown since the 1930s. You must try both.

🍕 Frank Pepe's — the Original Tomato Pie and White Clam pie are iconic
🍕 Sally's Apizza — same street, equally legendary
🔥 Both are on Wooster Street — walk between them
⏰ Lines can be long — go early or be patient
🍕 Celebration Dinner
Frank Pepe Pizzeria Napoletana
The birthplace of New Haven-style apizza (since 1925). The White Clam pizza is a national treasure — fresh littleneck clams, garlic, olive oil, no mozzarella. Life-changing.
💰 $$ · 📍 157 Wooster St, New Haven
If you can handle two pizza stops, walk next door to Sally's Apizza for a second pie. The rivalry is real and both are extraordinary.
Day 13 The Strip · Fremont Street · Arts District

Fly to Las Vegas — Neon Nights

Catch a flight to Las Vegas and arrive in the desert. Check in, walk the Strip, and experience the sensory overload of Vegas at night. This isn't about gambling — it's about the spectacle.

Morning

Flight: New Haven Area → Las Vegas

Fly from a nearby airport (JFK, Newark, or Hartford) to Las Vegas McCarran International. Budget for 5-6 hours of travel including flight time.

✈️ Fly from JFK/EWR/BDL → Las Vegas (LAS), ~5 hours
🚗 You'll need a rental car in Vegas for the Grand Canyon drive
💡 Pick up the rental car at the airport
Afternoon

The Las Vegas Strip Walk

Walk the Strip from the Bellagio fountains to the Venetian. Even if you don't gamble, the hotels are insane — the Bellagio conservatory, the Venetian canals, and the Cosmopolitan's chandelier bar are all free to explore.

⛲ Bellagio Fountains — free show every 15-30 min
🌸 Bellagio Conservatory — incredible seasonal botanical display (free)
🏛️ The Venetian — indoor canals and gondola rides
🍸 Chandelier Bar at The Cosmopolitan — three-story cocktail bar
Evening

Fremont Street Experience

Old Vegas! The Fremont Street Experience is a covered pedestrian mall with a massive LED canopy, live music, street performers, and vintage casinos. It's grittier and more fun than the Strip.

💡 The Viva Vision LED canopy is 1,500 feet long
🎰 Vintage casinos — Golden Nugget, Binion's, El Cortez
🍺 More affordable drinks and food than the Strip
🍽️ Dinner
Tacos El Gordo
Tijuana-style street tacos on the Strip. The adobada (al pastor) is carved from a massive spit and loaded into fresh tortillas. The best cheap eat in Vegas.
💰 $ · 📍 3049 Las Vegas Blvd S
Day 14 Las Vegas · Route 66 · Grand Canyon approach

Vegas Morning → Drive to the Grand Canyon

One more Vegas morning, then hit the road toward one of the world's great natural wonders. The drive through the desert is stunning — red rocks, Joshua trees, and wide-open sky.

Morning

Vegas Brunch & Departure

Fuel up with a big Vegas brunch before the road trip. The Arts District (18b) has great casual spots if you want to skip the Strip tourist traps.

🎨 18b Arts District — murals, galleries, and coffee shops
☕ PublicUs — excellent specialty coffee and brunch in the Arts District
☕ Brunch
Mon Ami Gabi
French bistro with a patio right on the Strip, looking out at the Bellagio fountains. Classic eggs benedict, steak frites, and people-watching.
💰 $$$ · 📍 Paris Las Vegas, 3655 Las Vegas Blvd S
Afternoon

Drive: Las Vegas → Grand Canyon South Rim

Hit the road for the ~4.5-hour drive to the Grand Canyon South Rim. The desert scenery is gorgeous — consider a stop in Kingman or Williams along old Route 66.

🚗 ~275 miles via US-93 S and I-40 E, about 4-4.5 hours
🛣️ Williams, AZ — "Gateway to the Grand Canyon" has Route 66 charm
⛽ Fill up in Williams — limited services closer to the canyon
🌄 Arrive by late afternoon for your first rim views
Evening

First Sunset at the Grand Canyon

Check into your lodging near the South Rim and head straight to Mather Point or Yavapai Point for sunset. The canyon transforms minute by minute as the light changes — this first view will stop you in your tracks.

🌅 Mather Point — most accessible sunset viewpoint
📸 The canyon is 277 miles long, 18 miles wide, and a mile deep
🏨 Stay at Tusayan (just outside the park) or in-park lodges (book far ahead)
🍽️ Dinner
El Tovar Dining Room
Historic dining room right on the South Rim inside the iconic El Tovar Hotel. The views are extraordinary and the food is surprisingly good for a national park.
💰 $$$ · 📍 El Tovar Hotel, Grand Canyon South Rim · Reservations recommended
Day 15 Grand Canyon South Rim · Bright Angel Trail

Grand Canyon — Rim Trail & Bright Angel

A full day at the Grand Canyon. Hike a portion of the Bright Angel Trail, walk the Rim Trail, ride the free shuttle to viewpoints, and watch the canyon change colors throughout the day.

Morning

Sunrise at Yavapai Point

Day 20 Grand Prismatic · Midway Geyser Basin · Firehole River

Yellowstone Day 2 — Grand Prismatic & Midway Basin

Today is all about color. The Grand Prismatic Spring is Yellowstone's most jaw-dropping feature — a 370-foot-wide rainbow of turquoise, orange, and yellow. Pair it with the Midway Geyser Basin and a scenic drive along the Firehole River.

Morning

Grand Prismatic Spring

The largest hot spring in the US and the third largest in the world. The colors are caused by heat-loving bacteria — the center is too hot for life (sterile blue), while the edges bloom in orange, yellow, and green. Absolutely unreal.

🌈 370 feet across — the size of a football field
📸 For the best overhead view, hike the Fairy Falls Trail to the Grand Prismatic Overlook (~1.6 miles round trip)
♨️ The steam can obscure views — early morning or clear days are best
🌡️ Water temperature: ~160°F in the center
Afternoon

Midway Geyser Basin

The boardwalk loop through Midway Geyser Basin passes Excelsior Geyser Crater (which pumps 4,000 gallons of boiling water per minute into the Firehole River) and of course Grand Prismatic itself.

💧 Excelsior Geyser Crater — massive boiling crater
🌊 Watch the turquoise water cascade into the Firehole River
🚶 Short boardwalk loop — about 0.6 miles

Firehole River Swim

One of the only places you can legally swim in Yellowstone! The Firehole Swimming Area is a stretch of the Firehole River warmed by geothermal activity. It's chilly but exhilarating — a truly unique experience.

🏊 Firehole Swimming Area — south of Madison Junction
🌡️ Water is warmed by hot springs but still cold — think 70°F
📍 Open seasonally, usually late May (check if open during your visit)
🥪 Lunch
Canyon Lodge Eatery

💰 $$ · 📍 Canyon Village

🔭 Bring binoculars for wildlife spotting
