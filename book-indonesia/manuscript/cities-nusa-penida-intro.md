Nusa Penida is Bali's offshore island — reached by a 45-minute fast boat from Sanur pier, home to Kelingking Beach, Broken Beach, and Angel Billabong, and the site of some of Indonesia's most consequential scooter injuries. The *Sanur–Nusa Penida fast-boat ticket overcharge and fake-operator fraud* is the first contact: touts at Sanur pier quote 600,000–1,200,000 rupiah round-trip when the real Maruti or Sanur-Fast fare is 350,000–450,000, and some "operators" selling from the beach are clones of the real ticket office. Buy tickets only at the marked Maruti or Sanur-Fast counters at the Sanur pier Blue Water Express office (check for the Bank Indonesia license and the printed ticket with a QR code), or book online through *sanur-fast.com* before you arrive.

Five further scams structure the Nusa Penida visit. The *Kelingking, Broken Beach, and Angel Billabong "park fee" padding and parking touts* works all three main viewpoints — a man in a vest collects a 50,000–100,000-rupiah "park fee" at the parking area that is technically a village donation (not a national-park fee) and not strictly required; the honest rate is 10,000–25,000 per person as a village parking tip, paid at the official wooden booth only, with the traveler keeping the small paper stub. The *scooter rental + dangerous road conditions injury scam* is the island's most serious scam — rental shops in Toyapakeh and Sakti Village quote 100,000 rupiah per day for scooters that are structurally fine, but the western coastal road to Broken Beach and Kelingking Beach has multiple kilometers of steep, gravelly, blind-corner descents that produce frequent spills, and the rental shops have a standing 5,000,000-rupiah "injury scam" repair bill waiting for any scooter with even cosmetic scratches on return. If you are not a confident scooter rider in wet conditions, hire a driver (600,000–800,000 rupiah per day, full-day round-trip); if you insist on the scooter, do a full walk-around video before riding off, and decline the "insurance" upsell (it doesn't cover this specific claim). The *full-day driver tour commission stops* loops four kickback venues into the day's itinerary — a "traditional Balinese lunch" restaurant, a "silver handicraft village," a "seaweed farm demonstration," and a "kopi luwak plantation" — at each stop the driver expects a purchase and receives a commission; politely decline at each, eat at marked roadside warungs, and buy only at stops you chose in advance. The *ATM scarcity and hotel cash-exchange padding* reflects genuine island scarcity — there are only three ATMs on the island, and hotels offering cash-exchange at 20–40% above the Bali rate exploit the shortage; bring enough rupiah from Bali for your stay, and use only the BCA ATM at Toyapakeh port for emergency withdrawals. Finally the *Manta Point snorkel-tour unlicensed-boat safety scam* runs out of Toyapakeh — operators quote 200,000 rupiah for a "Manta Point and Crystal Bay" snorkel tour on boats without life vests, without operational ballast, and without emergency communication; book only at the Toyapakeh Tourism Information Desk with a licensed operator (*penida.nl* publishes a vetted list), pay 400,000–500,000 for the safe boat version, and check for life jackets before boarding.
