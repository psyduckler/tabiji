Labuan Bajo is the Flores gateway to Komodo National Park — a fishing town turned resort strip at the western tip of Flores island, reached through Komodo Airport (LBJ). The entire town's economy runs around the boat-tour industry to Komodo, Rinca, Padar, and Pink Beach; the scam ecosystem reflects that concentration. The *Komodo Airport (LBJ) and hotel-transfer taxi overcharge* is the first contact: drivers quote 400,000–700,000 rupiah for the 4-km run from the airport to the Labuan Bajo waterfront strip when the real Grab (Gojek works here too in 2025) rate is 60,000–90,000. Most hotels offer a free airport pickup shuttle if booked in advance — arrange it through the hotel's direct email or booking-platform message, not at the curb.

Five further scams shape the Labuan Bajo visit. The *Komodo National Park entry-fee confusion* is the region's most expensive scam — unlicensed operators and dishonest guides quote foreign visitors a "$500 Komodo pass" or "5,000,000-rupiah annual permit" when the real park fee is 500,000 rupiah per person per day for foreign visitors (roughly $30 USD), plus a 150,000-rupiah conservation fee and a 50,000-rupiah anchoring fee per boat. The full official fee breakdown is published at *komodonationalpark.org*; pay fees only at the marked park-authority booth inside the Komodo/Rinca pier area, cash only (rupiah), and keep the printed ticket. The *Komodo day-tour boat operator safety and overcrowding scam* runs out of the Labuan Bajo waterfront strip — operators quote 1,000,000–1,500,000 rupiah for a "day tour to Komodo, Padar, Pink Beach" on boats that are structurally unsafe (capacity violations, no life vests, no engine redundancy); the defense is to book only at the Labuan Bajo Tourism Information Desk with a licensed operator (the *Tourism Department of West Manggarai Regency* publishes a vetted list), pay 1,800,000–2,200,000 rupiah for the safe boat version, and check for life jackets and emergency radio before boarding. The *Komodo liveaboard overpromising and quality-mismatch scam* applies to 2-to-4-night sailing Phinisi boat charters — operators advertise "luxury liveaboard" with photos of boats the traveler never actually boards and delivers a smaller, older vessel with a different cabin count and food quality; book through Booking.com / Klook / Traveloka with verified reviews, and pay by credit card so a chargeback is possible if the boat doesn't match the listing. The *Pink Beach / Padar / Rinca "mandatory ranger" and photo-spot fee padding* works the island stops — a man in a khaki vest demands a 300,000–500,000-rupiah "ranger fee" at photo viewpoints that are not part of the real park pass; the real park pass covers all viewpoints, and the vest-wearer has no authority. Finally the *Labuan Bajo ATM skimming and remote-area cash-fraud* works the short main-road bank row — use ATMs inside BCA or BNI bank lobbies, bring enough rupiah from a Bali or Jakarta withdrawal for your Komodo trip (boat operators often accept only cash), and count all money-changer exchange transactions in full view of the teller.
