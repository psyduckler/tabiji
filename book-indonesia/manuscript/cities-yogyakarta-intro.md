Yogyakarta is Java's cultural capital — the gateway to Borobudur and Prambanan, the center of the batik commission-kickback circuit, and the site of Indonesia's most aggressive "sunrise ticket" fraud. The *Malioboro batik-gallery commission scam* is the flagship: a friendly "local student" approaches on Malioboro with an offer to show you a "last day of the exhibition, government-sponsored batik" and walks you to a gallery off the main street where a back-room demonstration ends in 3–8 million rupiah "hand-drawn" purchases that a rotating sales pressure keeps you from refusing without embarrassment. *Tempo* and the *Jakarta Post* have documented the circuit for fifteen years; the defense is to never follow a stranger off Malioboro, to treat "exhibition closing today" as the signal to walk away, and to buy batik only at the government Beringharjo Market (first floor) or at Mirota Batik's main store (Jalan Malioboro), where prices are posted and non-negotiable.

Five further scams structure the Yogyakarta visit. The *YIA airport and Kulon Progo transfer overcharge* quotes 400,000–800,000 rupiah for the 45-km run to central Yogyakarta when Grab (Gojek works too) runs 130,000–180,000; the defense is to take the DAMRI airport bus (20,000 rupiah, 75 minutes) or the Bandara Kulon Progo–Tugu train (15,000 rupiah, 40 minutes) to the city center and then Grab to your hotel. The *becak and andong pedicab "free tour"* script works Malioboro and the Taman Sari palace approach — a driver offers "free ride, government sponsor" and routes through four batik and silver commission shops; decline from the start, or agree a single flat rate in writing before boarding. The *Borobudur sunrise fake-ticket and Manohara Hotel upsell* is the most expensive scam in Yogyakarta — a man with a lanyard at the outer gate tells arrivals the "regular sunrise is sold out" and offers a "Manohara Hotel sunrise package" at 850,000–1,500,000 rupiah that is not the real sunrise slot; the authentic Borobudur Sunrise ticket is 550,000 rupiah booked through *borobudurpark.com* or at the official Manohara Hotel front desk, and any intermediary quoting a higher price is running a markup. The *Prambanan "mandatory guide" and skip-the-line* fraud works the outer entrance — a man in a guide vest tells tourists the temple now requires a guide and quotes 400,000 rupiah; the real Prambanan ticket is a simple entry (375,000 rupiah for foreigners) with no guide required, and independent self-guided walking is welcomed. Finally the *Mount Merapi jeep lava-tour unlicensed-operator* scam quotes 300,000–800,000 rupiah for tours on unregistered vehicles with no emergency equipment; the defense is the Yogyakarta Tourism Office's licensed-operator list on *visitingjogja.com*, which runs vetted lava-tour jeep companies at 350,000 rupiah per vehicle.
