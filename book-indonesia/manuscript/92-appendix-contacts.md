# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication, but phone numbers do change, consulates move, and hospitals occasionally renumber their switchboards. If a number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus *Indonesia* and you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results.

**Before you travel**, we strongly recommend you double-check your own embassy or consulate's current number directly on its official website — the US State Department's travel.state.gov, the UK's gov.uk, Canada's travel.gc.ca, Ireland's dfa.ie, Australia's smartraveller.gov.au. Save the main switchboard and the after-hours duty-officer line on your phone before departure, and screenshot this appendix so you can read it on a Gili fast boat or a Grab ride where your signal cuts out. Even a verified number can become a wrong number between editions.

## The numbers that work everywhere in Indonesia

These are the lines to dial from any phone — mobile or landline, Indonesian SIM or roaming — anywhere in the country. All of them are free.

- **Tourist assistance hotline (Ministry of Tourism):** **+62 21 3838 899**. This is the number to call if you do not speak Indonesian. Dispatchers are staffed in Indonesian and English during business hours (9 AM – 5 PM Jakarta time) and via WhatsApp at the same number for out-of-hours contact. This is the *first* number to try for any scam, tour-operator dispute, overcharging case, or general orientation question involving a foreign visitor.
- **Police (emergency):** **110**. National police emergency line. The first operator may speak Indonesian only; repeat *"English, please"* slowly or say *"Tourist Police"* and in Jakarta, Bali, Yogyakarta, and Surabaya the dispatcher will locate an English speaker. Use this for theft, assault, harassment, and any incident that is unfolding in real time.
- **National emergency (SMS / all services):** **112**. Universal emergency line that routes police, ambulance, and fire. English is available in Jakarta, Bali, Yogyakarta, and Surabaya.
- **Fire and rescue:** **113**. National fire-and-rescue dispatch.
- **Emergency medical services (ambulance):** **118** or **119**. National ambulance dispatch.
- **Search and rescue (Basarnas):** **115**. For water, mountain, and remote-area rescue — relevant for Rinjani and Bromo treks, Komodo boat incidents, and Gili island emergencies.
- **Traffic police:** **110** (routed through the police line) — for collisions, hit-and-runs, and traffic-related disputes.
- **Immigration (visa overstay, passport issues):** *imigrasi.go.id*. Central Jakarta office: Jalan H.R. Rasuna Said, Kuningan. Bali Denpasar office: Jalan Niti Mandala, Renon. Visa-on-arrival extension and overstay-rectification queries.

## Card issuer emergency lines (callable from Indonesia)

When you call, have your card number (or the last four digits), the approximate transaction amount, and the time of the fraudulent charge ready.

- **Visa Global Customer Assistance (collect from Indonesia):** +1 303 967 1096. 24/7, English.
- **Mastercard Global Service (collect from Indonesia):** +1 636 722 7111. 24/7.
- **American Express International Service Center (collect):** +1 336 393 1111. 24/7.

If your card was issued by a bank (not directly by Visa, Mastercard, or Amex), the fastest route is the number printed on the back of the card. That line routes you to your own issuer's fraud desk and they can freeze the card in under a minute. Save this number in your phone under a searchable contact name before you travel — hunting for a number on a stolen card is impossible.

## City-by-city emergency contacts

For every city in this book, the *tourist-district police post (Polisi Pariwisata)* (if one exists locally) is the default destination for a *laporan polisi*. Where no tourist police post is within reach, the local *kantor polisi* (ward police station) handles the report; dialing **+62 21 3838 899** (Ministry of Tourism) or **110** (national police) anywhere in the country will give you the dispatcher who can tell you which station to walk to.

Where we are not certain of a specific street address, we tell you so rather than guess. The current address of any tourist-district police post can be verified by calling the numbers above, by Google Maps, or by your hotel's front desk — every Indonesian hotel keeps the nearest police station address at reception.

### Jakarta

- **Police report intake:** the **Jakarta Metropolitan Tourist Police** headquarters is at Jalan Raden Patah No. 11 in Central Jakarta; satellite desks cover Kota Tua, Blok M, Kemang, and Soekarno-Hatta Airport. The **+62 21 3838 899** dispatcher will direct you to the nearest open post.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospitals:** **Prime Plus Medical Jakarta** (Jalan Rumah Sakit, Pondok Indah, +62 21 2994 7555) and **Siloam Hospital Kebon Jeruk** (Jalan Raya Pejuangan Kav. 8, +62 21 5300 911) are the two main private international hospitals with English-speaking staff and direct billing with major international insurers. **RS Mayapada Jakarta** and **RSUP Cipto Mangunkusumo** (Jalan Diponegoro, the national referral hospital) are additional options. For walk-in care, **SOS Medika Klinik Kuningan** (Jalan HR Rasuna Said, Kuningan, +62 21 5794 8600) is widely used by expatriates and short-term travelers.
- **English-speaking medical care:** extensive across the private hospitals and clinics above.

### Yogyakarta

- **Police report intake:** **Yogyakarta Tourist Police** maintain a desk near the Tugu train station and at Malioboro's main stretch. The main *Yogyakarta Resor Police Station* (Jalan Reksobayan) handles formal *laporan polisi* filings.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Siloam Hospital Yogyakarta** (Jalan Laksda Adisucipto, +62 274 489 9000) is the main private international hospital. **Panti Rapih Hospital** (Jalan Cik Ditiro, +62 274 514 004) is a longer-established Catholic private hospital with English-speaking staff. **RSUP Dr. Sardjito** is the region's main public teaching hospital.
- **English-speaking medical care:** available at Siloam and Panti Rapih; more limited than Bali or Jakarta.

### Mount Bromo / Probolinggo

- **Police report intake:** the **Probolinggo Resor Police Station** (Jalan Panglima Sudirman, Probolinggo) handles formal filings for Bromo-area incidents. Dial **+62 21 3838 899** or the local Bromo national park office for guidance.
- **Major emergency hospital:** **RSU dr. Mohamad Saleh Probolinggo** (public) handles emergency care; serious cases are transferred to Malang (two hours by road) or to Surabaya (three hours).
- **Altitude medical note:** Bromo sits at 2,300 meters; altitude symptoms in susceptible visitors are common. Bring acetazolamide as a precaution.

### Ijen Crater / Banyuwangi

- **Police report intake:** **Banyuwangi Resor Police Station** (Jalan Ahmad Yani, Banyuwangi) handles formal filings. The Paltuding national park office has a ranger presence for on-site incidents.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **RSUD Blambangan Banyuwangi** (public) is the largest emergency department in the area. For serious cases, Bali-side hospitals (Denpasar) are accessible by ferry and road in about four hours.
- **Sulfur-exposure note:** the Ijen crater has routine SO₂ exposure; bring a quality N95 mask, and do not descend to the crater floor without a certified guide.

### Bali (Kuta / Seminyak / Canggu / Sanur / Denpasar)

- **Police report intake:** the **Bali Tourist Police (Polisi Pariwisata Bali)** maintain a main office at Jalan Padma No. 11, Kuta, Denpasar, with satellite desks at Ngurah Rai Airport, Seminyak's Jalan Raya Kerobokan, Sanur, and Ubud. The tourist-assistance hotline dispatcher will direct you to the nearest open desk.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospitals:** **BIMC Hospital Kuta** (Jalan Bypass Ngurah Rai 100X, Kuta, +62 361 761 263) and **BIMC Hospital Nusa Dua** (Kawasan BTDC Blok D, +62 361 300 0911) are the flagship international hospitals on the island, with hyperbaric chamber and dive-medicine expertise. **Siloam Hospital Denpasar** (Jalan Sunset Road, +62 361 779 900) and **Prime Plus Medical Bali** (Jalan Legian, +62 361 777 7766) are additional options. For public emergency care, **RSUP Sanglah** in Denpasar is the region's main teaching hospital.
- **Hyperbaric / dive medicine:** BIMC Kuta and BIMC Nusa Dua both coordinate recompression chamber access. For dive-related injuries, surface safely and call BIMC directly; the DAN Asia-Pacific 24-hour hotline (+61 8 8212 9242) is the global dive-medical consult line.
- **US Consular Agency:** Jalan Hayam Wuruk 188, Denpasar, +62 361 233 605.

### Ubud

- **Police report intake:** the **Ubud Police Station** (Jalan Andong, Ubud) handles filings. The Bali Tourist Police hotline coordinates with Ubud for tourist incidents.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Ubud Care Medical Services** (Jalan Monkey Forest, +62 361 974 911) handles walk-in urgent care. **Siloam Hospital Denpasar** (30 minutes by road) and **BIMC Hospital Nusa Dua** (60 minutes) are the nearest full hospitals.

### Seminyak

- **Police report intake:** **Seminyak Tourist Police desk** at Jalan Raya Kerobokan near the Seminyak-Kerobokan border; the Kuta main tourist police office is a 15-minute drive away.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Prime Plus Medical Bali** (Jalan Legian, Kuta, +62 361 777 7766) is the nearest international hospital at 10 minutes by road. **BIMC Hospital Kuta** is 15 minutes away.

### Nusa Penida

- **Police report intake:** **Nusa Penida Police Station** (Toyapakeh harbor area) handles filings. For serious incidents, Bali-side filing at Sanur or Kuta after the return ferry is often more practical.
- **Major emergency medical care:** Nusa Penida has limited public care; serious cases are transferred by fast-boat to **BIMC Sanur** (Sanur pier exit, +62 361 288 888) or **Siloam Hospital Denpasar**. For dive injuries, BIMC Nusa Dua has the nearest hyperbaric chamber — medical evacuation by boat or helicopter.
- **Scooter-injury note:** Nusa Penida's roads produce frequent spills; bring travel insurance with evacuation coverage for any scooter use.

### Gili Islands

- **Police report intake:** **Gili Trawangan Police Post** (near the main harbor) during high season; the nearest 24/7 station is on Lombok (Mataram).
- **Major emergency medical care:** Gili Meno Medical Center and Gili Trawangan Medical Clinic handle day-to-day care on the islands. Serious cases are evacuated to Lombok (Siloam Mataram) or to Bali (BIMC Kuta) by fast boat.
- **Diving-medical note:** Gili Trawangan has no hyperbaric chamber; the nearest is at BIMC Nusa Dua (Bali). DAN Asia-Pacific (+61 8 8212 9242) is the 24-hour dive-medical consult line.

### Lombok

- **Police report intake:** **Mataram Resor Police Station** (Jalan Langko, Mataram) and the **Lombok Tourist Police** at Senggigi handle filings.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Siloam Hospital Mataram** (Jalan Majapahit, +62 370 623 999) is the main private international hospital on Lombok. **RSUP NTB** is the region's main public teaching hospital.
- **Rinjani-trek note:** for trekking injuries, Rinjani National Park rangers coordinate with Senaru or Sembalun base-camp rescue; Basarnas (**115**) can evacuate by helicopter for serious injuries.

### Labuan Bajo / Komodo

- **Police report intake:** **Labuan Bajo Resor Police Station** (Jalan Soekarno-Hatta, central Labuan Bajo) handles filings.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Siloam Hospital Labuan Bajo** (opened 2023, Jalan Penjaringan, +62 385 242 4040) is the newest private hospital in Flores. **RSUD Komodo** (public) is the main emergency department. Serious cases are air-evacuated to Bali (BIMC Kuta) or Jakarta.
- **Boat-incident note:** Komodo water rescues go through Basarnas (**115**) or the harbor master at Labuan Bajo port.

### Batam

- **Police report intake:** **Polresta Barelang** (Jalan Laksamana Bintan, Sekupang, Batam) handles filings.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Awal Bros Hospital Batam** (Jalan Gajah Mada, +62 778 431 777) is the main private international hospital. **Santa Elisabeth Hospital** and **RSUD Embung Fatimah** are additional options. For short-stay Singapore-crossover travelers, returning to Singapore for medical care (via the ferry to Harbourfront or Tanah Merah) is often practical.

## If you lose your passport

1. **Report it first** — go to the nearest tourist police desk or ward police station and file a *laporan polisi* for a lost or stolen passport. You will leave with the signed and sealed report. This is the document your embassy wants to see before issuing an emergency replacement.
2. **Call your embassy's main switchboard** — or its after-hours line if it is a weekend — and request an emergency passport appointment. US, UK, Canadian, Irish, and Australian embassies in Jakarta (and Bali consular offices for the US, UK, and Australia) all issue same-day or next-day emergency travel documents; the fee varies.
3. **Bring** the *laporan polisi*, any photo ID you still have (a driver's license photo on your phone counts), passport photos (photo booths are common in Jakarta and Bali malls), and the payment method your embassy specifies.
4. **Immigration clearance for departure**: before flying out with an emergency travel document, you may need a short visit to **Indonesian Immigration** (Jakarta: Jalan H.R. Rasuna Said; Bali: Jalan Niti Mandala, Renon) to regularize your visa status — your embassy will advise. Airlines departing Soekarno-Hatta, Ngurah Rai, Komodo, and Lombok see emergency documents regularly; border police know the format.

## If your phone is stolen

1. **Freeze the SIM** by calling your carrier's customer-service line from any other phone. Indonesian carriers: **Telkomsel** 188 (in Indonesia) or +62 807 181 1811 (from abroad); **Indosat Ooredoo** 185 or +62 855 1000 185; **XL Axiata** 817 or +62 818 171 7. If you use a foreign SIM, call your home carrier's international line — most have a 24/7 theft-reporting number that forwards from Indonesia. Tourist eSIMs (Airalo, Saily, Holafly) can be suspended in-app.
2. **File a *laporan polisi*** at the nearest police station with your device's IMEI (if you recorded it) and its serial number. Insurance claims require the sealed report.
3. **Lock and wipe remotely** via Apple's Find My or Google's Find My Device from any browser. Do this before filing the *laporan polisi* — a wiped phone cannot leak your email and banking apps while you wait in line.
4. **Change passwords immediately** for any account whose app was installed on the phone: email, banking, social media. Start with email; everything else resets off it.

## If your credit card is stolen or skimmed

1. **Freeze the card** by calling the number on the back (or use the issuer's app's "lock card" feature, which works in seconds). This matters more than filing a police report first — every minute you wait is a minute the thief can use the card.
2. **Report to police** with a *laporan polisi*. Insurance reimbursement and the card issuer's fraud-dispute process both want the sealed report.
3. **Dispute the charges** with your issuer. Most European, US, Australian, and Japanese card issuers reverse fraudulent charges fully; the *laporan polisi* speeds the process.
4. **Watch for follow-on phishing** — a skimmed card often leads to email and SMS phishing pretending to be your bank. Never click a link in one of these messages; go to your bank's app or website directly.

## A closing note

This appendix is meant to be read once on the plane, screenshot for offline use, and forgotten about for the rest of your trip. Phone numbers change. Consulates move. Hospitals renumber their switchboards. If any number in this list fails, **+62 21 3838 899** — the Ministry of Tourism hotline — will still work, and your embassy's website will still have the current line for your country.

Travel safely, travel calmly, and if the worst happens, remember: a stolen phone is an inconvenience, not a crisis. Filing a *laporan polisi*, freezing a card, and calling an embassy are ordinary procedures — tourist-police officers and consular staff in Indonesia handle these cases every day, and most travelers resolve everything within a few hours and return to their trip. Indonesia is not a dangerous country; it is a practiced one. Help is close, and English is closer than you think.
