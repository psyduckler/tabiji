# How to Use This Book {-}

Indonesia is extraordinary. It is also, by the Ministry of Tourism's own admission, one of the most scam-prone countries in Southeast Asia for first-time visitors in 2024–2025. Bali's Ngurah Rai Airport runs a fake-Grab and taxi-mafia script the Bali Tourism Board posted multilingual warning signs about at the international arrivals exit. Jakarta's Blok M and Kemang nightlife zone operates an ongoing 7-million-rupiah "honeypot bar" extortion cycle the *Jakarta Post* calls a perennial problem for solo male travelers. Mount Bromo's Cemoro Lawang jeep-tour cartel and Ijen Crater's Paltuding mandatory-guide shakedown both run unchecked by Probolinggo and Banyuwangi local authorities. The national police general line (dial **110**) and the Ministry of Tourism's multilingual hotline (**+62 21 3838 899**) log tens of thousands of foreign-visitor complaints every year, concentrated on a dozen neighborhoods and a handful of island piers.

We want you to come home with stories about the nasi goreng, the Borobudur sunrise, the Komodo dragons, and the Ubud rice terraces — not about a 5-million-rupiah scooter "damage" claim on Nusa Penida or a drink-spiking incident at a Seminyak beach club.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents seventy-three specific scams that trip up first-time visitors to Indonesia, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been reported in the Indonesian press, cross-referenced against national police or local tourism-board records, and confirmed by recent traveler accounts on r/indonesia, r/bali, r/IndonesiaTravel, and the city- and island-specific subreddits.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First, the name of the scam, the category it falls into (pickpocket, transport, tourist trap, nightlife), and how serious and how common it is. Then two short sections: **How this scam works** — a plain narrative of how it unfolds in real time, with real Indonesian-rupiah amounts where we have them — and **How to avoid it**, the specific moves that shut it down. Each entry ends with where it happens and — for Indonesia — a four-panel Balinese Lontar palm-leaf-manuscript-style comic showing the exchange so you can spot the pattern visually, not just in words.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. Most scams in Indonesia are variations on six patterns. Learn the patterns and you will spot a new scam the moment it starts — even one that is not in this book. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will then read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters, you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **Indonesian (Bahasa Indonesia) exit-phrase card**. Short phrases you can say, word for word, at the moments when your politeness would normally get you into trouble. Bahasa Indonesia is Latin-scripted and non-tonal — the pronunciation guides are closer to how you would read Spanish or Italian than how you would read Vietnamese or Thai, and even a foreign accent carries the meaning clearly. Take a screenshot of it on your phone.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A step-by-step flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens, you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. Police, ambulance, embassies, and the nearest English-speaking medical care for every major Indonesian city and island, organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and skim the three appendices. Read the chapters for the cities and islands you will be visiting. Take a screenshot of the phrase card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book turns out to apply during your trip, open it again in the Grab car or in your hotel room and check what to do.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.

*Selamat datang, semoga berhasil* — welcome and good luck.
