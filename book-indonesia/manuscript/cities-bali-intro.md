Bali is Indonesia's flagship tourism destination — six million international visitors a year through Ngurah Rai Airport (DPS), a Kuta–Seminyak–Canggu–Ubud corridor packed with hotels and beach clubs, and the country's most developed and most well-documented tourist-scam ecosystem. The *DPS fake-Grab and taxi-mafia* script is the flagship: men in polo shirts and lanyards intercept arrivals inside the airport arrivals hall with "Taxi, fixed price, 300,000 rupiah to Seminyak" and block the Grab designated pickup zone with aggressive pacing and direct physical redirection. The real Grab fare from DPS to Seminyak is 70,000–120,000 rupiah, and the designated pickup zone is on the M-level parking floor (three-minute walk from arrivals exit). The *Bali Post* and *Tempo* have covered the airport taxi cartel for years; the defense is to walk past every lanyard-wearing figure, book in the Grab app before leaving the baggage area, and take the three-minute walk to the M-level zone.

Six further scams structure the Bali visit (Bali has seven total scams documented in this book — one more than most Indonesian cities because of the density of infrastructure). The *ATM skimmer fraud and card cloning* circuit is one of the most sophisticated in Southeast Asia — skimmers on freestanding ATMs across Kuta's Jalan Legian and Seminyak's Jalan Kayu Aya capture card data and PINs; the defense is ATMs only inside bank lobbies (BCA, BNI, Mandiri) during business hours, with the keypad covered and the transaction kept short. The *money-changer sleight-of-hand* works the "BEST RATE — 0% COMMISSION" boards along Legian and Seminyak — a teller appears to count the stack, then pulls a hidden slip-fold trick that shortchanges the final tally by 300,000–1,500,000 rupiah; use only PT Central Kuta or Dirgahayu Valuta Prima (branded booths with Bank Indonesia license posted), count every note yourself before handing over your dollars/euros, and leave if the teller wants to hand the stack across before you recount. The *scooter rental spare-key theft and pre-damage claim* is the island's signature rental scam — the shop photocopies the ignition key, then sends a confederate to "steal" the scooter while it's parked, and withholds the 2–3-million-rupiah security deposit; the defense is a walk-around video at rental, keys always in your own pocket, GPS tracker if possible, and never leaving the passport as deposit (cash only, ~1,000,000 rupiah). The *traffic police scooter bribe shakedown* stops foreign riders on rural roads and demands 200,000–500,000 rupiah to "avoid a real ticket"; say politely "*saya mau tilang*" ("I want the official ticket, please") and the officer either writes it (~150,000 fine at the court) or waves you on. The *"Bali tourist levy" / tourist tax confusion* — the real 150,000-rupiah Bali Tourist Levy is collected at the airport or through the official Love Bali app (*lovebali.baliprov.go.id*); websites that collect the levy at 300,000+ rupiah with "fast processing" are scam sites. Finally the *organized drink spiking at beach clubs* — Finns, Atlas, Potato Head, and Kuta's La Favela have all been the subject of reported incidents — the defense is the universal one: never accept an opened drink from a stranger, watch your glass, and use the club's official drink bracelets when they are offered.
