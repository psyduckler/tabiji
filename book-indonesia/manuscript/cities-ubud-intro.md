Ubud is Bali's spiritual-tourism hub — rice terraces, yoga retreats, the Sacred Monkey Forest, and the Tegallalang viewpoint. The scam economy runs on tourist-volume commission kickbacks rather than on the tour-operator cartels of Bromo or the rental scratches of Nusa Penida. The *Sacred Monkey Forest theft and aggressive monkeys* is the most likely problem a first-time Ubud visitor will encounter: the forest's long-tailed macaques are trained to recognize sunglasses, phones, and hats as valuable, and they execute fast grab-and-retreat raids that end with a "refund" at 50,000–100,000 rupiah negotiated with a nearby caretaker who is informally attached to the troop. The defense is to remove and bag all loose items before entering the forest, hold your phone below chest level at all times, and never carry food or sealed water bottles inside. The official rangers post notices at the entrance warning of these specific risks.

Five further scams structure the Ubud visit. The *kopi luwak "coffee plantation" animal-cruelty tour* runs out of Ubud's tourist-cafe strip — operators quote a "free plantation visit" and route tourists to small compounds where caged civet cats live in poor conditions, then sell 300,000–900,000-rupiah packages of "authentic" kopi luwak that is usually regular coffee with a premium label. Skip both the tour and the purchase. The *fake-guru yoga retreat and spiritual-healing scam* targets travelers through Instagram and Airbnb Experiences, offering "ancient Balinese healing" at 500–2,000 US dollars per retreat — the legitimate Ubud yoga scene runs through established centers (Radiantly Alive, The Yoga Barn, Intuitive Flow), all of which list public class schedules and accept drop-in walk-ins; any guru requiring a 1,200-dollar non-refundable deposit and no public class schedule is a fraud. The *Ubud taxi-mafia and anti-Grab blockade* is a real local informal agreement — taxi-tout networks on Jalan Hanoman, Jalan Monkey Forest, and Jalan Raya physically block Grab drivers who attempt pickups inside the blockade zone; the workaround is to walk 200 meters outside the visible blockade line (toward Campuhan Ridge Walk or toward Sayan) and re-book, or to use the hotel car service for the Tegallalang, Monkey Forest, or Ubud-to-airport runs. The *pushy massage and "extended service" upcharge* works walk-in spas along Jalan Monkey Forest and Jalan Hanoman where a posted 150,000-rupiah massage becomes a 500,000 "with extended treatments" tab; book only at established spas (Karsa Spa, Taksu, Sang Spa) with posted menus, and say "just the menu item, thank you" if an upsell starts mid-session. Finally the *Tegallalang rice terrace "mandatory guide" and photo-spot fees* works the terrace entrance — a man in a vest tells arrivals the terrace now requires a guide at 150,000–400,000 rupiah per person, and then charges 50,000 per "photo spot" at three separate viewpoints; the real entrance fee is 25,000 rupiah, no guide is required, and the "photo spot" fees are local farmer tips (5,000–10,000) if you use their bamboo swings, not viewpoint-entry charges.
