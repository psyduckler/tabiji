Jakarta is Indonesia's political and economic capital — twenty-five million people across the greater metropolitan area, an airport (Soekarno-Hatta, CGK) that handles most first-time arrivals, and a scam ecosystem concentrated on the airport-to-city run, the Kota Tua (Old Town) tourist circuit, and the Blok M / Kemang nightlife strip. The *CGK airport shark taxi* is the flagship: men in non-uniformed lanyards approach arrivals with "Taxi, fixed price, 600,000 rupiah to city" when the real metered Blue Bird run is 180,000–220,000 including tolls. The *Jakarta Post* has covered the airport-shark crew for a decade, and the official Blue Bird taxi desk just inside the terminal exit is the workaround — a uniformed desk agent takes the booking, hands you a printed card with the driver's license plate, and sends you to a marked curb where the real taxi is waiting.

The risk radiates from the airport into three Jakarta corridors. The *Blue Bird impersonator and meter-tampering* script runs on Jalan Thamrin, Sudirman, and outside Grand Indonesia mall — near-identical "Pusaka" or "Blue Pearl" taxis with tampered meters that run triple normal speed; the defense is to use the Grab app directly or to look for the genuine Blue Bird logo (silver on pale blue, with the word "GROUP" in small italic) and verify the meter starts at 7,500 rupiah. The *Grab/Gojek off-app cash-negotiation* script runs on late-night returns from Kemang and Blok M — a driver accepts the app booking and then, mid-ride, suggests "we just pay cash, cancel the app, good price"; the tell is the cancellation request, the defense is to insist on finishing the ride on-app. The *ATM skimming and card-cloning* circuit works the bank rows on Jalan Jaksa and the Sarinah-Thamrin corridor — cover the keypad, avoid freestanding ATMs, use ATMs inside bank lobbies or inside reputable malls (Plaza Indonesia, Grand Indonesia, Pacific Place). The *Kota Tua becak / bajaj "free tour"* script starts at Fatahillah Square with a friendly offer to "show you the old Portuguese cannon, no charge" and ends at four commission-paying antique shops and café stops with a 500,000-rupiah tab at the end; the polite defense is to decline from the start and use the marked Kota Tua Walking Tour (free, map at the tourism info kiosk). Finally the *Blok M / Kemang nightlife honeypot bar and Tinder extortion* circuit pairs a matched local date with a pre-selected bar where the tab inflates to 4–7 million rupiah with "hostess," "service," and "VIP room" charges the menu didn't show — the defense is the HCMC / Bangkok rule: never follow a stranger to an unchosen venue, pick your own bar (TripAdvisor-rated, menu on the wall, lit well), and if a bill is padded, ask for the menu and call **110** on speakerphone.
