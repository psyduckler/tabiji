Seminyak is Bali's upscale beach-club strip — the Jalan Kayu Aya (Eat Street) and Legian corridor, the Finns and Potato Head clubs, and a higher-end demographic that draws higher-stakes scams. The *money-changer sleight-of-hand on Jalan Kayu Aya and the Legian corridor* is the flagship: "BEST RATE — 0% COMMISSION" boards along the main road quote exchange rates 40–60 rupiah above the real Bank Indonesia rate, and the teller uses a hidden slip-fold trick to shortchange the final stack by 500,000–2,000,000 rupiah. The scale is larger here than in Kuta because the Seminyak demographic changes more in single transactions (500–1,000 US dollars at once). The defense is universal: use only PT Central Kuta or Dirgahayu Valuta Prima branded booths with a Bank Indonesia license posted on the wall, count every rupiah note yourself in full view of the teller, and leave if the teller tries to hand the stack across before you recount. Local Seminyak expat forums publish running lists of the current worst-offender booths on Instagram under #balimoneychangerscam.

Five further scams shape the Seminyak visit. The *beach-club drink spiking at Finns, Atlas, and Potato Head* has been the subject of multiple incident reports in 2024–2025 — the scale makes Seminyak clubs particularly attractive targets for theft and assault; the defense is the universal one (never accept an opened drink, watch your glass, use bracelet identification) plus the Seminyak-specific rule: go in a group, pre-book safe return transport (hotel car or Grab), and the clubs' own in-house security can be called via any bartender. The *villa rental fake-listing and booking-platform fraud* is particularly active in Seminyak's higher-end villa market — Instagram accounts and direct-message WhatsApp "hosts" advertise "exclusive Seminyak villas" at prices 30–40% below market and vanish after a 30% bank transfer deposit; book only through Airbnb, Booking.com, Agoda, or Expedia, never through Instagram DM, and when a "host" offers a "20% discount for bank transfer," recognize it as the off-platform fraud signal. The *beauty salon "add-on service" overcharge and card-fraud overlay* quotes a 200,000-rupiah pedicure and bills 900,000 with "nail art" and "luxury treatment" line items the customer never agreed to — and often captures the credit-card terminal for a follow-on cloning attempt; book at established salons (Cozy Nail Spa, Glo Nails, Sundari Day Spa) with posted itemized menus, refuse any credit-card terminal that is not clearly bank-branded, and check the receipt line by line before signing. The *tailor "custom suit" bait-and-switch* quotes 2,500,000–4,000,000 rupiah for a "full custom wool suit" with fabric samples that look premium and delivers a polyester-blend suit made from a different bolt; cross-reference three shops, ask to see and touch the actual bolt you are ordering from, get a fitting schedule in writing, and pay by credit card. Finally the *massage/spa "extended service" pressure and card-fraud overlay* — some spa/massage venues along Jalan Kayu Aya combine the "extended service" upsell pressure with the same card-terminal cloning risk as the beauty salons; book at spas with fixed menus (Bodyworks, Jari Menari) and decline any upsell before sitting down.
