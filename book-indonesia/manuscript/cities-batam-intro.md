Batam is the Riau Archipelago island 40 minutes by ferry from Singapore — the Nagoya Hill duty-free strip, the Barelang Bridge, and the weekend-getaway economy that draws a different scam demographic than the rest of Indonesia (heavy Singapore-Malaysia crossover, mostly short-stay). The *Batam ferry from Singapore Harbourfront/Tanah Merah overcharge and "tourist tax" scam* is the first-contact scam: at the Batam Center ferry terminal, arriving passengers are sometimes intercepted by men in lanyards at the customs exit who collect an "Indonesia tourist tax" of 150,000–300,000 rupiah that does not exist. The real Indonesia entry fee for short-stay Singapore/Malaysia passport holders is Rp 0 (visa-on-arrival is free for these passports up to 30 days), and the only legitimate fee at arrival is the Batam Center terminal fee (150,000 rupiah, posted on a printed sign at the ferry counter) — which was already built into your Singapore-side ferry ticket. Walk past any lanyard-wearing figure collecting "taxes" at the arrivals exit.

Five further scams shape the Batam visit. The *Batam Airport (BTH) and hotel-transfer taxi overcharge* quotes 250,000–500,000 rupiah for the short run to Nagoya Hill when the real Grab (or Gojek) rate is 80,000–130,000; Grab works at the designated pickup zone 100 meters from the arrivals exit. The *Batam massage parlour / KTV "plus plus" extras extortion* is the island's biggest scam category — weekend-crossover Singapore-Malaysia visitors are the target, with posted 300,000-rupiah massage rates that escalate mid-service to 2,000,000+ rupiah with "extras" tabs the customer didn't request; book at established hotel-chain spas (the Harris, Swiss-Belhotel, Best Western have in-house spas) with posted menus, decline any street-solicitation venue (especially late-night karaoke "VIP" invitations), and never hand over a passport or credit card to any massage operator before the session ends. The *Batam money-changer sleight-of-hand and street-corner exchange fraud* works the Nagoya Hill strip — booths with handwritten rate boards and no Bank Indonesia license badge do the same shortchange trick as Bali's Legian, but with SGD and MYR transactions the stakes are higher (single transactions often 500–1,500 SGD); use only PT Central Kuta branches or the money-changer counter inside Nagoya Hill Mall or BCP Mall, and count every note yourself before handing over the dollars. The *Batam duty-free / shopping mall street-tout commission scam* runs outside Nagoya Hill and BCP malls — touts offer to "guide you to the best shops" and walk you to partner stores where electronics, perfume, and branded goods are marked up 40–80% above the mall's own shops; decline the tout and use the mall directory. Finally the *Batam ATM skimming and credit-card fraud* network concentrates on the Nagoya Hill night corridor — use ATMs only inside BCA, BNI, or Mandiri bank lobbies during business hours, and cover the keypad on every withdrawal; dispute any unusual charges with your card issuer immediately.
