Sharm El Sheikh is built around the all-inclusive resort: a 30-kilometer ribbon of walled hotels along Naama Bay, Hadaba, and Sharks Bay, with most guests on a one-week package that limits exposure to local touts but concentrates the risk in a few predictable venues. Six documented scams, two rated high-severity. The Dodgy Dive Operator on the Naama-to-Sharks Bay strip runs intro Red Sea dives at $15 — a third of the established-operator rate — with regulators that taste metallic, BCDs that won't hold air, and "instructors" who can't produce a PADI card; the dive ends 15 minutes in with a $50 upcharge demand. The Romance Scam (Bezness) singles out solo travelers at hotel pool bars on day one, runs a 24-to-48-hour intimacy script, and lands the first $200 money request before you fly home — the opening move of a long-con that escalates over months once you're back. The Desert Safari Bait-and-Switch turns a $30 Sinai bundle into a $120 evening of fuel surcharges, photo fees, and a stale-bread "Bedouin dinner."

The risk is concentrated along the Naama Bay promenade and the SOHO Square nightlife strip — the desert safari kiosks, the dive shops, and the nightclub drink-inflate routine all run here, where a 800-EGP bill for four beers materializes after a "free first drink" flyer. Hadaba and Naama Bay hotel pool bars carry the bezness opener. The SH-65 desert road to Ras Mohammed National Park is the venue for the Closure Con — a performative phone call halfway to the park, then a redirect to a cousin's "private beach" with a folding-table cash collector. Sharks Bay marina runs the snorkeling-boat hustle: 40 people on a 20-person boat, 90 minutes idling at a floating shopping platform before 20 rushed minutes at a no-name reef.

A safe default for first-timers: dive Sharm only with Camel Dive Club, Sinai Divers, or Red Sea Diving College; verify the instructor's PADI or SSI number on the certifying body's online check tool before booking; and never send money to someone you met on holiday, regardless of the reason.
