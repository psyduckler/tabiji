# The Scams: City by City {-}

The chapters that follow document forty-three scams reported across seven Egyptian cities. Cities are ordered by the typical first-time itinerary — starting with Cairo and the Giza plateau, moving south to Luxor and Aswan along the classic Nile route, then east to the Red Sea resort coast at Hurghada and Sharm El Sheikh, west to Alexandria on the Mediterranean, and finally to Dahab on the Sinai's quieter shore.

If you are traveling to only a few of these cities, skip directly to the relevant chapters. If you are visiting more, skim them all — many scams (the camel-handler hostage, the felucca bait-and-switch, the "government-licensed" papyrus stop, the dollar-versus-pound currency switch at the curb, and the made-up site closure) appear in multiple cities with local variations. Knowing the pattern in Cairo makes you faster to spot it in Luxor or Hurghada.

## The Seven Cities at a Glance {-}

![Cairo — the Giza pyramids and the Sphinx at golden hour](assets/cities/cairo.jpg)

**Cairo** — The Giza pyramids, the Egyptian Museum, Khan el-Khalili, and the country's busiest scam ecosystem. The Camel Ride Hostage on the Giza plateau, where a 50-EGP photo flips to 500 once the camel rises; the Fake Tourist Police "registration fee" outside the Solar Boat Museum; the Papyrus and "Government Shop" Trap kickback chain run by Khan el-Khalili drivers; orange-vest taxi extortion at Cairo International quoting $50 for a 250-EGP ride; the felucca bait-and-switch on the Garden City Corniche; the fake photo fee inside the Egyptian Museum.

![Luxor — Karnak Temple's columned hall above the Nile](assets/cities/luxor.jpg)

**Luxor** — Karnak, the Valley of the Kings, the West Bank temples, and the densest scam-per-tomb ratio in this book. The Caleche Bait-and-Switch on the Corniche outside the Winter Palace Hotel; the Fake Temple Guide in the dim corridors of Ramesses VI's tomb; the West Bank Closed Attraction Redirect to alabaster workshops; the Photo Op Shakedown where a guard "looks the other way" then demands 200 pounds; the Felucca Wind Tax that activates a hidden outboard motor; the Broken Taxi Meter at Luxor International, where Uber does not operate.

![Aswan — feluccas at sunset on the Nile near Elephantine Island](assets/cities/aswan.jpg)

**Aswan** — Philae Temple, the Nubian Museum, Elephantine Island, and the most coordinated boat-and-taxi cartels in Upper Egypt. The Philae Temple Boat Gouge at the Shellal dock, where a 10-EGP posted rate becomes 500 across every boatman on the line; the airport-rank ambush quoting 500 EGP for a ride that should run 150; the Felucca Bait-and-Pressure that turns a sunset sail into a multi-day cruise pitch; the Spice Souk Switch that pours from a different bottle than the one you smelled; the Nubian Village tourist gauntlet at Gharb Soheil; the dollar-versus-pound taxi switch between the Nubian Museum and the train station.

![Hurghada — Red Sea reef and dive boats off Sahl Hasheesh](assets/cities/hurghada.jpg)

**Hurghada** — The Red Sea's biggest package-tourism gateway, with seven distinct scams clustered along the El Mamsha promenade and El Dahar old town. The Airport Visa Counter Scam where a Coral Travel "rep" charges $35 for a $25 visa; the Snorkeling Boat Trip Bait and Switch that produces a fishing boat instead of a catamaran; the Camel and Horse Ride Ransom on the El Mamsha beach; the All-Inclusive Wristband Shop Scam where merchants pose as hotel staff; the "government-certified" Perfume and Papyrus Factory Tour; counterfeit British pounds and short-changed Moroccan dirham coins at El Dahar exchanges; and the Resort Doctor who diagnoses £1,500 worth of treatment from a sunburn.

![Sharm El Sheikh — Naama Bay promenade and turquoise reef](assets/cities/sharm-el-sheikh.jpg)

**Sharm El Sheikh** — Naama Bay, Sharks Bay, and Ras Mohammed National Park, with six of the most-reported Sinai resort scams. The Desert Safari Bait-and-Switch where the $30 brochure becomes a $120 evening; the Dodgy Dive Operator on the Naama Bay strip where the regulator fails at fifteen meters; the Nightclub Drink Inflate where four beers cost 800 EGP and a bouncer drifts toward the door; the Snorkeling Boat Hustle that detours to a souvenir platform instead of Ras Mohammed; the Ras Mohammed Closure Con where a performative phone call redirects you to a private beach; and the long-con Bezness romance scam at the resort pool bar.

![Alexandria — the Qaitbay Citadel above the Eastern Harbor](assets/cities/alexandria.jpg)

**Alexandria** — The Bibliotheca Alexandrina, the Qaitbay Citadel, the Corniche seafood district, and the smallest, quietest scam set in this book. The Corniche street-taxi fare-renegotiation between San Stefano and the Mahatet Misr Station, where a 50-pound fare yells to 100 by Sidi Gaber; the Borg El Arab arrivals trap with its single Euronet ATM and rank drivers wanting 1,500 EGP for a 500-EGP ride; the freelance-guide tail at the Qaitbay Citadel and the Bibliotheca; the Corniche fish-restaurant no-menu price gouge between Bahary and Stanley; and the Cairo–Alexandria train ticket-booth tourist price markup.

![Dahab — the Blue Hole and Sinai mountains behind palm-shaded shore](assets/cities/dahab.jpg)

**Dahab** — The Blue Hole, Bells, the Canyon, and the Mashraba bungalow strip, with six scams aimed at the Sinai's quieter dive-and-backpacker market. The Blue Hole Dive-Op Safety Cuts where a $25 intro dive lands you on a reef with a documented death toll in the hundreds; the Sharm Airport to Dahab Taxi Quote Switch that flips $70 into $80 with fictitious "checkpoint fees"; the Mashraba Bedouin Tea-Camp Bill Inflation where free tea becomes a 1,200-EGP platter; the Bungalow Hidden-Charge Trap that adds 600 EGP in towel, AC, and water fees on checkout; the Camel Ride on the Promenade Ransom; and the Assalah Souk Currency Short-Count where the stack is two notes light at the high-speed count.
