Cairo is the volume's anchor city for a reason: six documented scams across four categories, three of them rated high-severity, and almost all of them clustered around a handful of monuments any first-time visitor will see in the first 48 hours. The Camel Ride Hostage at Giza pivots from a 50-pound quote to a 500-pound demand the moment the camel rises with your kid in the saddle. The Fake Tourist Police take $30 cash at a side desk near the Solar Boat Museum for a "registration" that does not exist. The Taxi No-Meter Extortion turns a 150-to-250-pound metered Zamalek run into a $50 dollar quote outside Terminal 2 arrivals. Every variant works on the same lever — Cairo's tourist sites are big, confusing, and dense with men in lanyards who can pass for staff for the 90 seconds it takes to extract a fee.

The risk is concentrated in three zones: the Giza plateau between the Sphinx ticket gate and the Pyramid of Khafre, the Tahrir Square corridor around the Egyptian Museum, and Cairo International Terminal 2 arrivals. Add the Khan el-Khalili tour route, where drivers pull off at "government-licensed" papyrus institutes that pay a 30 percent kickback on banana-leaf prints sold for $150. The Nile Corniche between Garden City and Dokki carries the felucca bait-and-switch — a 100-pound sunset cruise that grows a captain tip, an assistant share, and a fuel surcharge on a boat with no engine.

A safe default for first-timers: book Uber or Careem from the airport Wi-Fi before your luggage hits the curb (both apps quote fixed prices in Egyptian pounds and operate at Cairo International), refuse to mount any animal at Giza until the full price is typed on your phone screen, and treat every man in a navy polo and a printed lanyard as a freelancer until proven otherwise.
