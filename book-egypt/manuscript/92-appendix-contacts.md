# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication (verified 2026-04-27). Phone numbers do change — if a number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus *Egypt* and you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results. Where a number could not be verified against an official source, we have omitted it rather than guess; the entry instead points you to the search you should run before you travel.

**Before you travel**, we strongly recommend you double-check your own embassy or consulate's current number directly on its official website (the US State Department's travel.state.gov, the UK's gov.uk, Canada's travel.gc.ca, Australia's smartraveller.gov.au, Germany's auswaertiges-amt.de, France's diplomatie.gouv.fr). Save the number and the after-hours emergency line on your phone before departure. Even a verified number can become a wrong number between editions, and embassies sometimes change their main switchboards without notice.

## National (anywhere in Egypt)

- **Police:** **122**. The general police emergency line, free from any phone in Egypt, with or without a SIM card.
- **Tourist & Antiquities Police (Tourist Police):** **126**. The dedicated branch handling incidents involving foreign visitors — tourist-trap overcharging, freelance "guides" demanding fees, taxi-and-felucca disputes, and any incident at a temple, pyramid, museum, or other antiquity site. **This is the single most important number for the scam patterns in this book.** The Tourist Police operate kiosks at every major tourist gate (Giza Pyramids, the Egyptian Museum, Karnak, Luxor Temple, Valley of the Kings, the Citadel of Saladin, Khan El Khalili, Bibliotheca Alexandrina, the dive-resort marinas in Hurghada and Sharm El Sheikh) and most tourist-area officers speak English. If a scam is in progress, walking to the nearest Tourist Police kiosk often ends it before you have to make the call.
- **Ambulance:** **123**. Free from any phone. State your location as a landmark or hotel name rather than a street address — *"Anglo-American Hospital, Zamalek"* is more useful to a dispatcher than a transliterated street name.
- **Fire:** **180**.
- **Pan-Egyptian unified emergency:** **112**. Mobile phones in Egypt will dial 112 even without a SIM, with a locked screen, or with zero balance, provided there is any network signal. 112 routes to the same dispatch system as 122/123/180.

## Online crime reporting

Egypt has no online crime-reporting portal equivalent to Italy's *denunceonline*. The formal report — called a *mahdar* (محضر) in Arabic — must be filed in person at the nearest police station, where an officer will write up the complaint by hand and you will sign each page. For tourist incidents, the Tourist Police kiosk at a major site can sometimes handle a *mahdar* on the spot; for incidents away from a major site, the nearest neighborhood police station is the right destination. Carry your passport — every *mahdar* requires it. If you need to catch a flight or a Nile cruise the same day, the Tourist Police kiosk is your faster route; the neighborhood station is slower but unavoidable for higher-value reports (lost passport, theft of valuable items, fraud disputes that involve a bank).

If your card was used fraudulently, file the *mahdar* first and call your card issuer second — the issuer's fraud team will ask for the report number when they refund the charge.

- **American Express International Service Center:** +1-905-474-0870. 24/7.

## Tourist Police regional offices

The Tourist & Antiquities Police number — **126** — works from any phone anywhere in Egypt. The kiosks below are the in-person offices to walk to when a scam is unfolding at a specific site. Operating hours generally match the gate hours of the attraction they serve.

- **Cairo (headquarters):** Mohamed Ali Palace, El Manyal, Roda Island, Cairo. Additional kiosks at the Egyptian Museum (Tahrir Square), the Giza Pyramids (the entrance plaza on Pyramids Road), Khan El Khalili (the bazaar's main alley near the Hussein Mosque), and the Citadel of Saladin (the main gate).
- **Alexandria:** Tourist Police kiosks operate at the Bibliotheca Alexandrina entrance plaza, the Qaitbay Citadel approach, and Mahatet Misr (Misr Station). The municipal Tourist Police office sits on the Corniche near Saad Zaghloul Square.
- **Luxor:** Tourist Police kiosks at Karnak Temple (the entrance), Luxor Temple (the Avenue of Sphinxes side), the Valley of the Kings (the central rest-house), and Luxor Train Station. The municipal office is on Television Street near the river.
- **Aswan:** Tourist Police kiosks at the Aswan High Dam visitor area, Philae Temple ferry dock, and the Corniche felucca docks. The municipal office is on the Corniche near the EgyptAir office.
- **Hurghada:** Tourist Police kiosks at Hurghada International Airport arrivals, the Marina (downtown), and the Sahl Hasheesh and Makadi Bay resort gates. The municipal office sits in El Dahar near the General Hospital.
- **Sharm El Sheikh:** Tourist Police kiosks at Sharm El Sheikh International Airport arrivals, Naama Bay (the boardwalk), Soho Square, and the Old Market. The municipal Tourist Police office is in Hadaba near City Hall.
- **Dahab:** Tourist Police kiosk at the Masbat boardwalk (next to Dahab Central Hospital and the main police station). For dive-related incidents, the South Sinai Tourist Police regional office in Sharm El Sheikh is the escalation point.

If you cannot find a kiosk, dial **126** from any phone — operators will route the nearest unit to your location. State the closest landmark, not a street address.

If you can travel by taxi or Uber, the hospitals below are the standard tourist-medical destinations in each city, with English-speaking emergency-department staff. ### Other cities

For book cities not listed above, dial **123** for an ambulance and say *"English, please."* Major hospitals in every Egyptian governorate capital have at least one English-speaking doctor on staff, especially in their emergency departments. Your fallback is to ask your hotel's front desk to call on your behalf — concierges in tourist hotels routinely route guests to the right English-speaking doctor and they will accompany you if the situation warrants it.

## Major airports

Egypt's main international airports are listed below. Two of them — Hurghada (HRG) and Sharm El Sheikh (SSH) — are the airports where this book documents arrivals-hall scams: dynamic-currency-conversion ATM pulls, taxi-rank price-gouging at three to four times the in-app rate, and customs-counter cash demands for fees that do not exist on any official tariff. Read the relevant city chapters before you fly into either.

- **Cairo International Airport (CAI):** the country's main hub, three terminals (T1, T', T). Tourist Police kiosk at arrivals; Bank Misr and National Bank of Egypt counters in arrivals for visa-on-arrival ($25, exact USD); Uber, Careem, and Indrive all serve the airport-curb pickup zones.
- **Borg El Arab Airport (HBE)** (Alexandria): the city's main airport, 40–50 km southwest of central Alexandria. Use Uber or Indrive from arrivals; the documented Alexandria arrivals-hall pattern is a Euronet-only Terminal 2 ATM and a taxi-rank that quotes 3–4x the in-app rate. Bank Misr at Terminal 1 has fee-free withdrawals up to 4,000 EGP per transaction.
- **Luxor International Airport (LXR):** small, single-terminal. Pre-arranged hotel transfers and Uber are the standard tourist routes.
- **Aswan International Airport (ASW):** small, single-terminal. Pre-arranged hotel transfers are the standard route; Uber coverage is thin.
- **Hurghada International Airport (HRG):** the Red Sea hub; **the airport where the documented arrivals-hall ATM and taxi-rank pulls are most aggressive.** Pre-book your transfer through your hotel or resort before you fly, walk past the curb-side touts, and use the official airport taxi window (inside the arrivals hall) or a pre-booked transfer. Decline every dynamic-currency-conversion offer at the ATM screen.
- **Sharm El Sheikh International Airport (SSH):** the South Sinai hub; **the second airport where the same arrivals-hall scam pattern is documented.** Same defenses as Hurghada — pre-book your transfer, walk past the curb, decline DCC at the ATM, refuse any customs cash demand that arrives without a numbered receipt and ask to be escorted to the duty-free supervisor.

## A note on calling 122 / 123 / 126 with limited Arabic

If you dial 122, 123, or 126 and cannot explain what is happening in Arabic, say *"English, please"* clearly and wait. Egyptian emergency dispatchers in tourist areas are trained to recognize this request and will bring an English-speaking operator onto the line. State your location as a landmark — *"Giza Pyramids, main gate,"* *"Karnak Temple entrance,"* *"Sharm El Sheikh, Naama Bay boardwalk"* — rather than as a street address that is hard to pronounce. If you are calling from a mobile phone, the dispatcher can sometimes pull your approximate location automatically, so even a rough *"I am near the Egyptian Museum"* is enough to get help moving.

For English-speaking tourists who have just been scammed or pickpocketed, the magic word after *"English, please"* is *"mahdar"* — you want a formal police report, and that is the word that tells every dispatcher and officer what to do next. Pair it with *"Tourist Police"* (or its Arabic, *shorta siyaheyya*) if the incident happened at a temple, museum, hotel, or any other tourist-facing site — you will be routed to officers trained to handle exactly that kind of report.
