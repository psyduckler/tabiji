Alexandria runs lighter than Cairo, Luxor, or the Red Sea resorts. Five documented scams, two rated high-severity, and the city's monument-tourism intensity is a fraction of what you encounter at Giza or the Valley of the Kings — most arriving travelers are on a day trip from Cairo and never spend a night, which compresses the scam window and limits how aggressively any operator can run a long script. The headline pull is the Corniche street-taxi fare-renegotiation: black-and-yellow taxis run almost no meters, accept a verbal 50-to-80-pound fare at the door, and yell for double once you arrive at Mahatet Misr Station or the Bibliotheca Alexandrina drop-off. The Borg El Arab arrivals trap stacks two extractions — the Euronet ATM in Terminal 2 with dynamic-currency-conversion markups, and the airport rank that quotes 1,200 to 1,500 EGP for a downtown ride that runs 500 on Uber. The Cairo–Alexandria train ticket booth at Mahatet Misr quotes tourists 20 to 50 percent above the published Telago rate, the smallest documented Alexandria pull and the easiest to defeat by booking through a hotel concierge.

The risk is concentrated along the Corniche promenade between San Stefano and Stanley Bridge, at the Sidi Gaber and Mahatet Misr taxi ranks, and on the Bibliotheca Alexandrina entrance plaza where the freelance-guide tail intercepts you in the ticket queue and asks for 200 to 800 EGP at the end. The Qaitbay Citadel approach gate runs the same play. The Bahary-to-Stanley seafood strip carries the no-menu fish house gouge — the 600-pound fish at the iced display becomes 1,800 on the bill, with a 12 percent service charge and per-cover bread, dips, and tea added uninvited.

A safe default for first-timers: open Uber, InDrive, or Careem before walking to the curb at Borg El Arab and pay only in app, refuse the first English-language opener at the Citadel and Bibliotheca approaches, and decline any Corniche fish house that cannot show a printed per-kilogram price list before the fish leaves the ice.
