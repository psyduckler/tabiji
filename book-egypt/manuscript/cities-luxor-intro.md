Luxor's scam ecosystem is shaped by a single fact: the city is a temple, and almost every tourist arriving here is on a tight one-or-two-day window built around the Valley of the Kings, Karnak, and Luxor Temple. Seven documented scams cluster tightly around that itinerary. The Caleche Bait-and-Switch turns a 20-pound carriage ride along the Corniche el-Nil into a 200-pound demand at the temple gate, with the currency ambiguity of "pounds" doing the heavy lifting. The Fake Temple Guide in a galabiya and a homemade lanyard walks you through a Valley of the Kings tomb interior for ten minutes, then demands 200 pounds in the narrow corridor between you and the exit. The Closed Attraction Redirect — the most-cited Luxor scam on r/Egypt — has a friendly local at the West Bank ferry landing tell you the Valley is "closed for restoration," then steer you to an alabaster workshop where a 200-pound vase costs 1,500.

The risk is concentrated on the West Bank tour loop between the ferry landing, Hatshepsut Temple, and the Valley of the Kings — every alabaster workshop, every fake guide, every photo-fee shakedown runs along this corridor — and on the East Bank Corniche stretch between the Winter Palace Hotel and Luxor Temple, where the caleche and felucca crews work the curb. The Broken Taxi Meter pivot at Luxor International Arrivals lands a 300-pound quote on a metered fare that runs 50 to 80, and Luxor is one of the few Egyptian cities where Uber does not operate.

A safe default for first-timers: install InDrive before your flight lands (it shows the local fair-price band for Luxor routes), tell every driver before departure "no shops, direct to the site only," and refuse every unsolicited "guide" approach inside paid sites with a firm "La, shukran." The Ministry of Tourism's licensed guides carry photo IDs.
