# How to Use This Book {-}

Egypt is extraordinary. The Giza plateau, Karnak's columned hall, the Valley of the Kings, a felucca crossing the Nile at Aswan, the reefs of Ras Mohammed and the Blue Hole — there is nothing else like it on the planet. It is also, by a long way, one of the most scam-prone destinations a first-time traveler will visit. Camel handlers at the Sphinx ticket gate flip a fifty-pound photo into a five-hundred-pound demand once your child is four meters in the air. Men in tan polos with print-shop lanyards collect a "registration fee" outside the Egyptian Museum that does not exist. At Hurghada Airport, a Coral Travel "rep" charges $35 for a visa the bank counter sells for $25. Boatmen at the Shellal dock dismiss the official 10-pound rate to Philae Island and quote 500. Dive shops on the Naama Bay strip rent out regulators that fail at twelve meters.

We want you to come home with stories about the painted ceilings inside Ramesses VI's tomb, the call to prayer over the Cairo skyline, the silence of the desert above Dahab, and the taste of fresh karkadeh on the Aswan Corniche — not about a $50 airport taxi, a £1,500 resort doctor's bill, or a felucca captain who would not row back to the dock until the math doubled.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents forty-three specific scams that trip up first-time visitors across seven Egyptian cities, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been reported by recent travelers on r/Egypt, r/EgyptTravel, and r/EgyptTravelTips, cross-referenced against published investigations from One Mile at a Time, Discover Hurghada, and Tragento, and confirmed against embassy advisories and traveler-forum threads. Where dollar amounts appear, they are the amounts the scam actually charges. Where Egyptian pound rates appear, they are what the metered or posted price should be.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First, the name of the scam, the category it falls into (transport, overcharging, tourist trap, counterfeit, and so on), and how serious and how common it is. Then two short sections: **How this scam works** — a plain narrative of how it unfolds in real time, with real Egyptian-pound and US-dollar amounts where we have them — and **How to avoid it**, the specific moves that shut it down. Each entry ends with where it happens. If you can read one entry, you can read them all.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. The forty-three scams across these seven cities may sound different from each other, but almost all of them are variations on six patterns: the handler who literally restrains you on a camel or a felucca until you pay more than agreed; the stranger in an "official" costume; the currency mismatch at the ATM, the market, and the visa counter; the driver-and-shop kickback chain; the made-up site closure that redirects you to a commission shop; and the all-inclusive bundle whose inclusions evaporate once you board. Learn the six patterns and you will spot a new scam the moment it starts — even one we have not written down. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will then read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters, you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **Arabic exit-phrase card**. Short phrases you can say, word for word, at the moments when your politeness would normally get you into trouble — the phrase for "no thank you," the phrase for "I have already paid," the phrase for "take me to the police station." Take a screenshot of it on your phone.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A step-by-step flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens — a felucca that will not return to the dock, a dive operator who pockets your deposit, a resort doctor whose bill arrives in pounds sterling — you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. Tourist Police (126), general police (122), ambulance, the US Embassy in Cairo (+20 2-2797-3300), the Egyptian Consumer Protection hotline (19588), and English-speaking medical care for each of the seven cities, organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and skim the three appendices. Read the chapters for the cities you will be visiting. Take a screenshot of the phrase card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book turns out to apply during your trip, open it again in the taxi or in your hotel room and check what to do.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.

*Ma'a salama* — go in peace.
