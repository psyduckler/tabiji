Aswan is the Nile cruise corridor's southern bookend, and the scam profile reflects it: six documented hustles, every one of them keyed to a transit chokepoint between the train station, the airport, and the Philae Island boats. The defining Aswan scam is structural rather than individual — The Philae Temple Boat Gouge runs as a true cartel at the Shellal motorboat dock, where every boatman holds the same line and the official 10-pound posted rate has been replaced by a 200-to-500-pound quote. There is no land bridge to the Temple of Isis. The Airport Taxi Ambush at Aswan International runs the same way: a wall of drivers grabs your luggage before you can ask a price, every quote is 500 EGP for a 25-kilometer ride that should run 100 to 150, and ride-hailing apps do not operate at the airport. The Taxi Fare Bait-and-Switch leans on the same currency ambiguity that runs through Luxor — "25 pounds" becomes "25 dollars" at the destination, with your luggage in the trunk as leverage.

The risk is concentrated at three transit nodes: the Shellal motorboat dock south of the city, the Aswan International airport rank, and the unmetered taxi corridor between the Nubian Museum, the Old Cataract Hotel, the Unfinished Obelisk, and the train station. The Corniche between the Ferial Garden and the Elephantine Island ferry carries the felucca pressure script — a 100-pound sunset sail that pivots into upsells for a multi-day Luxor cruise, a Nubian village dinner, and his cousin's restaurant. Gharb Soheil, advertised as the "traditional Nubian village," is a curated gauntlet where the crocodile photo is 50 EGP, the henna is 200, and the guide takes a commission from each shop you enter.

A safe default for first-timers: arrange airport pickup through your Aswan hotel before you fly (every hotel offers it for a fixed 150 to 200 EGP) and book Philae as part of a hotel day-tour package where the boat is bundled at a fixed rate.
