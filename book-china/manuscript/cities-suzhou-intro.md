Suzhou — classical-gardens capital of Jiangnan, 35 minutes by high-speed rail from Shanghai — has a scam scene about a third the volume of Hangzhou's and concentrated in three zones: the **Pingjiang Road** (平江路) preserved-waterway district, the **Shantang Street** (山塘街) canal district, and the gates of the Humble Administrator's Garden (拙政园) and the Lingering Garden (留园).

The **Pingjiang Road silk-and-pearl certificate scam** is the volume risk. Small shops along the restored canal-side lanes sell "pure silk" and "natural pearl" items with laminated "appraisal certificates" that are printed in-house. Common products: 800-RMB "silk" scarves that are blended polyester, 1,500–4,000 RMB "natural freshwater pearl" strands that are plastic-coated glass, and 600-2,000 RMB "hand-embroidered" portraits that are machine-embroidered. Legitimate Suzhou silk — the city has a 2,500-year silk tradition — is sold at Suzhou Silk Museum Store, at the Suzhou Embroidery Research Institute showroom, and at the official China Silk (中丝) chain stores; real silk carries a GB 5296 or OEKO-TEX certification that is printed by an independent authority and traceable.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. **Fake "garden tour-guide" freelancers** attach to visitors at the Humble Administrator's Garden entrance offering "English audio tour" at 80–150 RMB; the official audio-guide rental inside the garden is 10 RMB. The **Shantang Street canal-boat private-hire** quotes 200–300 RMB for a "private boat" that becomes a shared boat with the same freelancer's cousins on board. And the **Shantang Street tea-house ceremony extraction** runs the Shanghai Bund script at lower volume — English-speaking pairs walk you from Shantang's first bridge to a 12-seat venue and present 500–800 RMB bills.

A safe default: take Suzhou Metro Line 1 (Pingjiang Road) or Line 2 (Suzhou Railway Station to Shantang); buy silk only at the official Suzhou Silk Museum Store (166 Renmin Road) or at the China Silk chain; decline freelance garden-guide offers — inside-garden audio guides are 10 RMB; ignore canal-boat private-hire touts on Shantang; refuse English-speaking tea-ceremony invitations; and call **110** or the Suzhou tourist-police line at **+86 512 6752 5555** from inside a Starbucks or hotel lobby. The Suzhou Public Security Bureau's tourist-assistance booth is at the main entrance of the Humble Administrator's Garden.
