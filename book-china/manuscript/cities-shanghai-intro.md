Shanghai is mainland China's most-visited city by foreign tourists and the country's second-warmest scam-approach environment. The volume is concentrated in two corridors: the **Bund waterfront** (Wai Tan, 外滩) along the Huangpu River, and **Nanjing Road Pedestrian Street** running west from People's Square. The *Shanghai Daily* has covered the "tea-house scam" (茶馆骗局) in at least six English-language reports between 2019 and 2025, and the Shanghai Municipal Public Security Bureau posts English-language warnings at both the Bund sightseeing tunnel entrance and at the Nanjing Road Walking Street information kiosks.

The script is identical every time. Two young English-speaking women approach solo or paired tourists with a request to "practice English." The conversation is warm and sustained for 5-10 minutes. They propose "showing you a traditional Chinese tea ceremony" at a tea house they know "just a couple of blocks away." The tea house is a small, purpose-built 6-12 seat venue on a narrow side street. An older "tea master" presents four to seven small cups of tea with theatrical ceremony. The bill on the Bund and Nanjing Road arrives at a total of 3,000–10,000 RMB for a party of two, often itemized with a "seat fee" and a "tea-master-performance fee"; the milder Yu Garden variant clusters at 800–1,200 RMB per person. Foreign credit cards go through reliably. Dispute is complicated by the language barrier and by the fact that the tea was in fact served; Shanghai PSB can sometimes negotiate a partial refund if you file a complaint in-person, same day.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Pudong International Airport (PVG) "black car" flat-rate touts** quote 400–600 RMB for a metered 180-RMB ride into the Bund — the Maglev (磁悬浮) to Longyang Road plus subway is 50 RMB and takes 25 minutes, and the official airport taxi rank charges the meter. The **Yu Garden Bazaar tea-leaf weight scam** sells "premium Pu'er" at 800-1,500 RMB per jīn (500g) after water has been pressed into the leaves to triple the weight; legitimate Pu'er sellers always weigh dry. And the **Nanjing Road walking-street English-practice invitation** — the same tea-scam operators have moved to Nanjing Road East between People's Square and the Shanghai No. 1 Department Store.

A safe default: take the Maglev or Line 2 subway from PVG; book DiDi from hotels; ignore every English-speaking approach on the Bund, on Nanjing Road, and at Yu Garden; buy Pu'er or silk only from shops recommended by your hotel's concierge (or from large department stores with posted prices); and call **110** or the Shanghai tourist hotline at **+86 21 962 020** from inside a Starbucks or hotel lobby if an approach turns pushy. The Shanghai SATE-equivalent is the **Tourist Service Center** at Nanjing East Road 561, near Line 2's Nanjing East Road station.
