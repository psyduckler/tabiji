Beijing runs the oldest and most practiced English-language approach-scam ecosystem in mainland China, and almost all of it converges on two corridors: **Wangfujing Pedestrian Street** (running south from Line 1's Wangfujing station to the Oriental Plaza) and the **Tiananmen–Forbidden City access route**. The *"art student gallery"* approach, documented in *China Daily* at least annually since 2010 and flagged in English-language scam advisories posted at major Wangfujing subway exits by the Beijing tourism authorities, begins with two young, fluent-English Chinese women — almost always described as "studying art at the Central Academy" — who invite you to "a free student exhibition" around the corner. The exhibition is a commercial gallery. A patient older "teacher" serves tea, walks you through "student pieces," and closes with 2,000–4,000 RMB catalogs. r/ChinaTravel threads from 2024 and 2025 log the same script almost verbatim, and Beijing Municipal Tourism has named it in annual advisory posts.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Tiananmen fake-tour-guide and jade-kickback circuit** intercepts visitors on Chang'an Avenue with "free guide" offers that lead to a single jade or silk shop where the "guide" takes a commission; Bank of China has a posted warning at the Tiananmen East subway exit. The **Capital International Airport (PEK) and Daxing Airport (PKX) flat-rate taxi touts** quote 300–400 RMB flat for regulated 90–140 RMB metered runs into central Beijing — the Airport Express metro is 25 RMB and the official taxi rank is staffed and queue-managed. And the **Houhai-Nanluoguxiang night-food-street skewer surprise** bills 600–900 RMB for what felt like a casual evening: the "set price" is per stick, the dip sauces are separately billed, and the tea served without asking is 60–100 RMB per pot.

A safe default: take the Capital Airport Express (机场快轨) or the new Daxing subway line (大兴机场线) from the airport; book DiDi from terminals or hotels, never accept a flat-rate cash quote; verify Forbidden City and Great Wall tickets only through **intl.dpm.org.cn/?l=en** (the official Palace Museum international ticket site) or a top-tier OTA (Trip.com, Klook); ignore every English-speaking approach on Wangfujing, Nanluoguxiang, and near Tiananmen; and call **110** (Public Security Bureau) or **12301** (China National Tourism Administration tourist-assistance hotline) from inside a hotel lobby or chain-store café the moment an approach turns physical. Beijing's tourist-police booths are visible at the Forbidden City and the Great Wall access villages and can intervene within minutes.
