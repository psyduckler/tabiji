Chengdu — Sichuan's provincial capital, panda-research base, and gateway to the Tibetan plateau — sustains a warmer, lower-volume scam ecosystem than Beijing or Shanghai. What happens, happens in four zones: the **Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding** access road in Chenghua District, the **Jinli Ancient Street** (锦里古街) south of the Wuhou Shrine, the **Kuanzhai Alley** (宽窄巷子) preserved-lane district, and the two airports (Chengdu Shuangliu CTU and Tianfu TFU).

The **panda-volunteer ticket scam** is the headline. Freelance touts outside the Chengdu Research Base entrance sell "panda-volunteer experience" tickets at 1,500–2,000 RMB, claiming the foreigner gets "behind-the-scenes" access to handle or feed the pandas. No such program is currently offered to foreign tourists through walking touts; the real panda-volunteer program at Dujiangyan Panda Base requires three-month-in-advance application through the official website, costs 700 RMB, and involves filling out health and insurance forms. The tout's "ticket" gets you the same general-admission entry as the 55-RMB regular ticket. r/ChinaTravel threads from 2024 document several 1,800-RMB cases.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Jinli Ancient Street tea-ceremony circuit** is Chengdu's version of the Shanghai tea scam: English-speaking young women invite tourists to a "traditional Sichuan tea house" and present 600–900 RMB bills; the defense is the universal refusal. **Hotpot restaurant service-fee scams** at tourist-menu venues on Kuanzhai Alley bill a 15-20% "cultural service charge" that wasn't on the displayed menu; always ask for an itemized bill (请给我详细账单, *qǐng gěi wǒ xiángxì zhàngdān*) and point at any unnamed line item. And **CTU / TFU airport freelance-taxi flat-rate quotes** of 250–350 RMB for rides that the meter runs at 90–150 RMB.

A safe default: take Chengdu Metro Line 10 from Shuangliu, or Metro Lines 18/19 from Tianfu, into central Chengdu; book DiDi for city rides; buy panda-base tickets only through **panda.org.cn** or Trip.com; never accept "panda-volunteer" offers from street touts; eat hotpot at chain locations (Shu Jiu Xiang, Hai Di Lao) where menus and service charges are posted; and call **110** or the Chengdu tourist-police line if an approach escalates. The Chengdu Tourism Bureau operates an English-speaking complaint line on **+86 28 9606 5656**.
