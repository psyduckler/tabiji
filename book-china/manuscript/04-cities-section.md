# The Scams: City by City {-}

The chapters that follow document every scam reported in sixteen mainland-Chinese destinations. Cities are ordered by how most visitors actually travel: starting with Beijing, the capital and the country's densest approach-scam ecosystem, then east to Shanghai, inland to Xi'an (the ancient capital) and the Sichuan cities of Chengdu and Chongqing, south to Guangzhou and Shenzhen on the Pearl River Delta, east again to the Jiangnan classical cities of Hangzhou and Suzhou, south-west to the karst landscapes of Guilin and Yangshuo, further south-west to Yunnan's Lijiang and Kunming, and finishing with three distinctive outliers — the walled Ming city of Pingyao, the Russian-influenced ice city of Harbin, and the sandstone-pillar park of Zhangjiajie.

If you're hitting only a few of these, skip to the chapter you need. If you're doing a longer loop, skim them all — the same core scams recur with local variations. The Beijing Wangfujing "art student" opener runs identically at the Bund in Shanghai, at the Muslim Quarter in Xi'an, and on Jinli Street in Chengdu. The airport flat-rate taxi refusal starts at Beijing Capital and repeats at every major gateway in this book. The tea-ceremony extraction runs citadel-to-citadel from Beijing to Shanghai to Xi'an to Chengdu to Guangzhou. Learn the pattern in one city, and you'll spot it the instant you step off the train in the next.

## The Sixteen Cities at a Glance {-}

![Beijing — the Forbidden City rooftops seen from Jingshan Park](assets/cities/beijing.jpg)

**Beijing** — The capital, the Forbidden City, the Great Wall, and mainland China's most densely-choreographed approach scene. The Wangfujing "art student" gallery circuit that pulls foreign tourists a block off the main pedestrian street for a 3,000-RMB print pitch; the Tiananmen-area "free-tour-guide" who leads you to a jade shop with kickbacks; the Capital Airport (PEK) and Daxing (PKX) flat-rate taxi touts quoting 300-400 RMB for metered runs under 140; and the Houhai lantern-night-food-street skewer surprise that bills 600 RMB for what looked like a 100-RMB evening.

![Shanghai — the Bund waterfront and Pudong skyline at dusk](assets/cities/shanghai.jpg)

**Shanghai** — The Bund, Nanjing Road, and the warmest scam-scene in China. The tea-ceremony scam documented in *Shanghai Daily* since at least 2019, run by smiling English-speaking pairs who walk you from the Bund to a 12-seat tea house and present 800-1,200 RMB bills per person; Pudong Airport (PVG) flat-rate touts quoting 400-600 for 180-RMB metered runs; Yu Garden tea-leaf weight tricks; and Nanjing Road "English practice" invitations that end at the same commercial galleries the Beijing art-student scam does.

![Xi'an — the medieval city wall at golden hour](assets/cities/xian.jpg)

**Xi'an** — The ancient capital, the Terracotta Warriors, and a Muslim Quarter where "auntie-tour" calligraphy-and-jade circuits extract 2,000-8,000 RMB per session. The Xi'an North Railway Station freelance-minibus "Terracotta Warriors tour" that substitutes four commission stops for the advertised itinerary; the Bell Tower and Drum Tower area fake-ticket reseller scene; the Muslim Quarter seafood and noodle per-jīn weighing tricks; and the "imperial jade" laminated-certificate shops clustered around the Great Mosque.

![Chengdu — a giant panda at the Chengdu Research Base](assets/cities/chengdu.jpg)

**Chengdu** — Pandas, Sichuan pepper, and the Jinli and Kuanzhai Alley tourist quarters. The Chengdu Research Base "panda-volunteer" ticket scam sold by walking touts at 1,800 RMB for access that is not actually available to foreign visitors; the Jinli Ancient Street tea-ceremony circuit; hotpot restaurants adding a "service fee" line item not disclosed on the menu; and Chengdu Shuangliu Airport (CTU) / Tianfu Airport (TFU) flat-rate taxi touts.

![Chongqing — the Hongya Cave stilt houses at twilight](assets/cities/chongqing.jpg)

**Chongqing** — Mountain-city terraces, the Yangtze confluence, and the Three Gorges cruise embarkation. Pier-side cruise-ticket touts selling downgraded cabins on legitimate cruise lines; Hongya Cave and Ciqikou Ancient Town hotpot restaurants with unposted per-hour charges; Chongqing Jiangbei Airport (CKG) taxi detours adding 80-150 RMB per ride; and the Liziba "light-rail-through-the-building" photo-spot touts demanding 50-100 RMB for photos tourists didn't ask for.

![Guangzhou — the Canton Tower on the Pearl River at blue hour](assets/cities/guangzhou.jpg)

**Guangzhou** — Trade capital, Cantonese food, and the South China gateway. Baiyun Airport (CAN) freelance-taxi flat-rate quotes; Shamian Island walking-tour-and-gallery circuit targeting English-speaking tourists; Beijing Road pedestrian-street "art student" invitation (same script as Beijing's Wangfujing); and Shangxiajiu Pedestrian Street dim-sum per-basket overcharges that arrive 2-3× the posted rate because a "table charge" and "tea charge" weren't disclosed.

![Shenzhen — the Ping An Finance Center above Shenzhen Bay](assets/cities/shenzhen.jpg)

**Shenzhen** — China's youngest megacity, Huaqiangbei electronics, and the Hong Kong border. Huaqiangbei fake-electronics resellers selling refurbished phones as new; Luohu and Shenzhen Bay border-crossing touts selling "expedited processing" that doesn't exist; OCT Loft gallery-pitch circuit targeting expat professionals; and Bao'an Airport (SZX) "black car" flat-rate freelancers quoting 300-RMB for 90-RMB metered runs.

![Hangzhou — the Leifeng Pagoda above West Lake](assets/cities/hangzhou.jpg)

**Hangzhou** — West Lake, longjing (Dragon Well) tea country, and Jiangnan classical gardens. Longjing tea village "tea master" per-gram overcharges at the door of scenic tea farms; West Lake private-boat quotes that double mid-trip; Hefang Street "silk" shops selling polyester as pure silk; and the Lingyin Temple "blessing donation" approach asking 500-2,000 RMB for a "lucky" jade pendant.

![Suzhou — a moon-gate and canal in the Humble Administrator's Garden](assets/cities/suzhou.jpg)

**Suzhou** — Classical gardens, Grand Canal, and Pingjiang Road's preserved waterway district. Pingjiang Road silk-and-pearl shops with laminated "certificate" scams; fake "garden tour-guide" freelancers attaching to visitors at the Humble Administrator's Garden and the Lingering Garden; canal-boat private-hire price doublings; and the Shantang Street tea-house ceremony extraction (same Shanghai pattern, smaller audience).

![Guilin — karst peaks rising from the Li River](assets/cities/guilin.jpg)

**Guilin** — The gateway to the Li River karst landscape. Guilin North Railway Station freelance-minibus "Li River tours" substituting a low-end rubber-raft trip for an advertised cruise; Reed Flute Cave "photo-guide" freelancers demanding 100-200 RMB for unposted photos; Guilin Liangjiang Airport (KWL) taxi flat-rate quotes; and Yangshuo Old Town West Street tea-house and cormorant-photo upcharges.

![Yangshuo — cormorant fisherman on a bamboo raft](assets/cities/yangshuo.jpg)

**Yangshuo** — Karst landscape, Li River floating villages, and West Street's rural-tourist party strip. Cormorant-fishing "shows" quoting a dock price and adding a "performance fee" on the water; bike-rental shops insisting on passport-as-deposit and then claiming damage; West Street bars charging "foreigner menu" prices that are 2-3× the Chinese menu on the same counter; and Yulong River bamboo-raft kickback circuits where the raft operator "runs out of time" and delivers you to a different — affiliated — pier.

![Lijiang — stone bridge and canal in Old Town](assets/cities/lijiang.jpg)

**Lijiang** — Naxi-minority UNESCO Old Town and the base for Jade Dragon Snow Mountain. Old Town tea-house minimum-spend scams advertising "free entry" with 400-RMB per-person minimums; Jade Dragon Snow Mountain combined-ticket packages sold by street touts at double the official price; and the Naxi-dress "photo-with-traditional-costume" touts around Sifang Street demanding 200-400 RMB after photos tourists didn't explicitly commission.

![Kunming — Stone Forest limestone pinnacles](assets/cities/kunming.jpg)

**Kunming** — Yunnan capital, Stone Forest (Shilin), and the gateway to Dali and Lijiang. Stone Forest "ethnic-minority guide" touts demanding 300-500 RMB after walking with visitors for an unrequested 20-minute tour; Kunming Changshui Airport (KMG) and railway station minibus "Dali/Lijiang tours" that substitute one-day kickback circuits for multi-day packages; Green Lake tea-house tourist-menu pricing; and Yunnan "Pu'er tea" shops selling generic tea at pressed-cake "aged" prices.

![Pingyao — Ming city wall and courtyard rooftops](assets/cities/pingyao.jpg)

**Pingyao** — Ming-Qing walled UNESCO city near Taiyuan. The combined-ticket scam where street touts sell "Pingyao all-access" tickets that duplicate what is already included in the official city-wall ticket; courtyard-hotel "upgrade" charges added on check-in for rooms already booked; antique-shop calligraphy-scroll "hand-painted" claims that are printed reproductions; and the Qiao Family Courtyard (the *Raise the Red Lantern* filming location) "photographer guide" demanding unposted fees.

![Harbin — Saint Sophia Cathedral in winter snow](assets/cities/harbin.jpg)

**Harbin** — Russian-influenced ice city and winter festival host. Ice and Snow World ticket markups at January-February touts selling same-day entry at 2× the official rate; Russian "amber" and "fur" shops selling plastic and synthetic fibre at real-amber and real-fur prices; Central Street (Zhongyang Dajie) Russian-cuisine restaurants with tourist-menu overcharges; and Harbin Taiping Airport (HRB) taxi flat-rate quotes.

![Zhangjiajie — sandstone pillars above sea of clouds](assets/cities/zhangjiajie.jpg)

**Zhangjiajie** — *Avatar*-like sandstone-pillar national forest park in Hunan. Gondola-and-shuttle-bus combined-ticket confusion where visitors pay separately for elements that are already in the park pass; "ethnic village performance" tickets sold for shows that are canceled or empty; Tianmen Mountain "skywalk" photo-tout fees; and Zhangjiajie Hehua Airport (DYG) minibus "tour" substitutions where the advertised glass-bridge and Tianmen-Mountain combo devolves into a single short visit plus four souvenir-shop stops.
