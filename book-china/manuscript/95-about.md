# About Tabiji {-}

Tabiji is a travel-safety research organization. We document scams, health risks, and local advisories for travelers across more than fifty cities worldwide, and we publish everything as a free public resource at **[tabiji.ai](https://tabiji.ai)**.

We started this series after noticing that most scam roundups online skip the one thing travelers actually need in the moment: the exact phrase, the exact move, that ends the interaction. Remembering a list of ninety-eight scams is hard when you are tired and under pressure. Remembering six patterns is much easier. That is the shape we have tried to give this book.

Every scam documented here was drawn from Chinese-press and China-facing English press — *China Daily*, *Global Times*, *Xinhua*, *People's Daily* (English edition), *Shanghai Daily*, *Beijing Review*, *South China Morning Post* — cross-referenced against Public Security Bureau advisories, Beijing Municipal Tourism and Shanghai Municipal PSB English-language public-safety posts, China National Tourism Administration (12301) complaint bulletins, State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) consumer-protection cases, and firsthand traveler accounts on Reddit's r/ChinaTravel, r/travelchina, r/Shanghai, r/Beijing, and the city-specific subreddits. Where we have named documented incidents, arrest dates, or specific RMB amounts, we cite them. Where a pattern has been reported but not formally investigated, we say so.

For new scams and live updates to the cases in this book, visit **[tabiji.ai/scams](https://tabiji.ai/scams)**. We maintain the database quarterly, and most corrections reach the site within a week of a credible report.

We hope you come home with stories about the dumplings, the Great Wall, the Li River, and the pandas.

— Tabiji
