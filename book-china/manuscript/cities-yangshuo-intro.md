Yangshuo — the karst-landscape rural-tourist town at the southern end of the Li River — has a scam scene shaped by foreign backpackers and the Chinese-domestic influencer economy. Volume is on **West Street** (西街, Xī Jiē), at the Yulong River bamboo-raft piers, and in the cormorant-fishing photo-tour circuit.

The **cormorant-fishing "show" price trick** is the signature risk. Freelance fishermen on bamboo rafts at the Li River and Yulong River evening piers offer "authentic cormorant fishing" demonstrations at 100–150 RMB per person on the dock. Once the raft is on the water, the fisherman announces a "performance fee" of an additional 200–400 RMB for the cormorants, citing "the birds need to eat." Refusing means being rowed back without the promised show; paying means a 5-minute performance for what began at 100 RMB. Legitimate cormorant-fishing photo-tour experiences (not authentic commercial fishing — that died out decades ago) run through licensed tour operators (the **Guangxi Tourism-bureau licensed tours**), cost 180–250 RMB all-in, and are marked by a posted QR code on the boat.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. **Bike-rental shops on West Street** demand passport-as-deposit and claim post-rental "damage" (chain stretching, tire wear, "scratched saddle") at 200–600 RMB on return; pay with a credit-card hold through Alipay/WeChat's Tour mode rather than leaving a passport, and photograph the bike from 8+ angles before riding off. **West Street bars with separate "foreigner" and "Chinese" menus** — same food, same drinks, 2-3× higher on the English menu; always ask to see the Chinese menu (中文菜单, *zhōngwén càidān*) and check the Dianping (大众点评) prices before ordering. And the **Yulong River bamboo-raft kickback circuit** — dock-side touts sell "3-hour Yulong River tour" at 300–400 RMB per raft; mid-trip the operator declares "out of time" and drops passengers at a different pier run by his cousin, where return shuttles cost 100–200 RMB.

A safe default: take official tourist-bus shuttles between Guilin and Yangshuo (40 RMB from Qinxing, marked operators); rent bikes only at the two shops with a green Tourism Bureau sticker on the front door (the sticker means they accept credit-card holds rather than passports); buy cormorant and raft tours only through Trip.com or through your hotel's licensed concierge; eat on side-streets off West Street (Diecui Road, Guihua Road) where the menu is one menu for everyone; and call **110** or **12301**. Yangshuo's Public Security Bureau sub-station is at the end of West Street near the Li River pier.
