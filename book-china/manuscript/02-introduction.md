# How to Use This Book {-}

China is extraordinary. It is also one of the most scam-prone countries for first-time visitors who don't speak Mandarin — less because of violent crime than because the sheer size, the language gap, and the cash-going-digital transition have produced an unusually rehearsed ecosystem of polite, friendly, patient approaches that end with a wallet lighter than when you stepped off the train.

Beijing's Wangfujing pedestrian street runs the **"art student gallery"** approach so consistently that the Beijing Tourist Police post warnings in English at every subway exit on Line 1. Shanghai's Bund and Nanjing Road have a **tea-house ceremony scam** — an invitation from smiling young English-speakers to sample the "local specialty" that ends with a bill of anywhere between 3,000 and 10,000 RMB you cannot dispute in Mandarin — that the *Shanghai Daily* has covered at least six times in five years. Xi'an's Muslim Quarter, Chengdu's Jinli Ancient Street, and Guilin's Yangshuo riverfront each have their own local variants. The China National Tourism Administration (dial **12301**) logs tens of thousands of foreign-visitor complaints every year, most of them concentrated on a handful of heavily-marketed "old street" scenic zones and a predictable set of airport and rail-station taxi queues.

We want you to come home with stories about the dumplings, the Great Wall, the Li River mist, and the pandas — not about 1,200 RMB of tea you didn't want or a 3,000-RMB "gallery" receipt for a print that wasn't really a print.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents ninety-eight specific scams that trip up first-time visitors to mainland China, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been reported in Chinese or China-facing English press, cross-referenced against Public Security Bureau (公安局, *gōng'ānjú*) advisories or CNTA 12301 complaint records, and confirmed by recent traveler accounts on r/ChinaTravel, r/travelchina, r/Shanghai, r/Beijing, and the city-specific subreddits.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First, the name of the scam, the category it falls into (pickpocket, transport, overcharging, tourist trap, and so on), and how serious and how common it is. Then two short sections: **How this scam works** — a plain narrative of how it unfolds in real time, with real RMB (¥) amounts where we have them — and **How to avoid it**, the specific moves that shut it down. Each entry ends with where it happens and — for China — a four-panel comic showing the exchange so you can spot the pattern visually, not just in words.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. Most scams in mainland China are variations on six patterns. Learn the patterns and you will spot a new scam the moment it starts — even one that is not in this book. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will then read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters, you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **Mandarin exit-phrase card**. Short phrases you can say, word for word, at the moments when your politeness would normally get you into trouble. Mandarin is tonal — the pronunciation guides are approximate, but each phrase appears in simplified Chinese characters (so you can show it on your phone), in Pinyin (so you can attempt to say it), and with a practical English cue. Take a screenshot of it on your phone.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A step-by-step flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens, you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. Public Security Bureau stations, tourist-help 12301 numbers, hospitals with English-speaking care, consulates, and the major foreign-card 24/7 lines that will actually work from inside mainland China (where Google, Facebook, WhatsApp, Gmail, and most Western messaging apps are not reachable without a VPN), organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A note on apps and connectivity

Mainland China is a different digital environment. Before you leave:

- Save all important contacts, tickets, and addresses **offline** on your phone. Gmail, Google Maps, WhatsApp, Facebook Messenger, and Instagram are blocked on the public internet.
- Download an offline map (Maps.me or a cached Google Maps region).
- Install **WeChat** (微信) and **Alipay** (支付宝) — both now accept foreign Visa and Mastercard via a "Tour" or "International" mode. Cash is increasingly hard to spend outside of taxis and small street vendors.
- Arrange a roaming SIM or eSIM that routes through Hong Kong (most Airalo, Holafly, and Nomad plans do) — these bypass the Great Firewall and keep your Google Maps working.
- Book a trusted VPN before you land. They cannot be installed inside mainland China.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and skim the three appendices. Read the chapters for the cities you will be visiting. Take a screenshot of the phrase card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book turns out to apply during your trip, open it again on the high-speed train or in your hotel room and check what to do.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.

## A note on prices and dates

All RMB amounts in this book were verified at the time the 2026 Edition went to press, drawn from press reports, scenic-area official ticket pages, and current traveler accounts. Mainland-Chinese ticket prices, bus fares, and metro rates do drift year-to-year — usually within 5–10% — and the USD-to-RMB exchange rate drifts a few percent annually. Use the numbers as order-of-magnitude anchors; verify any specific price on xe.com or the attraction's official site in the week before you travel.
