# How to Use This Book {-}

Vietnam is extraordinary. It is also, by the Vietnam National Administration of Tourism's own admission, one of the most scam-prone countries in Southeast Asia for first-time visitors in 2024–2025. Hanoi's Noi Bai Airport runs a "fake Grab driver" script that r/VietNam describes as "such a successful scam that's why Grab and Be set up personnel at the pickup zones." Ho Chi Minh City's Bui Vien walking street has an ongoing 4-million-VND bar-extortion cycle the local press calls a perennial warning. The tourist-assistance hotline (**1800 1552**, free from any Vietnamese phone) logs tens of thousands of foreign-visitor complaints every year, concentrated on a dozen neighborhoods and a handful of cruise piers.

We want you to come home with stories about the pho, the lantern-lit Hoi An evenings, the karst limestone islands of Ha Long, and the motorbike ballet of Saigon — not about a "broken" jet-ski deposit on Phu Quoc or a 40-million-VND "damaged" rental car in Da Nang.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents sixty-six specific scams that trip up first-time visitors to Vietnam, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been reported in the Vietnamese press, cross-referenced against Tourist Police or local authority records, and confirmed by recent traveler accounts on r/VietNam, r/vietnamtravel, and the city- and region-specific subreddits.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First, the name of the scam, the category it falls into (pickpocket, transport, tourist trap, nightlife), and how serious and how common it is. Then two short sections: **How this scam works** — a plain narrative of how it unfolds in real time, with real Vietnamese-dong amounts where we have them — and **How to avoid it**, the specific moves that shut it down. Each entry ends with where it happens and — for Vietnam — a four-panel comic showing the exchange so you can spot the pattern visually, not just in words.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. Most scams in Vietnam are variations on six patterns. Learn the patterns and you will spot a new scam the moment it starts — even one that is not in this book. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will then read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters, you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **Vietnamese exit-phrase card**. Short phrases you can say, word for word, at the moments when your politeness would normally get you into trouble. Vietnamese is tonal — the pronunciation guides are approximate, but the key words will carry the meaning even with a foreign accent. Take a screenshot of it on your phone.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A step-by-step flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens, you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. Tourist hotline, police, ambulance, embassies, and the nearest English-speaking medical care, organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and skim the three appendices. Read the chapters for the cities and regions you will be visiting. Take a screenshot of the phrase card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book turns out to apply during your trip, open it again in the Grab car or in your hotel room and check what to do.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.
