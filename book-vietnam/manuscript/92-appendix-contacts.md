# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication, but phone numbers do change, consulates move, and hospitals occasionally renumber their switchboards. If a number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus *Vietnam* and you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results.

**Before you travel**, we strongly recommend you double-check your own embassy or consulate's current number directly on its official website — the US State Department's travel.state.gov, the UK's gov.uk, Canada's travel.gc.ca, Ireland's dfa.ie, Australia's smartraveller.gov.au. Save the main switchboard and the after-hours duty-officer line on your phone before departure, and screenshot this appendix so you can read it on a Ha Long Bay junk boat or a Grab ride where your signal cuts out. Even a verified number can become a wrong number between editions.

## The numbers that work everywhere in Vietnam

These are the lines to dial from any phone — mobile or landline, Vietnamese SIM or roaming — anywhere in the country. All of them are free.

- **Tourist assistance hotline (VNAT):** **1800 1552**. This is the number to call if you do not speak Vietnamese. Dispatchers are staffed in Vietnamese and English; some speak Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Russian, and French as well. This is the *first* number to try for any scam, tour-operator dispute, overcharging case, or general orientation question involving a foreign visitor. The dispatcher will direct you to the nearest city tourist-police post and, in serious cases, coordinate with local police.
- **Police (emergency):** **113**. National police emergency line. The first operator may speak Vietnamese only; repeat *"English, please"* slowly or say *"Tourist Police"* and the dispatcher will locate an English speaker, particularly in Hanoi, HCMC, Da Nang, and other major tourist cities. Use this for theft, assault, harassment, and any incident that is unfolding in real time.
- **Fire and rescue:** **114**. National fire-and-rescue dispatch.
- **Emergency medical services (ambulance):** **115**. National ambulance dispatch. English is available at the major dispatch centers in Hanoi, HCMC, Da Nang, Hue, and Nha Trang; elsewhere the first operator may be Vietnamese-only and will locate an English speaker. For any serious injury, calling the nearest large international hospital directly (numbers below) is often faster than 115 in remote areas.
- **Traffic police:** **113** (routed through the police line) — for collisions, hit-and-runs, and traffic-related disputes.
- **Consumer protection hotline (VCCA):** **1800 6838**. Complaints against shops, restaurants, tailors, tour packages, and consumer fraud generally. Vietnamese-language first line; English via callback in most cases. Website: **vcca.gov.vn**.
- **Vietnam Railways lost-property / customer service:** **1900 0109**. Useful for items left on trains between Hanoi and Lao Cai (Sapa), Hanoi and Hue, and HCMC and Da Nang.
- **Vietnam Immigration Department:** *xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn*. Main Hanoi office: 44–46 Tran Phu Street; HCMC office: 254 Nguyen Trai Street. Visa extensions, overstay rectification, and passport-lost clearance for departure.

## Card issuer emergency lines (callable from Vietnam)

When you call, have your card number (or the last four digits), the approximate transaction amount, and the time of the fraudulent charge ready.

- **Visa Global Customer Assistance (collect from Vietnam):** +1 303 967 1096. 24/7, English.
- **Mastercard Global Service (collect from Vietnam):** +1 636 722 7111. 24/7.
- **American Express International Service Center (collect):** +1 336 393 1111. 24/7.

If your card was issued by a bank (not directly by Visa, Mastercard, or Amex), the fastest route is the number printed on the back of the card. That line routes you to your own issuer's fraud desk and they can freeze the card in under a minute. Save this number in your phone under a searchable contact name before you travel — hunting for a number on a stolen card is impossible.

## City-by-city emergency contacts

For every city in this book, the *tourist-district police post* (if one exists locally) is the default destination for a *biên bản*. Where no tourist police post is within reach, the local *ward police station* (*công an phường*) handles the report; dialing **1800 1552** anywhere in the country will give you the dispatcher who can tell you which station to walk to.

Where we are not certain of a specific street address, we tell you so rather than guess. The current address of any tourist-district police post can be verified by calling 1800 1552, by Google Maps, or by your hotel's front desk — every Vietnamese hotel keeps the nearest police station address at reception.

### Hanoi

- **Police report intake:** the **Hanoi Tourist Security police desk** inside the Old Quarter serves the foreign-visitor area; satellite posts are found near Hoan Kiem Lake, at the Temple of Literature, and at Noi Bai Airport. The 1800 1552 dispatcher will direct you to the nearest open post.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospitals:** **Hanoi French Hospital (Hopital Francais de Hanoi)** (1 Phuong Mai Street, +84 24 3577 1100) and **Vinmec International Hospital Times City** (458 Minh Khai Street, +84 24 3974 3556) are the two largest private international hospitals, both with extensive English-speaking staff and direct billing with major international insurers. **Family Medical Practice Hanoi** (+84 24 3843 0748) is widely used by expatriates and short-term travelers. For public emergency care, **Bach Mai Hospital** is Hanoi's largest public teaching hospital.
- **English-speaking medical care:** all the private hospitals above maintain international-patient coordinators.

### Ha Long Bay / Ha Long City

- **Police report intake:** **Ha Long Tourist Police** maintain desks at Bai Chay and at the Tuan Chau cruise pier during cruise-embarkation hours; the main *Ha Long City Police Station* handles overnight reports. Dial 1800 1552 for the current address.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Vinmec International Hospital Ha Long** (+84 203 3828 188) is the largest private hospital in the area with English-speaking staff. **Bai Chay Hospital** is the main public emergency department.
- **Cruise-passenger medical care:** most licensed Ha Long cruise operators carry a ship doctor for daytime hours and coordinate emergency transfers to Ha Long City within two hours.

### Sapa / Lao Cai

- **Police report intake:** **Sapa Town Police Station** is on Dong Loi Street near the central square; the **Lao Cai Provincial Police** handles cross-border incidents (Lao Cai sits at the Chinese border). Dial 1800 1552 for current office hours.
- **Major emergency hospital:** **Sapa General Hospital** handles day-to-day care; serious cases are transferred by road to **Vinmec International Hospital Ha Long** or to Hanoi (roughly six hours by car).
- **High-altitude medical note:** Fansipan summit (3,143 m) can cause altitude symptoms in susceptible visitors; the cable-car station has a basic medical desk.

### Hue

- **Police report intake:** **Hue Tourist Police** maintain a desk near the Imperial City entrance and a satellite post on Le Loi Street; the main *Thua Thien-Hue Provincial Police Station* handles overnight reports.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Hue Central Hospital** (+84 234 382 2325) is the region's flagship public teaching hospital with an international-patient desk; **Hue Family Medical Practice** (Truong Chinh Street, +84 234 382 3333) is the main expatriate-focused private option.
- **English-speaking medical care:** limited compared to Hanoi and HCMC; for non-urgent care, the short flight or train ride to Da Nang or Hanoi is often the cleaner option.

### Hoi An

- **Police report intake:** **Hoi An Tourist Police** maintain a desk on Tran Hung Dao Street near the Ancient Town main entry; the 1800 1552 dispatcher has the current hours. The main *Hoi An Police Station* handles formal *biên bản* filings.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Hoi An Hospital** (public) handles emergency care; serious cases are transferred to **Vinmec International Hospital Da Nang** (30 minutes by road). **Hoi An Medic Clinic** (Hai Ba Trung Street) handles minor injuries and English-speaking consultations.
- **English-speaking medical care:** widely available at the private clinics above.

### Da Nang

- **Police report intake:** **Da Nang Tourist Police** maintain a main office in central Da Nang and a satellite post near Dragon Bridge. The 1800 1552 dispatcher will direct you to the nearest open desk.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospitals:** **Vinmec International Hospital Da Nang** (Ngu Hanh Son, +84 236 3711 111) is the flagship international hospital on the central coast; **Family Medical Practice Da Nang** (Han Thuyen Street, +84 236 3582 699) handles walk-in consultations with English-speaking doctors. For public care, **Da Nang General Hospital** is the region's largest emergency department.
- **English-speaking medical care:** extensive across the private hospitals and clinics above.

### Nha Trang

- **Police report intake:** **Nha Trang Tourist Police** maintain a desk near the central Tran Phu beachfront and at the Cam Ranh Airport taxi rank. The 1800 1552 dispatcher has the current address.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Vinmec International Hospital Nha Trang** (42A Le Thanh Ton Street, +84 258 3900 168) is the largest private international hospital in town, with English-speaking staff and direct insurance billing.
- **English-speaking medical care:** widely available at Vinmec Nha Trang and at Family Medical Practice (Tran Phu Street).

### Dalat

- **Police report intake:** **Dalat Tourist Police** maintain a desk near Xuan Huong Lake; the main *Lam Dong Provincial Police Station* handles overnight reports. Dial 1800 1552 for current office hours.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Dalat General Hospital** is the largest emergency department in the city; serious cases are transferred by road to HCMC (six hours) or by short flight to HCMC's Cho Ray Hospital.
- **Canyoning-injury note:** Datanla and Elephant Falls canyoning injuries are most safely handled through the licensed operator's insurer; unlicensed-operator injuries often create insurance-dispute problems.

### Ho Chi Minh City (Saigon)

- **Police report intake:** the **HCMC Tourist Police Division** headquarters serves the whole metropolitan area; satellite desks are in District 1 near Ben Thanh Market and at Tan Son Nhat Airport. The 1800 1552 dispatcher will direct you to the nearest open booth.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospitals:** **FV Hospital (Franco-Vietnamese Hospital)** (6 Nguyen Luong Bang, District 7, +84 28 5411 3333) and **Vinmec International Hospital Central Park** (208 Nguyen Huu Canh, Binh Thanh, +84 28 3622 1166) are the two largest private international hospitals, both with extensive English-speaking staff. **Family Medical Practice HCMC** (Diamond Plaza, District 1, +84 28 3822 7848) is widely used by travelers for walk-in care. For public care, **Cho Ray Hospital** is the region's largest teaching hospital.
- **English-speaking medical care:** extensive across all major private hospitals.

### Can Tho / Mekong Delta

- **Police report intake:** **Can Tho Tourist Police** maintain a desk near Ninh Kieu pier; the main *Can Tho Police Station* handles formal *biên bản* filings. Dial 1800 1552.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Can Tho General Hospital** (public) is the largest emergency department in the delta. **Hoan My Cuu Long Hospital** (Cach Mang Thang Tam Street) is the main private option.
- **English-speaking medical care:** more limited than HCMC; serious cases are transferred north to HCMC by road (three to four hours).

### Phu Quoc

- **Police report intake:** **Phu Quoc Tourist Police** maintain a desk at PQC Airport and near Duong Dong town center during high season. The 1800 1552 dispatcher has current hours.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Vinmec International Hospital Phu Quoc** (Bai Dai Area, +84 297 398 5588) is the largest private hospital on the island. **Phu Quoc General Hospital** handles public emergency care; serious cases are air-evacuated to HCMC.
- **Dive-medicine note:** Phu Quoc's dive industry is smaller than Thailand's southern islands; the nearest recompression chamber is in Nha Trang (one-hour flight). DAN Asia-Pacific (+61 8 8212 9242) provides 24-hour dive-medical consult in English.

## If you lose your passport

1. **Report it first** — go to the nearest tourist police desk or ward police station and file a *biên bản* for a lost or stolen passport. You will leave with the signed and sealed report. This is the document your embassy wants to see before issuing an emergency replacement.
2. **Call your embassy's main switchboard** — or its after-hours line if it is a weekend — and request an emergency passport appointment. US, UK, Canadian, Irish, and Australian embassies in Hanoi (and their consulates in HCMC) all issue same-day or next-day emergency travel documents; the fee varies.
3. **Bring** the *biên bản*, any photo ID you still have (a driver's license photo on your phone counts), passport photos (photo booths are common in Hanoi and HCMC malls), and the payment method your embassy specifies.
4. **Immigration clearance for departure**: before flying out with an emergency travel document, you may need a short visit to **Vietnam Immigration** (44–46 Tran Phu, Hanoi, or 254 Nguyen Trai, HCMC) to regularize your visa status — your embassy will advise. Airlines departing Noi Bai, Tan Son Nhat, Da Nang, Cam Ranh, and Phu Quoc see emergency documents regularly; border police know the format.

## If your phone is stolen

1. **Freeze the SIM** by calling your carrier's customer-service line from any other phone. Vietnamese carriers: **Viettel** 198 (in Vietnam) or +84 98 198 (from abroad); **Vinaphone** 18001091; **MobiFone** 18001090. If you use a foreign SIM, call your home carrier's international line — most have a 24/7 theft-reporting number that forwards from Vietnam. Tourist eSIMs (Airalo, Saily, Holafly) can be suspended in-app.
2. **File a *biên bản*** at the nearest police station with your device's IMEI (if you recorded it) and its serial number. Insurance claims require the sealed report.
3. **Lock and wipe remotely** via Apple's Find My or Google's Find My Device from any browser. Do this before filing the *biên bản* — a wiped phone cannot leak your email and banking apps while you wait in line.
4. **Change passwords immediately** for any account whose app was installed on the phone: email, banking, social media. Start with email; everything else resets off it.

## If your credit card is stolen or skimmed

1. **Freeze the card** by calling the number on the back (or use the issuer's app's "lock card" feature, which works in seconds). This matters more than filing a police report first — every minute you wait is a minute the thief can use the card.
2. **Report to police** with a *biên bản*. Insurance reimbursement and the card issuer's fraud-dispute process both want the sealed report.
3. **Dispute the charges** with your issuer. Most European, US, Australian, and Japanese card issuers reverse fraudulent charges fully; the *biên bản* speeds the process.
4. **Watch for follow-on phishing** — a skimmed card often leads to email and SMS phishing pretending to be your bank. Never click a link in one of these messages; go to your bank's app or website directly.

## A closing note

This appendix is meant to be read once on the plane, screenshot for offline use, and forgotten about for the rest of your trip. Phone numbers change. Consulates move. Hospitals renumber their switchboards. If any number in this list fails, **1800 1552** — the VNAT tourist assistance hotline — will still work, and your embassy's website will still have the current line for your country.

Travel safely, travel calmly, and if the worst happens, remember: a stolen phone is an inconvenience, not a crisis. Filing a *biên bản*, freezing a card, and calling an embassy are ordinary procedures — Tourist Police officers and consular staff in Vietnam handle these cases every day, and most travelers resolve everything within a few hours and return to their trip. Vietnam is not a dangerous country; it is a practiced one. Help is close, and English is closer than you think.
