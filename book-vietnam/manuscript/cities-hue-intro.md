Hue is the old imperial capital on the Perfume River, and its scam ecosystem runs through the cyclo economy around the Citadel and the airport-taxi queue at Phu Bai (HUI). The *cyclo tourist-price and mid-ride extortion* gambit begins outside the Imperial City entrance, along the Le Loi hotel strip, and at the Dong Ba Market perimeter, where riders agree on 100,000 VND for "an all-day tour" and — halfway through the third temple stop — demand 500,000 VND before they will pedal another meter. The defense is to get the price on a phone note or a small piece of paper at the start, pay the agreed amount at the end, and simply walk to a hotel-recommended cyclo stand if a rider tries to renegotiate mid-ride. At the airport, the *Phu Bai 700,000-VND taxi overcharge* runs a flat-rate quote four times the Grab price (the real ride is 250,000 VND, about 15 km); the defense is to open Grab inside the arrivals hall before accepting a ride, and to walk out to the designated pickup zone if a dispatcher at the desk insists Grab "does not work here."

Inside Hue's old walls four further scams apply. The *Perfume River dragon-boat pressure sale* works off riverside hawkers who insist this is the "last boat" and that an "authentic royal-era singing performance" is included — with five identical boats visibly queued at the same dock, the defense is simply to slow down, compare prices, and board without pressure. The *Imperial City ticket single-entry re-entry trap* is worked by a man with a lanyard at the main gate who tells tourists their single-entry ticket cannot be used to re-enter after lunch, and demands a 500,000-VND "new ticket" — the real rule is that the day ticket allows re-entry, and a check at the official ticket booth ends the play. The *bicycle pusher and "granddaughter" guilt scam* runs along the riverside walking paths: an older woman, sometimes with a pushy handler behind her, asks for a "donation for a sick granddaughter" in amounts escalating from 200,000 to 1,000,000 VND — real aid in Hue goes through registered charities or the local Red Cross, not riverside approaches. The *motorbike-tour rental damage claim* at Le Loi Street rental shops demands 2,000,000 VND for invented "scratched bike" damage on return; the defense is a ten-photo pre-rental walkaround with time-stamps visible, a passport photocopy (never the real passport) as deposit, and a firm refusal to pay for any damage the photos already show. If a rental shop insists on holding the real passport, walk to the next shop — there are dozens along Le Loi.
