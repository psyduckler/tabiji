Nha Trang is a long crescent of Tran Phu beachfront with the Cam Ranh Airport (CXR) thirty-five kilometers south, and its scam ecosystem is shaped by a Russian-tourist corridor, a late-night massage-and-bar economy, and one of the most expensive airport-taxi overcharges in Vietnam. The *Cam Ranh Airport taxi overcharge* runs 1,200,000–1,500,000 VND quotes at the arrivals queue for a run that Grab prices at 380,000–450,000 VND; because the airport is 35 km from central Nha Trang most tourists feel they have no alternative, but Grab and Be both cover the route and the designated pickup zone is on the ground floor outside the arrivals hall. The *late-night central Nha Trang hail overcharge* runs the same pattern on Hung Vuong and Tran Phu from 11 p.m. — the defense is the same rule used at every Vietnamese airport: ignore the driver who approaches, book the app, walk to the zone.

Five further scams cluster along the beachfront. The *beachfront iPhone and valuables theft* is a two-person jog-and-snatch documented across Tran Phu, the Vinpearl ferry pier, and the An Vien Villa area — a jogger "trips" beside a tourist's beach towel, scoops the phone in one motion, and is on a waiting motorbike a moment later; the defense is to keep valuables in the hotel safe and carry a cheap beach phone only. The *late-night massage and bar solicitation scam* runs the 3 a.m. corridor of Tran Phu and Hung Vuong — a woman invites a solo male traveler into a side-street massage parlor, and the 500,000-VND menu service becomes a 5,000,000-VND tab with "companionship" lines; the defense is the same as HCMC's Bui Vien — never follow a solicitor to a venue, pay only menu prices, refuse anything not posted. The *4-island booze cruise and snorkel tour upsell* at Cau Da pier quietly adds "mandatory" drink and equipment fees that the booking listed as included — a screenshot of the booking resolves it. The *Russian-tourist-area price discrimination and dual-menu scam* runs restaurants along Tran Phu with English-menu prices 2–3× the Vietnamese menu for the same pho — asking for the Vietnamese menu, or using a translation app to read the Vietnamese side, ends the practice. Finally the *hotel off-platform payment fraud* mirrors the national Booking.com / Agoda pattern: the "host" messages through WhatsApp asking for direct bank transfer at a discount, and the booking then vanishes or the hotel denies the reservation on arrival. The defense is universal: on-platform payment only, every time, no matter the discount offered.
