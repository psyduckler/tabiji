Da Nang is central Vietnam's beach-and-beach-access city — DAD airport, My Khe's long crescent of sand, Marble Mountains, Ba Na Hills, and Dragon Bridge's Saturday-night fire show. Its scam ecosystem divides between *airport transport* and *beach-and-mountain daytime traps*. The *DAD fake-Grab and post-midnight taxi scam* follows the same script as Hanoi and HCMC — a smiling man in the arrivals hall flashes a phone screen that looks like Grab, a private car at the car-park exit is quoted at 800,000–1,000,000 VND, and the app would have shown 250,000. Post-midnight arrivals are especially targeted because real Grab driver density drops after 1 a.m. and tourists think flat-rate is the only option. It isn't; Grab and Be both run 24/7 in Da Nang, and if no driver accepts within ten minutes the walk to the designated pickup zone (not the taxi queue) resolves it. The defense is the same single rule that defeats every airport-car scam in this book: ignore any driver who approaches you; book in the app; walk to the zone the app names.

Four further scams work Da Nang's day-trip radius. The *Ba Na Hills cable-car "ruined my trip" scam* is winter-specific (December–February fog) and hits tourists who arrived early before the cable car's posted running hours — touts insist the cable car is "closed today" and offer a 2,000,000-VND private drive to a "private viewpoint," when the real Sun World cable car is running; the defense is the Sun World website or a simple five-minute wait at the posted official hours. The *Marble Mountains jade and souvenir shop pressure* plays the "free gift" guilt trick — a clerk "gifts" a bracelet and then expects a purchase in return; returning the bracelet with "*không, cảm ơn*" ends it. The *My Khe / An Thuong "where are you from?" tea scam* is the most expensive Da Nang play: a friendly-seeming local invites a tourist for "tea, local custom" at a nearby café, and the bill arrives at 6,000,000 VND with the café staff positioned between you and the door — the defense is to never follow a stranger to an unchosen venue, and to firmly refuse to pay anything above the menu price if a bill is padded. Call **113** from speakerphone. The *Dragon Bridge weekend fire-show pickpockets* work the Saturday-night 9 p.m. crowd along Tran Hung Dao — the defense is cross-body bags in front, phone in a zipped inner pocket, and a wallet with only the cash needed for the night. Finally the *self-drive rental-car damage scam* — 40-million-VND "invisible" damage claims on return — is specific to Da Nang; the defense is a thirty-second video walkaround before driving off and a refusal to sign the return form until all listed "damage" is shown on the car.
