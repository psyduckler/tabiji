Dalat is central-highland Vietnam — pine forests, pastel French-colonial villas, the Cremaillere railway, and a cooler climate that draws domestic tourists and a share of the backpacker canyoning scene. Its scam ecosystem runs through Lien Khuong Airport (DLI), an unlicensed adventure-tourism operator pool, and a fake-resort booking pipeline. The *Lien Khuong Airport taxi overcharge and "officials" scam* is the flagship: a man in a pretend-official vest tells arriving tourists the "government fixed airport rate" is 1,500,000 VND; the real Grab run to Dalat town (thirty kilometers) is 300,000–400,000. The "regulation" is a fiction — there is no such fixed rate — and the defense is the same single habit: open Grab or Be inside the arrivals hall, walk out to the designated pickup zone, and ignore any vest-wearing figure who tries to intercept.

Five further scams shape the Dalat visit. The *canyoning unlicensed-operator safety scam* is the most serious — roughly a third of canyoning deaths in Vietnam over the past decade have involved unlicensed Datanla and Elephant Falls operators, and the pattern is a cheap booking ("1,000,000 VND, includes everything"), no visible license, frayed harnesses on arrival, and no safety briefing. The defense is to check the operator against the Vietnam National Administration of Tourism licensed-operator list *before* booking — legitimate Dalat canyoning runs 1,800,000–2,500,000 VND and includes helmet, harness, certified wetsuit, and a pre-descent briefing. The *Flower Gardens and "Valley of Love" ticket and photo scam* runs scalpers at the entrance who sell 300,000-VND tickets for gardens that sell at 40,000 VND at the real booth forty meters inside; the defense is the same two-minute walk past the scalpers. The *fake resort booking* pattern works through Booking.com clones and WhatsApp referrals — "pay 20% by bank transfer for a discount" — and the resort turns out to be a vacant lot; refusing all off-platform payment defeats it. The *night-market food vendor overcharge and shrinking portions* play on the Phan Dinh Phung Street night market runs 250,000 VND for thumbnail-sized banh mi against 40,000 VND at the next stall over — the defense is to look at two stalls, ask the price before ordering, and move on if the first does not answer plainly. Finally the *"Easy Rider" overcharge* from Dalat-town solicitors quotes 3,000,000 VND for a full-day motorbike tour that the vetted Easy Rider collective runs for 700,000; the real collective has a shopfront at 5 Truong Cong Dinh Street and a guest book signed by decades of customers — booking there, not on the sidewalk, fixes the price.
