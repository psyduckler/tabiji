Ho Chi Minh City — Saigon to most locals — runs the second-densest tourist-scam ecosystem in Vietnam after Hanoi, and the scams cluster in three places: the Tan Son Nhat Airport (SGN) arrivals hall, the District 1 backpacker grid around Bui Vien and Pham Ngu Lao, and the Ben Thanh Market and Saigon Square shopping corridor. The *SGN fake-Grab driver scam* runs the same script as every other Vietnamese airport: a man in a polo shirt flashes a phone screen that looks like Grab, says "I am your driver," and walks jet-lagged arrivals to a car at the car-park exit — quoted at 700,000–1,000,000 VND for a run the app shows at 180,000–250,000. The designated official Grab / Be pickup is at the M-level car park, a sixty-second walk from the arrivals exit. Book in the app first; walk to the zone the app names; ignore anyone who speaks to you first.

Five further scams dominate District 1. The *street-taxi meter tampering and Vinasun copycats* scam puts taxis with near-identical paint jobs to licensed Vinasun (white with red stripes) and Mai Linh (green) into the District 1 taxi ranks — a single letter swapped in the company name, a tampered meter that runs three times normal speed — and if a flat-rate hail insists "meter broken" the only safe move is to get out at a well-lit corner and re-book on Grab. The *Bui Vien walking-street bar "hostess" 4-million-VND extortion*: a "hostess" takes a solo male traveler by the arm into a side-street bar; drinks and "companionship" charges pile on without a visible menu; the tab arrives at 4,000,000–8,000,000 VND with staff between the customer and the door. The defense is strict: choose the bar yourself (TripAdvisor-rated, menu on the wall, lit well); never follow a solicitor; if a bill is padded, ask for the menu and call **113** on speakerphone. The *District 1 motorbike bag-snatch* runs along Nguyen Hue, Dong Khoi, and Le Loi — a motorbike passenger reaches for the strap; the defense is a cross-body bag worn under the off-shoulder side of a jacket, zippers toward the chest, and walking on the inside of the sidewalk. The *Ben Thanh Market overcharge and Saigon Square confusion* plays tourists at Ben Thanh with "400,000 VND real silk" quotes when the same goods are 80,000 VND at Saigon Square shopping mall, which is directly across Le Lai Street — the defense is to walk both places before buying. The *HCMC Booking.com / Agoda off-platform payment fraud* mirrors the national pattern: a "host" messages through WhatsApp after booking asking for a direct bank transfer at a discount; the booking then vanishes. On-platform payment only, every time.
