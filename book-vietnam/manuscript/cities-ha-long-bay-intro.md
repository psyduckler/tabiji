Ha Long Bay's scams start online, weeks before you board. The UNESCO karst seascape draws roughly two million cruise passengers a year, and because most travelers book their overnight junk-boat experience from Hanoi or from abroad, the entire bay has a parallel ecosystem of *fake cruise-booking websites with ghost itineraries* — clone sites mimicking real operator domains, aggregators with stolen photos of licensed ships, and Google-ads top results that funnel bookings to Facebook pages that vanish the morning of embarkation. The Vietnam National Administration of Tourism's licensed-operator list at *vietnamtourism.gov.vn* is the check that saves the trip: if an operator is not on it, the "cruise" is a fiction. A parallel variant hits even travelers who booked through Booking.com and Agoda — the *off-platform payment fraud* where a "host" messages through WhatsApp after booking, offers a 20% discount for direct bank transfer, and the property either disappears or the real booking is cancelled after the wire clears. The signal is a request to pay anywhere outside the platform that took the booking; the defense is to refuse, every time, regardless of the story.

Once on the bay, two further scams apply. *Onboard cruise upcharges* for "mandatory" kayak fees, cave-entry fees, "premium" wine pairings, and photo packages run 300,000–800,000 VND each on cruises that the printed inclusion list promised would be all-included — the defense is to photograph or screenshot the inclusion list before boarding and show it to the manager if upcharges appear. The *fake "floating village" tour* on tender-excursion routes runs guests out to a staged dock with empty houses and a sales stop for souvenirs rather than the real Cua Van floating village on Ha Long's southern edge. On shore, the *Hanoi-to-Ha Long shuttle-bus and private-transfer overcharge* routes through Old Quarter "tourist offices" that quote 2,500,000 VND private transfers for a trip the shared shuttle completes for 300,000 VND; the *Tuan Chau / Bai Chay pier taxi overcharge* then quotes 800,000 VND for a 180,000-VND hotel ride that Grab handles at posted app rates. A safe default: book cruises only from the VNAT licensed list, pay only on the platform that took the booking, screenshot the cruise inclusion list before boarding, and at the pier walk past the first taxi queue to the marked Grab / Be pickup zone — which exists at both Tuan Chau and Bai Chay. If an "emergency" discount appears after your booking confirms, treat the discount itself as the scam signal.
