Rabat is Morocco's political capital — quieter than Casablanca, less touristed than Marrakech, and home to the embassies, ministries, and royal palaces. The scam volume here is correspondingly lower, and the city has a reputation among returning travelers as the most relaxed of the imperial-cities tour. But the few set-piece tourist destinations — the Kasbah des Oudayas, the Mausoleum of Mohammed V, the Chellah Necropolis, and the small medina — each have a documented faux-guide play, and the **Rabat-Salé Airport (RBA)** taxi rank runs the same petit-taxi refusal as Casablanca and Tangier.

The risk concentrates at four nodes. **Rabat-Salé Airport (RBA)** and the **Rabat Ville train station** taxi ranks. The **Bab Oudaia** entrance to the Kasbah des Oudayas. The **Mausoleum of Mohammed V** plaza and the parade space facing the Hassan Tower. And the small medina along **Rue Souika** and **Rue des Consuls**, where the spice shops, the argan-and-cosmetics *pharmacies*, and the menu-switch restaurants cluster.

The **Rabat-Salé Airport Petit Taxi Refusal** is the city's volume risk. RBA is much smaller than CMN, and most arriving travelers don't know the metered fare into central Rabat — drivers quote 200–350 MAD against the 60–80 MAD metered fare and refuse the meter. Identical script at the **Rabat Ville** train-station rank. The defense is the standard line: *"avec le compteur, s'il vous plaît"* before getting in. If the driver refuses, walk to the next taxi.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Kasbah des Oudayas Fake Guide** at Bab Oudaia walks tourists past the Andalusian Garden into a carpet cooperative and demands 100–200 MAD on the way out. The **Mausoleum of Mohammed V Royal Guard Photo Demand** plays on the iconic image of the green-and-red-uniformed *Garde Royale* on horseback at the parade space — a self-described guide offers to "arrange" a photo with the guard for 50–100 MAD; the photos are free and the guards do not interact with the photographer either way. The **Rue Souika Argan & Spice Pharmacy** runs a *"Berber pharmacy"* pitch with euro pricing, hidden 30–50% markup, and pressure-sales staff. The **Medina Restaurant Menu Switch** runs along Rue Souika and Rue des Consuls — different prices on the printed menu inside vs. the chalkboard outside; ask which one applies before sitting. The **Chellah Necropolis Self-Appointed Guide** is a milder version of the same play at the entrance to the Roman-and-Marinid ruins on the city's southeast edge.

A safe default: take the metered petit taxi from RBA to the Hassan district (about 60–80 MAD), photograph any printed menu before sitting, decline the *"royal guard photo arrangement"* and take the photo yourself, and ask your hotel to recommend a licensed ONMT guide if you want a real Kasbah des Oudayas tour. The Brigade Touristique desk is at the central commissariat off **Avenue Mohammed V** in the Hassan district; the US Embassy is at **Km 5.7 Avenue Mohammed VI, Souissi** (+212 537 63 72 00).
