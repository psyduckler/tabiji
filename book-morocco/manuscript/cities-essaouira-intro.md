Essaouira is the Atlantic-coast windsurfing town three hours west of Marrakech — a fortified white-and-blue medina protected by Portuguese-era ramparts, a working fishing harbor, a long beach south of the kasbah, and the country's argan-tree belt running inland to the east. The scam ecosystem here is moderate in volume and almost entirely concentrated on three things: the argan-oil cooperative pitch (Essaouira sits at the heart of the argan-growing region), the central square's restaurant terraces, and the camel-and-quad-bike rental on the southern beach.

The risk concentrates at four nodes. The **N8 highway** between Marrakech and Essaouira, where roadside *"women's argan cooperatives"* are clustered. **Place Moulay Hassan**, the central square inside the medina with its Gnawa-music performers and terrace cafes. The **Skala du Port** and the harbor where the blue fishing boats line up. And the **medina's Bab Doukkala / Bab Marrakech** entrances, where faux guides operate.

The **Fake Argan Oil Cooperative** is the volume risk. On the N8 between Marrakech and Essaouira, drivers (whether your tour driver or a grand-taxi driver) often pull over at a roadside stop sold as a *"women's cooperative"* — the building has women in traditional dress visibly cracking argan kernels, certificates on the wall, and a friendly French-speaking host. The product is genuine but dramatically over-priced: 200–400 MAD for a 100ml bottle that the same cooperative would sell to a Moroccan customer for 80–120, with a quoted euro price that obscures the conversion. Real argan cooperatives certified by the AMIGHA federation operate at smaller scale and don't pay tour-driver commission. The defense is to buy argan oil only from a shop your riad recommends, look for AMIGHA or Slow Food certification on the bottle, and assume any quoted euro price has a 30–50% tourist markup baked in.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Place Moulay Hassan Gnawa Music Donation** runs at the central square's evening sessions: the music is free, but a man with a ceramic bowl works the crowd and applies firm-volume pressure for 50–100 MAD donations from foreigners specifically. The **Beach Camel Ride Bait-and-Switch** on the beach south of the medina near the kasbah agrees an "hour-long ride" at 200 MAD per person and then upcharges 100–300 MAD partway through for "extension," "photos," or "private return." The **Place Moulay Hassan Restaurant Menu Switch** runs the standard no-menu trap on Rue de la Skala and the central terraces. The **Bab Doukkala Fake Medina Guide** walks visitors from the bus station to a carpet cooperative for a 100–200 MAD *"guide fee."* The **Skala du Port Fishermen Photo Demand** at the row of blue fishing boats has fishermen demanding 20–50 MAD per photo even when the photo is from public ground.

A safe default: arrive in Essaouira by **CTM** or **Supratours bus** (80–100 MAD from Marrakech), refuse any tour-driver argan-cooperative stop on the N8, photograph any restaurant menu before sitting, agree the per-person all-in camel-ride price in writing before mounting, and pay for argan oil only at AMIGHA-certified shops or at your riad's recommendation. The central police station is on **Avenue Lalla Aïcha** near the Bab Doukkala medina entrance; **Hôpital Sidi Mohammed Ben Abdellah** on Avenue de l'Hôpital is the public hospital.
