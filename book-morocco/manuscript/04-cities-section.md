# The Scams: City by City {-}

The chapters that follow document every scam reported in ten Moroccan destinations. Cities are ordered by how most visitors actually travel: starting with Marrakech, the country's flagship and densest scam ecosystem, then Fez for the second imperial-cities anchor, then Casablanca, Rabat, and Tangier along the Atlantic and Mediterranean spine, then Chefchaouen in the Rif, the Atlantic coast at Essaouira and Agadir, and finally the southern desert circuit at Merzouga and Ouarzazate.

If you are hitting only a few of these, skip to the chapter you need. If you are doing a longer loop, skim them all — the same core scams recur with local variations. The Marrakech *"that way is closed"* fake-guide play runs nearly word-for-word in Fez al-Bali and in the blue alleys of Chefchaouen. The Marrakech henna-ambush wrist-grab repeats on the Place Moulay Hassan in Essaouira and on Plaza Uta el-Hammam in Chefchaouen. The Casablanca petit-taxi meter refusal is the same script as the Tangier petit-taxi meter refusal, swapping airports and dirhām totals. Learn the pattern in one city, and you will spot it the instant you step off the *grand taxi* in the next.

## The Ten Cities at a Glance {-}

![Marrakech — Djemaa el-Fna at dusk with the Koutoubia Mosque minaret silhouetted against the Atlas Mountains](assets/cities/marrakech.jpg)

**Marrakech** — Morocco's tourist-economy heart and the country's densest scam ecosystem. Forty-five thousand riads and guesthouses inside a medina under three square kilometers, a year-round flow of European weekend-break visitors, and decades of practice on the touts' part. The flagship scam is the *"that way is closed today — festival"* fake-guide who walks you to his cousin's carpet shop and demands 200 dirhams; the henna-tattoo ambush at Djemaa el-Fna can run 200–300 MAD; the Souk Semmarine fake-argan-oil pitch can reach 300 MAD for diluted product worth 50; and the snake-charmer / monkey-photo trap on the central square typically opens at 200 MAD with two enforcers standing by.

![Fez — the leather tanneries of Chouara from a rooftop at golden hour, with the medina of Fez al-Bali behind](assets/cities/fez.jpg)

**Fez** — the older, quieter imperial-cities sister, with a medina of nine thousand alleys (genuinely — UNESCO listed Fez al-Bali on the World Heritage register in 1981 partly for the labyrinth itself). The Chouara tannery rooftop "viewing fee" runs 50–200 MAD on top of the suggested 20 dirham mint tip; faux guides at Bab Boujloud (the Blue Gate) work the same script as Marrakech with different street names; the Tala'a Kebira "I'll take you to the Qarawiyyin" detour ends in a carpet cooperative; and the *poterie* tour at Aïn Nokbi can include high-pressure pottery sales priced in euros not dirhams.

![Casablanca — the Hassan II Mosque rising over the Atlantic at sunset, silhouetted against the city skyline](assets/cities/casablanca.jpg)

**Casablanca** — Morocco's commercial capital, less a tourist destination than a transit point, but Mohammed V Airport (CMN) is the country's primary international gateway and the petit-taxi meter scam there is the most common single complaint. Drivers refuse the meter for the 30-kilometer ride into town and quote 400–600 MAD against the 250–300 MAD metered fare; the Hassan II Mosque has a faux-guide problem at the visitor entrance; the Maarif district restaurants are increasingly running an "indoor vs. terrace" two-price game; and the Habous Quarter has its share of carpet-shop bait-and-switch operators.

![Rabat — the Kasbah des Oudayas ramparts overlooking the Bouregreg river and the Atlantic](assets/cities/rabat.jpg)

**Rabat** — the political capital, much quieter than Casablanca or Marrakech, but Rabat-Salé Airport (RBA) has a petit-taxi refusal problem of its own and the Kasbah des Oudayas attracts faux guides at the main gate. The Hassan Tower / Mausoleum complex is largely scam-free; the medina is small and mostly safe; the most common single complaint is the airport-taxi script with the same shape as Casablanca.

![Tangier — the Bay of Tangier from the kasbah, with the medina above and the Mediterranean meeting the Atlantic](assets/cities/tangier.jpg)

**Tangier** — the ferry-port gateway from Spain, scam-density between Casablanca and the imperial cities. Ibn Battuta Airport (TNG) and the Tangier-Med ferry port run the petit-taxi refusal play at 250–500 MAD; the Petit Socco and the kasbah have a faux-guide ecosystem inherited from the international-zone era; the Cap Spartel "guide to the caves of Hercules" tour runs an entrance-fee surcharge; and the medina has its share of *"my brother's shop"* funneling.

![Chefchaouen — the blue medina alleys at golden hour with the Rif Mountains rising behind](assets/cities/chefchaouen.jpg)

**Chefchaouen** — the blue mountain town, two hours south of Tangier in the Rif. The most well-known single Moroccan scam runs here: the *hash-tout / fake-police shakedown*, where a young man on the medina alleys offers cannabis (the Rif is the country's growing region) and a "police officer" appears within minutes demanding 2,000–5,000 MAD in cash. The Tangier–Chefchaouen *grand-taxi* overcharge runs 700 MAD per person against the 70-dirham shared rate; Plaza Uta el-Hammam terrace cafes overcharge meals routinely; and the Spanish Mosque sunset path attracts teenage tip-extraction operators.

![Essaouira — the white-and-blue medina ramparts with seagulls and Atlantic surf](assets/cities/essaouira.jpg)

**Essaouira** — the Atlantic coast windsurfing town, three hours west of Marrakech. The fake argan-oil cooperative pitch runs heavily here (Essaouira sits at the heart of the argan-tree belt); the Place Moulay Hassan has a Gnawa-music donation script; the harbor has a fish-by-weight overcharge play at the seafront grills; and the camel/quad-bike rental on the southern beach has the same return-time damage-claim ecosystem as Merzouga.

![Agadir — the long curve of Agadir Bay with palm trees and the Atlantic](assets/cities/agadir.jpg)

**Agadir** — the Atlantic-coast resort, Morocco's beach-package gateway, and the only major city completely rebuilt after the 1960 earthquake — which means the scam ecosystem here is mostly resort-tourism rather than medina-tourism. The Agadir-Al Massira Airport (AGA) petit-taxi refusal runs 200–400 MAD; jet-ski deposit-and-damage scams run on the central beach in summer; the Souk El Had has a small fake-souvenir cooperative ecosystem; and the timeshare-sales-pitch hustle still operates on the corniche.

![Merzouga — the Erg Chebbi sand dunes at sunrise with a camel silhouette and ochre sky](assets/cities/merzouga.jpg)

**Merzouga** — the Sahara starting line at the eastern edge of the Erg Chebbi dunes, a ten-hour drive from Marrakech. The scam economy here is desert-tour scams, *auberge* scams, and equipment-rental scams. The Marrakech-to-Merzouga 3-day shared-tour bait-and-switch sells a luxury-tent-and-private-4×4 photo and delivers a basic-tent-and-shared-minivan; the camel-trek time-and-photo upcharge runs 200–400 MAD on top of the agreed price; the quad-bike "you damaged it" claim runs 1,000–3,000 MAD at return; and the fossil-vendor corridor between Erfoud and Merzouga sells cement trilobites at museum prices.

![Ouarzazate — the Aït Benhaddou ksar of red earth at golden hour](assets/cities/ouarzazate.jpg)

**Ouarzazate** — the gateway to the Aït Benhaddou ksar and the southern circuit, four hours from Marrakech over the Tizi n'Tichka pass. Aït Benhaddou has a fake-entry-fee operator at the parking-area approaches who collects 20–50 MAD before the (free) crossing of the riverbed; the Atlas Studios tour has a guide-tip pressure script; and the Ouarzazate–Marrakech grand-taxi route has a per-seat overcharge play.
