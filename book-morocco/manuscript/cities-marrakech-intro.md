Marrakech runs the densest tourist-scam economy in North Africa. The medina is barely three square kilometers, the Marrakech-Menara airport (RAK) feeds it daily flights from across Europe, and the touts, faux guides, henna teams, and shop-funneling operators have had decades to perfect their scripts. r/Morocco threads describe the experience the same way nearly every week: *"every day was relentless scamming of road closed or we are from your hotel."*

The risk concentrates in a tight zone. **Djemaa el-Fna** at the medina's south entrance, **Souk Semmarine** and **Souk el-Attarine** running north and east toward the Bahia Palace, the alleys between **Bab Doukkala** and the central square, and the rooftop-cafe strip overlooking the square — those four corridors are where almost every documented Marrakech scam happens. Step into the **Gueliz** new town or the **Hivernage** hotel district and the density drops by an order of magnitude.

The **'That Way's Closed' Fake Guide** is the volume risk and the city's signature scam. A man on the edge of Djemaa el-Fna says the route to the Bahia Palace is closed today — *festival, construction, his cousin's wedding* — and offers to walk you the right way. Fifteen turns later you are in his uncle's carpet shop and he wants 200 MAD ($20) when you try to leave. The original route was open the whole time. The defense is to keep walking past anyone who proactively volunteers route information, eyes on offline Google Maps, and respond to *"that's closed"* with *"la, shukran"* while continuing in your original direction.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Henna Tattoo Ambush** runs on Djemaa el-Fna and at the Koutoubia Mosque approach: a woman grabs your wrist, paints unsolicited, demands 200–300 MAD with two male relatives standing by. The **Snake Charmer Photo Trap** on the central square typically opens at 200 MAD with a cobra suddenly draped over your shoulders before any price was agreed. The **Fake Argan Oil and Spice Shop** in the Souk Semmarine and Souk el-Attarine alleys can charge 300 MAD for a 50-MAD diluted product, with euro pricing used to obscure the markup. A **Petit Taxi Meter Scam** at the Menara Airport and the Djemaa el-Fna rank quotes 100–250 MAD against a 60-MAD metered fare.

A safe default: book a riad inside the medina but agree the airport-pickup price in writing through the riad before arrival; from RAK take the metered petit taxi (~70 MAD to the medina) or the new airport bus 19 to Djemaa el-Fna for 30 MAD; photograph every restaurant menu before sitting; treat every *"the road is closed"* as a script; and call **19** or walk to the Brigade Touristique desk at the central commissariat off **Avenue Mohammed V** in Gueliz if an approach escalates. Inside the medina, Bab Doukkala commissariat is the closest police station to most riads.
