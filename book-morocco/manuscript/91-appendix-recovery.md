# Appendix B: Post-Scam Recovery Playbook {-}

If something in this book has happened to you, this appendix is where you start. The actions below are organized by urgency, not by severity. Even if you are not sure yet how serious the scam was, working through the first two timelines will put you in the best possible position to recover your money, your evidence, and your peace of mind.

## In the first fifteen minutes

Leave the venue. If you are physically inside a place where you were scammed — a carpet shop, an argan-oil cooperative, a tea-house with a suddenly inflated bill, a tour-operator desk, a quad-bike rental booth at the base of the Erg Chebbi — do not argue, do not try to negotiate, and do not pay with a second form of payment.

Do not sign anything, including credit-card receipts. A signed slip is much harder to dispute than one you refused to sign. If staff are blocking your exit, dial **19** (Sûreté Nationale) on your phone with the screen visible. Most station dispatchers in Casablanca, Rabat, Marrakech, and Tangier have French-language officers; in smaller cities, switch to *"je veux porter plainte"* (I want to file a report) or *"j'appelle la police"* (I am calling the police) — the words are universally understood.

Screenshot everything you can, as discreetly as possible: the bill, the sign outside the venue, the street intersection, any business-license certificate (the *patente* number is required to be displayed in every Moroccan business), the card terminal, and the face of the person who served you if you can do it without escalating.

If your bag or wallet was just lifted in the street, shout *"Sarrak!"* (thief). Moroccan crowd-response on this word is reliable — bystanders typically move toward the thief, and a Moroccan in a tourist corridor will usually try to help a foreigner who has just been stolen from.

## Within the first hour

Walk to the nearest **Brigade Touristique** desk or commissariat de police. The Brigade Touristique operates dedicated tourist-police desks in Marrakech (near Cyberparc Arsat Moulay Abdeslam, off Avenue Mohammed V), Casablanca (at the central commissariat near the Old Medina), Fez (near Bab Boujloud at the Place de l'Atlas), Tangier (near Place de France), Agadir (off Boulevard du 20 Août), and Rabat (off Avenue Mohammed V). In any other city, a regular Sûreté Nationale commissariat will take a tourist report.

Say *"bghit n-dīr shikaya"* (I want to file a report) or *"je veux porter plainte"*. You will walk out with a printed *shikaya* — the Moroccan legal name for a police report. Get a copy with the station's stamp and the report reference number. This is the document your credit-card issuer and your travel insurer will demand.

Freeze the card that was used. International emergency lines for the major card issuers are listed in Appendix C and are callable collect from any phone in Morocco. Maroc Telecom, Orange Maroc, and Inwi all carry collect-call routing to Visa, Mastercard, and American Express international centers.

## Within the first twenty-four hours

File a chargeback with your card issuer. Use the *shikaya* reference number as documentation. Chargebacks initiated within twenty-four hours have a significantly higher success rate than those filed a week later — Visa's and Mastercard's dispute timelines both penalize delay.

Call the **Allô Wlad Lablad** consumer hotline at **5050** (toll-free from Moroccan mobiles) for orientation if you are unsure which agency to escalate to. The line is staffed by the *Direction de la Protection du Consommateur*; English-language support is limited but French is fluent.

If your passport was photographed, held by an "officer," or taken, notify your embassy or consulate immediately. The US Embassy in Rabat and the US Consulate General in Casablanca both staff after-hours emergency lines; British, Canadian, and Irish embassies in Rabat do the same. Numbers are in Appendix C.

Email your travel-insurance provider with a summary, timestamps, the *shikaya* reference, and photographs of the bill.

If the scam involved an off-platform riad/hotel booking made by WhatsApp, Instagram DM, or direct bank transfer, open a bank-fraud dispute on the sending account. Western Union and MoneyGram disputes are nearly impossible to recover; credit-card and SEPA-bank disputes are recoverable if filed promptly.

If the scam involved a desert tour booked through a specific Tripadvisor listing, open the dispute through Tripadvisor's customer-protection program in addition to the credit-card chargeback.

## Within the first week

File a formal consumer-protection complaint with the **Direction de la Protection du Consommateur** at **[khidmat-almostahlik.ma](https://www.khidmat-almostahlik.ma)** or by phone at **5050**. The DPC reviews and forwards consumer complaints to the relevant regulator (banking → Bank Al-Maghrib; transport → Ministry of Transport; tourism → ONMT).

For **transport overcharging** — petit taxis, grands taxis, ride-hail — file a parallel complaint with the city's *Préfecture* transportation desk. Marrakech, Casablanca, and Tangier have dedicated tourist-transport oversight after the 2019 enforcement push.

For **banking and payment disputes** — escalate to **Bank Al-Maghrib** (the central bank) at **[bkam.ma](https://www.bkam.ma)** if your card issuer's chargeback is rejected. Bank Al-Maghrib has consumer-facing dispute mediation.

Submit the full travel-insurance claim with all evidence gathered so far.

Consider leaving a neutral, factual review on Google Maps or Tripadvisor. Do not name individual staff members; describe the business by its official name only. Moroccan defamation law is moderately strict; stick to verifiable facts and avoid emotion-laden descriptions.

## If your phone or SIM was stolen

Call your carrier immediately to suspend the line. The major Moroccan carriers:

- **Maroc Telecom (IAM)** — dial **0610** from any IAM mobile (toll-free), or +212 537 71 21 21 from abroad
- **Orange Maroc** — dial **0700** from any Orange mobile, or +212 522 88 88 88 from abroad
- **Inwi** — dial **120** from any Inwi mobile, or +212 522 88 88 88 from abroad

If you are on a tourist eSIM (Airalo, Saily, Holafly, Nomad), the app will let you suspend the line in a few taps.

File a police report including your phone's **IMEI** number. The IMEI lets the Sûreté Nationale flag the device and is required for any insurance claim.

Lock and wipe remotely via Apple's Find My or Google's Find My Device.

Change passwords immediately. Start with email, then banking, then social.

## If the scam involved drink-spiking or physical harm

Go to a hospital before you go to the police. Toxicology tests have a short window. Reliable 24/7 hospitals with English-speaking staff in each city are in Appendix C.

**Emergency numbers in Morocco:**

- **19** — Sûreté Nationale (police, urban areas)
- **177** — Gendarmerie Royale (police, highways and rural areas)
- **15** — SAMU (medical emergency / ambulance)
- **150** — Protection Civile (fire and rescue)
- **5050** — Allô Wlad Lablad (consumer-protection hotline)

Your embassy can help coordinate English-speaking medical care and, if your passport was involved, coordinate an emergency replacement. The US Embassy and the British Embassy in Rabat both maintain consular-doctor referral lists for Casablanca, Marrakech, Fez, and Tangier.
