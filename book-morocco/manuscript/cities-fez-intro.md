Fez is the older imperial-cities sister to Marrakech, with a medina UNESCO listed in 1981 partly for the labyrinth itself — Fez al-Bali genuinely contains nearly nine thousand alleys, and most of them dead-end into private houses or other alleys. The scam ecosystem here is quieter than Marrakech's but specific to that geography: the labyrinth is the script. Most of the volume runs as faux-guide work, with the tannery and Qarawiyyin University as the magnet destinations.

The risk concentrates at the medina's two main gates and along the *Tala'a Kebira*. **Bab Boujloud** (the Blue Gate at the western end), **Bab Rcif**, the **Tala'a Kebira** and **Tala'a Sghira** axes running from the Blue Gate down toward the center, and the **Chouara Tannery** rooftop strip — those four corridors absorb nearly all of the documented Fez scams. The Ville Nouvelle (the modern French-built city outside the medina) is essentially scam-free.

The **'Road Is Closed' False Guidance Scam** is the volume risk. A man near Bab Boujloud, sometimes wearing a vague vest that suggests authority, tells you the route to the Qarawiyyin or to the Bou Inania Madrasa is closed for prayers, for a moussem, or for restoration work. He offers to show you the way around. Twenty minutes through the medina later you are in a carpet cooperative or in his uncle's argan-oil shop, and the *"guide"* expects 100–200 MAD on the way out. The defense is identical to Marrakech: do not break stride for anyone who opens with route information, keep an offline-map app open, and say *"la, shukran"* while continuing in your original direction.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Unofficial 'Free' Guide Commission Tour** runs through the medina's spice and metal souks: a self-described guide walks you to four cooperative-owned shops where the operator earns commission on whatever you buy — typical price gap 30–50% above market for the same goods one street over. The **Tannery Viewpoint 'Mint Sprig' Entry Scam** at Chouara presents itself as a free rooftop view but expects 50–200 MAD on the way out, with the leather shop downstairs as the upsell pressure point. The **Moped Navigation Chaos** play uses Fez's narrow alleys against you — a moped or porter cart "accidentally" runs you toward an unfamiliar exit, where a *"helpful"* man waits to redirect you for a fee. **Counterfeit Argan Oil and Spice Products** appear in shops near Bab Rcif at euro prices that mask 4× markup over the dirham equivalent.

A safe default: enter the medina through Bab Boujloud, take photos of intersections you pass to retrace your route, accept the help of shopkeepers already at work in their shops (they tend to be honest because they have something to lose) but never of men who approached you, and ask your riad to call an ONMT-licensed guide if you want a real medina tour — the standard rate is 300–400 MAD for a half-day. The Brigade Touristique desk shares the **Place de l'Atlas commissariat** near the Bab Boujloud end of the medina; **CHU Hassan II** is the public hospital on the Route Sidi Hrazem.
