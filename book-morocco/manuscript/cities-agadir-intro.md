Agadir is Morocco's Atlantic-coast resort city, rebuilt almost entirely after the 1960 earthquake and now functioning as the country's beach-package gateway — flights from Manchester, Düsseldorf, Brussels, and Paris fill the all-inclusive hotels along Boulevard du 20 Août in season. There is no medina to speak of (the original was destroyed in 1960; what remains is Agadir Oufella, the kasbah ruins on the hill above) and the scam ecosystem reflects that: it is mostly resort-tourism scams rather than medina-tourism scams, concentrated on the airport, the corniche, and the souk.

The risk concentrates at four nodes. **Al Massira Airport (AGA)** taxi rank. The hotel-rank petit taxis along **Boulevard du 20 Août** between the Royal Atlas, Sofitel Royal Bay, and the central beach hotels. The **Agadir Corniche** promenade and **Marina Agadir**. And the **Souk El Had**, the city's main market, with its argan-oil and souvenir-craft stalls.

The **Hotel-Rank Petit Taxi Per-Person Trap** is the city's volume risk. The petit taxis on the Boulevard du 20 Août hotel rank operate the standard meter-refusal play but with a per-person twist: instead of a single ride at 200–400 MAD, drivers quote the same fare *per passenger* — a family of four can be quoted 800–1,500 MAD for a ride that should be 60–80 MAD on the meter. The defense is to insist on the meter (*"avec le compteur"*) and confirm aloud that the meter rate is the total, not per person.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Corniche Gift-and-Guilt Approach** runs along the central promenade and at the Marina Agadir entrance: a stranger gives you a small "gift" — a bracelet, a small ceramic, a sprig of dried herb — and demands payment afterward, with the guilt-and-stranger framing pressuring tourists to comply at 50–200 MAD. The **Souk El Had Argan Oil Dilution** runs in the spice and argan stalls of the central market with euro pricing on diluted product; the same stalls quote 30–50% lower in dirhams to Moroccan customers. The **Beach Parasol & Parking Shakedown** on Plage Tama and at Taghazout Beach (35 km north) charges arriving cars 20–50 MAD for "parking" on what is public access, and parasol attendants demand 50–100 MAD for chairs that should be free or very cheap; legitimate beach-club rentals come with a printed receipt.

A safe default: take the metered petit taxi from AGA (about 200–250 MAD to the central hotels) or pre-arrange transfer through your hotel; insist on *"compteur, total"* and confirm the meter is the all-in price; refuse all *"gifts"* on the corniche by physically not extending your hand; buy argan oil only at AMIGHA-certified outlets or the stall your hotel recommends; and pay for parking only when handed a printed ticket. The Brigade Touristique desk is off **Boulevard du 20 Août** near the central beach corniche; **Polyclinique Atlas Agadir** on Avenue Hassan II is the private hospital with English-speaking staff.
