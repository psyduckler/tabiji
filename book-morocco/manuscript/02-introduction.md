# How to Use This Book {-}

Morocco is extraordinary. It is also, for the first-time visitor, one of the most scam-active countries on the African continent. Marrakech's medina runs the densest faux-guide and shop-funneling operation in North Africa — the *"that way is closed today"* play has been documented in Moroccan press since at least 2012, and the Brigade Touristique still posts warning placards at Bab Doukkala and the entrances to Djemaa el-Fna every season. Chefchaouen's hash-and-fake-cop shakedown is so well known in the Rif that the US State Department and the British Foreign Office both warn about it on their Morocco country pages. Petit-taxi meter refusals hit Casablanca's Mohammed V Airport and Tangier's Ibn Battuta Airport on most arriving flights, and Reddit's r/Morocco threads document new variations almost weekly.

We want you to come home with stories about the call to prayer at dawn over the Fez medina, mint tea on a riad rooftop in Marrakech, the sand dunes at Erg Chebbi turning pink at sunrise, and the Atlantic crashing on the Essaouira ramparts — not about a 5,000-dirham hash shakedown in Chefchaouen or a wallet lifted in Djemaa el-Fna before you even reached your riad.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents 61 specific scams that trip up first-time visitors to Morocco, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been reported in Moroccan press — *Le Matin*, *Hespress*, *Yabiladi*, *Morocco World News*, *L'Économiste*, *Le360* — cross-referenced against Brigade Touristique and Sûreté Nationale records, US and UK embassy advisories, and confirmed by recent traveler accounts on r/Morocco, r/MoroccoTravel, and city-specific subreddits.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First, the name of the scam, the category it falls into (faux guide, transport, overcharging, counterfeit, drug-extortion, and so on), and how serious and how common it is. Then two short sections: **How this scam works** — a plain narrative of how it unfolds in real time, with real dirham amounts where we have them — and **How to avoid it**, the specific moves that shut it down. Each entry ends with where it happens and a watercolor comic showing the exchange so you can spot the pattern visually, not just in words.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. Most scams in Morocco are variations on six patterns. Learn the patterns and you will spot a new scam the moment it starts — even one that is not in this book. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will then read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters, you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **Darija exit-phrase card**. Short Moroccan-Arabic phrases you can say, word for word, at the moments when your politeness would normally get you into trouble. Darija is the spoken Moroccan vernacular — different enough from Modern Standard Arabic that even Arabic speakers from the Gulf find it hard. You do not need to be fluent. You need three or four phrases said clearly and not walked back. Take a screenshot of the appendix on your phone.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A step-by-step flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens, you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. The Sûreté Nationale, the Gendarmerie Royale, the Brigade Touristique, ambulance, embassies, and the nearest English-speaking medical care for every city in this book, organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A note on French

Morocco was a French protectorate from 1912 to 1956, and French remains the de facto language of business, banking, signage, and most service interactions. If your French is decent, it is more useful in the moment than your Darija. The phrases in Appendix A include a French-equivalent line where it matters.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and skim the three appendices. Read the chapters for the cities you will be visiting. Take a screenshot of the phrase card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book turns out to apply during your trip, open it again on the train from Casablanca to Marrakech, or in your riad, and check what to do.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.

*Triq slama* — safe road, safe travels.
