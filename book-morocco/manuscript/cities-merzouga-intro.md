Merzouga is the Erg Chebbi dunes — the eastern Sahara starting line, the postcard image of Morocco, and the most heavily-scammed tour destination in the country. Most Merzouga visitors arrive on a 3-day Marrakech-to-Merzouga shared tour booked online, and the scam economy here is shaped almost entirely by that pipeline. The scams are not medina scams; they are *tour-package* scams, *auberge* scams, and desert-equipment scams.

The risk concentrates at five nodes. The **online booking step** in Marrakech, where unverified operators and clone Tripadvisor pages sell tours that don't match what arrives. The **auberge check-in desk** at the Erg Chebbi-edge hotels in **Merzouga** and **Hassi Labied**, where the upgrade-pressure script runs. The **camel-trek departure points** at sunset, especially the Hassi Labied corridor. The **quad-bike rental booths** at the dune base. And the **Marrakech-to-Merzouga and Ouarzazate-to-Merzouga driving routes** themselves, where the carpet-shop detour is built into many tours.

The **Marrakech-to-Merzouga Tour Bait-and-Switch** is the volume risk and the country's most-disputed single scam category. An unverified operator (often a clone Tripadvisor listing or a fresh Instagram profile) sells a 3-day tour for €280–€350 with photographs of luxury Berber camp tents, en-suite bathrooms, and a private 4×4 SUV. What arrives is a basic-tent group camp shared with twenty others, a 2017 minivan that can break down on the Tizi n'Tichka pass, and a guide whose itinerary pivots toward four carpet-cooperative stops where the operator earns commission. By the time you can dispute, the operator has gone dark. The defense is to book only through verified operators with a published Marrakech office and visible Tripadvisor verification (Plan-It Morocco, Sahara Magic Tours, Get Your Guide local-listed partners) — expect to pay €600–€900 for a 3-day private 4×4 with a real desert camp rather than €300 for the bait-and-switch.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Camel Trek Time-and-Photo Upcharge** runs at the Erg Chebbi camel-trek departure points: an agreed 2-hour ride at sunset becomes 100–400 MAD ($10–40) of additional fees for "extension," "private photos," or "private return" partway through, with the camels stopped halfway as the negotiation venue. The **Auberge VIP Upgrade Pressure** at check-in sells the *"private dome tent with hot shower"* upgrade for 500–1,500 MAD on top of an already-paid package, framed as if the standard tent will be uncomfortable. The **Quad Bike & ATV Damage Surcharge** at the rental booths near Hassi Labied accepts a deposit, finds a "scratch" or "damage" at return, and demands 1,000–3,000 MAD. The **Driver-Routed Carpet Shop Detour** on the Marrakech-Merzouga and Ouarzazate-Merzouga roads is built into many tour itineraries — the driver receives a commission whether you buy or not. **Fake Trilobite & Ammonite Fossil Sales** run on the Erfoud-Merzouga roadside stalls — many of the *"fossils"* on offer are cement casts, priced at museum rates.

A safe default: book Sahara tours only through operators with a verifiable Marrakech office and recent Tripadvisor reviews; refuse all auberge upgrades on arrival ("the standard tent is fine, thank you"); agree the camel-ride duration and total in writing before mounting; take a smartphone-video walk-around of any quad bike before driving away; and assume any roadside fossil for sale below museum prices is cement. The nearest Gendarmerie Royale post is in **Rissani** (35 km north); the nearest hospitals are in **Erfoud** (basic) and **Errachidia** (larger). For serious medical issues, alert your travel insurer immediately — air-evac to Marrakech or Fez is the standard arrangement.
