# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication. Moroccan phone numbers shifted to 10-digit dialing in 2009; current numbers begin with **0** for domestic dialing or **+212** from abroad (drop the leading 0 when dialing internationally — *0537 63 33 33* becomes *+212 537 63 33 33*).

If a number below fails to connect, search the organization's name plus *Maroc* or the city name in French. Major Moroccan organizations publish a customer-service number on their primary website; the number on the back of any payment card is always callable collect from Moroccan land-lines and most mobile carriers.

**Before you travel**, double-check your own embassy or consulate's current number on its official website — travel.state.gov (US), gov.uk (UK), travel.gc.ca (Canada), dfa.ie (Ireland), smartraveller.gov.au (Australia).

## The numbers that work everywhere in Morocco

- **Sûreté Nationale (urban police):** **19**. The dispatcher routes to the nearest commissariat. French-speaking; English available at major-city stations.
- **Gendarmerie Royale (rural police, highways):** **177**. Used outside city limits. French and Arabic.
- **Medical emergency / SAMU ambulance:** **15**.
- **Protection Civile (fire, rescue):** **150**.
- **Allô Wlad Lablad (consumer-protection hotline):** **5050**. Toll-free from Moroccan mobiles. French; limited English.
- **Tourist information (ONMT):** **0537 27 83 00** (Rabat headquarters); regional offices in every major city.

## If you lose your passport

1. **Report it first** — go to the nearest Sûreté Nationale commissariat or Brigade Touristique desk and file a *shikaya* (police report). You will leave with a stamped report bearing a reference number. This is the document your embassy wants to see before issuing an emergency replacement.
2. **Call your embassy's main switchboard or after-hours line.** US, UK, Canadian, and Irish (via Lisbon) embassies and consulates all issue same-day or next-day emergency travel documents.
3. **Bring** the *shikaya*, any remaining photo ID, two passport-sized photos (any *photomaton* or photo studio in any Moroccan city center can produce them in 10 minutes), and the embassy's specified payment method.
4. **Exit-stamp clearance:** Moroccan border police at airports and ferry ports will accept the stamped *shikaya* + the embassy's emergency travel document together — but always confirm with your airline 24 hours before flying.

## If your phone is stolen

1. Freeze the SIM with the carrier lines above (Maroc Telecom 0610, Orange 0700, Inwi 120).
2. File a *shikaya* at the nearest commissariat with the phone's IMEI.
3. Lock and wipe remotely via Apple Find My or Google Find My Device.
4. Change passwords immediately. Start with email.

## If your credit card is stolen or skimmed

1. Freeze the card by calling the number on the back (or one of the international card-issuer lines above).
2. Report to police with a *shikaya*.
3. Dispute the charges with your issuer.
4. Watch for follow-on phishing — skimmed cards in Morocco have been linked to SMS phishing impersonating Moroccan banks. The bank-Al-Maghrib will never call to ask for your PIN.

## A closing note

This appendix is meant to be read once on the plane, screenshot for offline use, and forgotten about for the rest of your trip. Phone numbers change. Consulates move. Hospitals renumber their switchboards. If any number in this list fails, **19** — Sûreté Nationale — will still work everywhere in Morocco.

Travel safely, travel calmly, and if the worst happens, remember: a 2,000-dirham bill is an inconvenience, not a crisis. Morocco is not a dangerous country. Help is close, and French is closer than you think.
