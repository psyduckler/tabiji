Casablanca is Morocco's commercial capital and the country's primary international gateway. It is not a tourist city the way Marrakech and Fez are — most visitors fly into Mohammed V Airport (CMN) and immediately catch the train south — but the airport, the train stations, and the few tourist-magnet venues (the Hassan II Mosque, the Old Medina, the Habous Quarter) absorb a disproportionate share of the country's transit-scam volume.

The risk concentrates at three nodes. **Mohammed V International Airport (CMN)** taxi rank, where the petit-taxi meter refusal runs on most arriving flights. The **Marché Central** seafood-restaurant strip on Boulevard Mohammed V, where "no menu, no posted prices" is the script. And the **Old Medina** and **Habous Quarter**, where faux guides and pressure-sales carpet shops operate with the same playbook as Marrakech but lower volume.

The **Marché Central Seafood Restaurant Overcharge** is the city's volume risk and the most-reported single scam in the Casablanca tourist corridor. Tourists are seated at a fish restaurant near the central market, no posted price list appears, the waiter describes the fish in vague *"market price"* terms, and the bill at the end runs 800–2,000 MAD ($80–200) for two people on dishes that should run 150–300 MAD ($15–30) at any neighborhood Moroccan restaurant. The defense is to ask for the menu in writing — *"aafak la carte"* — before you sit, photograph the prices, and confirm aloud what you are ordering.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The **Petit Taxi Meter Refusal and Overcharge** at CMN, Casa Voyageurs, and Casa Port quotes 250–600 MAD for the airport-to-Maarif run against the metered fare of about 250 MAD daytime / 380 MAD with the legal night supplement after 8pm. The **Fake Guide and Medina Commission Hustle** runs from Bab Marrakech and through the Habous Quarter, with carpet showrooms and brass-and-copper cooperatives as the funnel destinations. The **Airport Currency Exchange Rip-Off** at Global Exchange and the airport ATM kiosks runs 20–30% above the Bank Al-Maghrib mid-market rate; the in-city ATMs of Attijariwafa Bank, BMCE, and Banque Populaire give a fair rate. **Pickpocketing on Public Transport** is unusually high on the Casa Port–Casa Voyageurs platforms and inside the Casablanca tramway during morning rush hour.

A safe default: take the **ONCF train** directly from CMN's airport-station basement to Casa Voyageurs (43 MAD, runs every hour); avoid the airport taxi rank entirely if you have a 20-minute window. From Casa Voyageurs, the city's metered petit taxis are red and insist on the meter when you say *"avec le compteur"*. For dinner, Boulevard Mohammed V, Maarif, and Anfa have hundreds of restaurants with posted menus — pick from those rather than from a *"market-price"* board near the Marché Central. The Brigade Touristique desk is at the central commissariat near the Old Medina off **Boulevard Mohammed V**; the US Consulate General is at **8 Boulevard Moulay Youssef** (+212 522 64 20 99).
