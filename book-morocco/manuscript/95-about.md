# About Tabiji {-}

Tabiji is a travel-safety research organization. We document scams, health risks, and local advisories for travelers across more than fifty cities worldwide, and we publish everything as a free public resource at **[tabiji.ai](https://tabiji.ai)**.

We started this series after noticing that most scam roundups online skip the one thing travelers actually need in the moment: the exact phrase, the exact move, that ends the interaction. Remembering a list of 61 scams is hard when you are tired and under pressure. Remembering six patterns is much easier. That is the shape we have tried to give this book.

Every scam documented here was drawn from Moroccan-press coverage — *Le Matin*, *Hespress*, *Yabiladi*, *Morocco World News*, *L'Économiste*, *Le360*, and *Maroc Hebdo* — cross-referenced against the Sûreté Nationale, Brigade Touristique advisories, and US and UK embassy country pages, and confirmed against firsthand traveler accounts on Reddit's r/Morocco, r/MoroccoTravel, r/travel, r/solotravel, and TripAdvisor's Morocco forums. Where we have named arrest dates or documented dirham amounts, we cite them. Where a pattern has been reported but not formally investigated, we say so.

For new scams and live updates to the cases in this book, visit **[tabiji.ai/scams/country/ma](https://tabiji.ai/scams/country/ma/)**. We maintain the database quarterly, and most corrections reach the site within a week of a credible report.

We hope you come home with stories about the call to prayer over the Fez medina at dawn, the Atlas snow above Marrakech in winter, the Sahara dunes turning pink at sunrise outside Merzouga, and the Atlantic surf rolling into Essaouira at sunset.

— Tabiji
