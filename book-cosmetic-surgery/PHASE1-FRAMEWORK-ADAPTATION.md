# Phase 1 — Framework Adaptation Map

Element-by-element mapping from `book-dental/` to `book-cosmetic-surgery/`.
Decides what reuses verbatim, what adapts with surgical examples, and what
is net new.

**Status:** Phase 1 deliverable. Locks during Phase 2 writing.

## The Five Rules

The five rules from the dental book transfer almost verbatim. The examples
and stakes change.

| Rule | Dental framing | Cosmetic surgery framing |
|---|---|---|
| **Rule 1: Do not buy a transformation before you understand the diagnosis.** | A treatment plan is not just a price list. It is a claim about what is happening in your mouth. | A treatment plan is not a procedure name. "Rhinoplasty" is a category; the actual plan describes which structures will be altered, the surgical approach, the expected change in proportion, and what cannot be predicted. |
| **Rule 2: The cheapest quote is not the cheapest outcome.** | Add flights, hotels, unpaid time off, complications care. | Same calculus, with higher complication-care costs. A $4,500 BBL package becomes substantially more expensive if it puts you in the CDC's documented Dominican Republic mortality data. |
| **Rule 3: You need a plan for aftercare before you need aftercare.** | Implants, crowns, bridges require maintenance. | Surgical recovery requires weeks of monitoring; revision rates run 5–20% by procedure; complication care can require hospitalization. Aftercare planning is more critical, not less. |
| **Rule 4: Pressure is information.** | Same. | Same, but cosmetic-surgery clinics use more aggressive influencer-style and discount-deadline pressure than dental. The pattern is more practiced. |
| **Rule 5: Keep your exit options open.** | Extracting teeth, reducing tooth structure, placing implants can close doors. | Most cosmetic surgery is even less reversible than most dental work. Once tissue is removed (liposuction, blepharoplasty) or moved (facelift, BBL), restoring baseline is rare. |

**Adaptation effort:** Light. Examples rewritten, rule text mostly unchanged.

## The Seven Leverage Points

Stage structure transfers exactly. Specific leverage examples differ.

| Stage | Dental leverage description | Cosmetic surgery leverage description |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Before the Search | Cost shock is a trigger, not a treatment plan. | Cost shock AND aspiration are both triggers. The aspirational entry point (transformation reels, influencer codes) is the cosmetic-specific addition. |
| 2. The Funnel | Once you inquire, you enter a sales process. | Same dynamic; cosmetic clinics often have more sophisticated funnels (longer message threads, more video content, more "concierge" upsells). |
| 3. Social Proof | Reviews reduce fear but hide complications. | Same; before/after photographic content is more curated and emotionally powerful in cosmetic; group dynamics on Instagram/RealSelf etc. add a layer dental doesn't have. |
| 4. Destination Fit | No best country; choose by fit. | Same — and stronger argument here because BBL specifically has shown destination-specific mortality patterns (CDC MMWR data). Fit ≠ ranking. |
| 5. The Quote | Make quotes comparable before comparing prices. | Surgical quote line items differ from dental: surgeon fee, anesthesia fee, OR/facility fee, implants, garments, lab work, follow-up, revision policy. |
| 6. The Treatment Timeline | Some treatments need healing time. | Cosmetic surgical timelines are more biologically constrained: skin healing, scar maturation, swelling resolution, implant settling. Some surgeries cannot be combined safely; timeline pressure is the most dangerous lever. |
| 7. The Return Home | The trip ends; the dental work does not. | The trip ends; the recovery is just beginning. Companion role, drain management, suture removal, lymphatic massage, follow-up imaging — all happen at home or via telehealth. |

**Adaptation effort:** Light to moderate. Same structure; examples rewritten.

## The 20-Minute Safety Pause

Eight questions. Mostly the same, with surgical specifics.

| # | Dental question | Cosmetic surgery question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What diagnosis is the plan based on? | What is the procedure-specific plan, in writing? "Rhinoplasty" or "BBL" is not enough; the plan must describe what structures are being altered and what is being preserved. |
| 2 | What records did the clinic review? | Photos alone are inadequate for surgical planning. In-person physical examination is non-negotiable for almost all cosmetic surgery decisions. |
| 3 | What parts of the plan are irreversible? | Almost all of it. Be specific. Tissue removal (lipo, blepharoplasty), tissue movement (BBL, facelift), and implant placement are all categorically harder to reverse than dental restorations. |
| 4 | What is included in the quote and what is not? | Surgeon's fee, anesthesia fee, OR/facility fee, implant fees, garments, lab work, hotel, transport, taxes, payment fees, follow-up, revision policy, complication policy. |
| 5 | What happens if something goes wrong after you return home? | Specifically: hospitalization coverage, revision policy, who pays for stateside complication management, who evaluates remotely, where is the documented protocol. |
| 6 | How many trips and how much time? | Minimum 7-day stay for most cosmetic surgery; longer for facelift, body procedures, combinations. Recovery extends weeks to months at home. |
| 7 | What is the clinic asking you to do before full information? | Deposit, flights, nonrefundable hotel, social media consents, demonstration-case agreements, photo/video marketing rights. |
| 8 | Have you received at least one independent opinion? | Same — and for cosmetic, the book adds a self-administered BDD screening tool (BDDQ) as part of the pause workflow. A positive screen is a reason to pause. |

**Adaptation effort:** Moderate. Question structure preserved; surgical specificity added.

## Decision Gates

Same recurring pattern as dental: "Pause if any of these are true." Cosmetic-surgery-specific items added.

### Pause Decision Gate (paste into manuscript Chapter 0/Front Matter and Chapter 13):

Pause before paying or traveling if:

- The plan describes a procedure name, not the procedure's specific anatomical scope
- The surgeon has not examined you in person and the clinic considers photos sufficient
- You are being asked to combine multiple procedures in one operation without explicit written discussion of combined risk
- The "all-inclusive package" price does not itemize the surgeon's fee separately from anesthesia and facility
- The clinic will not provide the named anesthesia provider's credentials before deposit
- The surgeon will not name the specific implant manufacturer for any implant device
- The clinic discourages or punishes a request for a second opinion
- A pre-op mental health screening was not offered or recommended
- You feel rushed by both the clinic and your family/partner
- You have answered yes to 3+ items on the BDD self-screen and have not yet had a mental health consultation

## Disclaimers

### Important Disclaimer (front matter)

Dental's disclaimer is the floor. Surgery adds:
- Explicit naming of anesthesia complications
- Explicit naming of surgical mortality and the documented patterns (CDC MMWR, ASPS warnings)
- Explicit naming of infection (M. abscessus outbreaks)
- Explicit naming of DVT, PE, fat embolism
- Stronger language about "this is education, not advice"
- Reference to the citation system (the book cites public-health sources)

### Appendix Disclaimer (back matter)

Dental's is the floor. Surgery adds:
- Body image / mental health framing language
- Specific reference to BDD and pre-op screening recommendation
- The editorial position on "procedures we recommend against" (defended as journalism based on cited evidence)

**Both disclaimers require legal review** before publication. Non-negotiable.

## Reviewer Workflow

Adapted from dental for higher-stakes content. See `BRIEF.md` §11 for cost and timeline.

```
Manuscript first draft (Phase 2)
    │
    │ Editorial Tier 1/2/3 (Phase 3, internal)
    │
    ▼
Reviewer-ready draft
    │
    ├──► Plastic Surgeon reviewer ──┐
    ├──► Anesthesiologist reviewer ─┤
    ├──► Malpractice attorney ──────┤
    ├──► Sensitivity reader ────────┤  (all parallel, Phase 5)
    ├──► Copyeditor ────────────────┤
    └──► (blurb solicitations) ─────┘
                                    │
                                    ▼
                                Post-review revisions (Phase 6)
                                    │
                                    │ Typesetting
                                    │
                                    │ Proofreader (Phase 6 continued)
                                    │
                                    ▼
                                KDP upload + proof copy + launch (Phase 7)
```

**Key difference from dental workflow:** plastic surgeon reviewer reviews FIRST and gets right of refusal on Chapter 28 ("procedures we recommend against") language. If the surgeon reviewer wants softer language than the cited evidence supports, that becomes a Phase 5 editorial decision before going to legal review.
