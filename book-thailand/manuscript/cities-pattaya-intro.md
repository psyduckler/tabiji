Pattaya’s scam economy is essentially Walking Street and the two beaches that frame it. Walking Street — the 500-meter neon strip between Beach Road and Bali Hai Pier — concentrates the go-go bar lady-drink escalation, the fake Muay Thai fight with photo surcharges, the “can I see your currency” money-switch crews, and the drink-bill extortion script in venues where four men position themselves between you and the door when the padded bill arrives. The lady-drink mechanic is the most common: a hostess sits down within 90 seconds, orders a drink, two more drinks appear “for her friends” at 250–400 baht each, and at midnight the bill is 7,500 baht for seven beers and nine drinks you cannot account for. r/Pattaya veterans describe the pattern as “test-the-waters” — hostesses probe kind-hearted men and escalate until the target either objects (smaller scam) or doesn’t (full one). The defense is the same every time: pay for each drink at the bar as you order it, never run a tab, and carry only 2,000–3,000 baht cash with cards left at the hotel.

The beach side runs the jet-ski damage shakedown that UK, US, and Australian government travel advisories name Pattaya for specifically. A 1,200-baht-per-hour rental turns into a 25,000-baht “scratch” on return, with a “police officer” materializing unusually fast to side with the operator. The *baht-bus* (red *songthaew*) overcharge — 300 baht retroactively reclassified as a “private charter” when locals pay 10 — runs on Second Road and the Jomtien route. The Indian and Middle Eastern “miracle supplement” crew works Beach Road in the afternoon; in November 2025, *Khaosod English* reported that a German tourist lost roughly 150,000 baht (€4,000) to the Pattaya coconut-oil variant, and the Pattaya Police ran a 2025 zero-tolerance crackdown tracking the rotation. A safe default: use Grab rather than flagged *baht-buses*, decline any bar a tout pulls you toward, book water sports through a resort concierge rather than beach operators, and save the Tourist Police number (1155) on speed dial before stepping onto Walking Street — English-speaking dispatchers answer 24/7 and most venues collapse at the word “police.”
