# The Scams: City by City {-}

The chapters that follow document every scam reported in eleven Thai destinations. Cities are ordered by how most visitors actually travel: starting with Bangkok, the flagship and the country’s densest scam ecosystem, then north to Chiang Mai and the Lanna temple circuit, back down through Ayutthaya’s central-plain ruins, east to the Gulf resort strip of Pattaya and the royal beach town of Hua Hin, across to the Andaman mainland (Phuket, Krabi), and finishing on the Gulf islands (Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, Koh Tao) and the Andaman island anchor (Koh Phi Phi).

If you’re hitting only a few of these, skip to the chapter you need. If you’re doing a longer loop, skim them all — the same core scams recur with local variations. The “Grand Palace is closed” *tuk-tuk* gem-shop tour that intercepts tourists on Thanon Na Phra Lan runs identically at Tha Phae Gate in Chiang Mai and at Wat Mahathat in Ayutthaya. The jet-ski damage-deposit shakedown documented on Patong Beach replays on Pattaya Beach, Jomtien, and Hua Hin with smaller dollar figures but identical choreography. The scooter passport-hostage damage charge — a shop holds your passport until you pay an invented repair bill — runs in Chiang Mai, Phuket, Krabi, Koh Phangan, and Koh Tao. And the meter-refusal and Grab-cancel-for-cash pattern starts at Suvarnabhumi arrivals and repeats at nearly every airport, pier, and cartel-controlled beach strip in this book. Learn the pattern in one city, and you’ll spot it the instant you step off the boat in the next.

## The Eleven Cities at a Glance {-}

![Bangkok — Grand Palace spires above Chao Phraya longtails](assets/cities/bangkok.jpg)

**Bangkok** — The Grand Palace, Sukhumvit, and one of Southeast Asia’s densest tourist-scam ecosystems. The “palace is closed” *tuk-tuk* gem-shop loop on Thanon Na Phra Lan that r/ThailandTourism has filmed in real time; the Suvarnabhumi airport meter-refusal that quotes 500 baht flat on a 220-baht run; a Patpong and Soi Cowboy ping-pong-show bill-extortion circuit that *Bangkok Post* tracked to an 87,000-baht shakedown of a French tourist; and the Khao San Road bucket-cocktail drink-spike that empties wallets by morning.

![Chiang Mai — Tha Phae Gate moat and Doi Suthep silhouette](assets/cities/chiang-mai.jpg)

**Chiang Mai** — Old City moat, Lanna temples, and a scam set that runs around Tha Phae Gate. The *tuk-tuk* “Wat Chedi Luang is closed for cleaning” gem-shop detour; a Sri Don Chai Road karaoke-bill extortion that put a British tourist in hospital after nine staff kicked him on the street in July 2025; the scooter passport-hostage damage charge (13,500 baht for a hairline scratch, documented on r/ThailandTourism); and fake-monk donation books on the 309-step naga staircase up to Doi Suthep.

![Ayutthaya — Buddha head in banyan roots at Wat Mahathat](assets/cities/ayutthaya.jpg)

**Ayutthaya** — UNESCO ruins an hour north of Bangkok and a station fleet of “mafia” *tuk-tuks* that intercept every train. Drivers quoting 800 baht per person for tours that should run 200–300 baht per hour for the whole vehicle; a fake “Tourist Information Centre” storefront near Hua Lamphong that sells 3,500-baht Ayutthaya packages built around commission shops; elephant-ride overcharges outside Wat Phra Si Sanphet; and the Bangkok “temple closed” script replicated at Wat Mahathat with detours to silk-weaving commission stops.

![Pattaya — Walking Street neon and Beach Road curve](assets/cities/pattaya.jpg)

**Pattaya** — Walking Street, Beach Road, and the Gulf’s most industrial-scale nightlife scam economy. Go-go “lady drink” bills that escalate to 7,500 baht without you ordering; fake Muay Thai cooperating-fighter shows with 200-baht photo surcharges that *r/MuayThai* calls “WWE Muay Thai”; the “can I see your country’s currency?” note-switch crew working the same strip; and a Pattaya Beach jet-ski damage shakedown named by UK, US, and Australian government travel advisories.

![Hua Hin — Royal railway-station pavilion at golden hour](assets/cities/hua-hin.jpg)

**Hua Hin** — Royal-resort town with an unusually aggressive taxi cartel. Eggs thrown at Grab windshields near Hua Hin Railway Station and Market Village, documented in 2025 news reports; a Soi Bintabaht strip-bar bill-extortion circuit that put one tourist in the street after refusing a padded 15,000-baht tab; dual-pricing markups at Hua Hin Zoo and beachfront restaurants where Thai-language price boards show one number and English menus show another; and the lighter Hua Hin Beach cousin of Pattaya’s jet-ski shakedown.

![Phuket — Patong Beach bay and Big Buddha ridge](assets/cities/phuket.jpg)

**Phuket** — Patong, Karon, Kata, and the Andaman’s most developed scam infrastructure. The Patong jet-ski damage shakedown that *Bangkok Post* covered in a January 2024 police sweep, with quotes that have hit 300,000 baht; a Bangla Road ping-pong-show bill-extortion circuit identical to Patpong’s; *tuk-tuk* and taxi mafias that physically block Grab drivers on Beach Road; and the scooter passport-hostage damage charge, including a 2025 r/phuket thread that escalated from a 100-baht dispute to a 30,000-baht demand and physical assault.

![Krabi — Railay limestone cliffs and longtail boats](assets/cities/krabi.jpg)

**Krabi** — Ao Nang, Railay, and a taxi cartel that rivals Phuket’s. Grab drivers messaging “cannot pick up Ao Nang, taxi mafia” while *tuk-tuks* quote 1,500 baht one-way to Tiger Cave Temple; 4-Island longtail tours sold at 3,500 baht per person for a trip that runs 800–1,200 baht through legitimate booths; the scooter passport-hostage damage charge on Ao Nang’s main strip; and a Maya Bay national-park fee (400 baht) double-billed by fly-by-night speedboat operators.

![Koh Samui — Chaweng Beach palms and Big Buddha temple](assets/cities/koh-samui.jpg)

**Koh Samui** — Chaweng, Lamai, Bophut, and the Gulf’s second-toughest taxi cartel. A Samui Airport *songthaew* and taxi-counter monopoly that blocks Grab pickups and prices short hops at 800–1,200 baht; named rental-shop scams (All Way Rent a Car’s fake 3,000-baht smoking fee, Hakunarent’s manufactured damage charges) that hold passports hostage; fly-by-night tour operators like NCK Travel and Funny Day Tours that vanish by morning; and Soi Green Mango “lady drink” escalations running 3,000+ baht a night.

![Koh Phangan — Haad Rin Full Moon Party on the sand](assets/cities/koh-phangan.jpg)

**Koh Phangan** — Haad Rin, Thong Sala, and a Full Moon Party that draws 30,000 tourists a month into ideal sting conditions. Undercover police drug stings on the Haad Rin periphery demanding 30,000–100,000 baht cash “or jail,” with Israeli soldiers arrested in October 2025 per *Bangkok Post* and *Khaosod English*; the Haad Rin scooter passport-hostage damage charge (11,000-baht repair quotes for underside scratches); Thong Sala dispensaries advertising impossible “70% THC” flower at 3–5x Bangkok prices; and Thong Sala Pier *songthaew* price-gouging for the ride to Haad Rin.

![Koh Tao — Sairee Beach sunset and Koh Nang Yuan viewpoint](assets/cities/koh-tao.jpg)

**Koh Tao** — Dive-school island with a dark reputation locals call “Murder Island.” PADI Open Water courses advertised at 9,500 baht that bill out at 11,500 once textbook, mask, cert card, and boat fees are stacked; “beach entrance fees” of 100 baht per head at Freedom Beach and Aow Leuk, technically illegal since Thai beaches are public to the high-tide line; scooter passport-hostage damage charges on Koh Tao’s genuinely dangerous hill roads; and a decade of unexplained tourist-death cases — Miller and Witheridge (2014), Dallemagne (2017), and more through 2025 — that shape how locals and regulars move after dark.

![Koh Phi Phi — Maya Bay cove and Tonsai twin bays](assets/cities/koh-phi-phi.jpg)

**Koh Phi Phi** — Maya Bay, Tonsai, Loh Dalum, and a car-free party island where every service runs through a middleman. The Maya Bay 400-baht national-park fee pocketed twice by fly-by-night speedboat operators; Tonsai Village bucket-cocktail drink-spiking at Sunflower Bar, Slinky Bar, and the Loh Dalum Beach strip; Ao Nang beach-booth ferry ticket markups running 50–75 percent over direct Chao Koh and Songserm pricing; and Tonsai pier porters who quote 100 baht at the dock and demand 500 per bag at the hotel door.
