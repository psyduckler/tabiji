Krabi’s scam economy is defined by one of Thailand’s most aggressive taxi cartels. The Ao Nang *tuk-tuk* mafia physically enforces a no-Grab zone across the beach strip — drivers message the cancellation line “Cannot pick up Ao Nang, sorry, taxi mafia” as soon as a booking lands inside the exclusion radius, and a r/ThailandTourism thread titled “Was I scammed? Ao Nang taxi mafia” confirms the community verdict: “it is not a scam. the taxi mafia exists. in some places in the town grab drivers don’t even pick you up.” A 25-km Ao Nang-to-Tiger-Cave-Temple run that would cost 280 baht on Grab is quoted at 1,500 baht each way, no meter, no negotiation. The workaround Ao Nang regulars use is to walk 500 meters inland, off the beach strip, before opening the app.

The boats and the scooter rentals run the next two layers. A 4-Island longtail tour (Chicken, Poda, Tup, and Pranang/Railay) has a fair community-verified rate of 800–1,200 baht per person; walk-up pier booths routinely quote 3,500. The Krabi scooter-rental shops on Ao Nang’s main road and in Railay use the same passport-hostage damage-demand script as Patong and Chiang Mai — a barely-visible scratch on the underside fairing returns as a 12,000-baht repair quote with your passport locked in the back-office safe. Tiger Cave Temple (Wat Tham Sua) has an “access fee” racket at the 1,237-step summit path — the temple is free, the summit climb is free, and the “tourist assistants” in unofficial uniforms demanding 300 baht per person have no legal basis for the charge. Phi Phi day trips run a national-park-fee double-billing scheme (the legitimate 400-baht Hat Noppharat Thara–Mu Koh Phi Phi fee gets extracted a second time at the boat), and the Ao Nang strip hosts the same rotating hair-supplement and weight-loss “visiting doctor from Dubai” crew that works Pattaya and Phuket. Restaurants two blocks off the strip charge 60–80 baht for pad thai; beachfront vendors charge 200 or more. A safe default: walk inland before calling a ride, book boat tours on 12go.asia or Klook rather than at the pier, never surrender an original passport for a scooter, and call the Tourist Police at 1155 or walk into the Ao Nang beach police box (response time typically under 30 minutes) if a rental shop refuses to return a deposit.
