Koh Phi Phi is walk-only — no cars, no *tuk-tuks*, no *songthaew* — which reshapes its scam economy into something distinct from the rest of Thailand. The signature Phi Phi operation is the bucket-cocktail drink-spiking at the Loh Dalum Beach bars: a 300-baht plastic sandcastle-style bucket of rum, Red Bull, and mixer served with several small plastic cups, drunk by a group, and followed by 3–4 hours of memory loss and an empty wallet. A r/travel thread titled “I fell for a tourist scam and feel really stupid” describes it directly — the rum-and-pineapple bucket as the delivery vehicle, with spirits mixed out of sight and benzodiazepines or GHB dissolving in sugary cocktails. Sunflower Bar and Slinky Bar are the names that come up most on community threads.

The second layer is the Maya Bay national-park-fee double-billing — the legitimate 400-baht Hat Noppharat Thara–Mu Koh Phi Phi foreigner fee extracted a second time at the speedboat when the tour price already included it. Ferry middleman markup is baked into the Ao Nang, Rassada, and Krabi Town booking booths, where the walk-up price is 1,400 baht for a return that Chao Koh Ferry or Songserm sells directly at 800. Tonsai Pier’s luggage porters quote “100 baht” on arrival and demand 500 per bag at the hotel (the 50–100-baht standard gets agreed in writing before you hand over bags). And the accommodation overbooking script — “sorry, we overbooked, but we have a better room for 500 baht extra” — depends on tourists accepting the “upgrade” on the spot without contacting Booking.com or Agoda. A safe default: never accept a bucket cocktail, book ferries directly on chaokohferryphiphi.com or Songserm, book Maya Bay tours on Klook or 12go.asia with “park fee included” stated in writing, and contact platform support via in-app chat immediately if the hotel tries to reroute you on arrival.
