Koh Tao has a darker reputation than most Thai islands, and it is not rhetorical. The “Murder Island” nickname emerged from a decade of unexplained tourist deaths — Irish backpacker David Miller and Briton Hannah Witheridge in 2014, Belgian Elise Dallemagne in 2017, Swiss tourist Nicole Sauvain-Weisskopf in 2018, a Russian tourist in 2020, and cases through 2024–2025 including a November 2025 arrest of a teen for sexual assault attempt on a Canadian tourist covered by *Khaosod English*. The scam layer sits on top of this and needs to be read in that context — a dispute with a local business that escalates to threats is not a dispute you should try to win; it is a dispute you should leave the island to resolve.

The routine scams cluster on Sairee Beach and Mae Haad. Open Water certification is advertised on sidewalk signs at 9,500 baht but totals 11,500–12,500 once textbook, mask rental, card processing, and boat-day fees are added. The scooter-rental passport-hostage damage scam runs here at the same scale as Phangan, on an island where the roads are genuinely dangerous and multiple tourist scooter deaths are documented. “Beach entrance fees” of 100 baht per person at Freedom Beach, Aow Leuk, and Tanote Bay are collected at informal gates — Thai beaches are legally public to the high-tide line, so the fees are structurally semi-legal at best. Tour booths in Sairee take cash deposits without receipts and charge full rebooking fees for 20-minute delays. A safe default: travel with a buddy and share your location with someone at home, stay on the Sairee main strip after dark, decline any drink or shroom shake from a stranger, budget 11,500–12,500 for PADI Open Water rather than believing the 9,500 sign price, and seriously consider skipping scooter rental altogether.
