Bangkok runs the oldest and most rehearsed scam ecosystem in Southeast Asia, and almost all of it converges on a single address: the approach to the Grand Palace on Thanon Na Phra Lan. The *“palace is closed today”* gambit — a friendly man in a pressed shirt who introduces himself as a teacher or palace officer, tells you there is a royal ceremony until 1pm, and flags down a waiting *tuk-tuk* for a 20-baht “hidden temple” loop — has been running long enough that the Tourism Authority of Thailand posts warnings in four languages inside the compound. The *tuk-tuk* makes three stops: a lesser *wat*, a “government tailor,” and a gem shop where staff in suits produce laminated certificates for “investment grade” sapphires. A Reddit user documented the exact choreography on r/ThailandTourism in a thread titled “I filmed the Grand Palace is closed scam.” The first refusal at the first shop collapses the entire revenue model and the driver will drop you.

The risk radiates out in three corridors. The airport-taxi racket at Suvarnabhumi and Don Mueang — “500 baht, no meter” to Sukhumvit when the metered fare is 220–350 baht plus 50 in tolls — peaks between 11pm and 6am when jet-lagged arrivals accept anything. The Sukhumvit–Asok–Nana skywalks run the approach-and-talk crews: Indian “lucky face” fortune-tellers, fake saffron-robed monks with laminated donation books, and well-dressed men who walk you to a tailor on Sukhumvit Soi 11 where a 48-hour suit turns out to be 40 percent polyester. Patpong, Soi Cowboy, and Nana Plaza run the bill-extortion script — laminated “100-baht beer” menus that turn into 8,000-baht bills with staff between you and the door, a pattern the *Bangkok Post* documented in a 2023 story about a French tourist hit with an 87,000-baht tab. Khao San Road adds its own layer: spiked bucket cocktails and wallets emptied in *tuk-tuks* back to the hotel. A safe default: verify Grand Palace hours on royalgrandpalace.th the morning of your visit, take the Airport Rail Link (45 baht) instead of a late-night airport taxi, use Grab or Bolt rather than flagged *tuk-tuks*, never accept a drink in a bucket, and call the Tourist Police at 1155 the moment a bill is padded or an exit is blocked — English-speaking dispatchers answer 24/7 and most venues fold at the word “police.”
