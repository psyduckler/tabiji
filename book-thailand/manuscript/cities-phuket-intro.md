Phuket’s scam ecosystem has a single gravitational center, and it is Patong — specifically the Beach Road and Bangla Road strip where the jet-ski damage shakedown, the *tuk-tuk* taxi cartel, the ping-pong-show bill extortion, and the scooter passport-hostage scam all run simultaneously within three blocks. The Patong jet-ski operation is named explicitly by UK, US, and Australian government travel advisories — a 1,500-baht-an-hour rental turns into a 30,000-baht “damage” demand, sometimes scaled up to 100,000 or more, with a tan-uniformed “police officer” materializing to side with the operator. A January 2024 *Bangkok Post* piece titled “Police bust Phuket jet-ski scammers” documented an active sweep, and a 2024 r/ThailandTourism thread titled “Jet Ski Damage in Phuket” walked through a six-figure demand that ended with the tourist’s credit card held hostage. Thailand’s Ministry of Tourism has called the pattern “a perennial problem.”

The Patong *tuk-tuk* cartel enforces inflated prices through physical intimidation of Grab drivers on Bangla Road and Beach Road — a 20-km Patong-to-Phuket-Town ride is 350 baht on Grab and 1,000 by tuk-tuk, and r/phuket describes the consensus bluntly: “Patong is a scam and the scams start at the Phuket airport with the taxis.” Bangla Road itself runs the ping-pong-show script with 12,000-baht bills for unordered “lady drinks,” “VIP seating,” and “show fees.” The scooter shops on Patong Beach Road and Takuapa Road in Phuket Town use the identical passport-hostage damage-demand script as Chiang Mai; a 2025 r/phuket thread documented a 100-baht refund request that escalated to a 30,000-baht damage demand and physical assault. Patong pharmacies pair this with a short-change-at-checkout micro-scam, Patong travel-agency storefronts vanish overnight after taking Phi Phi tour deposits, and men in loose uniforms run a “beach smoking fine” shakedown demanding 20,000-baht cash. A safe default: use Grab for every ride (walk one block off Bangla if drivers are blocked), never rent a jet ski outside a licensed resort provider, hand over only a photocopy and cash deposit for a scooter, book Phi Phi tours through 12go.asia or Klook, pay all rentals by credit card for chargeback leverage, and call the Tourist Police at 1155 — not the officer on scene — if any “police” appears beside an operator.
