Koh Samui runs one of the most aggressive taxi cartels in Thailand, and the airport at Samui (USM) is ground zero. Grab drivers accept fares to Bophut or Chaweng and then message the cancellation script within seconds: “Cannot pick up airport, sorry, taxi mafia.” The official airport taxi counter will then charge 800–1,200 baht for the same trip that would have been 300 on Grab. A r/kohsamui thread titled “Taxi Mafia Shock” captured the contrast bluntly: “In the early hours today we had taken a 35-minute Grab to BKK airport at a cost of 350 baht. In Samui they want 400 baht to go to our hotel in Bophut.” In a separate 2023 incident, a group of *songthaew* drivers physically blocked Nathon Pier demanding a ban on Grab and inDrive entirely. The workaround is the same as Ao Nang’s: walk roughly 500 meters outside the airport perimeter before requesting the ride.

The second layer is rental and tour fraud. r/kohsamui has named specific operators — “All Way Rent a Car” for a 3,000-baht “smoking fee” charged to a non-smoker on return, “Hakunarent” as a parallel car-rental trap, “NCK Travel” for a booked zipline trip that no-showed with the shop closed the next morning, and “Funny Day Tours” for an advertised full-day snorkel delivered as a 20-minute stop at a fish-free reef. Chaweng’s Soi Green Mango runs the lady-drink escalation at smaller numbers than Pattaya, and freelance “parking attendants” in faded high-vis vests collect 50–100-baht fees for a service that does not officially exist. A safe default: book tours through 12go.asia, Klook, or GetYourGuide; cross-reference rental shop names on r/kohsamui before committing; rent cars from major airport-counter brands (Budget, Avis, Thrifty) rather than Chaweng storefronts; and use Booking.com, Agoda, or Airbnb with pay-through-platform protection for any accommodation.
