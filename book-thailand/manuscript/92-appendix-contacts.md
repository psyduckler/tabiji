# Appendix C: Emergency Contacts {-}

Every phone number in this appendix was verified current at the time of publication, but phone numbers do change, consulates move, and hospitals occasionally renumber their switchboards. If a number below fails to connect, search the organization’s name plus *Thailand* and you will almost always find the current line on the first page of results.

**Before you travel**, we strongly recommend you double-check your own embassy or consulate’s current number directly on its official website — the US State Department’s travel.state.gov, the UK’s gov.uk, Canada’s travel.gc.ca, Ireland’s dfa.ie, Australia’s smartraveller.gov.au. Save the main switchboard and the after-hours duty-officer line on your phone before departure, and screenshot this appendix so you can read it on the BTS Skytrain or a longtail boat where your signal cuts out. Even a verified number can become a wrong number between editions.

## The numbers that work everywhere in Thailand

These are the lines to dial from any phone — mobile or landline, Thai SIM or roaming — anywhere in the country. All of them are free.

- **Tourist Police:** **1155**. This is the number to call if you do not speak Thai. Dispatchers are 24/7 and English-speaking nationwide; several speak Mandarin, Japanese, Korean, Russian, German, and French as well. This is the *first* number to try for any scam, theft, harassment, or assault incident involving a foreign visitor. The dispatcher will direct you to the nearest Tourist Police office and, in serious cases, send officers to meet you.
- **Royal Thai Police (general emergency):** **191**. The national police general-emergency line. The first operator may speak Thai only; repeat *“English, please”* or *“Tourist Police”* and you will be transferred. Use this if 1155 is slow to connect.
- **Emergency medical services (ambulance):** **1669**. The national EMS dispatch. English is available at the major dispatch centers in Bangkok, Chiang Mai, Phuket, Pattaya, and Koh Samui; elsewhere the first operator may be Thai-only and will locate an English speaker. For any serious injury, calling the nearest large private hospital directly (numbers below) is often faster than 1669 in remote areas.
- **Fire:** **199**. National fire-and-rescue dispatch.
- **Tourism Authority of Thailand (TAT) Tourist Assistance:** **1672**. Not a fraud-report line — this is the tourist-advice and orientation hotline. Use it for general guidance, disputes with registered tour operators, and as a backup translator if you are struggling to communicate with local authorities. 24/7, English available.
- **Tourist complaint hotline (Department of Land Transport):** **1584**. Complaints against licensed taxis, tuk-tuks, *songthaews*, buses, and other registered transport operators. Have the license-plate number ready.
- **Office of the Consumer Protection Board:** **1166**. Complaints against shops, restaurants, tailors, gem shops, tour packages, and consumer fraud generally. Thai-language first line; English via callback in most cases. Website: **ocpb.go.th**.
- **Bank of Thailand Financial Consumer Protection Center:** **1213**. Complaints against Thai banks, ATM and debit-card fraud, online-transfer disputes, loan-shark predation. Website: **bot.or.th**.
- **Thai Immigration hotline:** **1178**. Visa questions, overstay rectification, and the 24-hour TM30 accommodation-notification line used by hotels — useful if you have lost your passport and need to clarify your status before you can board a flight.

## Card issuer emergency lines (callable from Thailand)

When you call, have your card number (or the last four digits), the approximate transaction amount, and the time of the fraudulent charge ready.

- **Visa Global Customer Assistance (collect from Thailand):** +1 303 967 1096. 24/7, English.
- **Mastercard Global Service (collect from Thailand):** +1 636 722 7111. 24/7.
- **American Express International Service Center (collect):** +1 336 393 1111. 24/7.
- **American Express Thailand (local toll-free for Thai-issued cards):** 02 273 5544. 24/7, English and Thai.

If your card was issued by a bank (not directly by Visa, Mastercard, or Amex), the fastest route is the number printed on the back of the card. That line routes you to your own issuer’s fraud desk and they can freeze the card in under a minute. Save this number in your phone under a searchable contact name before you travel — hunting for a number on a stolen card is impossible.

Thailand hosts most countries’ embassies in Bangkok. The United States is the only major Anglophone country with a full consulate outside Bangkok — in Chiang Mai, serving northern Thailand. Numbers below are main switchboards; every embassy also operates a separate after-hours duty-officer line for citizens in distress, published on its own website.

## City-by-city emergency contacts

For every city in this book, the *Tourist Police office* (if one exists locally) is the default destination for a *bai jaeng kwaam*. Where no Tourist Police office is within reach, the local *Royal Thai Police station* handles the report; dialing **1155** anywhere in the country will give you the dispatcher who can tell you which station to walk to.

Where we are not certain of a specific street address, we tell you so rather than guess. The current address of any Tourist Police office or *sathanee tamruat* can be verified by calling 1155, by Google Maps, or by your hotel’s front desk — every Thai hotel keeps the nearest station address at reception.

### Bangkok

- **Police report intake:** the **Tourist Police Division** headquarters in Bangkok serves the whole metropolitan area and operates a walk-in desk for foreign visitors. Satellite Tourist Police booths are found on Sukhumvit Road (near Asok and Nana BTS stations), on Khao San Road, at Chatuchak Weekend Market, at Suvarnabhumi Airport, and at Don Mueang Airport. The 1155 dispatcher will direct you to the nearest open booth.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospitals:** **Bumrungrad International Hospital** (Sukhumvit Soi 3, +66 2 066 8888) and **Bangkok Hospital** (New Phetchaburi Road, +66 2 310 3000) are the two largest private international hospitals, both with extensive English-speaking staff and direct billing with major international insurers. **Samitivej Sukhumvit** (+66 2 022 2222) and **BNH Hospital** (Silom, +66 2 022 0700) are also widely used by travelers. For public emergency care, **Chulalongkorn Hospital** and **Siriraj Hospital** are Bangkok’s largest public teaching hospitals.
- **English-speaking medical care:** all the private hospitals above maintain international-patient coordinators. SOS International and International SOS both operate Bangkok clinics for members.

### Chiang Mai

- **Police report intake:** the **Chiang Mai Tourist Police office** is on the eastern side of the old city moat. The 1155 dispatcher will confirm the current address. The main *Chiang Mai Provincial Police station* also accepts reports from foreign visitors.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Chiang Mai Ram Hospital** (+66 53 920 300) is the largest private hospital with an international-patient center; **Bangkok Hospital Chiang Mai** (+66 52 089 888) is a newer option with direct-billing agreements. For public care, **Maharaj Nakorn Chiang Mai Hospital** (Suan Dok) is the region’s main teaching hospital.
- **US Consulate General:** Amity Hall; main switchboard +66 53 107 700.

### Ayutthaya

- **Police report intake:** Ayutthaya has a local Tourist Police desk near the main tourist area inside the historical park; the main *Ayutthaya Provincial Police station* handles formal *bai jaeng kwaam* filings. Dial 1155 for the current office address. If you are day-tripping from Bangkok, many travelers find it faster to file on return to Bangkok — same-day counts.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Phra Nakhon Si Ayutthaya Hospital** (public) and **Ayutthaya Ruamphet Hospital** (private) are the two largest emergency departments in the city.
- **English-speaking medical care:** limited compared to Bangkok; for non-urgent care, the short drive back to a Bangkok private hospital is often the cleaner option.

### Pattaya

- **Police report intake:** the **Pattaya Tourist Police station** is on Pattaya Beach Road (Soi 9 area) and is one of the busiest Tourist Police offices in the country, with multilingual staff. The 1155 dispatcher will confirm the current address.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospitals:** **Bangkok Hospital Pattaya** (+66 38 259 999) is the largest private international hospital in Eastern Seaboard and maintains strong relationships with dive-medicine specialists. **Pattaya Memorial Hospital** is an older private option.
- **English-speaking medical care:** Pattaya’s international-resident population means English-speaking GPs and dentists are easy to find; the Bangkok Hospital Pattaya concierge will refer.

### Hua Hin

- **Police report intake:** the **Hua Hin Tourist Police office** is near the main beach-road intersections and the railway station; the 1155 dispatcher has the current address. The main *Hua Hin Police station* handles *bai jaeng kwaam* overnight.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Bangkok Hospital Hua Hin** (+66 32 616 800) is the largest private international hospital in town, with English-speaking staff and direct insurance billing. **San Paulo Hospital Hua Hin** is a longer-established private alternative.
- **English-speaking medical care:** widely available at both hospitals above; Hua Hin has a large expatriate retirement community.

### Phuket

- **Police report intake:** the **Phuket Tourist Police** maintains a main office in Phuket Town and satellite desks in Patong (Bangla Road area), Karon, and Kata. The 1155 dispatcher will direct you to the nearest open desk.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Bangkok Hospital Phuket** (+66 76 254 425) is the flagship international hospital on the island, handles dive-medicine referrals, and works with the nearest hyperbaric chamber. **Bangkok Hospital Siriroj** (formerly Phuket International Hospital) and **Mission Hospital Phuket** are additional options.
- **Hyperbaric / dive medicine:** Bangkok Hospital Phuket coordinates recompression referrals for the Andaman coast; the nearest chamber is on Phuket itself. For dive-related injuries, surface quickly and dial 1669 or Bangkok Hospital Phuket directly — the DAN Asia-Pacific 24-hour hotline (+61 8 8212 9242) is the global dive-medical consult line.
- **English-speaking medical care:** extensive across all major private hospitals.

### Krabi (Ao Nang, Railay, Krabi Town)

- **Police report intake:** the **Krabi Tourist Police office** covers Ao Nang and Krabi Town; there is no police station on Railay itself — Railay incidents route through Ao Nang by longtail. Dial 1155.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Krabi Nakharin International Hospital** (+66 75 626 555) and **Bangkok Hospital Krabi** are the main private options with English-speaking staff. **Krabi Hospital** is the main public emergency department.
- **Rock-climbing and longtail-boat injuries:** Krabi Nakharin is the usual destination for climbing accidents on Railay; serious cases are air-evacuated to Phuket or Bangkok.

### Koh Samui

- **Police report intake:** the **Koh Samui Tourist Police station** is near Chaweng Beach; the 1155 dispatcher will confirm. The main island *sathanee tamruat* is in Nathon (the ferry port on the west coast).
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Bangkok Hospital Samui** (+66 77 429 500) is the flagship international hospital on the island, with a helicopter pad for Bangkok transfers. **Samui International Hospital** (+66 77 230 781) is the longer-established international option.
- **English-speaking medical care:** widely available; both hospitals above coordinate direct billing with international travel insurers.

### Koh Phangan

- **Police report intake:** **Koh Phangan Tourist Police** maintain a desk near Thong Sala pier during high season and a permanent presence around Haad Rin during the Full Moon Party. Dial 1155 for current hours.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital:** **Bandon International Hospital Koh Phangan** (+66 77 956 888) is the newest private hospital on the island; **Koh Phangan Hospital** (public) in Thong Sala handles emergencies. For serious cases, medical evacuation to Koh Samui (Bangkok Hospital Samui) or Surat Thani is standard.
- **Full Moon Party note:** the three days around each Full Moon see the highest ambulance call volumes in Thailand outside Bangkok. Plan your accommodation within walking distance of medical facilities if you are partying.

### Koh Tao

- **Police report intake:** Koh Tao is a small island served by the **Koh Tao Police station** near Mae Haad pier; Tourist Police coverage is limited — dial 1155 for dispatch guidance.
- **Major 24/7 emergency hospital / clinics:** Koh Tao has no full hospital. **Koh Tao Emergency Medical Centre** and **Koh Tao Community Clinic** handle day-to-day care; serious cases are evacuated to Koh Samui (Bangkok Hospital Samui) or Chumphon on the mainland.
- **Recompression chamber:** Koh Tao hosts one of Thailand’s two recompression chambers specifically for dive-related decompression sickness — the **Badalveda Koh Tao Recompression Chamber** at Sairee. Any dive school on the island can route you; DAN Asia-Pacific (+61 8 8212 9242) provides 24-hour dive-medical consult in English.

### Koh Phi Phi

- **Police report intake:** **Koh Phi Phi Tourist Police** maintain a desk on Tonsai Bay near the main pier. Dial 1155. For overnight reports, the dispatcher will coordinate with the Krabi mainland.
- **Major 24/7 emergency clinic:** **Phi Phi Island Hospital** (a small government clinic) handles day-to-day care; serious cases are evacuated by speedboat to Krabi (Krabi Nakharin or Bangkok Hospital Krabi) or by helicopter to Phuket (Bangkok Hospital Phuket).
- **Evacuation note:** no road vehicles on Phi Phi; evacuations are by boat. Your travel insurer’s assistance line will coordinate the transfer — have the number saved offline.

## If you lose your passport

1. **Report it first** — go to the nearest Tourist Police or Royal Thai Police station and file a *bai jaeng kwaam pheua perd/soohaay* (police report for lost or stolen passport). You will leave with the signed and sealed report. This is the document your embassy wants to see before issuing an emergency replacement.
2. **Call your embassy’s main switchboard** — or its after-hours line if it is a weekend — and request an emergency passport appointment. US, UK, Canadian, Irish, and Australian embassies in Bangkok (and the US Consulate in Chiang Mai) all issue same-day or next-day emergency travel documents; the fee varies.
3. **Bring** the *bai jaeng kwaam*, any photo ID you still have (a driver’s license photo on your phone counts), passport photos (photo booths are common in Bangkok malls and every BTS transit hub), and the payment method your embassy specifies.
4. **Immigration clearance for departure**: before flying out with an emergency travel document, you may need a short visit to **Thai Immigration** (Chaeng Wattana, Bangkok) to regularize your visa status — your embassy will advise. Airlines departing Suvarnabhumi, Don Mueang, Phuket, Chiang Mai, and Krabi see emergency documents regularly; border police know the format.

## If your phone is stolen

1. **Freeze the SIM** by calling your carrier’s customer-service line from any other phone. Thai carriers: **AIS** 1175 (in Thailand) or +66 2 271 9000 (from abroad); **True** 1242 or +66 2 900 9000; **DTAC** 1678 or +66 2 202 8000. If you use a foreign SIM, call your home carrier’s international line — most have a 24/7 theft-reporting number that forwards from Thailand. Tourist eSIMs (Airalo, Saily, Holafly) can be suspended in-app.
2. **File a *bai jaeng kwaam*** at the nearest Tourist Police office with your device’s IMEI (if you recorded it) and its serial number. Insurance claims require the sealed report.
3. **Lock and wipe remotely** via Apple’s Find My or Google’s Find My Device from any browser. Do this before filing the *bai jaeng kwaam* — a wiped phone cannot leak your email and banking apps while you wait in line.
4. **Change passwords immediately** for any account whose app was installed on the phone: email, banking, social media. Start with email; everything else resets off it.

## If your credit card is stolen or skimmed

1. **Freeze the card** by calling the number on the back (or use the issuer’s app’s “lock card” feature, which works in seconds). This matters more than filing a police report first — every minute you wait is a minute the thief can use the card.
2. **Report to police** with a *bai jaeng kwaam*. Insurance reimbursement and the card issuer’s fraud-dispute process both want the sealed report.
3. **Dispute the charges** with your issuer. Most European, US, Australian, and Japanese card issuers reverse fraudulent charges fully; the *bai jaeng kwaam* speeds the process.
4. **Watch for follow-on phishing** — a skimmed card often leads to email and SMS phishing pretending to be your bank. Never click a link in one of these messages; go to your bank’s app or website directly.

## A closing note

This appendix is meant to be read once on the plane, screenshot for offline use, and forgotten about for the rest of your trip. Phone numbers change. Consulates move. Hospitals renumber their switchboards. If any number in this list fails, **1155** — the Tourist Police — will still work, and your embassy’s website will still have the current line for your country.

Travel safely, travel calmly, and if the worst happens, remember: a stolen phone is an inconvenience, not a crisis. Filing a *bai jaeng kwaam*, freezing a card, and calling an embassy are ordinary procedures — Tourist Police officers and consular staff in Thailand handle these cases every day, and most travelers resolve everything within a few hours and return to their trip. Thailand is not a dangerous country; it is a practiced one. Help is close, and English is closer than you think.
