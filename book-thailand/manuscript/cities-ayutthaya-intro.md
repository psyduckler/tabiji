Ayutthaya is a day-trip city, which defines its scam economy almost completely. Most foreign visitors arrive on the 20-baht SRT third-class train from Bangkok’s Hua Lamphong station, step out onto the platform at Ayutthaya Railway Station, and walk directly into a fleet of *tuk-tuks* that have been waiting specifically for them. The quoted rate is 800 baht per person for a “full-day temple tour,” a number calibrated to sound defensible to someone who hasn’t yet learned Ayutthaya geography. The fair rate is 200–300 baht per *hour* for the whole vehicle, so roughly 800–1,200 baht for a half day split across four to six travelers — not per person. A r/ThailandTourism thread titled “Did I just almost got scammed or something?” captures the station arrival: “Lots of mafia tuk-tuks waiting outside. I had ordered a taxi on Indrive or…”

The second pattern is the fake Tourist Information Centre. A polite person in a branded shirt intercepts travelers near Hua Lamphong in Bangkok or on Khao San Road, walks them 50 meters to a storefront marked “Tourism Authority of Thailand,” and sells a 3,500-baht Ayutthaya package that would cost 200 baht done independently. The real TAT operates out of 108 Moo 3 Pratuchai in Ayutthaya and 1600 New Phetchaburi in Bangkok, and its staff do not recruit on the street. Once you arrive, the Grand Palace “closed-today” script replicates perfectly at Wat Mahathat — the temple with the famous Buddha head entwined in banyan roots — with the commission stop reframed as an “authentic Thai silk weaving center.” Combo “Ayutthaya + Damnoen Saduak floating market” van tours sold on Khao San routinely arrive at the floating market at 10:30am, long after the legitimate 6–9am market has wound down. And the elephant-ride operators outside Wat Phra Si Sanphet quote 400 baht up front, then add 600 baht mid-ride for an “extended route” you did not request. A safe default: take the 20-baht SRT train yourself, rent a bicycle for 50 baht a day (Ayutthaya’s ruins are within 5 km and cycling is how locals move), skip the elephant rides entirely on ethical grounds, and book any packaged day tour only on Klook, Viator, or GetYourGuide with verified operators rather than a storefront.
