Quepos is the Manuel Antonio service town — five kilometers north of the park, with the Marina Pez Vela sportfishing dock, the Sansa La Managua airstrip (XQP), and the Tracopa terminal that runs the SJO–Jacó–Manuel Antonio bus chain. Almost every Manuel Antonio visitor passes through Quepos for fuel, for groceries, for a charter, for the airstrip, or for the Tracopa bus, and the scam roster is built around those five chokepoints. The signature con is the Marina Pez Vela sportfishing advance-fee fraud: unlicensed brokers walk the floating dock on weekday mornings with laminated brochures and tablets, intercept anglers exiting hotel transfers, and quote the marina's premium boats at half-price — $300 to $1,500 wired up front to a personal account, then either a no-show on charter day or a substituted smaller boat with broken tackle. Marina Pez Vela's legitimate operators (Pelagic Fishing, JP Sportfishing, Bluewater Charters) all book through their own websites with credit-card deposits and ICT-registered captains. The next-most-common scam is the Calle 2 waterfront "gringo menu" — a no-prices English menu handed only to foreign passport holders, with the same dish that costs ₡4,500 for locals priced at $20 to $25. The Manuel Antonio strip's same trick runs five kilometers south; Quepos's version is just less polished. The La Managua airstrip pirate-taxi gouge is the third-most-common: Sansa flight 521 lands twelve to eighteen passengers on a single curb, and unmarked SUVs charge $40 to $80 for a $10 official red-taxi run into Quepos centro. Two more rounding out the catalog: the marina jet-ski and ATV damage-inflation deposit trap (a $500 to $2,000 credit-card hold against pre-existing scratches the operator photographs only after you sign), and the Tracopa terminal boarding-scrum pickpocket cell that lifts wallets, phones, and daypacks during the first five minutes of platform chaos when Route 27 buses load for San José.

The risk zones are Marina Pez Vela's floating dock at 6:00–8:00 AM (the charter-pickup window), the Calle 2 waterfront strip at lunch and dinner, the La Managua airstrip arrivals curb, the Tracopa terminal's coffee counter and platform during boarding, and any unattended ATV or jet-ski rental kiosk on Avenida Central or the marina boardwalk. The safe default: book sportfishing only through the operator's own website (a TripAdvisor link or visitcostarica.com listing is the verification) and never wire to a personal name; pay the deposit by credit card so chargeback stays available. At Calle 2 restaurants, ask for the Spanish menu (*"el menú en español, por favor"*) and confirm the bill includes the 13% IVA and 10% service before sitting down — Costa Rica's Ley 7472 requires *precio total* (all-in pricing) on every menu. From La Managua, ride only the official red taxis with the yellow-triangle dashboard ID (₡7,000–₡9,000 / $13–$17 to Quepos centro, ₡10,000–₡14,000 to Manuel Antonio) or pre-book Interbus at interbusonline.com; if no red taxi is at the curb on a Sansa landing, walk 200 meters to the road and flag one rather than accept the unmarked SUV. For jet skis or ATVs, photograph and video every panel, hull, and tire before signing — narrate defects into the mic — and demand the operator countersign your damage-condition photos. At the Tracopa terminal, daypack between feet, never in the overhead rack; phone in front pocket only; and step off for the snack counter only with valuables on you. Program 911, the Quepos Fuerza Pública (**2777-0196**), the Manuel Antonio Tourist Police (Playa Espadilla, opened September 2024, 24/7), and the Hospital Max Terán Vals (**2774-9500**) into speed dial on arrival.
