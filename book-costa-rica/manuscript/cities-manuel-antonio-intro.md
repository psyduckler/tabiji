Manuel Antonio is the most-photographed national park in Costa Rica and the most-scammed national-park approach in the country — the U.S. Embassy San José explicitly flagged Route 618's "impostors impersonating police officers and asking for money" in its November 25, 2025 Security Alert. Four separate r/CostaRicaTravel PSAs define the anchor scam. u/Electrical-Chair2300's 117-upvote "Manuel Antonio scams" (2024) describes three men 500 meters short of the real SINAC gate, one in khaki with a fake ID badge and a fake ticket scanner; when the OP refused their $12 parking lot, the scammer leaned into the window, pretended to phone the park, and shouted "you know what? I just cancelled your tickets, no entry for you today." u/rich8523's 101-upvote "Manuel Antonio scammers outside the park" and u/Independent-Ad4792's 53-upvote "PSA about Manuel Antonio National Park scammers" (December 2025) describe identical crews dressed "kinda like Indiana Jones — hats, khaki clothes, radios" blocking every tourist car; one scammer slapped the side of the car and flipped them off. u/Fearless-Tree-7478's 146-upvote "Beware Manuel Antonio" (January 2026) confirms the racket still runs; in the comments, u/1020goldfish was asked for "6 million colones" (~$11,000) and u/TheMedicator had a scammer "grab a metal rod and threaten to vandalize our car." Adjacent scams fill out the catalog: fake paper "SINAC ticket booths" at $20–$40 for hand-printed tickets that don't scan at the real turnstile ($18.08 online-only at sinac.go.cr since 2023); unofficial "ICT-certified" guides at the gate charging $60 per person for a mangrove shortcut that skips the sloth and monkey hotspots (the 90-upvote u/Ok-Network4810 "Scam Tour Guide" case named Glen, whose spotting scope "didn't even work"); the Playa Espadilla parking-and-umbrella racket run by a crew around "David" (u/fesme documented ₡15,000 for parking + umbrella that turned into a three-to-four-man intimidation at pickup, plus Google reviews of operators "throwing stones at cars" and u/TheMedicator's metal-rod threat); and the Tracopa SJO–Quepos bus rest-stop overhead-bin theft (20-upvote u/MidtownJunk PSA: thieves planted as passengers strip overhead racks of laptops while everyone steps off for the restroom break).

The risk zones are Route 618 from the "1 km" sign to the circular turnaround at the real SINAC gate, Playa Espadilla parking frontage, the main strip of Manuel Antonio village between the park and Quepos (English-only "gringo menus"), and the Tracopa bus rest stop north of Jacó. The safe default: buy park tickets **only at sinac.go.cr** ($18.08 adult, $5.65 child 6–12, cap 2,000/day, closed Tuesdays) 7–14 days ahead; save the PDF with the green QR code offline on your phone; anyone selling paper tickets on the road is a scammer, period. Drive to the **very end** of Route 618 past every khaki figure waving you over — legitimate lots (Chalo Parking, $5–$10/day) sit on the left right before the gate, past the circular turnaround. Lay on the horn and creep forward if blocked; scammers always move. Book guides in advance through ICT-certified operators (Jade Tours with Julian; Edwin's Manuel Antonio Tours with Saul) at $30–$45 per person with a working spotting scope — verify the *carné* ICT card and cross-check names at visitcostarica.com before paying. At Playa Espadilla park at the end-of-road circular turnaround (the correct $5–$10 lots) rather than on the beach frontage; lock the price into your phone's Notes app and have the vendor confirm before service. On the Tracopa bus, carry your daypack (phone, passport, laptop, cash) with you at every stop; never leave valuables in the overhead rack. File at the Playa Espadilla Tourist Police station (opened September 2024, 16 officers, 24/7) or dial 911.
