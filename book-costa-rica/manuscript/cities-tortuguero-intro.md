Tortuguero is the northern Caribbean turtle-nesting and canal village — reachable only by boat through the La Pavona dock and a one-and-a-half-hour public boat ride down the Rio Tortuguero through the national park's canal system. There are no roads in. There is no rental car to scam at the gate. What there is, instead, is a single-funnel transit chokepoint at La Pavona, a dusk-lit village beach where Sea Turtle Conservancy guides and unlicensed locals share the same 200-meter strip, and a cluster of jungle lodges on stilts where the last-night room theft pattern repeats season after season. The signature Tortuguero scam is the La Pavona pirate boat fare inflation: touts in unmarked polos intercept arriving travelers at the dock parking lot before they can read the schedule board, claim the next public boat is full or cancelled, and push them onto a private boat for $20 to $30 cash — when the official Tortuguero village run is 4,000 colones ($8). The shadow turtle-walk is the second-most-documented and the most ethically loaded: an unlicensed local loiters between Aracari Garden Hostel and the soccer field at dusk, offers a $15 cash walk to "his cousin's nesting beach" — but is barred from Sea Turtle Conservancy nesting sites, walks travelers to the wrong beach in the wrong direction with a flashlight that scares the turtles, and most often produces no laydown at all. The Sea Turtle Conservancy walk ($35, ICT-licensed guide, red-filter flashlight) is the only legal turtle-walk option from June through October. Three more scams complete the village's roster: the canal-tour cash-markup at the dock (boatmen with laminated wildlife photos quote $50 to $60 cash for three-hour canal tours that hotel-booked guides run for $25 to $35); the fake park conservation fee collector at the Cuatro Esquinas trailhead (a man in a green polo asks for $20 cash on top of the official $15 SINAC ticket, with no printed receipt book and no official kiosk presence); and the jungle-lodge last-night room theft at Mawamba, Pachira, Tortuga, and Aracari Garden, where stilt-cabin doors are often louvered for airflow and a small staff insider knows which guests are leaving on the next morning's 6 AM boat with their suitcases already packed.

The risk zones are the La Pavona dock parking lot at every arrival; the Tortuguero village dock and Main Street at boat-arrival times; the dusk-lit village beach between Aracari Garden Hostel and the lighthouse during turtle-nesting season (June–October); the Cuatro Esquinas SINAC trailhead; and any stilt-cabin or jungle-lodge room on the last night of a multi-day all-inclusive. The safe default: book the Tortuguero leg only through ICT-registered packages (Mawamba Lodge, Pachira Lodge, Tortuga Lodge, Aninga Lodge, Evergreen Lodge, all four-star ICT-registered with door-to-door transfer included) — these handle the La Pavona transit, the canal tour, the turtle-walk, and the lodge in one bundled price ($350–$650 per person for 2-3 nights). For independent travel, pre-book the Cariari–La Pavona–Tortuguero public boat with COOPETRACA, Bananero, or Clic Clic at the booth inside the La Pavona terminal — never the unmarked polo on the dock. Buy SINAC park tickets only at sinac.go.cr ($15 adult; the website is clunky but the green-QR PDF works at the trailhead) — anyone collecting cash at Cuatro Esquinas is a scammer by definition; ask for the printed receipt book and the kiosk staff. For the turtle-walk, book only through Sea Turtle Conservancy (conserveturtles.org, $35) or the ICT-listed lodge tour desk; refuse every $15-cash offer at the village beach. For the canal tour, book through your lodge's tour desk or with Modesto Watson, Daryl Loth, or another ICT-registered guide — never the boatman with the laminated photos. On the last night, lock cash, passports, electronics, and watches inside the in-room safe (not "in the desk drawer") and double-check the door deadbolt — louvered doors are tropical-house standard but easy to lift off-track. Program 911, OIJ **800-8000-645**, the nearest Fuerza Pública (Tortuguero village station, dial 911 routing to Limón regional command **2758-0365**), and Hospital Tony Facio in Limón (**2758-2222**) into speed dial; helicopter medevac to San José CIMA is the option for anything serious.
