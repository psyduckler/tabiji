# Appendix B: Post-Scam Recovery Playbook {-}

If something in this book has happened to you, this appendix is where you start. The actions below are ordered by urgency, not by severity. Even if you are not sure yet how serious the scam was, working through the first two timelines will put you in the best possible position to recover your money, your evidence, and your peace of mind.

## In the first fifteen minutes

Leave the venue. If you are physically inside a place where you were scammed, do not argue, do not try to negotiate, and do not pay with a second form of payment. The single most important thing is to get yourself out of the environment and somewhere quiet.

Do not sign anything, including receipts. A signed credit-card slip is much harder to dispute than one you refused to sign. If staff are blocking your exit — this happens at the Avis LIR counter, at downtown La Fortuna cash-deposit storefronts, and at Playa Espadilla beach-parking ambushes — this is coercion or unlawful detention under Costa Rican law, and the correct response is to dial **911** and stay on the line. The Tourist Police Operations Center (launched September 2025, with hubs in San José and Jacó) has English dispatch; otherwise, 911 operators usually speak some English or will route you to a translator.

Screenshot everything you can, as discreetly as possible: the bill, the venue's *cédula jurídica* (tax ID, printed on every *factura electrónica* or displayed at the register), the street outside, the business sign, the card terminal, and the face of the person who served you if you can do it without escalating. For named operators (Avis Liberia, Hertz Liberia, Fox, the Route 618 "ranger" crew, the Costa Joe WhatsApp number, monteverdetourscr.com WhatsApp +506 7114-7550), photograph any business card, ID badge, or phone screen they show you. The information you capture in these fifteen minutes is the evidence you will use for weeks afterward.

If your bag, wallet, or rental-car contents were just stolen — at a Tamarindo lunch, a Playa Cocles swim, a Tracopa rest stop, a Coca-Cola bus boarding — walk directly to the nearest *Fuerza Pública* station or hotel front desk and ask them to call police. Activate Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) on a companion's phone if you were separated from yours — the La Fortuna Vrbo and Tamarindo RFID-relay cases both showed stolen devices moving to Limón or San Ramón within two hours. Do not confront suspected thieves yourself; police prefer to intercept them via device location than risk a confrontation.

If you were just attacked, drugged, or held at gunpoint — the Volio Falls machete pattern, the Hotel Cocal drink-spiking, the Los Jobos Tamarindo home invasion, the Guanacaste Airbnb shoot-out — dial **911** immediately; hide if possible; do not resist. OIJ data repeatedly shows that resistance correlates with shooting.

## Within the first hour

File a *denuncia* (formal police report) at the nearest **OIJ** (*Organismo de Investigación Judicial*) office. OIJ is the investigative police; *Fuerza Pública* is the uniformed street-level force. For a scam, fraud, theft, or home invasion, OIJ is the correct agency — they issue the case number you will need for Vrbo/Airbnb refunds, travel insurance claims, and credit-card chargebacks.

- **OIJ fraud tip line:** **800-8000-645** ("800-8000-OIJ"), 24/7 nationwide.
- **OIJ fraud email:** [oij_fraudes@poder-judicial.go.cr](mailto:oij_fraudes@poder-judicial.go.cr) — the named-operator reporting channel (Costa Joe, monteverdetourscr.com, Lava Land Tours Julio).
- **Tourist Police 24/7 line:** **2258-1008** / **2258-1022** — English-friendly.
- **Tourist Police Operations Center** (launched September 2025) — English dispatch via 911.

Major regional OIJ subdelegaciones:
- **San José OIJ HQ** — 2nd Circuito Judicial, Ciudad Judicial
- **Liberia OIJ** — Avenida 25 de Julio
- **Jacó OIJ subdelegación** — Avenida Pastor Díaz
- **Quepos OIJ** (Manuel Antonio) — central Quepos; Tourist Police Playa Espadilla opened September 2024
- **Puerto Viejo Fuerza Pública** — Calle 217 (OIJ via Limón HQ)

Say, in plain English or Spanish: *"Fui víctima de un robo / fraude. Necesito presentar una denuncia."* ("I was robbed / scammed. I need to file a report.") You do not need fluent Spanish; the officers have scripts and will type your statement. Ask for the **denuncia number** — you will need it for card chargeback, Vrbo/Airbnb dispute, and travel-insurance claim.

Freeze the card that was used. Costa Rica-accessible emergency lines for the major issuers are listed in Appendix C. Have your card number (or the last four digits) and the approximate transaction amount ready. For restaurant, taxi, rental-car, or tour overcharges, also call your bank's fraud line directly — the number on the back of your card — because fraud desks sometimes freeze faster than Visa/Mastercard's generic lines.

If your passport was photographed, held, or taken by anyone involved — at a Puerto Viejo bike shop, a Hotel Cocal in-room safe, a fake LIR "porter" — notify your embassy or consulate immediately. Passport data tied to a scam reappears in fraud attempts for months afterward. U.S. citizens can call the Embassy in San José at **+506 2519-2000** (after-hours +506 2220-3127).

## Within the first twenty-four hours

File a chargeback with your card issuer. Use the OIJ *denuncia* number as documentation. Chargebacks initiated within 24 hours of the transaction have a significantly higher success rate than those filed a week later. Visa, Mastercard, and American Express global network rules give strong chargeback rights for unauthorized, materially misrepresented, or goods-not-delivered charges — one reason we recommend credit over debit cards for every Costa Rica trip.

The chargeback window is 60 days from the statement date for most issuers (120 days for some travel protections, up to 540 days for fraud with Amex Platinum). Do not wait. Chase Sapphire Reserve, Capital One Venture X, and Amex Platinum have all successfully refunded documented Costa Rica scams: monteverdetourscr.com ($1,000), Hotel Societal La Fortuna ($550 CAD), Avis Liberia phantom damage, Uber SJO fare gouges. PayPal chargeback is much slower and less reliable; Zelle / SINPE Móvil / Venmo / Wise / Western Union have **no chargeback mechanism** — your money is gone the moment it lands.

Report to **Defensoría del Consumidor** at **[consumidor.go.cr](https://www.consumidor.go.cr)** — Costa Rica's national consumer-protection body. It does not typically recover individual losses, but complaints feed enforcement and get bad operators fined. For Ley 7472 violations (menus without printed prices, missing *factura*, undisclosed service charges), this is the correct channel. For tour-operator fraud, also email ICT via **[visitcostarica.com/en/contact-us](https://www.visitcostarica.com/en/contact-us)** and file at **2299-5800** — they can revoke the operator's license.

Email your travel-insurance provider with a summary of what happened, timestamps, the OIJ denuncia number, and photographs of the bill, damage, or transaction. Even if you are not sure whether the loss is covered, opening the claim early protects your position. Allianz Global, World Nomads, SafetyWing, Chase Sapphire Reserve's travel protection, Capital One Venture X's travel benefits, and Amex Platinum's Premium Global Assist all accept CR claims when the OIJ denuncia is attached.

If the scam was a tour deposit, off-platform rental, or copycat-domain charge, open the dispute on the booking platform as well as with your card issuer:
- **Airbnb Resolution Center** — inside the Airbnb app, within 60 days of the issue.
- **Vrbo Host Damage / Guest Claim** — 14-day window; also email **trust-safety@vrbo.com** for pattern theft at named properties.
- **Booking.com Partner Support** — in-app or via the booking confirmation email.
- **Expedia / Hotels.com** — open the dispute in the app; escalate to card chargeback if Expedia stalls (documented in the $550 Societal La Fortuna case).
- **Viator / GetYourGuide / TripAdvisor Experiences** — marketplace refund protection applies; file within the operator's cancellation window.

## Within the first week

File the full travel-insurance claim with all evidence: OIJ denuncia number, card chargeback reference, photographs, itemized receipts, police report, hospital report if applicable, and a timeline.

Leave a neutral, factual review on Google Maps and TripAdvisor. Describe what happened, the amounts involved, and the date, without emotional language or accusations. Reviews from recent victims are the single most reliable way future travelers avoid the same scam — u/Sweet_Ad6953 and u/Fun_Jackfruit_3626 found u/rmh901's monteverdetourscr.com post via exactly this mechanism. Do not name individual staff members; describe the business by its official name and location only.

Consider a parallel complaint with the specific regulator:
- **Taxi overcharge (meter, flat rate, dynamic currency):** *Consejo de Transporte Público* at **2523-2000** or [ctp.go.cr/inconformidades](https://www.ctp.go.cr/inconformidades); include dashboard yellow-triangle ID number.
- **Tour operator misrepresentation:** ICT at **2299-5800**; specify the operator's ICT registration or lack thereof.
- **Rental-car mandatory-insurance ambush:** Defensoría del Consumidor + card chargeback + public review; Adobe and Vamos are ICT-registered and do not run the ambush.
- **Telecom/SIM fraud (Claro, Kolbi kiosk):** SUTEL (telecom regulator) at [sutel.go.cr](https://sutel.go.cr) + card chargeback + Ley 7472 complaint at consumidor.go.cr.
- **Airbnb/Vrbo home invasion or calendar-leak breach:** OIJ + Airbnb/Vrbo Resolution Center + card chargeback + U.S. Embassy (the November 25, 2025 Security Alert is updated from these reports).

## If the scam involved drink-spiking or physical harm

**Go to a hospital before you go to the police.** Toxicology tests have a short window (6–12 hours for most benzodiazepines and scopolamine / *burundanga*) and cannot be run retroactively. At the hospital, ask specifically for a drug screen or toxicology panel. Keep the hospital report; it is evidence.

Costa Rica's emergency number is **911** from any phone. English dispatch is available through the Tourist Police Operations Center. If you are inside a venue (Hotel Cocal pool bar, Monkey Bar, Le Loft) and drink-spiking is suspected, have a friend call 911 *before* you leave — bill pressure often escalates once the scammer realizes you are leaving.

**Private hospitals are better for foreigners.** The public CCSS system requires residency; private hospitals handle travel insurance directly. Primary private options:
- **Hospital CIMA San José** (Escazú) — +506 2208-1000 — the top private hospital, English staff, full ER.
- **Clínica Bíblica** (central San José) — +506 2522-1000 — private, English-friendly.
- **Hospital CIMA Liberia** — +506 2690-4800 — the Guanacaste beach-corridor option.
- **Hospital Max Terán Vals** (Quepos, Manuel Antonio area) — public, but the local option.
- **MEPE Hospital** (Puerto Viejo / Hone Creek / Limón corridor) — +506 2758-0580 — public; for serious Caribbean emergencies, helicopter transfer to San José CIMA.
- **CIMA Guanacaste Liberia / Hospital San Rafael Arcángel** (Liberia) — private, English-friendly.

Your embassy can coordinate English-speaking medical care and, if your passport was involved, coordinate a replacement. Consular registration before travel is worth doing — the U.S. **STEP** program (step.state.gov), the UK Foreign Office travel advisory registration, the Canadian Registration of Canadians Abroad (travel.gc.ca), and Australian Smartraveller all allow pre-registration that shortens the response time in an emergency.

If your passport was stolen, stop at the U.S. Embassy San José (Calle 98 Vía 104, Pavas) for an emergency replacement; walk-in services run 8 AM – 11:30 AM weekdays; after-hours +506 2220-3127. Bring a police report, a passport photo (CIMA Escazú has a photo booth), and $135 for a replacement.

## Passport and document replacement

- **U.S.:** U.S. Embassy San José, Calle 98 Vía 104, Pavas; **+506 2519-2000** / after-hours **+506 2220-3127**.
- **Canada:** Canadian Embassy San José, Oficentro Ejecutivo La Sabana, **+506 2242-4400**.
- **U.K.:** British Embassy San José, Paseo Colón, **+506 2258-2025**.
- **EU:** individual embassies in San José; consult the EU Commission delegation at **+506 2283-2959**.
- **Australia:** the nearest Australian mission is in Mexico City; CR contact via Honorary Consul in San José, check dfat.gov.au for current number.
- **Ireland / Germany / France:** embassies in San José; check the relevant foreign ministry site for the current Pavas / Rohrmoser address and number.

Bring a police report (OIJ denuncia), a passport photo, and the replacement fee. CIMA Hospital Escazú has a photo booth; several Pavas-area shops also issue compliant passport photos. For lost U.S. passports, the walk-in window is 8:00 AM – 11:30 AM weekdays; you will typically receive an emergency replacement within 24 hours.

If your credit cards were in the stolen wallet, call the card issuer emergency line (Appendix C), have the card expressed to the Embassy mail drop or your hotel. Visa, Mastercard, and Amex all offer emergency card replacement in Costa Rica within 24–72 hours.
