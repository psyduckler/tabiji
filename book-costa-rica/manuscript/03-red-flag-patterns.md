# The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns {-}

The fifty-four scams in this book may sound different from each other, but most of them are variations on just six patterns. Learn the patterns and you do not need to memorize every scam. You will recognize a new one the moment it starts to unfold, even if it has never been written down.

Costa Rican scam patterns are not European or Southeast Asian scam patterns. Pickpocketing concentrates in narrow zones (the Coca-Cola terminal in San José, a handful of beach pullouts, the Tracopa rest stops) rather than across every plaza. The classic European street games — gold-ring finds, bracelet tying, bird-poop-and-wallet — are rare. What Costa Rica has built instead is a layered con economy: fake uniforms at national-park gates, a counter-level rental-car insurance trap that converts a $123 online quote into $444, a drug-and-robbery pipeline on Jacó and Tamarindo main strips, key-fob jammers at Caribbean beach pullouts, inside-job burglaries at isolated Airbnbs, and an SEO-copycat-domain layer (monteverdetourscr.com, not monteverdetours.com) that silently drains tour deposits. Read each of the six patterns below once, then let them settle into the way you make choices on the trip.

## Pattern 1: A "uniform" on a public road is not an authority — verify every official

This is the single most Costa Rica–specific pattern in the book, and the U.S. Embassy San José flagged it directly in its November 25, 2025 Security Alert: "impostors impersonating police officers and asking for money."

On Route 618 to Manuel Antonio National Park, three-to-six-man crews in khaki "Indiana Jones" outfits with lanyards, radios, and printed "SINAC" or "PARK" lettering block every tourist car 500 meters to one kilometer before the real gate. A 146-upvote r/CostaRicaTravel thread (u/Fearless-Tree-7478, January 2026) has them shouting "you know what? I just cancelled your tickets, no entry for you today" when a driver refused their $12 parking lot; one commenter was asked for "6 million colones" (~$11,000); another had a "scammer grab a metal rod and threaten the car." The same template has migrated to the La Fortuna Waterfall access road (reflective-vest "permit collectors" 500 meters before the ADIFORT gate), to Rio Chollín (roadside "parking mafia"), and to Tamarindo's main strip (a 305-upvote r/travel thread on yellow-vested *guachimán* crews who box tourists in and demand $10+ to release them).

The defense is a single principle: real Costa Rican authorities are attached to permanent infrastructure, carry laminated credentials, and take payment at a turnstile or a bank — never on a shoulder. Buy Manuel Antonio tickets only at **sinac.go.cr** ($18.08 adult, $5.65 child). Buy La Fortuna Waterfall tickets only at the concrete ADIFORT gate at the end of the access road ($18, card only). Verify any ICT-licensed guide at **visitcostarica.com**'s directory of 2,500+ certified guides — photograph their *carné* ICT card before paying. Real *Fuerza Pública* officers wear dark-blue uniforms with a shield patch, carry a *cédula* (ID), and issue a *Boleta de Citación* that is paid at Banco de Costa Rica — never to the officer in cash. A "traffic cop" removing your license plate on a Playa Espadilla street at sunset is the scam. If you are blocked by a roadside "official," lay on the horn and creep forward; they always move. Report to the Tourist Police station on Playa Espadilla (opened September 2024, 16 officers), to the new Tourist Police Operations Center (English dispatch, launched September 2025), or dial **911**.

*You will see this pattern in: Manuel Antonio Route 618 fake-ranger roadblock, fake SINAC ticket booths, unofficial "ICT-certified" guides at the Manuel Antonio gate, La Fortuna Waterfall "unofficial parking permit" collectors, Rio Chollín "parking mafia," Tamarindo *guachimán* yellow-vest extortion, Jacó beach-parking reflective-vest scam, and any roadside "permit" collector anywhere on the Pacific coast.*

## Pattern 2: Any deposit outside a platform — SINPE, Zelle, cash on arrival — is a scam

The single most common fraud in the Costa Rican tour-and-rental ecosystem is the advance-fee deposit that evaporates. It runs through two layers. On the individual side is "Costa Joe" — real name Joseph Goodwin — a mid-twenties operator named in a 35-upvote r/costarica PSA and Facebook warnings for advance-fee deposits across car rentals, golf carts, Airbnbs, jet skis, and shuttles; he sets up multiple sock-puppet Facebook profiles that "vouch" for each other. On the domain side is monteverdetourscr.com, a copycat of the legitimate monteverdetours.com (operating since 2002); a 52-upvote r/CostaRicaTravel PSA (u/rmh901, November 2024) documents a $1,000 USD deposit lost for four people on an August 28 Monteverde tour that was "cancelled the day before" — thirty-five emails and three months later, no refund. The same domain farm runs "Jaco Tours" and "Arenal Tours" copycats. The only recovery that has worked is credit-card chargeback through Chase, Capital One, or Amex — not PayPal, not Zelle, not SINPE Móvil, not wire, not crypto.

Adjacent to the tour-deposit scam is the short-stay rental version. A Canadian traveler lost $550 CAD on a Societal La Fortuna Expedia booking (76 upvotes, u/Jaded_Organization69, May 2025): hotel cancelled on arrival, claimed "local government issues," Expedia couldn't reach them, refund denied. r/CostaRicaTravel "Hostel scam in La Fortuna" (u/maylyinmor) documents an $83 Arenal Backpackers "private room" that turned out to be "a bed inside a concrete tube with no floor space."

The defense is categorical. Book tours only through Viator, GetYourGuide, TripAdvisor Experiences, or Expedia Tours — all three carry platform refund protection. Book short-stays only through airbnb.com, booking.com, or vrbo.com via the official app, and pay with a credit card (Visa, Mastercard, Amex). Never pay any deposit by SINPE Móvil, Zelle, PayPal Friends-and-Family, Venmo, Wise, Western Union, or crypto for a rental or a tour in Costa Rica, ever. Cap prepayments at $50–$100 — legitimate operators accept the balance on arrival. If the operator's own website does not link out to their TripAdvisor or Viator listing, that is u/alextoria's red-flag test — walk away.

*You will see this pattern in: "Costa Joe" / Joseph Goodwin Jacó WhatsApp fraud, monteverdetourscr.com and "Roy's nature guide" Monteverde copycat fraud, Societal La Fortuna Expedia freeze-out, La Fortuna Lava Land Tours (Julio) sloth-tour bait-and-switch, SJO fake-shuttle WhatsApp prepayment fraud, Jacó ATV WhatsApp rentals, Puerto Viejo fake MEPE shuttle tickets, and any Facebook Marketplace accommodation listing.*

## Pattern 3: Mandatory insurance isn't mandatory — book Adobe or Vamos and bring your credit card CDW letter

The highest-volume complaint in r/CostaRicaTravel across 2024–2026 is the rental-car "mandatory insurance" bait-and-switch. The mechanics are identical at every location: a third-party broker (Expedia, Priceline, Kayak, Hotwire, Economybookings) advertises a too-good-to-be-true rate; at the LIR or SJO counter the tourist is told either to accept a $25–$40/day local insurance stack OR to place a $2,000–$32,000 credit-card deposit with "20% withheld for any damage"; the credit-card CDW coverage letter from Chase Sapphire, Capital One Venture X, or Amex Platinum is refused as "not valid in Costa Rica." A 109-upvote r/CostaRicaTravel thread (u/Live-Yogurt5880, March 2025) describes one family left on the side of the road with five suitcases at SJO after refusing a $2,032 mandatory-insurance demand. A 34-upvote PSA "Don't rent Avis in Liberia" logs a $350 online quote becoming $700 at the counter. A 29-upvote thread "Dont rent from Avis liberia airport" calls it "a highly fraudulent and scamming location." The pattern repeats at Hertz, Fox, Europcar, Budget, and NextCar at LIR — and at Hertz, Fox, and Budget at SJO. A separate 187-upvote thread (u/fineartbydan, January 2025) documents $14,000 in camera gear stolen from a "locked" Tamarindo rental via RFID key-fob relay theft.

Costa Rican law requires only **Third-Party Liability (TPL/SLI)**, about **$10–$20 per day**, covering injuries to other people. Everything else — Collision Damage Waiver, Theft, Supplemental Liability — is optional, and most major U.S. credit cards (Chase Sapphire Reserve primary, Amex Platinum, Capital One Venture X primary) cover it.

The defense is two-layered. Layer one: book DIRECT with **Adobe** (adobecar.com; MyTanFeet $25 discount) or **Vamos** (vamosrentacar.com; 10% cash discount) — both are ICT-licensed, 4.5+ Google, all-in transparent pricing. If you use a U.S. brand, use the U.S. site (alamo.com, not alamo.cr) — one 18-upvote thread documented a $300 savings for 16 days. Skip Expedia, Priceline, Kayak, Hotwire, Economybookings for Costa Rican rentals entirely. Bring a printed credit-card CDW coverage letter on bank letterhead specifically covering Costa Rica. Accept only TPL/SLI; decline everything else in writing. Budget $1,500–$3,000 of credit-card room for the deposit hold. Layer two: video walk-around of every panel (top, bottom, both sides, undercarriage, spare tire, odometer, fuel, dashboard) on pickup, narrate defects into the mic, save to cloud. Repeat at drop-off with the agent visible. Buy a Faraday key-fob pouch ($10 on Amazon) before the trip — blocks RFID relay theft on keyless-entry vehicles. Never leave anything in the car: cameras, laptops, passports, AirTag/Tile trackers, designer sunglasses all go in the hotel safe. "*En el carro nunca.*"

*You will see this pattern in: Avis LIR "mandatory insurance" $123→$444 ambush, Budget/Hertz/Fox/Europcar LIR counter phantom-fee stack, SJO Hertz/Fox/Expedia $2,000 ambush, SJO Adobe/Vamos as the safe default, Tamarindo RFID relay theft outside Witch's Rock, and the *pinchonazo* flat-tire robbery on Route 21.*

## Pattern 4: "Your bill is counterfeit" — watch your own note leave your hand

Costa Rica's financial con surface has three classic moves, all close relatives. The first is the *cambista* rate gouge in Parque Central San José and around Plaza de la Cultura: whispered "cambio, cambio?" offers at ₡450 per USD when the Banco Central de Costa Rica (BCCR) posts ₡515–₡530 — a 10–20% skim. The second is the counterfeit-bill swap: the *cambista* or taxi driver takes your real $100 or ₡20,000 note, palms it, and returns a crisp counterfeit that feels plastic-smooth and lacks the Carlos Luis Fallas watermark and color-shifting security thread. A 10-upvote r/CostaRicaTravel reply (u/ODA564, 2025): "pay in colones (and beware of the 'this bill is counterfeit' scam)." The third is the Tamarindo / Liberia ATM-skimming and dynamic-currency-conversion combo — card-skimming overlays on standalone curb ATMs, plus a 5–9% worse rate when the machine asks "USD or colones?" and the tourist picks USD.

Adjacent variants: the Manuel Antonio and Jacó "gringo menu" with 13% IVA (VAT) and 10% service added on top of quoted prices; the Playa Espadilla beach-service cash-only ambush where "₡15,000 for parking + umbrella" escalates to "₡30,000" at pickup under Costa Rican law (Ley 7472) that technically requires *precio total* (all-in pricing); the Delta / Recope gas-station "pump not zeroed" trick where you pay ₡10,000 for ₡4,000 of fuel; the Global Exchange LIR airport kiosk offering ₡450/USD while BCCR is at ₡515.

The defense is a single rule: **change money only at a bank branch or inside-lobby ATM, and pay in colones everywhere**. Skip LIR's Global Exchange and any whispered "cambio" approach. Withdraw colones from Scotiabank, BAC, BCR, or Banco Nacional lobby ATMs during business hours (8 AM – 4 PM) — the mid-market rate is ~2% off BCCR, vs the airport kiosk's 6–10%. Check every ₡20,000 note for the Fallas watermark and the color-shifting thread (it flips green/blue when tilted). Refuse dynamic currency conversion every time — always "colones." For restaurants, ask *"Está incluido el IVA y el servicio?"* — the menu is legally required to say yes. At the gas station, get out and verify the pump reads ₡0.00 before the attendant starts. If a driver or *cambista* claims your ₡20,000 is counterfeit, refuse to take it back and demand to see the original in their hand — photograph it and call **911**. The swap itself is the scam.

*You will see this pattern in: Parque Central San José *cambista* counterfeit swap, Tamarindo Banco Nacional ATM skimming + bill-swap, Liberia Global Exchange rate gouge + Delta gas-station "pump not zeroed" + colón short-change, Manuel Antonio "gringo menu" hidden service charges, Playa Espadilla beach-parking cash-escalation, and the Puerto Viejo beach-service cash-only ambush.*

## Pattern 5: Fake emergencies, fake flat tires, fake "park is full" — the urgency is the tell

The *pinchonazo* ("puncture") is Costa Rica's signature rental-car robbery. On the Liberia–Guanacaste corridor (Route 21, Pan-American Highway, the 29-kilometer run to Playas del Coco), an accomplice pokes a tire valve or wedges a nail while you fuel at a Delta or Recope station. A few kilometers down the road you hear a wobble; a "good Samaritan" motorcyclist arrives within two minutes of your pullover; one works the jack while the other empties your back seat. Stolen gear — laptops, cameras, passports — reappears in Limón pawn shops within a week. The January 2025 r/CostaRicaTravel "ROBBERY WARNING" (187 upvotes) logs $14,000 in camera equipment lost to the variant that runs on "locked" keyless-entry rentals in Tamarindo.

The same urgency template runs through the fake-ranger Route 618 scam ("the park is CLOSED, you can't go further"), the Puerto Viejo "young ladies, your car is being towed" extortion, the Monteverde "the reserve is SOLD OUT, my cousin has special access" bait-and-switch, the Costa Joe "last golf cart available today only" WhatsApp deposit push, and the monteverdetourscr.com "tour cancelled, water levels too high" force-majeure refund dodge. In every case the pressure to decide *now* is the scammer's core weapon. Slowing down breaks the pattern.

The defense is a refusal to accept any emergency that wasn't in your own planning. On a wobble: **keep driving** to the next populated area — a staffed gas station, a police post, a shopping center — never pull over on a remote shoulder. Call the rental agency's 24/7 roadside line — **Adobe 2542-4800**, **Vamos 4000-0557** — for a 45-minute legitimate dispatch. Reject unsolicited help that arrives within two minutes; that is the thief tell. At the park gate, drive past every khaki figure to the real turnstile at the end of the road. At the Monteverde or La Fortuna reserve, hold your pre-booked PDF on your phone — the timed entry and green QR code is the only valid ticket. When an operator says "today only," treat that as a *No*. Legitimate Costa Rican tour rates are stable: Selvatura zipline $80, Manuel Antonio $18.08, La Fortuna Waterfall $18, Monteverde Cloud Forest $29 per circuit, Don Juan coffee tour $52, Caribe Shuttle Puerto Viejo $54 door-to-door. Anything demanding immediate payment 30% below those rates is the bait.

*You will see this pattern in: Liberia *pinchonazo* Route 21 flat-tire robbery, Manuel Antonio "park is full / park is closed" roadblock, Monteverde "reserve sold out, cousin has slots" ambush, Jacó beach-parking "fine or tow truck" shakedown, La Fortuna jeep-boat-jeep "today only" kiosk overpricing, and the Tamarindo *guachimán* "pay $10 or the windshield gets smeared" variant.*

## Pattern 6: Do not follow a stranger — into a jungle path, into a dark villa, into your own hotel room

This is the most physically serious pattern in the Costa Rica book, and it runs through both the Caribbean and the Central Pacific. On the Caribbean side, the Volio Falls and Bribrí / Kekoldi waterfall trails have been the scene of machete robberies since 2023. A 210-upvote r/CostaRicaTravel thread (u/kentasasakado, July 2025) — "CRIME: The Machete guy did happen; proof attached" — pairs a police report with a map circling the attack spot. A 111-upvote thread (u/Nervous-Rabbit2253, March 2025) describes three men surrounding a woman's car after stalking her in Puerto Viejo; her mother had to run. A 1,876-upvote r/news thread (February 2026) reported the Cahuita home-invasion murder of American surfer Kurt Van Dyke. A parallel pattern runs in Tamarindo and San José: isolated beachfront villas in Los Jobos, Playa Negra, Playa Grande, and Guanacaste-outskirts Airbnbs are hit by 4–6-man gangs who scout via the Airbnb/VRBO public calendar and breach a second-floor gate at 9 PM–2 AM. The July 2025 Los Jobos Tamarindo killing of Canadian life coach Christopher Deir — shot in the hallway when his partner's screams drew him out — is the anchor case. A 369-upvote r/CostaRicaTravel thread (u/2Coneone, October 2025) describes a family held at gunpoint for thirty minutes at a Guanacaste Airbnb; robbers "kept asking for the cocaine" — a scouted job.

Drink-spiking runs the same "follow a stranger" pattern in Jacó and Tamarindo. The Hotel Cocal & Casino pool bar in Jacó has 187+ TripAdvisor reviews naming it as a drink-spiking and in-room-safe theft zone — one reviewer was robbed of "$2k and my passport while I was out fishing." Tamarindo's Monkey Bar / Crazy Monkey / casino district runs the "sexpat" variant: a flirty bartender or dancer says she lives "an hour away, needs somewhere to stay"; next morning she demands $500 with a pimp threat; if you refuse, her "male friends will be very effective in collections." Drug-dealer stalking in Puerto Viejo — "you want weed?" followed by a 20-meter walk down a dark jungle path — ends at a machete-point robbery or a baggie of oregano.

The defense is layered. For short-stays, book only 24-hour guarded hotels (Hotel Grano de Oro, Intercontinental Real Costa Rica, Hyatt Place San José, Hotel Belmar Monteverde, Diria Tamarindo, La Costa de Papito, Witch's Rock Surf Camp) or Airbnbs inside guarded gated communities (Hacienda Pinilla, Reserva Conchal, Avenida Escazú, Río Oro, Forum Tower). Message any host in writing before booking: "24/7 armed guard? CCTV? panic button? patrol logs?" — "yes to all four" is the minimum bar. Calendar-block ±2 days so the arrival is not visible. Never geo-tag the property on social media. Program **911**, **OIJ 800-8000-645**, and **U.S. Embassy San José +506 2519-2000** into your phone speed dial on landing. For nightlife, drink only bottled/canned drinks opened in front of you, never accept a drink from a stranger, never bring a bar companion back to your room, and take an Uber or tuk-tuk home rather than walking Puerto Viejo's beach path or Jacó's unlit side streets. For jungle hikes, use only ICT-certified operators (Caribe Shuttle, Terraventuras, Exploradores Outdoors, Jaguar Rescue Center); never a "guide" offering a $20–$30 cash hike on the main street. For drugs, do not buy; the mugging, fake-product, and bust-risk stack is absurdly high.

If something does happen: do not resist. OIJ data repeatedly shows that resistance correlates with shooting. Hide, call 911, comply. File at **Fuerza Pública** and **OIJ** within 24 hours — the *denuncia* number is required for Vrbo/Airbnb refunds, travel insurance claims, and card chargebacks.

*You will see this pattern in: Puerto Viejo Volio Falls machete robbery, Puerto Viejo "you want weed?" drug-dealer robbery setup, Cahuita home-invasion murder, Los Jobos Tamarindo home invasion, Playa Negra honeymoon Vrbo attempted robbery, San José Airbnb calendar-leak armed robberies, La Fortuna Vrbo electronics inside-job, Hotel Cocal Jacó drink-spiking, and Tamarindo casino / Monkey Bar "sexpat" extortion.*
