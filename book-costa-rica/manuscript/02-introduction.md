# How to Use This Book {-}

Costa Rica is extraordinary. Arenal's volcano cone lit up at dusk, a sloth twenty meters up in the canopy at Manuel Antonio, Monteverde's cloud-forest hummingbirds in a low mist, the Pacific breaking at Playa Tamarindo, a plate of *gallo pinto* on a wooden balcony in Santa Teresa, a boat gliding across Lake Arenal between two rain squalls. The country welcomed more than 3.2 million international tourists in 2024 (ICT / *Instituto Costarricense de Turismo* figures) and earned its reputation on a single phrase — *pura vida* — that is genuinely meant and repeatedly lived up to. Tico hospitality is real. Most interactions you will have on this trip will be warm, straightforward, and safe.

It is also, for first-time visitors, a country with a very specific catalog of high-value scams that cluster in predictable places. The November 25, 2025 U.S. Embassy San José Security Alert explicitly flagged the rise in vacation-rental armed robberies and in "impostors impersonating police officers and asking for money." *Tico Times* 2025 coverage of rising crime named Jacó and Tamarindo specifically. Avis Liberia airport has three separate 2025–2026 Reddit PSAs with dollar losses attached — a $123 online quote that becomes $444 at the counter; a $1,372 "mandatory insurance" ambush on arrival; a $300 flat-tire charge at return. Manuel Antonio's Route 618 "fake park ranger" crews block every tourist car 500 meters short of the real SINAC gate and quote "6 million colones" for parking. An inside-job burglary at a La Fortuna Vrbo with 239 upvotes on r/CostaRicaTravel cost a family their laptops, cameras, and cash on the last night of the trip. A Canadian life coach was shot during a home invasion at a Los Jobos Tamarindo rental in July 2025.

We want you to come home with stories about the volcano, the sloths, the coffee, the waves, and the *pura vida* — not about a $2,000 "mandatory insurance" stack at LIR or a $1,000 deposit lost to a monteverdetourscr.com "tour" that never happened.

That is why this book is short and practical. It documents fifty-four specific scams that trip up first-time visitors across eight Costa Rican cities, and it tells you the exact moves that stop them. Nothing in it is theoretical. Every scam has been posted to r/CostaRicaTravel, r/costarica, or a related subreddit, cross-referenced against U.S. Embassy Security Alerts, OIJ reports, *Tico Times* coverage, TripAdvisor operator complaints, and confirmed by multiple traveler accounts with dollar or colón losses attached.

## How each scam is written

Every scam entry follows the same short format. First, the name of the scam, the category it falls into (overcharging, fraud, robbery, pickpocket, drink-spiking, platform fraud, and so on), and how serious and how common it is. Then two short sections: **How this scam works** — a plain narrative of how it unfolds in real time, with real USD and colón amounts where we have them — and **How to avoid it**, the specific moves that shut it down. Each entry ends with where it happens. If you can read one entry, you can read them all.

## The single most important chapter

The next chapter, **The Six Universal Red-Flag Patterns**, is the most important part of this book. Most scams in Costa Rica are variations on six patterns. Learn the patterns and you will spot a new scam the moment it starts — even one that is not in this book. Spend ten quiet minutes on that chapter before your trip. The rest of the book will then read as practical examples of patterns you already understand.

## The three bonuses at the back

After the city chapters, you will find three appendices that we hope you treasure more than the city chapters themselves.

The first is the **Costa Rican exit-phrase card**. Short Spanish phrases you can say, word for word, at the moments when your politeness would normally cost you money. Take a screenshot of it on your phone.

The second is the **recovery playbook**. A step-by-step flow of what to do in the first fifteen minutes, hour, day, and week after a scam. Most scam books only tell you how to avoid scams. If the worst happens, you want this.

The third is the **emergency contacts appendix**. Police, ambulance, tourist lines, embassies, and the nearest English-speaking medical care, organized so you can find what you need in seconds.

## A suggested way to use the book

A week or two before your trip, read the first two chapters and skim the three appendices. Read the chapters for the cities you will be visiting. Take a screenshot of the phrase card and the emergency contacts on your phone. If anything in this book turns out to apply during your trip, open it again in the taxi or in your hotel room and check what to do.

For most visitors, the two biggest preventable losses are at the airport rental counter and at the short-stay rental itself. If you do nothing else, read the LIR and SJO rental-car sections before you fly in and the Airbnb / Vrbo sections for any coastal city you plan to sleep in. Booking direct with Adobe or Vamos — not through Expedia, Priceline, or Kayak — and choosing a 24-hour guarded hotel over an isolated beachfront villa avoids most of the damage most tourists actually take.

You will almost certainly not need most of it. That is the point.

Welcome to Costa Rica. *Pura vida.*
