Santa Teresa is the Nicoya tip's surf-and-yoga town — a single-road village strung along a Pacific cliff between Mal Pais, Playa Carmen, and Playa Hermosa Norte, reached only by Paquera ferry from Puntarenas plus a thirty-minute washboard climb on Route 624. The geography drives the scam roster: a road too rough for 2WD compacts, a nightlife strip everyone funnels through after dark, and isolated dirt-road villas that scammers can scout with a drone. The anchor case is the 2024 fake-police villa heist that OIJ traced back to drone-scouted luxury rentals: eight men in dark-blue uniforms, badges, and a credible authority demand at the front gate zip-tied ten guests inside an isolated Mal Pais villa for over an hour. The same template — uniformed crew, dinner-out timing, drone-confirmed empty house, breach via the front gate — has run repeatedly on isolated Playa Hermosa Norte, Carmen, and Mal Pais rentals through 2024 and 2025. Sitting alongside that is the Route 624 underbody-damage rental inflation: the spine-shaker between Cobano and Mal Pais grinds along washboard, river crossings, and rutted dirt that tears at oil pans on any 2WD compact, and crooked agents at the LIR and SJO drop-offs invent $500 to $2,000 of "underbody" or "chassis" charges with no inspection-on-pickup baseline to dispute against. TripAdvisor's Santa Teresa forum has flagged this for years — budget a 4WD Adobe or Vamos rental just for the chassis clearance. Three more scams round out the roster: the Pranamar-lookalike yoga-retreat deposit fraud (Instagram-promoted shalas with Calendly booking pages that take $1,500 to $3,000 by Zelle or wire to a personal U.S. routing-number account, then either downgrade arrivals to a bare Mal Pais Airbnb or vanish entirely — Pranamar, Florblanca, Anamaya, and Vajra Sol are the real ICT-registered Santa Teresa retreats); La Lora Amarilla and Banana Beach Club drink-spiking with forced ATM withdrawals (a small ring spiking welcome shots at the two Playa Carmen-strip clubs, then walking drugged solo travelers to the nearest curb ATM for $300–$500 cashouts before the spiked tourist wakes up alone in a side-street); and the Paquera ferry unlicensed-van shuttle markup (English-fluent touts at the ferry terminal with fake "Tropical Tours" clipboards charge $50 to $80 for the Cobano hop the public collectivo bus runs for $4 to $8).

The risk zones are isolated dirt-road villas in Mal Pais, Playa Hermosa Norte, Carmen, and the back streets above Santa Teresa Centro after sunset; the Route 624 stretch between Cobano and Playa Carmen for both rental damage and unsolicited "help" stops; the Paquera and Naranjo ferry terminal on every arrival; and the Playa Carmen nightlife strip — La Lora Amarilla, Banana Beach Club, Kooks — between 9:00 PM and 2:00 AM. The safe default: book lodging only at staffed hotels with on-site security (Pranamar Villas, Florblanca Resort, Latitude 10, Hotel Tropico Latino, Anamaya — all guarded, all real, all ICT-registered) and skip the isolated dirt-road villa entirely on a first trip. If you must book a villa, message the host in writing — *"24/7 on-site staff? gate intercom? CCTV? gardener present overnight?"* — and decline anything without all four. Calendar-block ±2 days on the Airbnb / Vrbo public calendar so the arrival date is not visible. Rent only through Adobe (adobecar.com) or Vamos (vamosrentacar.com), choose a 4WD SUV — never a 2WD compact — and film a slow 360° walk-around of the chassis, undercarriage, oil pan, and tires before driving off the lot, narrating defects into the mic. At the LIR or SJO drop-off, demand the inspecting agent stand visible in the same frame for the return video. For yoga retreats, verify ICT registration at visitcostarica.com and pay only by credit card direct on the verified retreat's website — never Zelle, never wire, never a Calendly link from an Instagram DM. At nightlife, drink only bottled or canned drinks opened in front of you; never accept a "welcome" shot; travel in pairs after midnight; and if you feel a sudden numbness, slurred speech, or wobble, ask the bartender to call **911** and stay inside the venue. From Paquera, ride only the Costa Rica Tours collectivo bus or Interbus (interbusonline.com) — never an unmarked van. Program 911, the OIJ tip line **800-8000-645**, the Cobano Fuerza Pública (the nearest station, **2642-0211**), and the U.S. Embassy San José (**+506 2519-2000**) into speed dial on arrival.
